Title: Adverse case and permanent-loss risks
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/886982/000095012308011074/y71185exv99w1.htm (published 2008-09-16); https://www.goldmansachs.com/pressroom/press-releases/2008/berkshire-hathaway-invest (2008-09-23 term sheet only); https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/bcreg20080921a.htm (2008-09-21 bank-holding-company approval)
Date: 2008-09-23

The bear case is not merely lower investment-banking fees. A funding run, counterparty loss, collateral calls or illiquid asset marks can impair capital faster than a normal earnings model suggests. A preferred is junior to creditors and its dividend is not a Treasury guarantee. Warrants may expire worthless. A prestigious franchise cannot protect an investor from losses if leverage and liquidity exhaust the equity cushion. [Source above.]

Technical context: a financial institution's earning assets are supported by deposits, wholesale funding, creditors and equity with different seniority. Regulatory capital cannot all be distributed to investors. Analyze credit quality, liquidity and capital separately: a solvent institution can still face a funding run, and liquid assets do not prove all credit losses are recognized. For a preferred claim, identify whether dividends are cumulative, the issuer's redemption rights and the creditor cushion ahead of the security. For common, normalize provisioning and assess return on tangible equity rather than using industrial free-cash-flow conventions. Reported book value, tangible common capital and market capitalization are different denominators. [Source above; analytical framework rather than additional issuer measurements.]

Risk discipline: the adverse cases above are analyst stress tests grounded in the disclosed business and current circumstances, not quotations attributed to an unnamed contemporary short seller. No authenticated bearish price target was recovered. Separate a temporary quotation decline from permanent impairment of earning power or of the investor's claim. Ask whether the issuer can fund necessary operations without assuming favorable capital markets. Identify which changes are within management control and which are not. A large cash balance at the investor does not cure an issuer's leverage, a faulty operating thesis or an excessive purchase price. Do not soften a serious downside merely because an admired investor might be interested.

Read the financial, risk and fund-context documents together; no individual accounting ratio supplies a complete investment conclusion.
