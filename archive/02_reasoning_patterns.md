# Recurring reasoning patterns: 22 decision templates with failure cases

**As of 2026-10-25.** Analyst synthesis, not 22 numbered commandments Buffett published. Examples are dated by the explanation available to us, often retrospectively. Sizing bounds below are qualitative unless an actual dated policy is specified. No invented permanent 10%/20% cap, stop-loss or DCF discount rate. Each pattern cites the source IDs resolved below; section-level context is in companion letter notes. Decider Buffett except explicitly identified deputies/Abel.

## 1. Hidden securities inside an ignored operator

- **Trigger / evidence:** Securities portfolio materially exceeds quoted equity value; verify liabilities, taxes and ownership rights.
- **Valuation:** Sum of conservatively realizable assets less tax/friction; separately value the operating business.
- **Sizing bounds:** Concentrated only if realization and control are feasible; no universal weight.
- **Catalyst / exit:** Exchange, split or distribution; exit on realization or failure of the unlock.
- **Dated examples and counterevidence:** Sanborn (1961-01-30, account of 1960 separation, P60); Dempster (1963-07-10, securities accumulated after asset conversion, P63). Negative: Berkshire textiles (1990-03-02 retrospective, L89), where cheap assets did not solve economics.

## 2. Working-capital liquidation plus an operator

- **Trigger / evidence:** Inventory/receivables consume capital without adequate operating return; require an executable operating plan and a trusted manager.
- **Valuation:** Haircut inventory and receivables; credit only cash actually released and sustainable residual earnings.
- **Sizing bounds:** Control may be required; cap exposure by asset downside and attention/liquidity, not apparent book equity.
- **Catalyst / exit:** Release cash, reduce debt, reinvest outside the operation; sell if the operating plan cannot restore acceptable economics.
- **Dated examples and counterevidence:** Dempster/Harry Bottle (1963-07-10, P63); Berkshire/Ken Chace capital release into insurance (1978-03-14, L77). Negative: repeated textile recovery forecasts disappointed (same L77); final retrospective criticizes Berkshire purchase (L89).

## 3. Defined-event workout

- **Trigger / evidence:** A tender, merger, redemption or contractual exchange establishes a potential payoff and timetable; assess all approvals.
- **Valuation:** Probability-weighted spread net of break loss, carrying cost, tax and delay.
- **Sizing bounds:** Cash substitute only within loss and liquidity tolerance; no borrowed rule that all workouts are tiny or safe.
- **Catalyst / exit:** Tender/closing/redemption; abandon if deal probabilities deteriorate or switch thesis only after new diligence.
- **Dated examples and counterevidence:** General Dynamics (1993-03-01, L92) began as a Dutch-tender arbitrage then became long-term ownership; opportunistic arbitrage (1988-02-29, L87); Sanborn exchange (1961-01-30, P60). Negative: a transaction label alone does not establish a riskless return, as the four-question arbitrage discussion in L87 makes explicit.

## 4. Brand plus pricing power with little capital

- **Trigger / evidence:** Customer preference survives ordinary price increases; rising profit does not require proportionate net tangible assets.
- **Valuation:** Normalized owner cash after maintenance reinvestment; economic goodwill, not book premium alone.
- **Sizing bounds:** Potentially meaningful if durability and price align; no justified fixed cap.
- **Catalyst / exit:** Ordinary compounding and distributable cash; sell public stock if durability or prospective return breaks, no compulsory catalyst.
- **Dated examples and counterevidence:** See’s (1978-03-14, L77; 1984-03-14 goodwill appendix, L83); Coke/Gillette as inevitables (1997-02-28, L96). Negative: Dexter initially praised (March 1994, L93), then advantage disappeared (February 2008, L07 retrospective).

## 5. Buy a minority rather than pay for control

- **Trigger / evidence:** Public-market price buys the same underlying earnings cheaper than a whole-business negotiation; management already excellent.
- **Valuation:** Look-through earnings and intrinsic value per fractional interest, adjusted for taxes/governance.
- **Sizing bounds:** Large minority positions permitted; respect issuer constraints and group liquidity.
- **Catalyst / exit:** Business performance and price/value convergence; control not required and may be undesirable.
- **Dated examples and counterevidence:** Capital Cities (1978-03-14, L77); GEICO minority followed by full acquisition (1997-02-28, L96). Negative: buying control of Berkshire (L89 retrospective) was not automatically superior to passive ownership.

## 6. Insurance float as a funding engine

- **Trigger / evidence:** Underwriting produces long-lived investable funds; evaluate claims reserves, tail duration, concentration and manager discipline.
- **Valuation:** Cost of float relative to safe alternative funding, plus underwriting economics and investment capacity.
- **Sizing bounds:** Never equate float with free equity; catastrophe capacity and cash obligations constrain investment size.
- **Catalyst / exit:** Underwriting cycles and retained earnings; shrink premiums when price inadequate rather than chase share.
- **Dated examples and counterevidence:** National Indemnity entry (1978-03-14 retrospective, L77); super-cat capacity after Andrew (1993-03-01, L92). Negative: Gen Re underwriting reputation outlived reality (February 2008 retrospective, L07).

## 7. Crisis liquidity sold at attractive contractual terms

- **Trigger / evidence:** Strong issuer needs immediate credible capital; security gives explicit yield and equity participation.
- **Valuation:** Coupon plus redemption and warrant/convertible value under survival/default scenarios; do not price as plain common equity.
- **Sizing bounds:** Preserve group liquidity and evaluate issuer failure; no automatic bailout-size formula.
- **Catalyst / exit:** Issuer refinancing/call or warrant exercise; contractual duration matters.
- **Dated examples and counterevidence:** Goldman/GE/Wrigley (2009-02-28 account, L08); BofA (February 2012 account, L11). Negative: USAir preferred (L92/L96) and Energy Future bonds (L11) show yield/priority cannot replace business analysis.

## 8. Industry consolidation changes an old verdict

- **Trigger / evidence:** An industry previously rejected may now have different capacity discipline or competitive structure; require evidence, not a slogan reversal.
- **Valuation:** Normalized cycle earnings after full capital and competitive costs; downside for renewed price wars and debt.
- **Sizing bounds:** Sector basket may reduce issuer-specific uncertainty but not common demand risk; exact fixed bound unknown.
- **Catalyst / exit:** Sustained rational competition; exit when assumptions about demand/capital permanently widen.
- **Dated examples and counterevidence:** USAir 1989 thesis reviewed in L96; four-airline basket defended in 2017 CNBC interview (Q17); April 2020 full exit explained 2020-05-02 (M20). Negative: this is a reversal with loss, not a proven airline moat.

## 9. Installed-base stickiness and customer switching cost

- **Trigger / evidence:** Enterprise dependence and embedded processes appear to preserve supplier relevance; talk to informed operating buyers, not only management.
- **Valuation:** Durable cash after necessary innovation, buybacks and capital allocation; stress competitive substitution.
- **Sizing bounds:** Can become large only with forecast confidence; falling price does not authorize unlimited averaging.
- **Catalyst / exit:** Customer retention and per-share cash growth; sell if competitive assessment lowers value.
- **Dated examples and counterevidence:** IBM entry explanation (2011-11-14, I11); continued repurchase arithmetic (February 2012, L11); downward revaluation (2017-05-04, I17). Negative: original understanding of competitors was inadequate; the 2017 explanation cannot be backdated into 2011.

## 10. Consumer behavior can make a technology product understandable

- **Trigger / evidence:** Assess habitual use, ecosystem attachment and consumer willingness to switch rather than forecasting each technical feature.
- **Valuation:** Durable owner earnings, capital needs and per-share effects of issuer repurchases.
- **Sizing bounds:** Buffett may scale beyond a deputy’s initial holding; distinguish originator from scaler and avoid a synthetic weight ceiling.
- **Catalyst / exit:** Ordinary earnings and buybacks; price/tax/risk can justify trims even with franchise intact.
- **Dated examples and counterevidence:** Apple consumer-product explanation (2017-02-27, Q17); dual Apple/Berkshire repurchase effect (2021-02-27, L20); tax-related trim (2024-05-04, M24). Negative: IBM (I17) shows technology-adjacent familiarity did not always imply correct durability.

## 11. Incremental capital can make growth destructive

- **Trigger / evidence:** Revenue rises while maintenance/growth capital absorbs all cash; examine competitive pricing and return on new capacity.
- **Valuation:** Incremental return on capital and discounted distributable cash, not revenue growth or adjusted EPS alone.
- **Sizing bounds:** Zero if growth destroys value; larger asset-heavy commitments require a separate regulated/strategic return case.
- **Catalyst / exit:** Capital discipline or reinvestment at adequate returns; exit public stock if permanent economics worsen.
- **Dated examples and counterevidence:** See’s versus FlightSafety versus airlines (February 2008, L07); BNSF reasonable-return acquisition (2009-11-13, R09). Negative: USAir/airline capital consumption in L07; the comparison is not a blanket ban on capital intensity.

## 12. Large permanent capital prefers a durable adequate-return elephant

- **Trigger / evidence:** A small extraordinary investment cannot move Berkshire; require a large enduring cash engine and management.
- **Valuation:** Long-lived owner cash relative to all-in acquisition cost including stock given up, debt and reinvestment.
- **Sizing bounds:** Acquisition scale bounded by liquidity, solvency and value given, not desire to deploy.
- **Catalyst / exit:** Long-term operating cash; generally no resale catalyst for an acquired subsidiary.
- **Dated examples and counterevidence:** Marmon (February 2008, L07); BNSF (2009-11-13, R09); Lubrizol (February 2012, L11). Negative: Precision Castparts overpayment (2021-02-27, L20) despite an admired manager and franchise.

## 13. Commodity cash flows require price humility

- **Trigger / evidence:** Producer economics depend on commodity prices; distinguish asset quality and financial resilience from a bullish spot forecast.
- **Valuation:** Stress normalized prices, depletion, capital needs and balance sheet; do not capitalize peak earnings.
- **Sizing bounds:** Avoid allowing a commodity forecast to dominate group permanent-loss risk; disclosed ownership is not a recommended weight.
- **Catalyst / exit:** Capital return, debt reduction and sustainable production; reduce when economics/value fail.
- **Dated examples and counterevidence:** ConocoPhillips near-peak purchase error (2009-02-28, L08); PetroChina value realization sale (February 2008, L07); Occidental management/assets view and no takeover plan (2024-02-24, L23). Negative: Conoco is an explicit error, not a precedent to chase a commodity rise.

## 14. Foreign businesses plus matching-currency finance

- **Trigger / evidence:** Understandable diversified foreign businesses offer attractive earning yields; compare dividends, buybacks, governance and currency liabilities.
- **Valuation:** Owner earnings and capital return; model yen funding cost separately from equity return and FX translation.
- **Sizing bounds:** Respect publicly stated ownership understandings rather than presume hostile control; borrowing is not blanket permission for leverage.
- **Catalyst / exit:** Long-term dividends and repurchases; no macro currency prediction required; revisit if financing/economics diverge.
- **Dated examples and counterevidence:** Japan commitment made in 2019, explained 2024-05-04 (M24); management visit/additions (2024-02-24, L23); increased ownership agreements and financing discussed 2025-02-22 (L24). Negative/boundary: Irish bank losses (L08) show foreign geographic diversification is no substitute for business underwriting.

## 15. Long-dated derivatives as priced contractual risk

- **Trigger / evidence:** Premium available for an obligation Berkshire can understand and fund; distinguish terminal settlement from mark-to-market and collateral.
- **Valuation:** Expected distribution of terminal liability, discounting, premium use, counterparty and contract mechanics.
- **Sizing bounds:** Exposure bounded by obligations and liquidity, not option premium received; notional is not maximum loss for every instrument.
- **Catalyst / exit:** Expiration/settlement or negotiated termination; do not impose a stock-style stop loss mechanically.
- **Dated examples and counterevidence:** Equity-index puts and credit contracts (2009-02-28, L08, Derivatives); Gen Re legacy risks discussed in L07 retrospective. Negative: warnings about systemic derivatives risk coexist with deliberate writing; “never derivatives” is false.

## 16. Own-share repurchase competes with every other allocation

- **Trigger / evidence:** Shares below conservatively estimated value and liquidity well above required obligations.
- **Valuation:** Per-share value accretion after purchase price; book value is only a dated proxy, not intrinsic value itself.
- **Sizing bounds:** 2011 ceiling 110% book; 2018 replaced then-120% ceiling; 2018 cash floor $20bn was dated, not permanent.
- **Catalyst / exit:** Buy while discount persists; stop when price/liquidity fails even if cash is abundant.
- **Dated examples and counterevidence:** 2011 $67m purchases (February 2012, L11); 2018-07-17 policy amendment (B18); $24.7bn 2020 repurchases (2021-02-27, L20); price-dependent reaffirmation (2024-02-24, L23). Negative: halting repurchases is consistent with discipline, not proof of a market forecast.

## 17. Tax-aware trimming without declaring a franchise broken

- **Trigger / evidence:** Appreciated holding faces current versus possible future tax differences; compare after-tax reinvestment alternatives.
- **Valuation:** After-tax future value under retain/sell scenarios, not pre-tax price gain alone.
- **Sizing bounds:** Trim can differ from full exit; concentration and group cash needs remain relevant but motives must be sourced.
- **Catalyst / exit:** Tax realization and redeployment; no implied automatic sale of all appreciated holdings.
- **Dated examples and counterevidence:** Apple tax explanation (2024-05-04, M24); Sanborn plan tax minimization (1961-01-30, P60); Dempster after-tax compounding discussion (1963-07-10, P63). Negative: BofA 2024 trim rationale unknown here; do not transplant the Apple tax statement.

## 18. Residual cash rather than forced marginal investment

- **Trigger / evidence:** No sufficiently attractive large opportunities and substantial obligations; compare safe liquidity with offered prospective returns.
- **Valuation:** Opportunity cost and permanent-loss protection; earning a T-bill yield is not the same as valuing a bond-like stock at any multiple.
- **Sizing bounds:** Zero equity deployment is allowed; no historical cash amount is a perpetual optimum.
- **Catalyst / exit:** Wait for a business/security meeting hurdle; no scheduled deployment or crash date.
- **Dated examples and counterevidence:** 1969 dissolution (P69); 2017 expensive acquisition market (2018-02-24, L17); 2024 cash comments (2024-05-04, M24); Abel continuity (2026-02-28, L25). Negative: missed crisis opportunities/2020 inaction criticism must not be erased by later buybacks.

## 19. Re-underwrite and admit the original thesis failed

- **Trigger / evidence:** New evidence lowers durable value or makes forecast range unacceptable; cost basis is irrelevant to the next allocation.
- **Valuation:** Re-estimate intrinsic value/downside from today rather than waiting to break even.
- **Sizing bounds:** Reduce to zero if original uncertainty cannot be resolved; a smaller position needs its own justification.
- **Catalyst / exit:** Prompt exit/redeployment; no universal percentage stop loss.
- **Dated examples and counterevidence:** IBM revision (2017-05-04, I17); airlines exit (2020-05-02, M20); Paramount sold with responsibility accepted (2024-05-04, A24). Negative: Tesco dawdling (February 2015, L14) was itself a mistake.

## 20. Use stock as real currency, not free acquisition funding

- **Trigger / evidence:** A seller demands Berkshire shares; assess intrinsic value surrendered and dilution, not just purchase accounting.
- **Valuation:** Intrinsic value of shares issued versus acquired owner cash; deferred opportunity cost compounds.
- **Sizing bounds:** Issue only value-for-value, not to avoid paying cash or flatter reported EPS.
- **Catalyst / exit:** Per-share value accretion, usually permanent subsidiary ownership; stop when economics cannot justify value transferred.
- **Dated examples and counterevidence:** Dexter original stock-funded purchase (March 1994, L93); FlightSafety cash/stock structure (1997-02-28, L96); Dexter postmortem (February 2008, L07). Negative: Dexter cost was far larger than initial nominal price in forgone Berkshire value.

## 21. Delegation with differentiated authority and accountability

- **Trigger / evidence:** Exceptional managers or investors can operate independently; clarify actual control, incentives and capital-allocation rights.
- **Valuation:** Business-level return and capital allocation; investor track record and integrity, not a pooled celebrity label.
- **Sizing bounds:** Mandate-specific allocations; 2012 few-billion-dollar deputy portfolios are dated, not permanent bounds.
- **Catalyst / exit:** Manager performance and autonomy; replace/act when capability or integrity fails.
- **Dated examples and counterevidence:** Combs/Weschler portfolio expansion (February 2012, L11); Abel succession (2025-11-10, N25); Pilot management-control delay (2026-02-28, L25). Negative: Buffett’s N25 admits failure to act on some incapacitated CEOs; deserved trust is not absence of oversight.

## 22. Regulated capital deployment has political and liability risk

- **Trigger / evidence:** Utility/rail investments can absorb large sums at acceptable returns, but regulation, safety and tort liabilities shape distributable cash.
- **Valuation:** Allowed/realized returns after required capex, financing and adverse regulatory/liability scenarios.
- **Sizing bounds:** Subsidiary financing and group resilience both matter; no equity-like leverage template copied from See’s.
- **Catalyst / exit:** Authorized reinvestment and operating improvement; refuse growth if compensated return is inadequate.
- **Dated examples and counterevidence:** MidAmerican structure (2000-03-01, L99); BNSF acquisition (2009-11-13, R09); BHE adverse regulatory surprise (2024-02-24, L23); Abel risk/reward condition (2026-02-28, L25). Negative: Buffett explicitly says he had not considered adverse regulatory-return developments in L23.

## Replay safeguard

Use only sources available by the decision cutoff. Examples are not calibration data packs. Multiple later explanations of one transaction are not independent successful trades. A negative boundary is explicitly called such rather than fabricated into a named rejected deal.
