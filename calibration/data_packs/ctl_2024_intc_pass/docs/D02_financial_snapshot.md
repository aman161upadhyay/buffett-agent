Title: Financial record and owner-earnings bridge

Date: 2024-08-02

Second-quarter revenue was $12.8 billion, down 1%. GAAP net loss attributable to Intel was $1.6 billion and EPS negative $0.38; non-GAAP EPS was positive $0.02. GAAP gross margin was 35.4%, versus 35.8%, while non-GAAP gross margin was 38.7%, versus 39.8%. GAAP operating margin was negative 15.3%; adjusted operating margin was 0.2%. Operating cash flow was $2.3 billion and dividends paid $0.5 billion. The company guided next-quarter revenue to $12.5–13.5 billion, GAAP EPS to negative $0.24 and adjusted EPS to negative $0.03. It announced more than $10 billion of operating and capital spending reductions against prior plans, including headcount reductions exceeding 15%. Gross capex guidance was $25–27 billion for the year, while net capital spending after partner contributions and offsets was $11–13 billion. Dividend suspension begins in the fourth quarter. [S1]

The August 2 close was $21.48, down approximately 26% in one session. An ordinary trailing earnings multiple is not meaningful against the reported loss. Annualizing two cents of adjusted quarterly EPS would produce eight cents and a mechanical 268.5 times multiple; that is not normalized valuation. A genuine bull instead values recoverable earning power, foundry options and assets after debt and ongoing cash needs. A low stock price and a suspended dividend are not themselves a margin of safety. Gross and net capex must not be mixed when estimating owner cash. [S1,S2; calculations]

### Underwriting distinction

Owner earnings are not established merely by naming an adjusted profit measure. Start with the reported result; reconcile material noncash items, economic compensation, maintenance investment and working-capital requirements. An acquisition can improve reported per-share growth while requiring cash outside operating expenditure. In a financial institution, liquidity and capital requirements constrain what can be distributed even when accounting profit is positive. In an operating business, underinvestment can temporarily improve cash while reducing franchise value. [Analytical interpretation of S1]

The sourced figures below are a dated record, not a single normalized earnings estimate. Quarterly annualization is a sensitivity tool only. No undocumented maintenance-capex split, adjusted debt number or forward consensus is supplied. Where the evidence cannot support an owner-earnings estimate, the analyst should retain that uncertainty rather than insert a sector-average cash conversion rate. [Analytical interpretation of S1]
