Title: Adverse case and permanent-loss risks
Source: https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2023ltr.pdf (published 2024-02-24); https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/qtrly/2ndqtr24.pdf (2024-08-03)
Date: 2024-11-02

The bear case against waiting is that inflation erodes purchasing power, future short rates fall, and attractive companies compound while cash remains idle. A huge reserve can signal a lack of scalable opportunities, but it can also represent missed deployment. The opposite error is forcing a purchase simply to reduce a headline cash number. Insurance needs and committed spending mean that gross liquidity is not entirely discretionary. [Source above.]

Technical context: cash, bills, long bonds and productive assets serve different purposes. Short government instruments preserve nominal liquidity but expose holders to reinvestment rates and purchasing-power erosion. A long bond fixes nominal payments while adding duration risk. A public equity is a residual ownership claim whose value depends on sustainable distributable cash, not tomorrow's quote. A controlled operating business adds capital-allocation rights and illiquidity. Compare after-tax expected economics on consistent horizons, retaining enough liquidity for obligations before allocating discretionary capital. The size of a cash balance alone does not identify a market forecast or explain every trade. [Source above; comparative investment framework, not a new macroeconomic estimate.]

Risk discipline: the adverse cases above are analyst stress tests grounded in the disclosed business and current circumstances, not quotations attributed to an unnamed contemporary short seller. No authenticated bearish price target was recovered. Separate a temporary quotation decline from permanent impairment of earning power or of the investor's claim. Ask whether the issuer can fund necessary operations without assuming favorable capital markets. Identify which changes are within management control and which are not. A large cash balance at the investor does not cure an issuer's leverage, a faulty operating thesis or an excessive purchase price. Do not soften a serious downside merely because an admired investor might be interested.
