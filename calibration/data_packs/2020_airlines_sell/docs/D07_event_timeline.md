The original positions date from 2016 and were publicly explained through consolidation and improved industry economics. Pandemic restrictions and the March 27 support legislation precede this cutoff. No May annual-meeting statement, April sale disclosure or later recovery path appears in this chronology. The original thesis should be tested against new demand facts without anchoring on its historical purchase cost. [Source above.]

Technical context: airline economics have high fixed and semi-fixed costs and perishable capacity. Load factor, fare, ancillary revenue and unit cost should be read together; fuller aircraft can still produce weak returns if prices fall. Aircraft leases, pension liabilities and committed fleet purchases belong in the capital-risk assessment. Low fuel prices improve a period's profit but may induce competitive fare cuts. A four-company basket reduces single-carrier concentration but retains shared exposure to travel demand, fuel, labor and credit availability. Earnings before aircraft ownership costs can exaggerate cash available to common owners. [Source above; analytical framework, not an aggregate forecast.]

Chronology discipline: distinguish operating period, publication date, transaction date and disclosure date. An ownership report describes a historical balance and does not establish the exact day a trade was executed or the reason it was made. A cutoff within a quarter also does not make that quarter's unreleased earnings public. This digest contains only the developments described above and the technical implications that can be drawn from them. It does not use a later return, revised investment thesis, completed transaction or eventual portfolio weight. The absence of a specific date means the date is unresolved, not that the next convenient calendar day is the execution.

Evidence boundary: missing figures are not available in contemporaneous sources recovered for this digest. Analytical questions are not additional reported facts or claims about the investor's private thinking. Read the financial, risk and fund-context documents together; no individual accounting ratio supplies a complete investment conclusion.
