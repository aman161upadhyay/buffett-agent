## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**2006–08:** Berkshire already held COP in 2006; the famous error concerned buying a large amount in 2008 near peak oil/gas prices. These are not necessarily identical entry valuations. [1–2]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“Without urging from Charlie or anyone else” he bought; “I have been dead wrong.” He still expected higher future oil prices but acknowledged that “terrible timing” cost several billion dollars. **February 2009 post-mortem.** [2]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Held 17.938 million shares at December 2006; 84.896 million at December 2008. Reduced to 37.711 million at December 2009. [1–3]

## What happened after
The 2008 cost/market gap was approximately $2.61 billion, an unrealized checkpoint, not the complete eventual realized loss. Some sales also financed crisis deals and liquidity. [2–3]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Commodity view:** Buffett retained a favorable long-run expectation. [2]
- **Mistake:** Did not anticipate the dramatic price drop. [2]
- **Price:** Future recovery would not erase the capital-allocation error. [2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: explicit sole responsibility; do not attribute it to Munger or deputies. Compare PetroChina's value-disciplined entry/exit. [2]

## Public discussion
The candid annual-letter admission is verified; independent contemporary debate not recovered. [2]
