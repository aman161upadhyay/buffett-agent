Title: Preferred income, redemption and warrant mechanics
Source: https://www.goldmansachs.com/pressroom/press-releases/2008/berkshire-hathaway-invest (2008-09-23; financing terms only, acceptance and provider identity withheld)
Date: 2008-09-23

The security offered for underwriting combines $5 billion perpetual preferred stock with warrants to purchase $5 billion common stock. The preferred dividend rate is 10% annually. On a fully funded $5 billion principal balance, that is $500 million annual contractual dividend income. A dividend on preferred equity is not an unconditional government-guaranteed interest payment. [September 23 terms.]

The issuer can call the preferred at a 10% premium. Redemption at that contractual price would return $5.5 billion principal and premium, separate from dividends owed under the instrument. Perpetual means no ordinary fixed maturity requiring principal repayment on a specified calendar date. The issuer's call right makes the timing of principal return different from a bond investor's right to payment at maturity. [September 23 terms; arithmetic and instrument interpretation.]

The warrants have a five-year term and a $115 exercise price. Dividing $5 billion by $115 gives approximately 43.478 million common shares, subject to the actual instrument's adjustments. This calculation describes the initial underlying quantity, not an immediately issued common position. Exercising all warrants requires another $5 billion consideration; an investor must not count that potential outlay as already invested. [September 23 terms; calculated quantity.]

Underwriting should separate three sources of potential return: preferred dividends, any redemption premium, and common upside through the warrants. They do not have identical risk. The warrants may expire without economic value, while a well-performing preferred could continue paying dividends. Conversely, issuer distress can impair preferred principal and dividend capacity even if the stated yield appears large. [Analytical interpretation.]

No independently recovered fair-value allocation between preferred and warrants is supplied. Therefore neither the preferred's stand-alone effective purchase price nor a modeled option value is invented. A common-stock earnings multiple cannot value the entire package, and counting the warrant notional as free common equity would overstate the initial capital received. Evaluate the combined claim against liquidity, capital losses and competing securities of similar seniority. [Analytical interpretation.]

This digest deliberately removes the identity and endorsement of the capital provider and whether the proposal was accepted. It describes the same-day financing opportunity rather than supplying the allocation verdict. No subsequent redemption, exercise, realized return or issuer recovery enters the evidence. Intraday availability relative to a particular execution time was not authenticated. [Information-boundary note.]
