## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**1991:** Guinness offered consumer-brand earnings across countries. No verified entry-date multiple or first purchase month was recovered; subsequent annual-letter descriptions must not be treated as publicly known purchase instructions. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
Guinness “earns its money in much the same fashion as Coca-Cola and Gillette.” The surrounding stated search was for “understandable, enduring and mouth-watering economics,” able managers and a sensible price. **February 1992 explanation, after purchase.** [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
The 1991 letter identifies Guinness as a new position and Berkshire's first significant investment in a foreign-domiciled company. The 1992 letter reports 38.335 million shares, up from 31.247 million. [1–2]

## What happened after
The 1992 market value was below cost. This is an interim mark, not a verified sale loss; subsequent merger into Diageo and ultimate disposition are outside the verified evidence here. Status is at the last sourced checkpoint. [2]
