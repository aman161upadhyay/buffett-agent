## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**April 1962 announcement:** Union Oil proposed to buy this small producer. Officers/directors owned about 40% and had negotiated the deal; Buffett considered shareholder rejection, title and antitrust risks negligible. The principal complication was an IRS ruling concerning USC's tax-exempt status in an ABC production-payment financing. Exact original purchase dates and weighted equity/warrant costs are not repeated here without the ledger. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“I never act on such information” — **January 18, 1964 retrospective**, referring to earlier takeover rumors rather than announced terms. [1]

“While getting this ironed out created delay, it did not threaten the deal.” Same appendix, his assessment of the tax-ruling obstacle. This was a judgment, not proof that every tax-dependent transaction is safe. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
**The partnership** bought $264,000 face of 6.5% debentures callable at 104.25, 64,035 common shares and 83,200 warrants to buy shares at $3.50. Purchases occurred during roughly six months from announcement to closing. Bill Scott attended the May shareholder meeting; calls to officials repeatedly checked timing. The IRS ruling arrived late September and the asset sale closed October 31, 1962. [1]

## What happened after
Bonds were called November 13. Warrants were exercised; common paid $3.50 on December 14, 1962, $3.90 on February 4, 1963 and $0.15 on April 24, 1963, versus the initial $7.42 expected total, with another $0.04 still anticipated at the January 1964 letter. Bond cost was $260,773 and capital gain $14,446 plus interest, average holding just under five months. `closed` denotes the substantially completed workout; a small tail remained, so no exact final exit date is asserted. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Public catalyst:** did not enter merely because a rumor later proved correct. [1]
- **Votes:** controlling-holder sponsorship reduced approval risk. [1]
- **Legal:** title and antitrust were assessed individually, not ignored. [1]
- **Tax/timing:** detailed monitoring distinguished delay from deal failure. [1]
- **Security selection:** multiple claims monetized the same transaction differently. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: a clean workout calibration case separating downside probability, duration and expected distribution. The final return exceeding estimates does not make the repeated timing delays disappear. [1]
