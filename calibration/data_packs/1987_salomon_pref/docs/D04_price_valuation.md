## Cutoff evidence — reconstructed

The investment price is par on a proposed $700 million preferred with 9% dividend and conversion at $38 after three years. Its initial contractual cash yield is 9%; this is not a promise of total return and is not equivalent to an earnings yield on common shares. The option has value only if future common-stock value exceeds the conversion price while the enterprise remains solvent. The September 25 common quote is now supplied above; an independently valued conversion option remains unrecovered. Scheduled redemption matters, but does not make the issue a Treasury bond. No invented discount rate, option volatility or probability-adjusted price is supplied.

## Economic reading of the supplied facts

Industry economics: a securities dealer finances inventory and counterparty exposures as well as providing advice, underwriting and distribution. Earnings can be large while requiring substantial financing and exposing the enterprise to abrupt changes in liquidity. A reputation for reliability is part of the franchise because clients and lenders can move their business quickly. Published book equity is not the same as immediately realizable cash if markets become disorderly. A preferred investment can offer a defined dividend and priority over common equity, but it does not eliminate the dealer's obligations to its creditors. Conversion gives participation in better operating results while leaving the holder exposed to the issuing company's solvency. These security distinctions matter more than a generic common-stock earnings multiple.

Valuation interpretation: the relevant asset is the stream of cash that can be withdrawn over the life of the business without weakening its competitive position. A low multiple is informative only when the earnings denominator is sustainable. A high return on tangible capital can justify a premium to book value, but does not justify an unlimited purchase price.

No closing quotation is inferred from a subsequent transaction average or from the low for a later month. If a dated market price is unavailable, a precise market capitalization, initial earnings yield and implied return are unavailable as well. A negotiated security can instead be evaluated from its proposed cash payments and priority, subject to credit risk. The adverse case should reduce sustainable earnings and allow for additional capital needs rather than simply apply a smaller multiple to optimistic earnings. Interest rates, taxes and reinvestment determine the opportunity cost; no unsupported discount rate or terminal growth estimate is presented as an observed fact.
