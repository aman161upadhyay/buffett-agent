The latest public annual Berkshire cash baseline is approximately $144.1 billion at year-end 2020; the 2021 annual report is not yet public. A deputy's disclosed pre-bid shares must be separated from any proposed Buffett-sized arbitrage overlay. New mode means the new workout strategy, not first-ever issuer ownership. A position limit should reflect deal-break loss and correlated regulatory exposure, not simply the size of available cash. [Source above.]

The public context includes insurance float, major operating companies and a concentrated listed portfolio, rather than only a cash account. A reconciled float and equity-portfolio total as of the exact replay boundary was not recovered. Do not add an insurance liability to cash and call the sum discretionary capital, or infer a complete portfolio weight from the US 13F alone. [Fund baseline cited above.]

Technical context: merger arbitrage is a contingent cash-flow problem. Separate the promised consideration, financing, approvals, termination rights, outside date and possible remedies. Gross spread is not expected return: completion probability, time and the break-price loss all matter. A bidder's ability to pay does not settle regulatory approval. Stand-alone valuation is important even if the intended holding period ends at closing, because the target remains a public business if the transaction fails. Do not treat an original pre-announcement stock purchase and a post-announcement workout as the same investment thesis merely because they use the same ticker. [Source above; analytical framework.]

Allocation discipline: Berkshire is an operating and insurance company, not a redeemable investment partnership. The metadata liquidity figure is a dated scale reference, not contemporaneous discretionary NAV. Cash supports insurance, subsidiaries and contractual obligations before any new allocation. Equity-portfolio weight, percentage of issuer ownership and percentage of cash are different sizing measures. An existing position's basis affects tax but does not determine current intrinsic value. Distinguish a deputy's mandate from a Buffett-scale commitment, and avoid assigning a named decision maker without evidence. This context does not disclose the action being evaluated; the analyst must weigh opportunity cost and the durability of the underlying business independently.

Evidence boundary: missing figures are not available in contemporaneous sources recovered for this digest. Analytical questions are not additional reported facts or claims about the investor's private thinking. Read the financial, risk and fund-context documents together; no individual accounting ratio supplies a complete investment conclusion.
