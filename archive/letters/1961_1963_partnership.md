# Partnership 1961–1969: Dempster and dissolution evidence note

**Information date:** 1963-07-10 (month-only where precise public release day was not independently established). **Era:** A. **Author:** Buffett. **Primary source:** [letter](https://www.buffett-letters.com/en/02-letters/partnership/1963-Partnership-Letter-(1)). All rules below cite sections of this source; section names are used rather than fabricated PDF page numbers. Annual-period labels are not publication dates.

## Process rules and decision explanations

- Dempster: Harry Bottle converted idle assets into cash, paid debt and made remaining assets productive. The investor supplies allocation discipline; the operator supplies implementation.
- Do not value both the released cash and the original operating assets at full book: avoid double counting.
- Seasonality and tax carry-forwards complicate annualizing interim earnings. The letter explicitly resists simplistic extrapolation.
- Judge after-tax compounding and permanent-loss exposure, not market-index forecasts.

## Verbatim evidence anchors

- **Dempster Mill Manufacturing; Delegated asset conversion to Harry Bottle to generate cash for reinvestment.** “Harry did an extraordinary job of converting unproductive assets into cash which we then, of course, began to invest in undervalued securities.”
- **Dempster Mill Manufacturing; Delegated operational management to Harry Bottle to improve asset productivity.** “Harry has continued this year to turn under-utilized assets into cash, but in addition, he has made the remaining needed assets productive.”
- **Dempster Mill Manufacturing; Decision to hold rather than sell the controlling interest in Dempster.** “Dempster earned a very satisfactory operating profit in the first half (as well as a substantial unrealized gain in securities) and there is little question”
- **Dempster Mill Manufacturing; Decision to maintain valuation at $51.26 per share due to operational and tax considerations.** “Because of a very important- seasonal factor and also the presence of a tax carry forward, however, the earning power is not nearly what might be inferred”

## Attribution and point-in-time boundary

Buffett is the source author, but an acquired subsidiary’s bolt-on or operating action is not automatically his personally originated investment. Individual decider unknown where not identified. Historical narratives are available only from this information date. A section discussing earlier purchases is retrospective; do not load its later performance, confession or corrected valuation into the original entry replay.

## Coverage limitation

This is a decision-process extraction, not a full transcription or a complete portfolio ledger. Numbers outside the source passage are not estimated. The Dempster analysis uses the interim report, not a complete anthology. The later American Express and Berkshire-control entry cases require additional original contemporaneous evidence; Berkshire's purchase is addressed retrospectively in the 1989 and 2024 notes.

## 1969-05-29 — dissolution, with a workout postmortem

**Additional information date:** 1969-05-29. **Source:** [May dissolution letter, third-party transcription](https://buffettknowledge.com/letters/1969-midyear-letter), opening performance discussion and liquidation plan.

“I am not attuned to this market environment and I don't want to spoil a decent record by trying to play a game I don't understand just so I can go out a hero.”

The letter describes disappointing workout experience as an improper assessment of a fast-developing governmental trend, not bad luck. It says holdings would be liquidated through the year while retaining controlled companies, selected long-term securities and small workout stubs requiring longer cleanup. Thus dissolution was not an instantaneous sale of every security or a simple prediction of a market fall. Tax/legal arrangements and management alternatives for partners were implementation constraints. These are May 1969 observations and must not enter a July 1963 replay.
