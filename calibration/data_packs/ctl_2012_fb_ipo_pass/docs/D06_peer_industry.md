Google competes for digital advertising, while other social services compete for users' time. Comparing total users across services without accounting for engagement, ad load and geographic monetization is not a valuation. Search advertising captures explicit intent; social advertising relies more on identity and inferred interests. The planned Instagram transaction illustrates both strategic adjacency and the possibility of paying heavily to protect user relevance. No harmonized May 17 forward earnings multiple table is recovered. [S1; analytical comparison]

The social network lets users maintain identity, relationships, messages, photographs and shared content; advertisers pay to reach targeted audiences. Additional users and connections can improve the usefulness of the product for existing users. A large advertising platform can have substantial operating leverage because an extra ad impression does not require another physical store. The affirmative franchise case rests on habitual engagement and a difficult-to-replicate social graph, not the scarcity of IPO shares. [S1; analyst interpretation]

First-quarter revenue was $1.058 billion, versus $731 million, while net income was $205 million versus $233 million. Monthly active users reached 901 million at March 31 and daily active users averaged 526 million in March. Mobile monthly active users numbered 488 million. Advertising supplied approximately 85% of 2011 revenue; payments and other fees supplied the remainder. ### Underwriting distinction

Comparable-company analysis is useful only after matching the economic claims. A supplier, a platform, a lender and a brand owner can all report revenue growth while requiring very different reinvestment and balance sheets. Growth funded by customers is different from growth funded by new common equity or acquisition debt. Market-share evidence should therefore be read alongside retention, pricing, capital requirements and the company's ability to retain incremental profit. [Analytical interpretation of S1]

No peer multiple is invented where a same-date, same-definition series is missing. The more useful comparison in that case is structural: who owns the customer, who can raise price, who must invest to preserve competitiveness, and who bears a sudden liquidity demand. The company-specific figures below supply the scale and margin evidence. A sector label is not a substitute for those economics. [Analytical interpretation of S1]
