Title: Berkshire's public starting portfolio and capital constraints
Source: https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2024ltr.pdf ; https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/qtrly/1stqtr25.pdf ; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1067983/000095012325005701/0000950123-25-005701.txt ; https://www.cnbc.com/2020/02/24/warren-buffett-says-apple-is-probably-the-best-business-i-know-in-the-world.html
Date: 2025-06-30

Berkshire is an insurance and operating-business owner, not a fully invested mutual fund. Insurance float finances assets but is also a liability to future claimants; it is not a second pool of free equity. A reported cash balance includes precautionary reserves and must not be equated with a mandate to buy securities immediately. Operating subsidiaries, minority equities, Treasury bills and own-share repurchases compete for capital. The annual letter emphasizes per-share intrinsic value, understandable businesses and financial strength. [fund sources]

Equity-portfolio value is a sizing reference, not total corporate net worth. The 13F covers specified U.S.-reportable securities, omits many wholly owned businesses and foreign holdings, and reports quarter-end marks rather than purchase costs. Manager codes identify reporting arrangements, not who originated each investment idea. A changed share count is observable only after the relevant filing is public. A mark-to-market change without a share change is not a trade.

The analyst should consider concentration, capital-gains taxes, liquidity and opportunity cost separately. Exact tax lots, private execution dates and subsidiary capital availability are not available in contemporaneous sources recovered here. No current order or future shareholder action is supplied. Prior public operating and ownership information below is context, not an instruction about the decision under assessment.

The May 15 public 13F reports 300,000,000 Apple shares at March 31, down from the much larger historical peak but still a major concentrated exposure. Those are the latest publicly known shares as of June 30; they are not replaced with a later June-quarter disclosure. In his February 24, 2020 CNBC interview Buffett called Apple "probably the best business I know in the world" and described it as a business, not merely a stock. This is a prior quality/ownership thesis, not a present trading instruction or a product-launch forecast.

Berkshire's May 3 report supplies approximately $347.7 billion aggregate cash/Treasury liquidity and $173 billion float, a future claims liability rather than free equity. A low-basis owner must weigh tax realization against concentration and future return. The annual letter emphasizes retaining excellent businesses and evaluating capital allocation economically, not mechanically. No exact Apple tax-lot basis is supplied. Buffett's known broad stewardship of the investment does not establish who directs any particular forthcoming transaction. The metadata uses March liquidity as a scale proxy rather than presenting a confidential-adjusted equity portfolio as publicly known AUM.
