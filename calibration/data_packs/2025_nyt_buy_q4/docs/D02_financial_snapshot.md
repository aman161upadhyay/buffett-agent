Title: Revenue growth with improving operating leverage
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/71691/000007169125000117/pressrelease06302025.htm ; https://s23.q4cdn.com/152113917/files/doc_events/2025/Apr/30/The-New-York-Times-Company-2024-Annual-Report.pdf
Date: 2025-09-30

The annual revenue sequence rises from approximately $2.31 billion to $2.43 billion to $2.59 billion over the three disclosed years. Current quarterly revenue grows faster than operating costs: 9.7% versus 6.2%. Operating margin is 15.5%, and adjusted operating margin 19.5%. The $27.2 million difference between reported and adjusted operating profit must not be ignored when judging cash earnings. [1,2]

The balance sheet reports $951.5 million cash/marketable securities and no outstanding debt, with a $400 million revolving commitment. First-half operating cash flow was $212.7 million and free cash flow $193.2 million. These measures differ from adjusted operating profit, which excludes depreciation, amortization and special items. Generative-AI litigation cost $3.5 million in the quarter versus $2.0 million: the adjustment does not erase the cash expense. The Athletic's adjusted profit was $5.8 million versus a $2.4 million loss. No maintenance/growth-capital split is recovered. [1]

Evidence ledger: annual revenue was $2.308321 billion in 2022, $2.426152 billion in 2023 and $2.585919 billion in 2024. Second-quarter revenue of $685.9 million grew 9.7%; operating profit was $106.6 million, up 34.2%, and adjusted operating profit $133.8 million. Diluted EPS was $0.50, or $0.58 adjusted. Total subscribers were 11.88 million, digital-only 11.30 million, and bundle/multiproduct 6.02 million. Digital-only ARPU was $9.64, up 3.2%. Digital subscription revenue was $350.4 million, total subscription revenue $481.4 million, digital advertising $94.4 million and total advertising $134.0 million. Affiliate/licensing/other revenue was $70.5 million. Marketing costs were $69.2 million; journalism and subscriber-servicing spending remain real operating costs. [1,2]

Accounting discipline: all dollar amounts in this document retain the period and basis given in the evidence ledger. Revenue is not owner earnings; neither adjusted profit nor operating cash flow alone measures distributable cash. Maintenance investment, working capital, compensation dilution and financing claims must be considered separately. A quarter is not a full economic cycle. Where a stand-alone balance sheet, maintenance-capital split or historical consensus series is absent, it is unavailable rather than assumed to be zero or inferred from a later filing.
