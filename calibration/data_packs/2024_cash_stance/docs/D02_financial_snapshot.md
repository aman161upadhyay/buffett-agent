The last pre-decision public quarter shows approximately $276.9 billion broad cash and Treasury bills at June 30. Definitions matter: bills classified separately from cash equivalents must not be counted twice. The third-quarter report published on November 2 is the withheld action disclosure for this replay. The analyst inputs therefore use the prior public liquidity baseline rather than reproduce the current cash outcome. [Source above.]

Technical context: cash, bills, long bonds and productive assets serve different purposes. Short government instruments preserve nominal liquidity but expose holders to reinvestment rates and purchasing-power erosion. A long bond fixes nominal payments while adding duration risk. A public equity is a residual ownership claim whose value depends on sustainable distributable cash, not tomorrow's quote. A controlled operating business adds capital-allocation rights and illiquidity. Compare after-tax expected economics on consistent horizons, retaining enough liquidity for obligations before allocating discretionary capital. The size of a cash balance alone does not identify a market forecast or explain every trade. [Source above; comparative investment framework, not a new macroeconomic estimate.]

Accounting discipline: the figures stated above are reported observations unless expressly labeled arithmetic. A period-end balance is not a flow and a market mark is not purchase cost. Do not mix fiscal years, currencies, share bases or enterprise and equity values. To construct owner earnings, begin with the relevant reported profit, reconcile noncash items and subtract the investment required to maintain economic position. Changes in working capital and recurring compensation remain economic costs even when a convenient adjusted metric excludes them. If maintenance investment, leverage or the proper share denominator is missing, state that the yield cannot be completed rather than present false precision.

Evidence boundary: the historical financial record and June liquidity baseline do not reproduce the withheld same-day quarterly allocation disclosure. Insurance float is an economic funding source, not freely distributable shareholder capital.
