Olin and Westlake identify relevant chlor-alkali/PVC comparison families, but product mix, integration and geography differ. Dow supplies a broader, less direct chemical-cycle comparison. D04 provides dated prices and deliberately qualified enterprise-value sensitivities rather than a stand-alone target acquisition multiple. Product necessity is common to the industry and therefore does not itself distinguish one producer's returns. [1,2]

### Peer operating record (Q2 2025 versus Q2 2024)

| Consolidated peer, USD millions | Sales 2025 / 2024 | EBITDA 2025 / 2024 | EBITDA basis | 2025 margin, calculated |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| Olin | 1,758.3 / 1,644.0 | 176.1 / 278.1 | adjusted | 10.0% |
| Westlake | 2,953 / 3,207 | 210 / 744 | reported | 7.1% |
| Dow | 10,104 / 10,915 | 703 / 1,501 | operating | 7.0% |

Westlake adjusted EBITDA was $340m, excluding a $130m facility-closure charge; its adjusted margin was 11.5%, not the reported 7.1%. Olin Q2 net loss was $1.3m and Dow's $801m; Westlake's loss was $142m. Olin's sales growth alongside declining adjusted EBITDA cautions against equating revenue growth with spread recovery. Dow's 7% sales decline and operating-EBITDA decline of 53.2% demonstrate operating leverage.

OxyChem's $213m is segment pretax income, not adjusted EBITDA; comparing its 17.4% pretax/sales ratio directly with the EBITDA margins above would overstate comparability. Product mix also differs: the peers contain ammunition, housing materials or broader plastics businesses. Neither a cost-curve rank nor a maintenance-capital advantage is quantified by these consolidated figures. The three actual releases, rather than a generic “industry multiple,” support the cycle stress. D04's price/book and annualized-quarter multiples retain their limitations; a fully harmonized trailing EV/EBITDA and contemporaneous consensus panel remain unrecovered.

The August disclosure provides evidence of export demand for both caustic soda and PVC, but not a guaranteed selling price or quantified permanent cost gap versus rivals. A long-term industrial owner can tolerate a cyclical quarter if the asset remains competitive and financing is conservative. That does not justify overlooking environmental obligations or the amount of investment required to keep the facilities competitive. Comparing the segment with a specialty formulation business purely on EBITDA would risk confusing different economics. There is no asserted market share, peer multiple or proprietary cost-curve position without a recovered source.

Evidence ledger: Occidental's August 6 release reports OxyChem second-quarter pre-tax income of $213 million. Excluding comparability items, income was relatively unchanged sequentially; negative inventory adjustments were offset by improved export demand for caustic soda and PVC. Earlier releases report OxyChem pre-tax income of $436 million in the second quarter of 2023 and $260 million in the first quarter of 2024. These are different quarters, not an annualized normalized earnings record. For the parent as a whole, second-quarter operating cash flow was $3.0 billion and capital spending $2.0 billion; those figures must not be assigned to the chemicals segment. The August earnings call discusses about $300 million lower OxyChem spending in the following year. No stand-alone acquisition agreement or price is publicly established in this dated source set. [1-4]

Comparison discipline: industry membership does not make two businesses economically identical. Geography, customer mix, contract duration, capital intensity, leverage and accounting adjustments can dominate headline growth comparisons. No synchronized consensus-earnings dataset was recovered for this cutoff; approximate observed-price comparisons do not establish relative cheapness. The useful comparison is between sources of durable return and exposure to common pressures. A competitor's weakness is not automatically this company's moat, and an industry recovery is not required to be uniform across participants.
