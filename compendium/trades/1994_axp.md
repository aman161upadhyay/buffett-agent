## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**1994–95:** This was a new Berkshire-era common-stock accumulation, following a 1991 preferred investment, not the 1960s partnership trade. The preferred paid 8.85% on $300 million and had contractual conversion features. Exact conversion settlement date requires original security documents. [1,3]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
The **1994 letter**, written March 1995, describes “enlarging our holdings” and says “long-term familiarity with a company and its products is often helpful in evaluating it.” The 1991 letter explicitly says the preferred was “not a normal fixed-income security.” These statements do not supply a complete stock-specific valuation model. [1,4]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Retained common exposure after the preferred phase and bought additional shares in 1994–95. At December 1994, the table reported 27,759,941 shares costing $723.919 million. By the March 1995 letter-writing date, Buffett said cost had risen to $1.36 billion; **that is not the December 1995 figure**. At December 1995, the table reported 49,456,900 shares costing $1.3927 billion, valued at $2.0463 billion, approximately 10% ownership. [2,4]

The preferred contract originally required conversion three years after issuance, subject to a possible one-year extension if the common was below $24.50; value received was capped at $414 million with no floor. Exact actual settlement date remains unverified. Do not replace these terms with an ordinary uncapped convertible-bond description. [1]

## What happened after
This case ends at accumulation; subsequent compounding is not calculated as a purchase-date forecast. Status is historical, not a current-ownership assertion. [2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Franchise:** Expanded an existing investment rather than a new crisis arbitrage. [2–3]
- **Instrument:** Preferred yield and conversion economics need separate treatment. [1]
- **Evidence restraint:** Exact common-stock valuation rationale unknown; do not substitute the 1964 thesis. [1–3]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: transition from contractual security economics to enduring common ownership, with a clear boundary between two historical American Express episodes. [1–3]

## Public discussion
The February 15, 1995 International Herald Tribune report documents surprise at the enlarged stake; it is contemporaneous reporting, not Buffett's own complete explanation. [3]
