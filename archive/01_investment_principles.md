# Investment principles: dated evolution, not a timeless checklist

**Cutoff: 2026-10-25.** Quotes are source excerpts; operating rules outside quotes are synthesis. Source dates are explanation dates, not necessarily entry dates. Later confessions must not contaminate original decisions. Decider: Buffett unless otherwise specified.

## A: 1950s–1960s — Graham bargains, workouts and controls

### 1. Realizable assets before extrapolated earnings

The partnership's generals, workouts and controls had different routes to realization. Sanborn was an investment portfolio trapped inside a declining map operation, not a prediction that map earnings would rebound. “We hoped to separate the two businesses, realize the fair value of the investment portfolio and work to re-establish the earning power of the map business.” [1960 partnership letter, **1961-01-30**, Sanborn Map](https://www.buffett-letters.com/en/02-letters/partnership/1960-Partnership-Letter).

**Method:** conservative asset realizations minus liabilities, tax, costs and delay. Graham net-nets are one balance-sheet bargain category; not every early Buffett purchase was literally below net current assets. Sanborn's securities portfolio and Dempster's operating working capital require different valuation adjustments. A workout requires an identifiable transaction, probability and timetable, rather than mere low P/E. [1960 letter above; 1963 interim below.]

### 2. Control can unlock assets; it cannot cure terrible economics

“Harry did an extraordinary job of converting unproductive assets into cash which we then, of course, began to invest in undervalued securities.” [**1963-07-10**, Dempster section](https://www.buffett-letters.com/en/02-letters/partnership/1963-Partnership-Letter-(1)). Sanborn's exchange returned portfolio assets to exiting holders; Dempster required Harry Bottle's operating actions. Control is a tool, not intrinsically a good investment. Buffett later described his 1965 Berkshire control purchase: “Though I knew its business - textile manufacturing - to be unpromising, I was enticed to buy because the price looked cheap.” [1989 letter, **1990-03-02**, Mistakes of the First Twenty-five Years; retrospective](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1989.html).

**Sizing/catalyst:** enough influence to implement the plan, with capital at risk bounded by conservative realizations and liquidity; no universal percentage inferred. Exit can be an exchange, operating-asset sale or redeployment, not a permanent holding. [1960/1963 sources above.]

### 3. Stop when the available game no longer fits

The dissolution announcement dated **1969-05-29** is an actual return-capital decision, not a forecast of the next crash: “I am not attuned to this market environment”. [Dissolution letter, third-party transcription](https://buffettknowledge.com/letters/1969-midyear-letter). Scarce suitable opportunities and personal objectives should not be compressed into a universal market-timing signal. A partnership wind-down is also not the same as later Berkshire holding cash indefinitely.

## A → B: 1972–1990 — Munger, See's and quality at a fair price

### 4. See's changed the asset model

See's was acquired by Blue Chip at the beginning of **1972**. The **1977 letter, 1978-03-14**, reports growing pretax earnings “with little additional capital investment.” [Blue Chip Stamps](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1977.html). The **1983 letter, 1984-03-14**, explains economic goodwill using See's returns on its small tangible capital base. [Goodwill and its Amortization appendix](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1983.html).

“It's far better to buy a wonderful company at a fair price than a fair company at a wonderful price.” [1989 letter, **1990-03-02**, Mistakes; retrospective](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1989.html). This is a later formulation of the shift, not a verbatim 1972 purchase memorandum. The **2023 letter, 2024-02-24**, explicitly credits Munger as Berkshire's architect. [Opening tribute](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2023ltr.pdf).

**Rule:** pricing power, customer attachment and low incremental capital needs can make above-book-value purchases rational. This is not a ban on asset bargains or regulated businesses; nor is it permission to pay any price for a brand.

### 5. The 1977 checklist was actually revised

“We want the business to be (1) one that we can understand, (2) with favorable long-term prospects, (3) operated by honest and competent people, and (4) available at a very attractive price.” [**1978-03-14**, Insurance Investments](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1977.html).

The **1992 letter, 1993-03-01**, explicitly changes “a very attractive price” to “an attractive price” because of market conditions and Berkshire's size. [Common Stock Investments](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1992.html). Do not silently apply a tiny-partnership hurdle to a much larger permanent-capital institution. The same section says: “Growth is always a component in the calculation of value”. Growth adds value only when incremental returns justify capital consumed; low multiples do not define value.

## B: 1980s–1990s — franchises, concentration and the boundary of knowledge

### 6. Own the business, not the quotation

“Our goal is to find an outstanding business at a sensible price, not a mediocre business at a bargain price.” [1987 letter, **1988-02-29**, Marketable Securities—Permanent Holdings](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1987.html). Mr. Market offers prices; he does not supply the investor's estimate of value. Minority stock can be superior to control when existing management is better and the price much lower: Capital Cities is the explicit **1977** example. [1977 letter, Insurance Investments](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1977.html).

Coke and Gillette appear as “The Inevitables” in the **1996 letter, 1997-02-28**, Common Stock Investments. [Source](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1996.html). The label concerns competitive position, not an invariant stock valuation or a guarantee against future disruption. Dexter was initially believed to have a durable advantage, then became the counterexample. [1993 letter, March 1994, Dexter Shoe](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1993.html); [2007 letter, February 2008, retrospective](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2007ltr.pdf).

### 7. Cash flows, margin of safety and a circle of competence

The **1992 letter, 1993-03-01**, defines asset value through future cash inflows/outflows discounted at an appropriate interest rate. “If we calculate the value of a common stock to be only slightly higher than its price, we're not interested in buying.” [Common Stock Investments](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1992.html). No universal fixed discount rate or 30%-discount requirement is established here.

The **1999 letter, 2000-03-01**, refuses technology forecasting: “Predicting the long-term economics of companies that operate in fast-changing industries is simply far beyond our perimeter.” [Investments](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1999htm.html). That is a dated knowledge boundary, not a permanent sector prohibition. Its subsequent IBM and Apple exceptions must remain visible.

### 8. Preferred protection does not replace underwriting the business

USAir's 1989 convertible preferred appeared to offer yield plus upside. By **1993-03-01** Buffett wrote: “industry economics had soured before the ink dried on our check.” [1992 letter, Fixed-Income Securities](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1992.html). The **1996 letter, 1997-02-28**, identifies the overlooked fiercely competitive deregulated market. [USAir](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1996.html). It eventually recovered, but that does not validate the original underwriting. Gillette's preferred and USAir's preferred had different franchise economics. [1989 letter, convertible preferred discussion](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1989.html).

## C: 2007 taxonomy, elephants and crisis financing

### 9. Great, good and gruesome are capital-return categories

The **2007 letter, February 2008**, calls the section “Businesses – The Great, the Good and the Gruesome.” See's generates cash with little added capital; FlightSafety is good but needs substantial reinvestment; airlines exemplify growth consuming capital without adequate reward. “The worst sort of business is one that grows rapidly, requires significant capital to engender the growth, and then earns little or no money.” [Section source](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2007ltr.pdf).

Do not classify every capital-intensive business as unbuyable: BNSF and utilities can deploy large sums at acceptable returns. Nor should the See's model be used to value a utility without its reinvestment and regulatory obligations.

### 10. Large scale makes adequate-return elephants valuable

BNSF required a long-lived, capital-intensive asset rather than a See's clone. “Reasonable return is good enough, Charlie.” is Buffett's BNSF formulation in the **2009-11-13** Charlie Rose interview, broadcast before the merger closed. [Transcript filed **2009-11-16**](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1067983/000119312509235940/d425.htm). This must not become a claim that any large acquisition is sensible. Precision Castparts later demonstrated the cost of paying too much. [2020 letter, **2021-02-27**, opening write-down discussion](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2020ltr.pdf).

### 11. Crisis liquidity earns a contractual return plus optionality

The **2008 letter, signature 2009-02-27, released 2009-02-28**, explains purchases of Wrigley, Goldman Sachs and GE fixed-income securities and equity participation. [Investments](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2008ltr.pdf). “we made purchases totaling $14.5 billion in fixed-income securities issued by Wrigley, Goldman Sachs and General Electric.” This is not an ordinary common-stock dip-buying template. Coupon, redemption rights, warrants, counterparty survival and liquidity all matter. The **2011 letter, February 2012**, records the issuers calling away the Goldman/GE/Swiss Re positions. [Opening developments](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2011ltr.pdf).

BofA's **2011** preferred/warrant financing and the later common holding are separate security decisions. Preferred protection does not eliminate the permanent-loss risk shown by USAir or Energy Future bonds. [2011 letter, Investments and opening mistakes](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2011ltr.pdf).

### 12. Deputies institutionalize capital allocation, not impersonation

“Each will be handling a few billion dollars in 2012” refers to Todd Combs and Ted Weschler. [**February 2012**, 2011 letter, opening developments](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2011ltr.pdf). Their hiring/portfolio expansion spans **2010–2012**. Treat a delegated purchase as Combs, Weschler or unknown, not Buffett by default. Initial Apple buying was a deputy decision; Buffett's own large-scale buying began later. [2020 letter, Family Jewels](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2020ltr.pdf); [CNBC interview, **2017-02-27**](https://www.cnbc.com/2017/02/27/billionaire-investor-warren-buffett-speaks-with-cnbcs-becky-quick-on-squawk-box.html).

## C → D: buyback policy is dated and price-dependent

### 13. 2011: book-value ceiling; 2018: conservatively estimated intrinsic value

“At our limit price of 110% of book value, repurchases clearly increase Berkshire’s per-share intrinsic value.” [2011 letter, **February 2012**, Share Repurchases](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2011ltr.pdf). The original announcement was **September 2011**; the later ceiling was 120%. The **2018-07-17** release removed that book-value ceiling and required Buffett and Munger to consider price below intrinsic value, conservatively determined, while retaining a **then-$20bn** cash/T-bill floor. [Official policy amendment](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/news/jul1718.pdf).

“All stock repurchases should be price-dependent.” [2023 letter, **2024-02-24**, Non-controlled Businesses](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2023ltr.pdf). No buyback simply because cash is abundant. The **2020 letter, 2021-02-27**, reports $24.7bn of repurchases; the **2024 letter, 2025-02-22**, must be read alongside contemporary quarterly disclosures to date the halt. [2020](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2020ltr.pdf); [2024](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2024ltr.pdf). Do not carry the 2018 $20bn floor forward as a permanent current minimum.

## D: 2024 cash discipline and Apple/BofA trims

### 14. Stated tax rationale belongs to Apple; BofA's motive remains unverified

“And we are paying a 21% federal rate on the gains we're taking in Apple.” Buffett discussed the possibility of a higher future corporate tax rate and continued admiration for Apple at the **2024-05-04** meeting. [Morning, Apple question; posted **May 6**](https://buffett.cnbc.com/video/2024/05/06/morning-session---2024-meeting.html). This is evidence that tax contributed to that trim; it is not proof tax fully explains every subsequent sale or that Apple's business thesis had collapsed.

BofA was also sold in **2024**, but **a Buffett statement attributing those sales to the same tax rationale was not verified**. Tax, valuation, concentration, succession preparation and macro fear are competing outside interpretations, not interchangeable primary reasons. [Reuters, **2024-11-02**](https://www.reuters.com/markets/us/berkshires-cash-sets-record-buffett-sells-apple-bofa-operating-profit-falls-2024-11-02).

### 15. No mandatory deployment quota

“We’d love to spend it, but we won’t spend it unless we think we’re doing something that has very little risk and can make us a lot of money.” [**2024-05-04** statement quoted by Reuters **2024-08-03**](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/berkshires-cash-hits-277-bln-buffett-slashes-apple-stake-operating-profit-sets-2024-08-03). The **2024 letter, 2025-02-22**, says the equity preference remains despite extraordinary cash. [Where Your Money Is section](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2024ltr.pdf). Cash is a residual of opportunities, liabilities and prices, not sufficient evidence of a dated crash prediction. Opportunity cost is real; critics' disappointment in 2020 is retained in the forums archive.

## E: 2025–2026 authority and continuity

### 16. Hand-over changes the decider, not the attribution of old trades

“Greg Abel will become the boss at yearend.” [Buffett's **2025-11-10** letter, opening](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/news/nov1025.pdf). The **2026 proxy** confirms CEO succession on **2026-01-01**. [Succession/leadership section](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/meet01/2026proxystatement.pdf).

**Abel, not Buffett**, writes: “We first invested in Pilot in 2017; however, our ability to manage it was contractually delayed until 2023. That mistake will not happen again.” [First Abel annual letter, **2026-02-28**, Pilot](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2025ltr.pdf). The same letter retains price-dependent buybacks and discusses OxyChem and Bell Laboratories announced in 2025. Preserve the distinction between an author explaining inherited transactions and an identified original decider. This is a genuine governance refinement, not evidence that every 2026 portfolio change is Buffett's decision.

## Exceptions and reversals: no slogan overrides the record

| Apparent ban | Actual exception / reversal and its dated evidence |
|---|---|
| Never airlines | USAir 1989; 2016-era four-airline common basket; all sold in April 2020. “The world has changed for the airlines.” [**2020-05-02** meeting](https://www.cnbc.com/2020/05/02/warren-buffett-says-berkshire-sold-its-entire-position-in-airlines-because-of-the-coronavirus.html). Entry consolidation thesis did not survive the demand/capital shock. |
| Never tech | IBM bought in 2011 on IT-department stickiness and management/capital allocation; Apple later treated as consumer franchise. [**2011-11-14** IBM transcript](https://www.cnbc.com/2011/11/14/cnbc-transcript-warren-buffett-explains-why-he-bought-107b-of-ibm-stock-part-5.html); [**2017-02-27** Apple interview](https://www.cnbc.com/2017/02/27/billionaire-investor-warren-buffett-speaks-with-cnbcs-becky-quick-on-squawk-box.html). |
| Falling price always means buy more | IBM: “I've revalued it somewhat downward” after competitors changed the appraisal. [**2017-05-04**](https://www.cnbc.com/2017/05/04/warren-buffett-has-revalued-ibm-downward-cites-big-strong-competitors.html). Lower intrinsic value can justify selling at a loss. |
| Never derivatives | Berkshire wrote long-dated equity-index puts and credit derivatives while warning about leverage, collateral, counterparty risk and valuation. [2008 letter, **2009-02-28**, Derivatives](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2008ltr.pdf). Premiums upfront and contractual terms differ from margin-financed speculation. |
| Only US franchises / no foreign currency borrowing | Five Japanese trading houses, yen financing and long-duration ownership. [2023 letter, **2024-02-24**, Non-controlled Businesses](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2023ltr.pdf); [2024 letter, **2025-02-22**, Berkshire Increases its Japanese Investments](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2024ltr.pdf). Matching currency economics is not an unhedged FX forecast. |
| All commodity bets are forbidden | Occidental admired for assets, management and capital allocation, but no intent to purchase/manage the company. [2023 letter, **2024-02-24**](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2023ltr.pdf). ConocoPhillips' peak-price mistake remains a negative comparison. [2008 letter, Investments](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2008ltr.pdf). |
| Never sell wonderful businesses | Controlled-business reluctance is not a public-stock ban: PetroChina sold around assessed value; Apple trimmed; Paramount exited as mistake. [2007, Investments](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2007ltr.pdf); [2024 meeting, afternoon](https://buffett.cnbc.com/video/2024/05/06/afternoon-session---2024-meeting.html). |

**Open precision:** partnership-era net-net case detail beyond Sanborn/Dempster is not exhaustive. Some historic annual-letter notes use verified month rather than an unverified exact release day. No invented quantitative hurdle, stop-loss, fixed position cap or universal tax explanation is supplied.
