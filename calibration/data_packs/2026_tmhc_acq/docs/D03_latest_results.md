Title: Sequential backlog repair versus year-on-year contraction
Source: https://s27.q4cdn.com/448041563/files/doc_earnings/2026/q1/earnings-result/TMHC-1Q26-Earnings-Release_Final.pdf
Date: 2026-05-29

April's first-quarter release gives two different comparisons that must both be retained. Backlog grew 23% sequentially to 3,465 homes as orders exceeded closings; finished speculative homes fell 30% to 863. To-be-built orders increased to 38% of mix from 28% in the preceding quarter, and incentives declined sequentially by more than 100 basis points. These are indicators of better inventory and order balance. [1]

Against the prior-year quarter, however, closings volume fell about 26%, home closings revenue 28% and adjusted gross margin 420 basis points. Orders fell approximately 14%. Resort-lifestyle demand was the only customer segment to grow year on year, supported by 9% Esplanade sales growth. Management reaffirmed annual guidance, including approximately 11,000 home closings and around $2 billion land investment. That is a forecast, not an accomplished year. The mixed evidence makes it inappropriate to describe the quarter as either an unqualified boom or a collapse of every business line. [1]

Evidence ledger from April 22: first-quarter total revenue was $1.387092 billion, home closings revenue $1.311421 billion and net income $98.625 million. Reported diluted EPS was $1.01, adjusted $1.12. There were 2,268 closings at $578,000 average price and 2,914 net orders at $603,000. Backlog was 3,465 homes worth $2.3 billion. Home closings gross margin was 20.0%, adjusted 20.6%, versus 24.0% and 24.8% a year earlier. The company owned/controlled 75,626 lots, 51% off balance sheet, and invested $503 million in land/development. Liquidity was approximately $1.6 billion including $653 million cash; net homebuilding debt/capital was 20.5%. Book value per share was $64. The company repurchased about 2.5 million shares for $150 million at roughly $61 per share. [1]

Interpretation: the issuer's latest released quarter is the information boundary, not the quarter containing this assessment date. Forward guidance is management's expectation, not a realized result. Growth percentages must be matched to the stated comparison period and accounting basis. Where adjusted measures differ from GAAP, both are retained rather than mixing the favorable numerator from one with the denominator from the other. A favorable quarter does not settle the durability question; the operational causes and cash conversion still require scrutiny.
