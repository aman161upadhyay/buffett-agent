## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**Before October 1987:** the February 27 annual letter already said Berkshire had found no new marketable-equity ideas in 1986, had paid down debt/stockpiled funds, and expected to retain three primary holdings permanently. The contemporaneous year-end 1986 table still included Handy & Harman, Lear Siegler and other holdings. Thus “sold everything except three” is false as a literal inventory claim. [1]

The May 19, 1987 meeting reconstruction describes almost all non-permanent holdings being sold and funds placed in tax-free bonds. It is a later reconstruction, not an authenticated complete original transcript. [3]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“Under current stock market conditions, we have little hope of finding equities to buy for our insurance companies.” **Pre-crash**, February 27, 1987. [1]

“We haven't the faintest idea when that might happen.” Same passage, on when attractive markets would return. This explicitly limits market-timing interpretations. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Maintained Capital Cities/ABC, GEICO and Washington Post as permanent holdings and reduced other investment opportunities when unattractive. At year-end 1987 it reported no major common-stock holdings over $50 million except permanent or arbitrage positions; it still owned arbitrage, credit, cash and operating businesses. Medium-term tax-exempt bonds were around $900 million. Exact security-by-security pre-crash sale dates remain unknown. [1–2]

## What happened after
The February 1988 letter says some stocks briefly became attractive during October's break, but Berkshire could not make meaningful purchases before they rebounded. Coke was bought in summer **1988**, not on Black Monday 1987. `flat` denotes an allocation/pass case without a single attributable P&L, not a measured claim about crash avoidance returns. [2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Valuation:** scarcity of attractive equity prices, not an asserted crash date. [1]
- **Relationships:** permanent holdings were exempt from routine price-based turnover. [1]
- **Alternative:** bonds were a temporary, imperfect capital parking place. [1–2]
- **Uncertainty:** both pre-crash and post-crash letters reject short-term market foresight as the basis. [1–2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: a selective reduction and refusal-to-buy, not an index hedge or abandonment of all equities. The policy began before 1987, so the filename is the requested decision-window locator rather than proof every sale occurred that year. [1–3]

## Public discussion
The Kingswell reconstruction is dated 2024 and distinguished from contemporary source material. Claims that Buffett bought Coca-Cola during the 1987 crash conflict with his own 1989 retrospective dating its start to summer 1988; see the Coke dossier.
