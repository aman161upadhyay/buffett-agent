## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**September 23, 2008:** Goldman sought fresh capital during the crisis and announced a parallel common-equity offering. Preferred-plus-warrants terms are not equivalent to buying ordinary shares at the same day's quotation. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“An unrivaled global franchise, a proven and deep management team.” **Contemporaneous announcement.** The following-year letter separately explains satisfactory current yields and bonus equity participation. [1–2]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Agreed $5 billion perpetual preferred at 10%, callable at a 10% premium; received five-year warrants to buy $5 billion common at $115 per share. Warrants require exercise consideration; they are not $5 billion free common stock. [1]

## What happened after
This file verifies the initial economics and 2009 ownership checkpoint, not lifetime redemption/warrant P&L. [2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Franchise:** Institutional strength under panic. [1]
- **People:** Management depth, not just one named executive. [1]
- **Structure:** Contractual income plus asymmetric participation. [1–2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: Berkshire's capital/reputation earned bespoke terms unavailable to ordinary common shareholders. [1–2]

## Public discussion
Goldman's release presents the investment as validation of franchise and liquidity; that is issuer messaging, not independent risk analysis. [1]
