## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**Late 1989:** Berkshire could negotiate a large preferred investment in Champion. Buffett acknowledged no ability to forecast paper-industry economics; the security structure, manager and price therefore mattered rather than a permanent paper-production moat. Original capital structure, entry multiple and transaction agreement remain unrecovered. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“We only want to link up with people whom we like, admire, and trust.” **Post-purchase**, March 2, 1990, naming Andy Sigler among the managers satisfying that requirement. [1]

“our preferred stock investments should produce returns moderately above those achieved by most fixed-income portfolios” — same letter's group-level expectation; do not present it as a Champion-specific operating forecast. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Purchased $300 million convertible preferred late in 1989, dividend 9.25%, mandatory redemption in ten years, conversion at $38/share. In August 1995 Champion's ability to call at 115% of cost forced a choice. Berkshire converted before the pending call and sold common to the company at a modest discount rather than retain a paper-company common position. [1–2]

## What happened after
The 1995 table reports $388 million sale proceeds. Buffett describes capital gain of about 19% after tax over six years, plus a good after-tax dividend yield. **19% is a cumulative capital gain, not an annualized return**, and excludes the separate preferred dividends. He emphasizes that he and Munger never developed conviction about the paper industry. [2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **People:** the initial trust test was explicit and the later letter calls management candid and honorable. [1–2]
- **Income:** coupon and redemption features supplied much of initial value. [1–2]
- **Option/uncertainty:** common upside was a kicker, not evidence of a strong paper forecast. [1–2]
- **Alternative return:** the bar was moderately above fixed income, lower than a wonderful undervalued business. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: a successful structured financing that remained outside his preferred long-term common-equity circle. A rational exit after conversion avoids confusing a financing investment with love of the underlying industry. [1–2]

## Public discussion
The 1995 letter cautions that press accounts overstated insurers' after-tax dividend yields by ignoring the 1987 tax-law change. Original 1989 public deal debate remains unrecovered. [2]
