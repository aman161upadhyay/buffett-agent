## Cutoff evidence — reconstructed

The available seller-level picture is a profitable established regional confectioner rather than an operating turnaround. A complete freshly closed fiscal-year statement, store-by-store sales series and monthly holiday-season margin report are not available in contemporaneous sources recovered here. Entry-scale earnings and capital figures are reconstructed and rounded. Price realization and physical poundage must be separated: higher dollar sales can reflect increased prices rather than more units. Customer attachment creates a possible ability to recover higher ingredient and labor costs, but there is no quantified forecast of an immediate price increase. The important operating investigation is the persistence of local demand and the cost of preserving product quality.

## Economic reading of the supplied facts

Industry economics: boxed chocolates are purchased both for consumption and as gifts. The purchaser's concern with the recipient's reaction can make dependable quality and an established local name important. That does not eliminate substitution by other sweets or gifts. The retailer must carry inventory, staff shops and maintain product freshness even when demand is concentrated in holidays. Profits depend on realized price per pound after ingredients, labor, occupancy and selling costs. A premium brand has value only if the realized premium exceeds the extra cost of sustaining quality. Regional recognition cannot be assumed to transfer automatically to a new city. These are business-model implications, not an assumption of national expansion or a promised price increase.

Reporting discipline: a full fiscal year's results are not available merely because part of that year has elapsed. The observations above are restricted to the reporting periods identified, and no later quarter is backfilled. An annualized partial period would be a calculation, not a reported result; no such annualization is used unless expressly identified.

For decision purposes, the key question is whether the recent change alters recurring earning power or only its timing. Volume, realized price, operating expenses and capital requirements should be considered separately. A price increase that merely offsets higher input costs is not necessarily an improvement in economics. Similarly, a recovery in accounting profit is not proof that competitive advantage has returned. Where a current operating statistic has not been recovered, this document makes no assertion about its direction. The evidence permits a conditional assessment of durability, not a precise estimate of an unavailable earnings announcement.
