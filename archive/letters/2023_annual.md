# 2023 Berkshire annual letter

**Information date:** 2024-02-24 (month-only where precise public release day was not independently established). **Era:** D. **Author:** Buffett. **Primary source:** [letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2023ltr.pdf). All rules below cite sections of this source; section names are used rather than fabricated PDF page numbers. Annual-period labels are not publication dates.

## Process rules and decision explanations

- Charlie Munger—The Architect: Buffett credits the move away from bargain mediocre businesses; the tribute is retrospective and must not be passed off as a 1965 memo.
- The Scorecard in 2023: BHE regulatory-return disappointment and BNSF operating issues challenge the idea that regulated assets are riskless.
- Non-controlled Businesses That Leave Us Comfortable: Coke/Amex long holds; Japan visits with Abel followed additions; long-term owner economics differ from short-term quotations.
- Same section: Occidental assets and leadership are admired, but Berkshire states no interest in purchasing or managing the whole company. Warrants and common stock are distinct.
- Same section: all repurchases must be price-dependent; favorable per-share arithmetic disappears if buybacks overpay.

## Verbatim evidence anchors

- **The Scorecard in 2023; Buffett admits to a mistake regarding the failure to predict regulatory return changes affecting Berkshire Hathaway Energy.** “I did not anticipate or even consider the adverse developments in regulatory returns and, along with Berkshire’s two partners at BHE, I made a costly mistake in not doing so.”
- **Non-controlled Businesses That Leave Us Comfortable; Buffett and Abel decided to increase their investment in five Japanese companies following management discussions.** “We increased our holdings in all five last year after Greg Abel and I made a trip to Tokyo to talk with their managements.”
- **Non-controlled Businesses That Leave Us Comfortable; Buffett decided to define the scope of Berkshire's involvement in Occidental, explicitly excluding a full purchase or management role.** “Though we very much like our ownership, as well as the option, Berkshire has no interest in purchasing or managing Occidental.”
- **Charlie Munger – The Architect of Berkshire Hathaway; Munger identified Buffett's purchase of Berkshire as a mistake and provided guidance on how to rectify it.** “Three years later he told me – correctly! – that I had made a dumb decision in buying control of Berkshire. But, he assured me, since I had already made the move, he would tell me how to correct my mistake.”
