## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**First half 1966:** a privately owned Baltimore department store was available by negotiation. Buffett's July 12 letter says the quantitative and qualitative features were weighed against price and alternative investments. His account identifies excellent existing managers but provides no recoverable acquisition-date earnings multiple. He cautioned that public notice of an entire business purchase overstated its financial importance to the partnership. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“Even if the price had been cheaper but the management had been run-of-the-mill, we would not have bought the business.” **Near-contemporary, post-purchase**, July 12, 1966. [1]

“It's far better to buy a wonderful company at a fair price than a fair company at a wonderful price.” **Hindsight**, 1989 letter, contrasting this and other bargain purchases with the better approach. [2]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
The partnership and two 10% partners purchased all the stock during first-half 1966, using the Diversified Retailing structure. Buffett left operations with incumbent management. On December 1, 1969, DRC sold Hochschild Kohn to Supermarkets General for $5,045,205 cash plus $2 million and $4.54 million non-interest-bearing notes due in 1970 and 1971; the latter notes' combined present value was estimated around $6 million. Those are sale consideration components, not original acquisition cost. [1]

## What happened after
Buffett's later postmortem says the bargain and fine people did not overcome the economics; he describes escape roughly at break-even. Do not call the deal a spectacular monetary loss merely because it was a strategic mistake, nor infer precise P&L from sale proceeds without acquisition and intervening cash-flow accounting. [1–2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Price:** the actual 1966 letter expressly compares price against quantitative/qualitative business attributes. [1]
- **People:** management quality was necessary, not incidental, in Buffett's original justification. [1]
- **Economic override:** later failure shows that good people and low asset price were insufficient. This is a failure label, not information available in 1966. [2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: an early negotiated control purchase and a formative cigar-butt mistake. It should not be conflated with Associated Cotton Shops merely because both sat inside DRC. [1–2]

## Public discussion
Buffett's 1966 letter explicitly addressed the publicity-versus-position-size confusion. No original independent acquisition article or buyer's 1969 announcement was recovered. [1]
