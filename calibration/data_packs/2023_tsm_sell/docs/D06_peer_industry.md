The target's Q4 wafer revenue mix was 32% from 5nm and 22% from 7nm, 54% combined (January 12 release); these are node mix shares rather than industry market share. Share counts above describe common equity, not wafer capacity. Pure foundry, integrated manufacturing, analog manufacturing and licensing models have different reinvestment requirements, limiting direct margin comparisons.

Compare manufacturing competitors and alternative global investments on both business quality and jurisdictional risk. A different investment need not be a better foundry to be a better capital allocation. Reported margins and technological leadership answer an operating question; production location answers a separate tail-risk question. Overseas factories involve different costs, subsidies and ramp-up requirements, not a free replication of the Taiwan franchise. [Source above.]

Technical context: foundry manufacturing has scale and process barriers but requires continual expensive reinvestment. Gross margin is not owner earnings after fabs, equipment, research and working capital. Distinguish leading-edge from mature processes, production location from customer location, and booked revenue from durable through-cycle utilization. A technically excellent company can have concentration risks outside management control. Overseas facilities require time, qualification and capital; they are not immediately interchangeable with an existing production cluster. An ADR may represent several ordinary shares, so EPS and price must use the same unit. A low multiple on a peak quarter is not automatically conservative. [Source above; analytical framework.]

Comparison discipline: compare businesses on cash generation and required capital rather than treating the nearest ticker as an exact substitute. Normalize accounting differences before interpreting relative multiples. Relative cheapness cannot prove absolute value. A manufacturer with stronger current margins can still have larger reinvestment needs or greater geographic concentration; the numerical table is a starting point rather than a uniform industry average.

Evidence boundary: historical margins and node mix are dated operating observations. They do not settle future utilization, capital needs or geopolitical loss severity.
