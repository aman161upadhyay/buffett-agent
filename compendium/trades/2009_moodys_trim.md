## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**July 2009:** Berkshire held a large Moody's stake amid intense scrutiny of credit ratings. The public controversy is not itself a verified specific reason for each sale. [1–2]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
The 2009 letter says Charlie and Buffett believed the sold stocks would “likely trade higher in the future,” explaining sales partly by cash needs for Dow/Swiss Re and BNSF. **February 2010 aggregate explanation**, not a July trade-specific instruction. [2]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Sold 7.99 million shares for $217.6 million during the July reporting window, cutting ownership to 16.98%. Moody's was also among the year's largest sales. `closed` refers to this completed trim decision, not the whole holding. [1–2]

## What happened after
Moody's remained owned. Exact cost allocation and realized profit for the particular lots unknown. [1–2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Allocation:** Other committed investments required cash. [2]
- **Liquidity:** Future acquisition funding mattered. [2]
- **Boundary:** A reduction need not mean business failure or full exit. [2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: evaluate relative opportunity and portfolio funding, not just conviction in issuer upside. [2]

## Public discussion
Reuters reported sharp Moody's share-price weakness after disclosure. That reaction does not prove Buffett's private motivation. [1]
