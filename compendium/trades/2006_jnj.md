## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**2006 disclosure cohort:** Exact first purchase month, entry multiple and stock-specific investment thesis not recovered. A portfolio table establishes ownership, not a direct healthcare-sector forecast. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
The 2008 letter says he sold holdings he “would have preferred to keep,” naming J&J among them. **Later capital-allocation explanation**, not original entry rationale. [3]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Held 21.335 million shares at December 2006, enlarged to 64.272 million at December 2007. Sold substantially in late 2008 to finance Wrigley/Goldman/GE; added earlier in 2009 and sold later that year. [1–4]

## What happened after
The 2009 letter ties sales to Dow/Swiss Re and BNSF liquidity needs while saying the stocks would likely trade higher. No exact lifetime return established. [4]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Gap:** Original issuer-specific thesis unknown. [1]
- **Opportunity cost:** Attractive alternatives can justify selling a still-liked stock. [3–4]
- **Liquidity:** Cash commitments take priority over maximizing exposure. [3]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: partial sales need not mean a broken business thesis. [3–4]

## Public discussion
Public “rotation into healthcare” narratives cannot replace Buffett's documented financing explanation. [3–4]
