## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**September 30, 1981:** two days earlier Arcata's directors agreed in principle to sell to KKR. Offer: $37/share plus two-thirds of incremental government compensation for redwood acreage. Arcata contested compensation for a 1978 taking of 10,700 acres, initially paid $97.9 million. Financing was a condition; the amount and interest treatment of the litigation claim were uncertain. The later settlement value must not be assumed known at entry. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“KKR's past record for closing had been good.” **Hindsight**, February 28, 1989, explaining his original assessment of the financing condition. [1]

“If KKR went away, Arcata would likely find another buyer, though of course, the price might be lower.” Same retrospective: a bounded fallback, not certainty. He initially valued the claim rhetorically “somewhere between zero and a whole lot.” [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
| Date | Action / change |
|---|---|
| September 30, 1981 onward | Started around $33.50; about 400,000 shares bought over eight weeks. [1] |
| January 1982 | After definitive agreement, bought more around $38, reaching 655,000 shares; increasingly optimistic about redwoods. [1] |
| February–March 1982 | Financing review and KKR repricing disrupted original deal; rival buyer eventually offered $37.50 plus half the claim. [1] |
| June 4, 1982 | Received $37.50/share; retained contingent claim interest. [1] |
| 1988 | Received large settlement distribution; further amount expected in 1989. [1] |

## What happened after
Closing proceeds were $24.6 million versus $22.9 million cost, approximately six-month average holding period and 15% annualized return excluding claim. A 1988 settlement produced another $29.48/share, about $19.3 million; approximately $800,000 further was expected in 1989, not confirmed paid in this source. The common stock closed in 1982 but contingent cash flows extended for years; therefore no single misleading holding-period figure is used. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Closing probability:** buyer record mattered despite financing escape clause. [1]
- **Fallback:** seller willingness supported another transaction, though lower pricing remained possible. [1]
- **Claim:** initially uncertain upside later motivated paying above the original cash offer. [1]
- **Time and financing:** delays and repricing materially changed realized return. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: public-announcement arbitrage with explicit probabilistic questions, not rumor speculation. The unusually lucrative contingent recovery should not be treated as a predictable recurring arbitrage yield. [1]

## Public discussion
The letter recounts an Arcata spokesman's reassurance when lenders reconsidered; Buffett treats that reassurance skeptically. Original deal circulars and contemporaneous litigation filings were not recovered. [1]
