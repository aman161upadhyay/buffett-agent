## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**1951; December 6 recommendation-date snapshot, not necessarily purchase-date information:** GEICO sold automobile insurance directly to selected eligible groups, without agents or branch offices. Buffett reported 1950 premiums written of $8,016,975.79 and 143,944 policyholders; underwriting profit/premiums earned was 18% in 1950 versus 3% for Best's comparison aggregate. He acknowledged that 1951 earnings would be lower than 1950, with summer rate increases expected to help 1952. The exact first purchase month is unknown. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“Probably the biggest attraction of GEICO is the profit margin advantage it enjoys.” **Contemporary**, December 6, 1951. [1]

“At the present price of about eight times the earnings of 1950, a poor year for the industry, it appears that no price is being paid for the tremendous growth potential of the company.” Same article; this is a recommendation valuation, not his verified average acquisition price. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
This was **Buffett personally**, before the partnership, not a Berkshire purchase. His 1995 letter recalls buying 350 shares for $10,282 during 1951 and selling all of them for $15,259 during 1952. Exact execution dates remain unknown. His 1976 rescue investment is a separate dossier. [2]

## What happened after
The sale produced a nominal dollar gain of $4,977 before any unverified transaction costs or taxes (arithmetic from [2]). Buffett says the shares sold would have been worth $1.3 million about twenty years later. That counterfactual is not his realized profit, nor a claim that uninterrupted holding through GEICO's later crisis was riskless. [2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Low-cost distribution:** absence of agents supported customer discounts as high as 30% off manual rates. [1]
- **Margin advantage:** the comparison with industry underwriting economics was explicit. [1]
- **Runway and valuation:** broader licensing and low penetration accompanied an approximately eight-times historical earnings valuation. [1]
- **Risk selection:** no agent pressure to accept poor applicants; promotion could stop in inadequately priced states. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: a business-quality and growth argument already existed alongside Graham-style valuation in 1951. The quick sale breaks the later permanent-ownership ideal; do not retrofit the latter into his original holding plan. [1–2]

## Public discussion
The *Commercial and Financial Chronicle* article is actual contemporary public advocacy by Buffett, not independent market consensus. Authenticated opposing press commentary was not recovered. Later social-media summaries must not replace this dated recommendation. [1]
