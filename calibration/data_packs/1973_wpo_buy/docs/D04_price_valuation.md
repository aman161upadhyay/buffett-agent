## Cutoff evidence — reconstructed

A precise May 1 closing print was not recovered; the table above gives a labelled approximate quotation and the verified December 1972 share count. Private-market reference points available to an analyst in spring 1973: newspaper and broadcasting properties were changing hands at roughly 10–12x pre-tax earnings or 2–3x revenues (e.g. the 1969–72 round of station and newspaper sales reported in Broadcasting and Editor & Publisher), against which the company's $21.8m of operating income and $218m of revenue imply a private-owner value several times the ~$130m public capitalisation; the analyst must reduce any such appraisal by debt ($37m) and by the control discount implied by the Graham family's Class A voting shares.

## Economic reading of the supplied facts

Industry economics: newspapers and broadcasters sell audiences to advertisers while supplying news and entertainment to readers or viewers. An established local position can be difficult to replicate because a new entrant must build both audience and advertising support. Broadcast licenses add regulatory obligations and are not equivalent to perpetual unregulated monopolies. Advertising is sensitive to business conditions; labor, programming and newsprint costs need not decline when advertising weakens. The distinction between a valuable local franchise and the price paid for it is essential. Scale across properties can help administration, but no unverified synergy is counted in the economics presented here. This paragraph describes the competitive mechanism rather than assigning a private-market valuation to any individual property.

Valuation interpretation: the relevant asset is the stream of cash that can be withdrawn over the life of the business without weakening its competitive position. A low multiple is informative only when the earnings denominator is sustainable. A high return on tangible capital can justify a premium to book value, but does not justify an unlimited purchase price.

No closing quotation is inferred from a subsequent transaction average or from the low for a later month. If a dated market price is unavailable, a precise market capitalization, initial earnings yield and implied return are unavailable as well. A negotiated security can instead be evaluated from its proposed cash payments and priority, subject to credit risk. The adverse case should reduce sustainable earnings and allow for additional capital needs rather than simply apply a smaller multiple to optimistic earnings. Interest rates, taxes and reinvestment determine the opportunity cost; no unsupported discount rate or terminal growth estimate is presented as an observed fact.
