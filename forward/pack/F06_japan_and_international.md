## Yen liabilities and economics

The February letter says borrowing approximately matches invested yen cost, average coupon **1.2%**, weighted-average life **5.75 years** at December. [June 10-Q, issued 2026-08-08, note 15](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/qtrly/2ndqtr26.pdf): yen senior-note principal **¥2.481tn** June 30; April issuance **¥272.3bn**, weighted average **2.4%**, maturities 2029–2056, alongside **¥133.9bn** maturing repayments. Do not apply the old 1.2% average to the new tranche or infer new equity purchases from bond issuance alone.

Matched currency liabilities reduce cost-basis currency exposure, but leave equity gains/dividends, maturity refinancing and business risk. Trade/resource cyclicality and affiliate leverage survive a funding hedge. A positive yield spread to US Treasuries is not a locked arbitrage; yen opportunity costs and tax must also be considered.

## Other foreign exposure

Chubb's Swiss domicile and CPKC's Canadian domicile should not be confused with where revenue is earned; Chubb is a June Berkshire holding, CPKC is only a candidate [F01/F03]. Foreign ordinary shares need not appear on US 13F. Diageo ADR was present December and absent March; historic BYD/TSMC ownership is not a verified current position [SEC ledgers cited F01]. Bending Spoons is an Italian issuer: October 14's [6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2004711/000200471126000018/bsp-20261014x6k.htm) identifies the pending $330m common subscription after July's $600m IPO investment. No new foreign-control acquisition or complete current non-US equity inventory is established here. Berkshire's foreign operating subsidiaries likewise are not marketable-security candidates merely because they operate abroad.
