## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**1984 recently established position:** the next annual letter identifies Exxon as Berkshire's fourth-largest holding. It discusses Exxon's aggressive share repurchases alongside GEICO, Washington Post and General Foods. No original entry multiple, oil-price forecast or announced corporate transaction was recovered. The December 1984 position is a later observation, not necessarily the initial purchase size. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“Exxon, in which we have our fourth largest holding, has also wisely and aggressively repurchased shares but, in this case, we have only recently established our position.” **Post-purchase**, February 25, 1985. [1]

“We feel very comfortable owning interests in businesses such as these that offer excellent economics combined with shareholder-conscious managements.” Same paragraph, covering the named repurchasers together. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Held 3,895,710 Exxon shares at year-end 1984, $173.401 million cost and $175.307 million market value. Exxon is absent from the following year's major-holdings table. That absence supports a major reduction or exit but does not establish a precise complete sale date. No merger/tender arbitrage contract is identified in the cited evidence. [1–2]
