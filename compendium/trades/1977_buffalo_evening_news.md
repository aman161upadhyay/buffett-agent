## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**April 1977:** Blue Chip acquired an established Buffalo newspaper. It did not yet have the later Sunday circulation or proven post-competition margins. The subsequent Sunday launch faced the incumbent Courier-Express and litigation. Original acquisition financials, consideration and litigation probability assessment were not recovered, so later profitability must not be placed into the entry snapshot. [1–2]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“Our respect for the News - high when we bought it - has grown consistently ever since the purchase” — **hindsight**, February 27, 1987. The same letter credits editor Murray Light and manager Stan Lipsey through “dark days of financial reversals and litigation” without claiming that their later success was certain at entry. [2]

“High penetration, in turn, makes a newspaper particularly valuable to retailers since it allows them to talk to the entire community through a single ‘megaphone.’” **Later economic explanation**, March 2, 1990. No original 1977 purchase quote was recovered. [3]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Blue Chip acquired the newspaper in April 1977 and launched a Sunday edition late that year. Berkshire's 1983 Blue Chip merger consolidated the remaining indirect minority interest. Expansion and editorial investment were operating commitments, not a passive newspaper-stock trade. [1–2]

## What happened after
Blue Chip's 1978 report shows its share of the News loss at approximately $1.427 million, after specified acquisition-accounting adjustments. By the 1986 letter, Sunday penetration was 83%, selling about 100,000 more copies than the predecessor competitor had ten years earlier despite a declining population. The 1989 letter reports $46.047 million pretax earnings. Those later results support success, but lifetime sale proceeds and realized return are not reconstructed. [1–3]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Franchise:** Buffett says his respect was already high at purchase; original valuation detail remains a gap. [2]
- **Sunday expansion:** actual launch created growth but carried competition and legal risk. [1–2]
- **Advertising scale:** penetration, rather than circulation for its own sake, explains economics. [3]
- **Quality/management:** Buffett credits editorial product and local operating execution. [2–3]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: a strong local franchise requiring painful initial investment and endurance, not a no-risk monopoly acquired with its final economics already assured. [1–3]

## Public discussion
The reports document litigation and early losses, but the original court record and competitor's contemporary arguments remain unrecovered. A modern celebratory account cannot substitute for those missing opposing sources. [1–2]
