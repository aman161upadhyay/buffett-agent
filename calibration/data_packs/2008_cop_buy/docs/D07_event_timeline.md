Technical context: petroleum accounting profits depend on realized prices, volumes, royalties, taxes and the treatment of exploration and development costs. A reserve is not cash in the bank: it needs extraction, transport and often replacement investment. Distinguish upstream earnings from refining and chemical margins rather than assuming they move together. For owner earnings, separate maintenance needed to sustain economic production from expansion capital. Check whether reported free cash flow deducts preferred distributions and whether acquisitions are being called capital expenditure. Proved reserves, production and reserve life use different units and assumptions. A conservative analysis should explicitly test a lower realized-price case without claiming to forecast the commodity. Chronology discipline: distinguish operating period, publication date, transaction date and disclosure date. An ownership report describes a historical balance and does not establish the exact day a trade was executed or the reason it was made. A cutoff within a quarter also does not make that quarter's unreleased earnings public. This digest contains only the developments described above and the technical implications that can be drawn from them. It does not use a later return, revised investment thesis, completed transaction or eventual portfolio weight. The absence of a specific date means the date is unresolved, not that the next convenient calendar day is the execution.

Evidence boundary: missing figures are not available in contemporaneous sources recovered for this digest.
