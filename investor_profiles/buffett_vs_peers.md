# Buffett versus peers: shared disciplines, different implementations

Prepared 2026-10-25 for process retrospectives. **Not a ranking and not a single composite persona.** Columns summarize documented philosophies, not immutable rules. Later explanations are not contemporaneous trade evidence. Source keys link dated primary anchors. Instructions and grading recommendations are analyst synthesis.

## What is common to all great investors vs. idiosyncratic to Buffett

### Shared principles are abstractions, not identical portfolios

Across these writings, investment compares price with a supported estimate of economic value, examines loss paths and preserves the capacity to act. Graham's asset/coverage approach, Lynch's company story and Klarman's realizable-value approach need not produce Buffett's portfolio. Their authored accounts do not supply one common required number of holdings, multiple or holding period. This is a synthesis of the cited prescriptions, not an empirical claim that every successful investor obeys them. [B1,B4,G1,P1–P2,K1]

Independence means a supported view, not reflexive opposition. An asset discount, underappreciated franchise or fixable governance problem supplies different possible explanations for an opportunity; the mechanism and price must still be established. “Quality,” “cheap,” “activist” and “contrarian” are not complete theses. The companion [what_makes_a_great_investor.md](what_makes_a_great_investor.md) discusses the separate empirical limitations; this file does not turn those findings into Buffett quotations. [B1,B3,L1,I1–I2; synthesis]

Mistakes do not disappear because the manager later explains them well. Buffett's Dexter, Tesco and Precision accounts are admissions made after the relevant purchases; Ackman's Valeant postmortem is likewise later evidence. Preserve original forecasts, dates and feasible alternatives. A profitable result is not sufficient proof of sound underwriting, and a loss is not sufficient proof of an irrational decision. [B5,B7,B11,A2; last two sentences are proposed evaluation controls]

### What Buffett retained from Graham and changed with Munger

Buffett's 1989 letter describes the move toward better businesses rather than repeated mediocre bargains; his 2014 review explicitly credits Munger's blueprint. Margin of safety and price discipline remain, but capital requirements, durable advantage and the ability to redeploy earnings become central. These are Buffett's **retrospective explanations**, not a warrant to apply the mature Berkshire method unchanged to an early partnership control situation. [B3,B7,B14]

The 1996 letter emphasizes competence boundaries. The 1986 owner-earnings discussion requires estimated maintenance investment rather than mechanical reliance on reported profit or depreciation. The 2007 See's/FlightSafety/airline comparison distinguishes businesses by incremental capital needs; it does not ban every capital-intensive investment. BNSF is direct counterevidence to such a ban. [B1,B4–B5,B9]

### Where he departs

1. **Ownership without a manufactured catalyst.** Outstanding public businesses can compound without a proxy campaign. Buying an entire business, controlling an early workout and owning a listed minority are different roles. CP's management-change mechanism is not required to justify Berkshire's BNSF investment. [B2,B9,B14,A6]
2. **The financing vehicle is part of the process.** Berkshire's insurance float entails underwriting and payment obligations; it is not free permanent equity. Its derivative contracts must be evaluated by terms and liquidity demands, not dismissed because Buffett warned about other derivatives. Pershing's bought credit protection is a different contractual exposure. [B8,A4]
3. **Scale and incremental capital matter.** The BNSF interview accepts a large continuing reinvestment requirement for adequate long-run returns. The See's comparison favors little required capital, but adequate-return scale can still be rational for Berkshire. Copying the small franchise alone misses this trade-off. [B5,B9; final sentence is synthesis]
4. **Inactivity is an allocation choice, not a crash forecast.** Buffett's 2017 letter describes inadequate acquisition opportunities at offered prices; his 2020 meeting explains why liquidity could not be treated as entirely deployable excess. Neither source establishes a universal optimal cash percentage or a standing prohibition on buying in crises. [B8,B12–B13]
5. **Long ownership does not immunize a thesis.** Tesco's delayed sale is an admitted error; the airline exit reflects a changed industry assessment. A price fall and a deterioration in prospective economics require different analysis. Do not replace re-underwriting with “forever,” or convert every later lesson into an earlier hard rule. [B2,B7,B13; last two sentences are synthesis]

### Retrospective questions for the team

For each replay, ask: (a) Which era, vehicle and actual decision-maker apply? (b) Which economic facts made the business understandable? (c) Did the analysis estimate maintenance capital rather than merely quote adjusted earnings? (d) Was reinvestment value supported by incremental returns? (e) Was a catalyst invented where ownership cash flows were enough? (f) Were insurance, debt and liquidity obligations preserved? (g) Did price and opportunity cost matter independently of admiration for management? (h) Did the agent update the original thesis without importing later admissions? These are proposed evaluation controls, not additional historical claims.

## What is distinctive about Buffett's process that the agent must reproduce

- **Bound the knowable before valuing it.** The February 28, 1997 letter makes competence boundaries central. Reproduce an explicit explanation of what can be forecast and a legitimate pass when that evidence is absent—not an IQ score or sector blacklist. [B1; implementation synthesis]
- **Estimate owner earnings, including maintenance investment.** The February 27, 1987 letter separates distributable economics from reported earnings and acknowledges that required capital spending must be estimated. Show that estimate and uncertainty; do not label all depreciation cash profit. [B4]
- **Price growth through incremental capital returns.** The February 2008 letter contrasts See's with businesses needing much more capital. Its historical See's explanation is **hindsight** about the 1972 purchase. Require reinvestment economics, not merely a sales-growth forecast. [B5]
- **Make price compete with quality.** The March 2, 1990 letter's quality-versus-price formulation and the February 27, 2021 Precision admission belong together. The latter is **hindsight**: admired operations and management did not prevent overpayment. Never grade “wonderful” as permission to ignore price. [B3,B11]
- **Choose the ownership role, not a compulsory intervention.** The February 1989 letter describes exceptional listed holdings; the January 30, 1961 partnership account describes Sanborn's separation. The latter is **after-event evidence** of an early control/workout, not a mature public-campaign default. [B2,B14]
- **Underwrite funding as carefully as assets.** The February 27, 2009 letter explains float, liquidity and written derivatives. Separate underwriting profit/loss, claims obligations, collateral and contractual payoff from common-stock valuation; do not give the agent imaginary free financing. [B8]
- **Allow large, adequate-return reinvestment.** Buffett's November 13, 2009 BNSF interview, filed November 16, discusses a capital-intensive railroad and confidence in long-run US demand. It supports a different economics template from See's, not a capital-intensity exemption at any price. [B9]
- **Permit concentrated knowledge, not unconditional concentration.** The March 1994 letter distinguishes a knowledgeable business analyst from investors better served by diversification. Justify size through understanding and downside while keeping portfolio and group obligations distinct. [B6,B8]
- **Reprice Berkshire shares and stock consideration as real capital.** The February 22, 2020 letter conditions repurchases on value and ample cash; the February 2008 Dexter review, **hindsight**, measures the cost of issuing Berkshire shares. Compare repurchases and acquisition currency with other allocations rather than treating treasury stock or equity issuance as free. [B5,B10]
- **Change the decision when economics change; identify who decided.** At the May 2, 2020 meeting Buffett explained the airline exit; at the May 4, 2019 meeting he attributed Amazon to an investment manager; at the May 4, 2024 afternoon session he accepted personal responsibility for Paramount. Those statements support updating and attribution, not a universal stop-loss, an invented deputy identity or automatic attribution of small holdings to deputies. [B13,B16–B17]

## Implications for grading

### Expected divergences, not defects

1. **Different from Ackman is often correct.** Permit passive ownership without a CEO-replacement plan, a whole-company acquisition, a negotiated security, an early-era workout, a cash decision or a pass outside competence. Select the historically appropriate ownership/funding template, not a composite value-investor personality. [B1–B2,B8–B9,B14–B15; proposed rubric]
2. **Different from the mature Buffett stereotype can be correct.** BNSF's reinvestment, Sanborn's realization mechanism and written derivatives are not automatic failures of fidelity. The relevant era and contract govern. [B8–B9,B14; proposed rubric]
3. **Disagreement across dates is not a controlled experiment.** Do not require the same verdict for pre-conservatorship GSE shares and later encumbered common, or for two railroads with different operating plans. Date, claim, price and role must match before direction is compared. [B9,B19,A6,A8; proposed rubric]
4. **A loss or exit is not itself a process defect.** Airlines, Paramount and Netflix illustrate the need to distinguish the original purchase from the later update. Score the evidence available at each checkpoint, not subsequent price recovery or the eloquence of a later apology. [B13,B17,A3; proposed rubric]

### Defects even when the final direction matches

- **Hindsight leakage:** use of a later letter, managerial appointment, crisis outcome or admission before its publication. The cases above provide historical source dates, not permission to violate the pack cutoff.
- **False attribution:** assigning every Berkshire trade to Buffett, assigning Amazon to a named deputy without evidence, or calling Paramount automatically a deputy's mistake. Score decider accuracy separately from BUY/SELL accuracy. [B16–B17]
- **Mechanism substitution:** generic “quality at a discount” instead of maintenance-adjusted owner earnings, incremental returns and price; inventing activism, a recapitalization timetable or a hedge that the pack cannot establish. [B1,B4–B5,A8; proposed rubric]
- **Financing fantasy:** treating float as equity, ignoring claims/collateral, transferring Berkshire's scale to a redeemable fund, or assigning a fee-free Berkshire structure to HHH. [B8,A12; proposed rubric]
- **Mechanical mythology:** forever for every security, no derivatives ever, no capital-intensive businesses, no sales at a loss, a timeless cash floor, a universal initial yield or a fixed discount rate attributed to Buffett without dated evidence. The cited counterexamples defeat these absolutes; the numeric thresholds in a local prompt must not become invented historical quotations. [B4–B6,B8–B10,B13]
- **Fabricated reasons or silence:** grading Fannie/Freddie sales, Ackman's Berkshire exit, or an unobserved Buffett Herbalife decision against an invented driver. Known action and unknown reasoning are separate labels; the unknown component is unscored pending primary evidence. [B19,A10; proposed rubric]

A direction match with invented reasoning is not a faithful reconstruction; a reasoned disagreement should be recorded rather than silently relabeled correct. Missing primary reasons warrant an explicit evidence-gap flag, not a synthetic answer key.
