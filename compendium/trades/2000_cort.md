## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**November 1999 approach / 2000 acquisition cohort:** A fax described an aborted CORT buyout. Buffett reviewed SEC reports before meeting CEO Paul Arnold November 29. First legal close date remains unknown here. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“A fine though unglamorous business, an outstanding manager, and a price ... that made sense.” **February 2001 retrospective.** [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Bought CORT for Wesco for about $386 million cash. Berkshire then owned 80% of Wesco; treating this simply as a wholly owned direct Berkshire purchase loses the vehicle distinction. [1]

## What happened after
The letter said early performance looked capable of beating expectations. This is not evidence of a realized acquisition return. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Price:** A failed deal supplied a usable starting valuation. [1]
- **Manager:** Arnold impressed Buffett immediately. [1]
- **Business:** Furniture rented temporarily to offices/apartments, explicitly unlike rent-to-own. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: understandable niche economics and operator continuity. Munger's association with Wesco does not erase Buffett's documented origination work. [1]
