## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**March 2006:** Tesco was expanding internationally; UK investors debated execution and competitive threats. Financial results reported in April were not yet published at the March purchase date. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
Original purchase quotation unknown. **2015 post-mortem:** “I made a big mistake with this investment by dawdling.” This explains sale failure, not original purchase intent. [3]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Reportedly bought 57.6 million shares in March 2006. Built to 415 million by end 2012; sold 114 million during 2013 with $43 million profit; sold remaining shares during 2014. Exact final day unknown. [1,3]

## What happened after
Market share, margins and accounting worsened. Total after-tax investment loss was $444 million, not the $43 million gain on the early partial sale. [3]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Entry gap:** Do not invent a price/earnings target. [1–2]
- **Management:** Buffett soured on then-management during 2013. [3]
- **Execution:** He admitted selling too slowly. [3]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: patience becomes error when deteriorating facts invalidate the assessment. Initial GEICO routing alone does not prove Simpson authorship; Buffett personally owns the later error in his letter. [1,3]

## Public discussion
The Independent documented doubts about overseas expansion and rivals at disclosure. Its positive interpretation is journalism, not a direct Buffett quotation. [1]
