# 2014 Berkshire annual letter

**Information date:** 2015-02 (month-only where precise public release day was not independently established). **Era:** C. **Author:** Buffett. **Primary source:** [letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2014ltr.pdf). All rules below cite sections of this source; section names are used rather than fabricated PDF page numbers. Annual-period labels are not publication dates.

## Process rules and decision explanations

- Investments: Tesco management concerns were recognized before full exit, but selling too slowly magnified loss; hesitation is the admission, not an automatic sell-on-price-decline rule.
- The Year at Berkshire: Van Tuyl combines an existing proven operator with a long runway for additional dealerships; decentralized management is an acquisition asset.
- Same section: bolt-ons redeploy subsidiary knowledge and cash without requiring a giant parent transaction every year.
- IBM additions show continued conviction at this information date; the 2017 downward appraisal must not contaminate this record.
- Fiftieth-anniversary reflections: distinguish Buffett’s signed account from Munger’s separate text in the same document. Do not attribute all anthology statements to Buffett.

## Verbatim evidence anchors

- **Investments; Sale of Tesco shares** “In 2013, I soured somewhat on the company’s then-management and sold 114 million shares, realizing a profit of $43 million. My leisurely pace in making sales would prove expensive.”
- **Investments; Mistake in timing the sale of Tesco** “I made a big mistake with this investment by dawdling. At the end of 2012 we owned 415 million shares of Tesco, then and now the leading food retailer in the U.K.”
- **The Year at Berkshire; Purchase of Van Tuyl Automotive** “In October, we contracted to buy Van Tuyl Automotive, a group of 78 automobile dealerships that is exceptionally well-run. Larry Van Tuyl, the company’s owner, and I met some years ago.”
