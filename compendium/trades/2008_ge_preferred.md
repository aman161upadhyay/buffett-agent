## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**October 1, 2008:** GE announced major capital raising. Berkshire's preferred terms differed materially from ordinary GE common exposure, particularly income and redemption premium. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“They have strong global brands and businesses with which I am quite familiar.” **October 1, 2008 issuer release:** Buffett said he expected GE's continued success. The following-year letter separately groups GE with Wrigley/Goldman as satisfactory current yields with equity participation “as a bonus.” These are respectively contemporary brand/business confidence and subsequent group-level income explanations. [1–2]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Agreed $3 billion perpetual preferred paying 10%, callable after three years at 110%; received warrants for $3 billion common at $22.25 per share. Exact closing day unknown. [1]

## What happened after
The 2008 letter says purchases required sales of other liked stocks while preserving ample cash. Lifetime GE redemption/warrant results remain a gap. [2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Income:** Preferred yield supported the underwriting. [2]
- **Optionality:** Warrants added equity upside. [1–2]
- **Liquidity:** Funding did not justify depending on emergency external financing. [2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: evaluate contractual security terms rather than assuming this was a permanent endorsement of every GE business. [1–2]

## Public discussion
The contemporaneous issuer release is verified; independent critical credit analysis not recovered. [1]
