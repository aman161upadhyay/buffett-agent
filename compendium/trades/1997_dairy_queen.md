## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**1997 agreement:** The system had 5,792 Dairy Queen stores in 23 countries, all but a handful franchised, plus Orange Julius/Karmelkorn operations. Management had rationalized an earlier jumble of franchise contracts and financing. Rudy Luther's death created an estate need to sell stock. This is the subsequent shareholder-letter account, not a recovered purchase-day model. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“They are understandable; possess excellent economics; and are run by outstanding people.” **February 1998 explanation jointly describing Dairy Queen and Star.** Buffett had met John Mooty and Mike Sullivan a year before the transaction and was impressed. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Agreed to acquire International Dairy Queen in 1997 and completed the transaction early in 1998. Naming the file 1997 follows the agreement year, not an invented 1997 legal closing. [1]

Offered shareholders cash or Berkshire stock of slightly lower immediate value, encouraging cash elections because Berkshire preferred not to dilute existing business ownership. Nevertheless only 45% elected cash. [1]

## What happened after
The letter records the addition to Berkshire's operating collection. No independently calculated long-run purchase return is supplied. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Franchise:** Branded consumer demand and franchise operations are the relevant business, not commodity dairy prices. [1]
- **Management:** Operating continuity, not a documented activist replacement plan. [1]
- **Ownership:** Acquisition rather than a minority stock position. [1]
- **Consideration:** Issuing Berkshire shares has an opportunity cost; cash preference did not ensure an all-cash deal. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: established franchise economics plus trusted operators. Valuation multiple and exact contemporaneous financial model remain unknown; product familiarity alone is not the investment thesis. [1]
