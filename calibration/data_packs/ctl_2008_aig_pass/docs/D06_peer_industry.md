Berkshire, Chubb and Travelers are useful underwriting-model comparisons, but a property/casualty combined ratio cannot value a conglomerate containing life guarantees, securities lending, aircraft leasing and structured credit. Buying a subsidiary with ring-fenced liabilities differs from buying common shares of the holding company. A bid must identify reserves, catastrophe exposure, investments, required regulatory capital and transition arrangements. No consistent September peer valuation table or executable subsidiary bid is recovered. [S1; analytical comparison]

The group owns international property/casualty and life-insurance franchises, retirement products and financial-service operations. Distribution relationships, licensing and policy renewals have value distinct from the financial-products subsidiary. An insurer with substantial permanent capital could potentially underwrite a clean purchase of selected operating businesses rather than assume the whole parent's liabilities. The affirmative proposition is that valuable insurance franchises can be bought during a liquidity panic. It is not that every dollar of reported parent equity is distributable. ### Underwriting distinction

Comparable-company analysis is useful only after matching the economic claims. A supplier, a platform, a lender and a brand owner can all report revenue growth while requiring very different reinvestment and balance sheets. Growth funded by customers is different from growth funded by new common equity or acquisition debt. Market-share evidence should therefore be read alongside retention, pricing, capital requirements and the company's ability to retain incremental profit. [Analytical interpretation of S1]

No peer multiple is invented where a same-date, same-definition series is missing. The more useful comparison in that case is structural: who owns the customer, who can raise price, who must invest to preserve competitiveness, and who bears a sudden liquidity demand. The company-specific figures below supply the scale and margin evidence. A sector label is not a substitute for those economics. [Analytical interpretation of S1]
