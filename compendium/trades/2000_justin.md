## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**2000:** Justin combined Western boots with Acme Brick, the larger operation. It was not simply a shoe-manufacturing purchase analogous to Dexter. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
In its territory Acme enjoyed “unquestioned leadership.” **Following-year explanation:** Buffett also emphasized the managers groomed by John Justin. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
After a May 4 referral and meetings in Fort Worth, acquired Justin in July for $570 million cash. Harrold Melton at Acme and Randy Watson at Justin Boot ran their operations autonomously. [1]

## What happened after
No transaction-level realized return established. The letter's regional-brand evidence is acquisition explanation, not a claim of a nationwide brick monopoly. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Brick:** Transport economics make the relevant market regional. [1]
- **Boots:** Named established brands were part of the business. [1]
- **Operators:** Succession had produced capable managers. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: disaggregate the conglomerate before forming the thesis; the most visible consumer product was not the largest operation. [1]
