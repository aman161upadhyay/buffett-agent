## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**Mid-1973:** quoted equity value around $100 million against an estimated $400–$500 million private business value. Buffett's 1985 letter says this range would have been shared by most knowledgeable media analysts/brokers, not a private technological discovery. These are retrospectively stated entry estimates. Precise original purchase months, then-current audited earnings and individual broadcast-property values are unknown here. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“Our advantage, rather, was attitude” — **hindsight**, 1985 letter. Buffett says he bought “at a price of not more than one-fourth of the then per-share business value of the enterprise.” [1]

The letter describes the underlying rule as “the purchase of shares in good businesses when market prices were at a large discount from underlying business values.” No invented 1973 verbatim pitch is supplied. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Bought the original position in mid-1973 for approximately $10.6 million. The 1984 table shows 1,868,600 shares at $10.628 million cost. Proportional redemptions generated cash while maintaining ownership percentage; these are not an ordinary discretionary sell-down. The 1987 letter classed Washington Post with GEICO and Capital Cities/ABC as permanent holdings. Complete post-1989 disposition accounting is outside the verified evidence in this file. [1–3]

## What happened after
The 1985 letter puts year-end value plus that year's redemption proceeds at about $221 million. It credits Kay Graham's superior capital-allocation decisions and says the now much larger market valuation made a repeat of early appreciation less likely. Successful historical business-value correction did not imply any price was attractive afterward. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Discount:** the $100 million versus $400–$500 million comparison is the principal entry driver. [1]
- **Business quality:** the assets were profitable media franchises, not obsolete liquidation inventory. [1]
- **Management/repurchases:** important to subsequent compounding, but do not label all later decisions known in 1973. [1–2]
- **Independence:** Buffett contrasts his business-value approach with institutional price-setting conventions. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: a quality franchise bought at an extreme discount, followed by patient ownership. Later permanent-holding status is an evolution, not proof that the initial purchase carried an unconditional promise never to sell. [1,3]

## Public discussion
Buffett's 1985 description of contemporary institutional practice is a participant's retrospective, not a sampled 1973 press survey. Original opposing research and public reaction remain gaps. [1]
