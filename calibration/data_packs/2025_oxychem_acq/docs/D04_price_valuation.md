Title: A whole-business valuation with no public asking price
Source: OxyChem dated sources [1–4] below; Q2 2025 Olin/Westlake/Dow earnings releases linked in D06; https://www.digrin.com/stocks/detail/OLN/price and corresponding WLK/DOW histories (September 2025 observations, historical retrieval); https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS10 and DGS3MO/DGS2/BAMLH0A0HYM2/VIXCLS/SP500 (2025-10-01 observations); https://www.multpl.com/s-p-500-pe-ratio/table/by-month and dividend-yield table (completed September 2025)
Date: 2025-10-01

There is no separate publicly traded OxyChem common stock and no independently established stand-alone transaction price as of October 1. Occidental's quoted common-stock price values the entire energy and chemical parent and is not a purchase price for this segment. A precise acquisition multiple, enterprise-value bridge or premium to an unaffected target share price is therefore not available in contemporaneous sources. [1,2]

These are explicitly rough sensitivities, NOT reported trailing EV/EBITDA. Shares are Olin June-end 114.6m, Westlake Q2 diluted-average 128.239m and Dow Q2 diluted-average 709.5m. Debt/cash are respectively $2,996.7m/$223.8m, $4,654m/$2,085m and $16,797m/$2,399m; total book $1,987.8m/$10,791m/$18,592m includes minority/preferred-definition differences. Full EV requires those claims and updated shares. All three had quarterly GAAP losses, so no positive Q2 P/E is asserted. A current quarter multiplied by four is not cyclically normal EBITDA, and these mixed peers cannot establish the unquoted target's value.

For a transparent sensitivity, four times the latest quarterly pre-tax income of $213 million is $852 million. That arithmetic is not normalized annual income: chemicals spreads, inventory adjustments, outages and investment cycles can change it materially. A whole-business analysis requires a known price, liabilities transferred, ongoing capital requirements and management arrangements. Pension, environmental and working-capital claims also need a stand-alone definition. A low accounting multiple would not be persuasive if continuing cash costs were omitted. Those missing terms cannot be filled using a later announcement, and no hypothetical offer is presented as an authentic data point. [1-4]

Evidence ledger: Occidental's August 6 release reports OxyChem second-quarter pre-tax income of $213 million. Excluding comparability items, income was relatively unchanged sequentially; negative inventory adjustments were offset by improved export demand for caustic soda and PVC. Earlier releases report OxyChem pre-tax income of $436 million in the second quarter of 2023 and $260 million in the first quarter of 2024. These are different quarters, not an annualized normalized earnings record. For the parent as a whole, second-quarter operating cash flow was $3.0 billion and capital spending $2.0 billion; those figures must not be assigned to the chemicals segment. The August earnings call discusses about $300 million lower OxyChem spending in the following year. No stand-alone acquisition agreement or price is publicly established in this dated source set. [1-4]

Valuation discipline: the calculations in this document are analyst arithmetic, not management targets or a Berkshire underwriting model. Earnings multiples, book multiples and cash-flow yields answer different questions and are not interchangeable. A low multiple offers no protection if normalized earning power has been overstated. Conversely, growth has value only after the incremental capital required to obtain it. No verified contemporary consensus forecast or complete historical multiple distribution is supplied; an exact intrinsic value should therefore not be reverse-engineered from a subsequent transaction.
