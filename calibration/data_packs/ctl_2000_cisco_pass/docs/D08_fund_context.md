Date: 2000-03-01

The metadata benchmark is the $37.49 billion market value of common stocks in Berkshire's 1998 annual letter, the last complete annual letter publicly available before this cutoff. The 1999 annual letter is not used because its publication is after March 1. The portfolio contains consumer and financial franchises such as Coca-Cola, American Express and Gillette. Buffett's publicly discussed 1999 market framework distinguishes economic growth from shareholders' purchase-price returns and emphasizes limits to forecasting. [S3,S2]

### Dated operating baseline

Fiscal second-quarter sales were $4.35 billion versus $2.85 billion, up 53%. GAAP net income was $825 million, or $0.23 per share, versus $282 million, or $0.08. Pro-forma net income was $906 million, or $0.25 per share, versus $609 million, or $0.17. The pro-forma measure excludes purchased in-process R&D, acquisition costs, goodwill and purchased-intangible amortization and certain investment gains. Six-month revenue was $8.26 billion, up 52%; actual six-month net income was $1.25 billion, or $0.34 per share, while pro-forma income was $1.73 billion, or $0.48. The company announced a two-for-one split, with February 22 record date and expected March 22 distribution. Per-share figures in this release precede that distribution. Seven completed acquisitions are discussed; three were accounted for as poolings, with relevant historical results restated. Revenue growth is demonstrated, but comparability needs acquisition and accounting review. [S1]

### Underwriting distinction

The denominator is capital available to Berkshire, not the issuer's enterprise value or an outside asset manager's client AUM. Insurance float carries obligations; reported cash is not all surplus. A new security should be compared with existing portfolio opportunities, retaining liquidity for operating subsidiaries and negotiated whole-business purchases. No leverage at the parent is assumed to make the proposal fit. The question is an incremental allocation within a large permanent-capital enterprise. [Analytical interpretation of S3]

For a public stock, a deputy-scale position and a Buffett-scale position need different sizing even when both are plausible investments. For a negotiated financing, the relevant cash commitment includes follow-on needs rather than only the opening check. No future trade, retrospective explanation or confidential public-equity holding is supplied as a recommendation. The issuer's current operating scale below helps compare the proposed exposure with the available fund capital. [Analytical interpretation of S1,S3]
