## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**July 2002:** Telecom distress contrasted sharply with bubble-era valuations. The security was subordinated convertible credit, so it must not be represented as secured debt or a technology-stock capitulation. [1–2]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
No authenticated standalone Buffett quotation recovered. Coupon/conversion terms are documented facts; their appeal is research interpretation, not a verbatim thesis. [1–2]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Committed $100 million; Longleaf $300 million and Legg Mason $100 million. **Correction:** $500 million was the whole financing, not Berkshire's own check. [1–2]

## What happened after
November 2003 reporting said Berkshire cut shares from 19.9 million to 1.6 million during Q3 and described doubling its money. Complete bond conversion/cash-flow reconciliation remains unknown. [3]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Structure:** 9% coupon and conversion participation. [1–2]
- **Risk:** Junior/subordinated, not risk-free. [1]
- **Price regime:** Post-bubble financing, distinct from 1999 abstention. [1–2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: security-specific opportunism can coexist with avoiding speculative common equity. [1–2]

## Public discussion
Contemporary coverage highlighted Buffett's previous technology avoidance; that framing does not erase instrument differences. [2]
