## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**2001:** Fruit was in bankruptcy after excessive debt and disappointing acquisitions. Rehired CEO John Holland had begun repairing deliveries, costs and customer relationships. Those conditions differ from an undamaged ordinary stock investment. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
The letter identifies Holland and the brand as key assets. Berkshire required Holland to remain available as CEO; no financing condition was attached to its offer. **February 2002 disclosure while creditor approval remained pending.** [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Contracted in 2001 and completed the purchase in 2002. The file year denotes agreement; it does not claim a 2001 close or purchase of bankrupt common shares. [1–2]

## What happened after
Transaction-level realized return unknown. Buffett's 1955 Graham-Newman experience is background, not Berkshire's acquisition entry date. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Brand:** Franchise value despite balance-sheet failure. [1]
- **Operator:** Management repair was specific and conditional. [1]
- **Structure:** Certain cash financing aided the bankruptcy offer. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: buy a salvageable operating franchise with the right manager, not “buy every bankrupt brand.” [1]

## Public discussion
Original court documents and opposing creditor arguments remain gaps. [1–2]
