Net earnings margins are not upstream operating margins: integrated refining, sales taxes and commodity marketing affect revenue denominators. Shares describe diluted common stock, not reserves or industry share. Acquisition-driven production growth should not be mistaken for same-asset growth.

Compare Occidental with Chevron, Exxon Mobil and other producers on resource life, operating costs, debt, preferred claims, capital spending and cash returns. Integrated downstream exposure differs across the peer set. Hollub's stated allocation priorities are observable evidence to test against reported cash uses. Do not infer that all producers are attractive merely because the commodity price has risen. [Source above.]

Technical context: petroleum accounting profits depend on realized prices, volumes, royalties, taxes and the treatment of exploration and development costs. A reserve is not cash in the bank: it needs extraction, transport and often replacement investment. Distinguish upstream earnings from refining and chemical margins rather than assuming they move together. For owner earnings, separate maintenance needed to sustain economic production from expansion capital. Check whether reported free cash flow deducts preferred distributions and whether acquisitions are being called capital expenditure. Proved reserves, production and reserve life use different units and assumptions. A conservative analysis should explicitly test a lower realized-price case without claiming to forecast the commodity. [Source above; analytical accounting framework, not additional reported figures.]

Comparison discipline: compare businesses on the source of their cash and capital required to earn it, rather than treating the nearest ticker as an exact substitute. Normalize financing and accounting differences before interpreting relative multiples. A peer can have a stronger balance sheet but a worse asset base. Relative cheapness alone cannot prove absolute value. Compare industry figures without turning differently integrated producers into a fictitious uniform average.

Evidence boundary: reported production and earnings are historical observations. Integrated-company net margins are not substitutes for normalized upstream operating returns.
