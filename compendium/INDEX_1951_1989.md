# Buffett trade compendium — 1951–1989

## Reading this index

**48 evidence-weighted dossiers:** all 43 requested cases plus five additional directly documented cases (Commonwealth Trust, Texas National Petroleum, Waumbec, the original Capital Cities position and RJR takeover arbitrage). This is not a complete ledger of every personal, partnership, Berkshire or subsidiary transaction. Source URLs and document dates are in each linked dossier; this table summarizes that evidence rather than adding unsourced facts.

- **Era labels:** personal prelude in 1951–1954; **A = Partnership & early Berkshire, 1956–1979**; **B = 1980–1999**, with this index covering B only through 1989. Subsequent outcome evidence is labeled hindsight, not inserted into the original information set.
- **Dates:** `entry_date` means actual first purchase, not a filing or annual letter. Where only a year, an observation or “right after year-end” is established, machine-readable fields remain `unknown`. Filename years are research locators when exact initial entry is not verified. A letter's signature date identifies the source; it is not necessarily its release day or the first public disclosure ever. `disclosure_date` is a recovered dated disclosure where available, not a guaranteed first-ever announcement.
- **Verified corrections:** Dempster began in **1956**, before 1961 control. Handy & Harman was already held in **1979**, not first proved in 1981. The 1984 Exxon primary account supports a **business/repurchase investment**, not merger arbitrage. Fannie Mae was **partly bought then sold**, not a zero-exposure omission. Freddie Mac's nominal preferred was **economically common**, not a negotiated redeemable convertible. Coke started in **summer 1988**, not the October 1987 crash.
- **Size/price:** observed positions are not necessarily lifetime peaks. Cost and market value are distinguished. Historical share counts are not retroactively split-adjusted unless the source explicitly does so. A legal 4% issuer limit is not 4% of Berkshire NAV. Company-level operating profit is not Berkshire-attributable realized investment P&L.
- **Attribution:** personal/BPL actions are not literally Berkshire purchases, despite the fixed template's section heading. Blue Chip/DRC/Wesco subsidiary structures are named. Freddie's detailed thesis is Munger's, while Buffett endorsed the holding; restricted-schema `decider: unknown` avoids inventing an individual order attribution. Verified ownership alone is not verified original rationale.
- **Outcome/status:** `unknown` follows the schema's overriding unknown rule rather than fabricating a return or disposition. `open` means retained ownership at the cited historical follow-up, **not a 2026 ownership audit**. `win` can mean well-supported appreciation/catalyst success with final P&L unknown. `flat` on allocation/financing cases means no comparable single-security return, not zero benefit. Fannie's `loss` is forgone opportunity, not a cash loss. USAir's bad underwriting and eventual recovery are both preserved.
- **No double counting:** Berkshire's initial stock/control mistake, NICO acquisition and textile shutdown overlap. Blue Chip, See's, Wesco, Buffalo and the Blue Chip merger overlap. Preferred-to-common conversions and Cap Cities/Disney exchanges are transformations, not independent realized profits to sum. RJR common, RJR takeover arbitrage and RJR bonds are separate exposures.
- **Grounding:** the strongest cases use named primary letters. Exact original pitches remain missing for some entries. Amex has a verified Buffett 1980 retrospective comparison but thin original trade accounting; Wesco's original 1973 thesis, Handy's original pitch, Reynolds' original rationale, Blue Chip's original entry date and many exact exits remain gaps. Explicit unknown driver slots are **not** calibration ground truth.
- **Books/media:** no uninspected book chapter is cited or invented. Contemporary press, retrospective press, owner commentary and reconstructed meeting notes are identified separately. Missing independent public debate is recorded rather than replaced with fictional quotes.

## Priority evidence gaps / calibration cautions

1. Recover original Amex liabilities, actual purchase/sale ledger and 1964 underwriting; the primary 1980 comparison now grounds the broad franchise thesis.
2. Recover Blue Chip's initial execution/vehicle history, Wesco's original 1973 rationale, Handy & Harman's original Buffett pitch and Reynolds' original rationale. Do not assign substitute generic moat/float explanations as exact historical reasoning.
3. Recover original acquisition agreements for retailers and Scott Fetzer; distinguish equity consideration, total-company valuation, debt and accounting adjustments.
4. Recover original pre-crash meeting transcript and security-level reductions. Preserve the pre-crash primary letter, but do not train a crash-prediction claim.
5. Recover complete preferred agreements, credit issue identifiers, conversion/redemption and coupon-inclusive return ledgers. Temporary markdowns, lifetime profits, foregone upside and underwriting mistakes are different labels.
6. Original independent contemporary opposition/press is thinner than management commentary in many cases. Every dossier says where that evidence was not recovered rather than inventing a contemporary consensus.
