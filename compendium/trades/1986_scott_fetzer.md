## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**Early 1986, immediately after year-end:** Scott Fetzer had approximately $700 million annual sales across 17 businesses, including World Book, Kirby, Campbell Hausfeld and Wayne. World Book represented about 40% of sales and somewhat more of earnings. Buffett describes good-to-excellent returns on invested capital in most units and a long public sale process with earlier transactions failing to close. These are immediate post-purchase descriptions; exact closing month, detailed original valuation and final closing adjustments remain gaps. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“Scott Fetzer is a prototype - understandable, large, well-managed, a good earner.” **Immediate post-purchase explanation**, March 4, 1986. [1]

“Ralph's operating and capital-allocation record is superb” — same letter, about CEO Ralph Schey. Product quality at World Book plus modest customer prices also helped justify Berkshire's purchase price. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Acquired the company just after 1985 year-end for about $320 million, with roughly $90 million pre-existing debt left in place. The following annual letter's purchase-accounting appendix uses **$315 million** paid against $172.4 million book net assets. These are retained as differently stated approximations/accounting presentations, not silently forced into one exact enterprise value. Schey stayed in charge. [1–2]

## What happened after
The 1989 letter says aggregate earnings had increased each year since purchase and credits Schey's management of both operations and capital. It describes cash generated for Berkshire to make other commitments. The 1986 appendix uses Scott Fetzer to explain why acquisition accounting can obscure owner earnings; depreciation/amortization adjustments are not automatically cash expenses. Exact cumulative distributed cash is not reconstructed here. [2–3]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Understandability/returns:** actual operating economics, not conglomerate complexity for its own sake. [1]
- **World Book:** valuable product and leadership supplied a specific business case. [1]
- **Operator:** Schey's rationalization and prior allocation record were explicit attractions. [1]
- **Cash:** later evidence confirms distributable earnings and capital management; do not pretend the full later outcome was certain at purchase. [2–3]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: a controlled good-business acquisition supported by owner earnings rather than reported post-acquisition GAAP profit alone. `open` denotes retained ownership in the cited follow-up, not a current disposition audit. [1–3]

## Public discussion
The source describes competing sale efforts and failed closing plans but original banker materials and independent bidder assessments were not recovered. Preserve the distinction between equity consideration, assumed debt and accounting purchase price. [1–2]
