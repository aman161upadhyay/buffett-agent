Date: 2016-02-11

The February 11 unadjusted close was $150.47, up $6.80 that day, as reported contemporaneously by Associated Press. Modern split-adjusted prices around $10.03 must not be mixed with the original share count. A profitable historical EPS multiple is unavailable because the company has no consistent GAAP profit base in the evidence. Owner earnings require actual operating cash, maintenance and growth capital allocation, working capital and dilution. The forecast $1.5 billion capex is a cash claim on the bull thesis, not an add-back to earnings. [S1,S2,S6; analytical framework]

Management reports luxury-sedan share gains and rising orders despite falling gasoline prices. It says customers emphasize performance, technology, safety and style rather than fuel savings alone. Those observations support a consumer-brand interpretation instead of a pure oil-price or subsidy bet. The Model X ramp, new factory capacity and planned lower-priced vehicle could broaden the addressable market. A bull sees a strong brand under a production-growth cloud, with current spending building a much larger recurring installed base. [S1; bull interpretation]

### Dated operating baseline

The February 10 shareholder update reports more than 107,000 vehicles on roads in 42 countries and almost two billion cumulative miles driven. Fourth-quarter deliveries grew more than 76% year on year. Global Model S orders grew more than 35%. Fourth-quarter 'core operational cash flow' was $179 million, constructed from $30 million of GAAP cash used in operations plus $209 million received from sales to leasing partners. It is not positive GAAP operating cash flow. Management planned 60–80% vehicle-sales growth, approximately $1.5 billion of capital investment and full-year non-GAAP profitability for the current year; modest GAAP profitability was forecast only for the fourth quarter. The letter targeted Model S gross margin near 30% by year-end. A February 9 amendment increased asset-based credit commitments from $750 million to $1 billion. Model X production had been limited in January for quality reasons. [S1,S2]

### Underwriting distinction

Price is the amount paid for a particular legal claim, not a percentage below a previous high. A cash-rich balance sheet, a large backlog or an admired brand may support valuation, but none fixes the distribution to that claim. Common shares, a preferred security and an operating-asset acquisition require different loss and return calculations. For ordinary equity, estimate distributable earnings after recurring investment and liabilities; for financing, identify coupon, priority, security and conversion economics before computing an expected return. [Analytical interpretation of S1]

The figures below are the denominator checks for any valuation. No observed decline is called an adequate margin of safety without a justified earnings range. Missing contemporaneous prices or maintenance-investment figures remain missing. Forward scenarios may be useful, but their probabilities are analyst assumptions rather than facts or Berkshire's stated forecasts. [Analytical interpretation of S1]
