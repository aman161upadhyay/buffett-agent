FRED daily observations and historical Multpl monthly tables are later retrievals of the stated periods, not live quotes. Completed April, not a future full-May average, supplies the valuation observation. This mixes a May 1 price with April valuation context explicitly; it is not a synchronized forward-P/E model. Drawdown uses closing levels only through the cutoff, not later intraday highs. No cutoff-dated analyst consensus is asserted.

### Peer multiples / competing liquid claims

| Claim | Current-income or earnings comparison | Distinction |
|---|---:|---|
| Treasury bills | 3.68% yield | short duration; reinvestment risk |
| Ten-year Treasury | 4.39% yield | longer nominal-rate duration |
| Broad equities | 3.92% trailing earnings yield; 1.16% dividend yield | earnings are neither guaranteed nor fully distributable |

Index earnings yield is 100/25.49, approximately 0.24 percentage points above bills and 0.47 points below the long bond. These are arithmetic comparisons, not equity-risk-premium estimates: growth, inflation, taxes and cyclicality differ. High-yield OAS is a credit spread, not a quoted Treasury-plus-OAS realized return. VIX measures implied volatility, not the probability of permanent capital loss. The comparison supplies opportunity costs without predicting the market's subsequent direction.

A large cash balance earns a liquid contractual return but exposes the owner to inflation and missed compounding if productive assets become attractive. Buying Berkshire stock below conservatively estimated value is another use, but the letter gives no current intrinsic-value target or authorization to ignore price. A portfolio stance should therefore reflect the availability of sound individual investments and the value of resilience, rather than claim an exact index fair value. There is no crash forecast, market top date or mechanical cash target derived from a market-cap/GDP ratio in the recovered record. [1]

Evidence ledger from the February 28 letter and annual report: 2025 operating earnings were $44.5 billion versus $47.4 billion in 2024; operating cash flow was $46 billion. Float was approximately $176 billion versus $171 billion a year earlier. The insurance-and-other cash, cash-equivalent and Treasury-bill balance, net of unsettled purchases, was $369.0 billion; broader headline cash definitions differ. The letter says total cash and U.S. Treasury holdings exceed $370 billion. Equity securities were $297.8 billion at year end, including holdings outside the U.S. 13F perimeter. There were no own-share repurchases during 2025. Holding-company/BHFC-type borrowings excluding BNSF and BHE were $45.8 billion. The report emphasizes redundant liquidity and independent financing capacity, not a guaranteed forecast of stock-market direction. [1,2]

Valuation discipline: the calculations in this document are analyst arithmetic, not management targets or a Berkshire underwriting model. Earnings multiples, book multiples and cash-flow yields answer different questions and are not interchangeable. A low multiple offers no protection if normalized earning power has been overstated. Conversely, growth has value only after the incremental capital required to obtain it. No verified contemporary consensus forecast or complete historical multiple distribution is supplied; an exact intrinsic value should therefore not be reverse-engineered from a subsequent transaction.
