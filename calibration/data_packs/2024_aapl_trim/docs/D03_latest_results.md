Technical context: a consumer ecosystem may monetize both a device and recurring services, but they are linked rather than independent annuities. Customer retention, replacement frequency, pricing and the required cost of innovation determine owner economics. Separate hardware sales, installed-base activity and services revenue, avoiding double-counting value from the same customer relationship. Cash returned through buybacks must be compared with share issuance and purchase price; authorization is not execution. Foreign-held cash, taxes and operating liquidity needs affect deployable excess capital. Use one split basis for price, shares and EPS, since mismatched adjustments can make an otherwise correct earnings multiple meaningless. [Source above; analytical framework.]

Results discipline: establish which period the release covers and which publication was actually available by the information boundary. Compare growth in units, prices, margins and cash separately, since a higher EPS figure can come from a smaller share count or an accounting item rather than stronger demand. Treat management targets as claims to test against demonstrated history, not as achieved earnings. A weak period may be cyclical, structural or exceptional; describe the evidence needed to distinguish these possibilities. Do not introduce a subsequent quarterly report to explain an ambiguous current observation, and do not confuse disclosure day with the date cash was earned.

Evidence boundary: quarterly results, management outlook and capital-return authorization have different evidentiary status. An authorization is not an executed buyback or an intrinsic-value estimate.
