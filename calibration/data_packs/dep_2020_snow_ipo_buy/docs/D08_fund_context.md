Berkshire's June report, already published in August, reports approximately $146.6 billion of cash, cash equivalents and Treasury bills, the metadata benchmark. The last annual report's equity portfolio was approximately $248 billion. Combs and Weschler have independent smaller mandates with a wider universe than Buffett's personally originated ideas. A proposed sub-$1 billion software allocation should be evaluated within that mandate. This document does not treat Berkshire's name as a guarantee of intrinsic value or import the future execution into the analyst's recommendation. [S3,S4]

### Dated operating baseline

The IPO registration reports fiscal-year revenue of $264.7 million versus $96.7 million, up approximately 174%. Fiscal-year net loss was approximately $348.5 million. For the six months ended July 31, revenue was $242.0 million versus $104.0 million, up approximately 133%, while net loss was $171.3 million versus $177.2 million. Net revenue retention was 158% at July 31. The customer count was 3,117, and 56 customers generated more than $1 million of trailing-twelve-month product revenue. Retention measures the expansion of an existing cohort; it is not consolidated growth or guaranteed contractual revenue. The prospectus describes a consumption model rather than fixed subscription revenue. Two share classes give Class B ten votes per share and Class A one. The contemplated primary issuance is 28 million shares, excluding the possible underwriters' option. A $250 million private placement and a 4,042,043-share secondary block are distinct financing legs. [S1]

### Underwriting distinction

The denominator is capital available to Berkshire, not the issuer's enterprise value or an outside asset manager's client AUM. Insurance float carries obligations; reported cash is not all surplus. A new security should be compared with existing portfolio opportunities, retaining liquidity for operating subsidiaries and negotiated whole-business purchases. No leverage at the parent is assumed to make the proposal fit. The question is an incremental allocation within a large permanent-capital enterprise. [Analytical interpretation of S3]

For a public stock, a deputy-scale position and a Buffett-scale position need different sizing even when both are plausible investments. For a negotiated financing, the relevant cash commitment includes follow-on needs rather than only the opening check. No future trade, retrospective explanation or confidential public-equity holding is supplied as a recommendation. The issuer's current operating scale below helps compare the proposed exposure with the available fund capital. [Analytical interpretation of S1,S3]
