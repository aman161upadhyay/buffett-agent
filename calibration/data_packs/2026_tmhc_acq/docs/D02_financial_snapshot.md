Title: Inventory, lots and financial resilience
Source: https://s27.q4cdn.com/448041563/files/doc_earnings/2026/q1/earnings-result/TMHC-1Q26-Earnings-Release_Final.pdf
Date: 2026-05-29

The March balance sheet reports $6.238563 billion real estate inventory, plus $388.277 million land deposits. Those are substantial commitments relative to a quarter's $98.625 million net income. Approximately 51% of the lot supply is controlled off balance sheet; that structure can reduce capital intensity, but does not make deposits or contractual obligations valueless in a downturn. [1]

Reported home closings margin is 20.0%, adjusted 20.6%; the difference includes $8.2 million inventory impairment charges. Book value per share increased 11% to $64, while share repurchases reduced the denominator. Gross homebuilding debt/capital was 26.6%, net 20.5%. Liquidity includes revolving availability and is not entirely cash. These observations support a resilient operating platform but do not establish liquidation value of land at book. A complete warranty-liability stress, maintenance/growth split and normalized full-cycle owner-earnings series have not been independently reconstructed in this pack. [1]

Evidence ledger from April 22: first-quarter total revenue was $1.387092 billion, home closings revenue $1.311421 billion and net income $98.625 million. Reported diluted EPS was $1.01, adjusted $1.12. There were 2,268 closings at $578,000 average price and 2,914 net orders at $603,000. Backlog was 3,465 homes worth $2.3 billion. Home closings gross margin was 20.0%, adjusted 20.6%, versus 24.0% and 24.8% a year earlier. The company owned/controlled 75,626 lots, 51% off balance sheet, and invested $503 million in land/development. Liquidity was approximately $1.6 billion including $653 million cash; net homebuilding debt/capital was 20.5%. Book value per share was $64. The company repurchased about 2.5 million shares for $150 million at roughly $61 per share. [1]

Accounting discipline: all dollar amounts in this document retain the period and basis given in the evidence ledger. Revenue is not owner earnings; neither adjusted profit nor operating cash flow alone measures distributable cash. Maintenance investment, working capital, compensation dilution and financing claims must be considered separately. A quarter is not a full economic cycle. Where a stand-alone balance sheet, maintenance-capital split or historical consensus series is absent, it is unavailable rather than assumed to be zero or inferred from a later filing.
