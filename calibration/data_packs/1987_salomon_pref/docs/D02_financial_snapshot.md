## Cutoff evidence — reconstructed

The proposed security is $700 million of 9% convertible preferred. The contractual annual dividend is $63 million, calculated as principal times coupon, before taxes and credit impairment. Conversion is at $38 per common share after three years, implying about 18.42 million shares if the full principal converts, subject to exact instrument terms. If unconverted, redemption is scheduled ratably over five annual installments beginning on the contract's later specified redemption commencement date. The complete prospectus, detailed covenant package and a fresh dealer balance-sheet stress test are not recovered. Preferred priority is over common, not over all financing or counterparty obligations.

## Economic reading of the supplied facts

Industry economics: a securities dealer finances inventory and counterparty exposures as well as providing advice, underwriting and distribution. Earnings can be large while requiring substantial financing and exposing the enterprise to abrupt changes in liquidity. A reputation for reliability is part of the franchise because clients and lenders can move their business quickly. Published book equity is not the same as immediately realizable cash if markets become disorderly. A preferred investment can offer a defined dividend and priority over common equity, but it does not eliminate the dealer's obligations to its creditors. Conversion gives participation in better operating results while leaving the holder exposed to the issuing company's solvency. These security distinctions matter more than a generic common-stock earnings multiple.

Measurement notes: amounts in this reconstruction are nominal dollars, not inflation-adjusted dollars. A historical balance-sheet observation is not a current market quotation. Reported profit, distributable cash and return on tangible operating capital are different quantities. Where a component is missing, owner earnings cannot be calculated precisely by relabeling net income as free cash flow.

The useful reconciliation begins with recurring earnings, adds genuinely noncash charges, and subtracts the expenditures and working capital needed to maintain competitive position. Interest and taxes must be treated consistently with the security being valued. A pretax business yield must not be compared directly with an after-tax common-stock yield. Minority interests, debt, preference claims and cash needed for operations can all change what belongs to the common shareholder. Missing line items are not set to zero. No complete balance sheet, earnings bridge or leverage ratio is implied by the selected figures reproduced here.
