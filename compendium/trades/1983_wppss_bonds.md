## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**October 1983:** WPPSS Projects 4 and 5 had defaulted July 1 on $2.2 billion bonds. Berkshire instead bought obligations of **Projects 1, 2 and 3**, with different obligors, promises and properties and support from Bonneville Power Administration. The default cast a cloud across the issuer name, but nondefaulted projects also had real independent risks. The $139 million cost/$205 million face snapshot is **year-end 1984**, not the initial position. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“We extend this business-valuation approach even to bond purchases such as WPPSS.” **Explanation after accumulation**, February 25, 1985. [1]

“Despite these important negatives, Charlie and I judged the risks at the time we purchased the bonds and at the prices Berkshire paid ... to be considerably more than compensated for by prospects of profit.” The same letter acknowledges a slight possibility of total loss and delayed interest. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Insurance subsidiaries continuously bought from October 1983 through June 1984, attempting to buy nearly all available bonds but obtaining less than 3% outstanding. By year-end 1984: $139 million cost, $205 million face, $22.7 million annual tax-exempt interest, a 16.3% cash return on cost. At year-end 1987 cost had grown to $240 million and market value $316 million. These are different dated inventories. [1–2]

## What happened after
In 1989 Berkshire sold several low-coupon issues that had roughly doubled and paid 15%–17% annually tax-free, while retaining high-coupon bonds. Exact aggregate realized lifetime P&L and final maturity/disposal schedule remain unknown. The quoted coupon receipts should not be mechanically compounded as if reinvestment at the same distressed yield was available forever. [3]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Security differentiation:** did not simply buy the defaulted obligations because they were cheap. [1]
- **Credit:** evaluated guarantees and project-specific legal/economic risks. [1]
- **Businesslike yield:** compared $22.7 million contractual cash earnings with the price of a whole operating company earning the same amount. [1]
- **Risk-bearing capacity:** Buffett explicitly says comparable concentration would be inappropriate for most insurers. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: a bond treated as an unusual business, with capped upside and nonzero ruin risk explicitly recognized. Not a general endorsement of municipal distressed debt or long-duration fixed income. [1]

## Public discussion
The 1984 letter contrasts Berkshire's owner incentives with managers' fear of looking foolish and describes the issuer-wide cloud. Original bond indentures and contemporary legal opinions remain gaps; guarantees are not treated here as an unconditional US sovereign guarantee. [1]
