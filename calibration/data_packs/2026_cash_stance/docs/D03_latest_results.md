Title: The annual letter is the latest complete public capital framework
Source: https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2025ltr.pdf and https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/2025ar/202510-k.pdf (2026-02-28); https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/qtrly/3rdqtr25.pdf (2025-11-01, nine-month financial statements for subtraction)
Date: 2026-05-01

February 28 brings Abel's first annual letter and the full annual report. It states cash/Treasuries exceed $370 billion, explains a preference for ownership of productive businesses and rejects the interpretation that substantial cash automatically means retreat. The capital criteria include understandable economics, durable advantages, high-integrity managers, selective concentration and patience. Repurchases should occur below conservatively determined intrinsic value. [1]

The annual report shows no 2025 repurchases and significant liquidity, but neither alone proves a forecast of market decline. No March-quarter results are public by May 1 in this pack. A completed accounting quarter is not a public financial update until released. The question is how to apply the disclosed capital framework to a supply of opportunities, obligations and competing uses of funds. The next annual meeting's comments or balance sheet are not included. Existing governance and capital-allocation authority are public context, not evidence that every future security decision will be originated personally by the CEO. [1,2]

For auditability: annual operating earnings are $44,486m/$47,437m (2025/2024), versus nine-month $34,286m/$32,910m. Revenue subtraction is $371,444m−$277,212m and $371,433m−$276,517m. Annual and nine-month EPS have different weighted-average share denominators, so their simple difference is NOT presented as exact quarterly EPS.

For the full year, OCF rose 50.3% to $45,969m while property/lease-equipment cash purchases increased 10.3% to $20,927m; the resulting simple cash residual was $25,042m versus $11,616m. This is not evidence that all $25bn is available for security purchases. Underwriting cycles, tax timing, subsidiary capital expenditure and liquidity reserves can create large quarter/year differences. No numerical first-quarter 2026 update is inferred from the calendar or a later meeting.

Evidence ledger from the February 28 letter and annual report: 2025 operating earnings were $44.5 billion versus $47.4 billion in 2024; operating cash flow was $46 billion. Float was approximately $176 billion versus $171 billion a year earlier. The insurance-and-other cash, cash-equivalent and Treasury-bill balance, net of unsettled purchases, was $369.0 billion; broader headline cash definitions differ. The letter says total cash and U.S. Treasury holdings exceed $370 billion. Equity securities were $297.8 billion at year end, including holdings outside the U.S. 13F perimeter. There were no own-share repurchases during 2025. Holding-company/BHFC-type borrowings excluding BNSF and BHE were $45.8 billion. The report emphasizes redundant liquidity and independent financing capacity, not a guaranteed forecast of stock-market direction. [1,2]

Interpretation: the issuer's latest released quarter is the information boundary, not the quarter containing this assessment date. Forward guidance is management's expectation, not a realized result. Growth percentages must be matched to the stated comparison period and accounting basis. Where adjusted measures differ from GAAP, both are retained rather than mixing the favorable numerator from one with the denominator from the other. A favorable quarter does not settle the durability question; the operational causes and cash conversion still require scrutiny.
