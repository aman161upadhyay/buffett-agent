A proposed control transaction must be evaluated on total equity consideration, debt assumed and the value of any acquirer's shares issued. No definitive offer terms public by this cutoff are reproduced. A railroad's recurring maintenance capex and interest burden make EBITDA alone an inadequate measure of distributable cash. [Source above.]

Technical context: track and terminal networks are costly to replicate but also costly to sustain. Depreciation is an accounting allocation, not a reliable measure of current maintenance investment when equipment prices and traffic requirements change. Separate maintenance and capacity expansion without assuming every reported dollar is optional. Earnings depend on traffic density, mix, fuel, labor and pricing; a network moat does not guarantee stable quarterly volume. Debt belongs in enterprise value and interest belongs in the bridge to equity earnings. A controlling investor's own stock is consideration with economic cost, not free acquisition currency. [Source above; analytical framework, not additional measured network statistics.]

Valuation discipline: use a consistent claim and denominator. Enterprise value should be compared with cash generation before financing; common value should be compared with cash available after senior claims. Explicitly distinguish sustainable earnings from annualizing one quarter. A current quote from a different date, an eventual transaction price and an investor's later disclosed average cost are not substitutes for the cutoff price. State a range of operating assumptions, compare the result with liquid alternatives and require a margin of safety that survives a less favorable case. When a current input is unavailable, leave the numerical valuation incomplete instead of embedding an untraceable estimate.
