Title: Financial record and owner-earnings bridge
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885590/000119312515057297/d878630dex991.htm; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1009356/000119312515059044/d879266dex991.htm
Date: 2015-03-02

The February 23 presentation reports fourth-quarter revenue of $2.3 billion versus $2.1 billion, up 10%; management's cash EPS was $2.58 versus $2.15, up 20%. GAAP operating cash flow was $816 million versus $280 million. That cash figure included a $287 million net Allergan transaction gain; management's adjusted operating cash flow was $624 million versus $607 million, only 3% higher. Restructuring and integration costs were $47 million. Reported same-store organic sales growth was 16%; Bausch + Lomb organic growth was 8% for the quarter and 11% for the year. The presentation explicitly warns that forward cash-EPS and adjusted-cash-flow guidance is not reconciled to GAAP because excluded items are difficult to forecast. The contemplated Salix transaction and financing are additional commitments, not part of the historical quarter's recurring cash earnings. Cash EPS is a management-defined earnings measure, not distributable owner cash. [S1]

The March 2 close and a reliable last-preceding-session quote were not recovered from contemporaneous sources, so no exact entry multiple is invented. The reported $2.58 cash EPS annualizes to $10.32 but excludes costs and is not a forecast. Quarterly adjusted operating cash flow of $624 million is not a substitute for annual owner earnings or financing capacity after Salix. Enterprise value must include acquisition debt and distinguish announced transactions from completed balance-sheet assets. Valuation upside from cost savings should be probability-weighted and not counted twice in both the earnings base and growth multiple. [S1,S2; calculations]

### Underwriting distinction

Owner earnings are not established merely by naming an adjusted profit measure. Start with the reported result; reconcile material noncash items, economic compensation, maintenance investment and working-capital requirements. An acquisition can improve reported per-share growth while requiring cash outside operating expenditure. In a financial institution, liquidity and capital requirements constrain what can be distributed even when accounting profit is positive. In an operating business, underinvestment can temporarily improve cash while reducing franchise value. [Analytical interpretation of S1]

The sourced figures below are a dated record, not a single normalized earnings estimate. Quarterly annualization is a sensitivity tool only. No undocumented maintenance-capex split, adjusted debt number or forward consensus is supplied. Where the evidence cannot support an owner-earnings estimate, the analyst should retain that uncertainty rather than insert a sector-average cash conversion rate. [Analytical interpretation of S1]
