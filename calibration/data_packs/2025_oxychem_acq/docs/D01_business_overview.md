Title: An operating chemical platform, not an oil reserve
Source: https://www.oxy.com/news/news-releases/occidental-announces-2nd-quarter-2025-results ; https://www.oxy.com/siteassets/documents/investors/quarterly-earnings/oxy2q25transcript.pdf ; https://www.oxy.com/siteassets/documents/news-releases/pr-080223_occidental-announces-2nd-quarter-2023-results.pdf ; https://www.oxy.com/news/news-releases/occidental-announces-1st-quarter-2024-results
Date: 2025-10-01

OxyChem is Occidental's chemical subsidiary. Its products include chlorine, caustic soda and PVC, with essential industrial and building-related uses. The segment's economics depend on physical assets, feedstock and energy costs, operating reliability, product spreads and customer demand. A low-cost producer can earn attractive cash flows across a cycle; an essential product does not automatically grant pricing power over other producers. The August results identify export demand as an important variable. [1,2]

The business should be evaluated independently of Occidental's oil and gas reserves. Parent cash flow includes production, midstream and other operations, and cannot serve as a proxy for chemicals owner earnings. Berkshire's existing investment exposure to the parent is described separately in D08; it does not mean Berkshire owns or controls this business. No assumption of cost synergies, new management appointments or a negotiated transaction is part of the public information set. The assets have to earn an adequate return after maintenance, safety and environmental spending. [1-4]

Evidence ledger: Occidental's August 6 release reports OxyChem second-quarter pre-tax income of $213 million. Excluding comparability items, income was relatively unchanged sequentially; negative inventory adjustments were offset by improved export demand for caustic soda and PVC. Earlier releases report OxyChem pre-tax income of $436 million in the second quarter of 2023 and $260 million in the first quarter of 2024. These are different quarters, not an annualized normalized earnings record. For the parent as a whole, second-quarter operating cash flow was $3.0 billion and capital spending $2.0 billion; those figures must not be assigned to the chemicals segment. The August earnings call discusses about $300 million lower OxyChem spending in the following year. No stand-alone acquisition agreement or price is publicly established in this dated source set. [1-4]

Analytical distinction: a large customer base is not itself proof of a moat. The relevant test is whether customers keep paying enough to cover the full cost of serving them, including reinvestment and risk. Product descriptions and reported segment economics below are observations; a prediction that their advantages persist is an underwriting judgment. No terminal growth rate, private management forecast, or guaranteed future market share is supplied. Historical success must be tested against the specific competitive threats, not substituted for that test.
