Title: Financial record and owner-earnings bridge
Source: https://digitalassets.tesla.com/tesla-contents/image/upload/IR/Q4_15_Tesla_Update_Letter; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1318605/000119312516457674/d124215d8k.htm; https://www.sltrib.com/news/business/2016/02/11/u-s-stocks-fall-for-a-fourth-straight-day/
Date: 2016-02-11

The February 10 shareholder update reports more than 107,000 vehicles on roads in 42 countries and almost two billion cumulative miles driven. Fourth-quarter deliveries grew more than 76% year on year. Global Model S orders grew more than 35%. Fourth-quarter 'core operational cash flow' was $179 million, constructed from $30 million of GAAP cash used in operations plus $209 million received from sales to leasing partners. It is not positive GAAP operating cash flow. Management planned 60–80% vehicle-sales growth, approximately $1.5 billion of capital investment and full-year non-GAAP profitability for the current year; modest GAAP profitability was forecast only for the fourth quarter. The letter targeted Model S gross margin near 30% by year-end. A February 9 amendment increased asset-based credit commitments from $750 million to $1 billion. Model X production had been limited in January for quality reasons. [S1,S2]

The February 11 unadjusted close was $150.47, up $6.80 that day, as reported contemporaneously by Associated Press. Modern split-adjusted prices around $10.03 must not be mixed with the original share count. A profitable historical EPS multiple is unavailable because the company has no consistent GAAP profit base in the evidence. Owner earnings require actual operating cash, maintenance and growth capital allocation, working capital and dilution. The forecast $1.5 billion capex is a cash claim on the bull thesis, not an add-back to earnings. [S1,S2,S6; analytical framework]

### Underwriting distinction

Owner earnings are not established merely by naming an adjusted profit measure. Start with the reported result; reconcile material noncash items, economic compensation, maintenance investment and working-capital requirements. An acquisition can improve reported per-share growth while requiring cash outside operating expenditure. In a financial institution, liquidity and capital requirements constrain what can be distributed even when accounting profit is positive. In an operating business, underinvestment can temporarily improve cash while reducing franchise value. [Analytical interpretation of S1]

The sourced figures below are a dated record, not a single normalized earnings estimate. Quarterly annualization is a sensitivity tool only. No undocumented maintenance-capex split, adjusted debt number or forward consensus is supplied. Where the evidence cannot support an owner-earnings estimate, the analyst should retain that uncertainty rather than insert a sector-average cash conversion rate. [Analytical interpretation of S1]
