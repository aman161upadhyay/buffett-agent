Title: Industry structure and comparable economics
Source: https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2022/Meta-Reports-Third-Quarter-2022-Results/default.aspx
Date: 2022-11-04

Alphabet, Snap, TikTok and other media platforms compete for advertisers and time; Apple and mobile operating systems influence targeting and distribution. Network effects can coexist with rapidly changing formats and low user switching costs for attention. Ad impressions are not a directly comparable unit across services. Mature cash-generative app operations should be valued separately from speculative hardware/platform expenditure. No harmonized November 4 consensus forward P/E series for competitors is recovered. [S1; analytical comparison]

The company operates global social and messaging networks monetized primarily through advertising, plus a loss-making virtual/augmented-reality hardware and software operation. Its customer asset is repeated attention and the ability to match advertisers with specific audiences. A bull can separate the established family of applications from discretionary spending on new platforms. Billions of users and a net-cash balance support the argument that the franchise is intact even while reported earnings are under pressure. [S1; analyst interpretation]

### Dated operating baseline

Third-quarter revenue was $27.714 billion, down 4%; operating income was $5.664 billion, down 46%; net income was $4.395 billion, down 52%; diluted EPS was $1.64, down 49%. Operating margin fell from 36% to 20%. Family monthly active people were 3.71 billion, up 4%, and daily active people 2.93 billion, up 4%. Facebook alone had 2.96 billion monthly active users. Ad impressions grew 17% while average price per ad fell 18%. Quarterly capital expenditure including finance-lease principal was $9.52 billion. Cash and marketable securities totaled $41.78 billion and long-term debt $9.92 billion. Buybacks consumed $6.55 billion. Headcount reached 87,314, up 28%. Management projected full-year capital expenditure of $32–33 billion and next-year capital expenditure of $34–39 billion; projected next-year expenses were $96–101 billion, including office-consolidation costs. These are forecasts, not completed investments or realized savings. [S1]

### Underwriting distinction

Comparable-company analysis is useful only after matching the economic claims. A supplier, a platform, a lender and a brand owner can all report revenue growth while requiring very different reinvestment and balance sheets. Growth funded by customers is different from growth funded by new common equity or acquisition debt. Market-share evidence should therefore be read alongside retention, pricing, capital requirements and the company's ability to retain incremental profit. [Analytical interpretation of S1]

No peer multiple is invented where a same-date, same-definition series is missing. The more useful comparison in that case is structural: who owns the customer, who can raise price, who must invest to preserve competitiveness, and who bears a sudden liquidity demand.
