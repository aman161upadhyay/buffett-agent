## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**March 1969:** a bank in Rockford, Illinois, was available for acquisition. Buffett's May 29 letter called it a “$100 million plus, extremely well-run bank”; that phrase must not be relabeled a $100 million purchase price. Acquisition-date capital ratios, credit-loss history and exact consideration were not recovered. The March entry month is supplied by the 1978 letter's retrospective. [1–2]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“I certainly have no desire to sell a good controlled business run by people I like and admire, merely to obtain a fancy price.” **May 29, 1969**, discussing controlled-company managers, explicitly including the newly purchased bank. This is a post-purchase ownership principle, not a bank-specific valuation formula. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Berkshire bought the bank in March 1969, retained its operating management, and later held 97.7% through Rockford Bancorp. Banking-law restrictions required divestiture by December 31, 1980. On that date Berkshire completed an exchange involving 41,086 Rockford Bancorp shares for Berkshire shares, with shareholders able to participate proportionately. This was a distribution/exchange, not necessarily a cash sale at market value. [2–3]

## What happened after
Letters praise banking performance and management; the exit was compelled by regulatory separation, not evidence that Buffett abandoned the bank's economics. Exact cumulative dividends, acquisition basis and exchange-value P&L remain unverified. Holding-period months are approximate calendar arithmetic between March 1969 and December 1980, not day-weighted duration. [2–3]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Operating quality:** “extremely well-run” is Buffett's actual near-entry description. [1]
- **People:** long-term confidence in controlled-business managers included the bank. [1–2]
- **Ownership:** intended continuing control contrasts with the later legally forced separation. [1–3]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: a controlled financial business rather than a diversified bank-stock bet. The regulatory exit is an important discipline distinction: divestiture does not necessarily mean deteriorated thesis. [1–3]

## Public discussion
The cited letters document the legal divestiture timetable. Original banking-regulator orders and contemporary independent acquisition coverage remain gaps; no unsourced purchase price is substituted. [2–3]
