Title: Berkshire capital, mandate and opportunity cost
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/5272/000095012308008953/y64849exv99w1.htm; https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/2007ar/2007ar.pdf
Date: 2008-09-15

The latest annual cash benchmark used here is Berkshire's $44.329 billion at December 2007, not a September 15 available-cash assertion. The 2007 report lists approximately $74.999 billion of equity securities and $58.698 billion of insurance float. Policyholder obligations and already announced capital commitments compete with any rescue. Berkshire's own insurance operations provide relevant expertise but do not eliminate legal-entity risk. A transaction in selected businesses should be compared with new exposure to the consolidated parent. [S3]

### Dated operating baseline

Second-quarter net loss was $5.36 billion, or $2.06 per diluted share, versus $4.28 billion of profit a year earlier. Six-month net loss was $13.16 billion, or $5.11 per share. Adjusted quarterly net loss was $1.32 billion; the adjustment does not make derivatives harmless. AIG Financial Products' super-senior credit-default-swap portfolio generated a $5.56 billion pre-tax unrealized valuation loss in the quarter. Investment impairments and other realized capital losses were $6.08 billion pre-tax. May's approximately $20 billion capital raise included $7.47 billion of common shares, $5.88 billion in equity units and $6.91 billion of high-equity-content fixed maturities. June 30 shareholders' equity was $78.09 billion and book value was $29.04 per share. Consolidated assets were $1.050 trillion. The scale of assets, reported equity and previously raised money is known; the ultimate cash required in a collateral stress is not bounded by these figures. [S1]

### Underwriting distinction

The denominator is capital available to Berkshire, not the issuer's enterprise value or an outside asset manager's client AUM. Insurance float carries obligations; reported cash is not all surplus. A new security should be compared with existing portfolio opportunities, retaining liquidity for operating subsidiaries and negotiated whole-business purchases. No leverage at the parent is assumed to make the proposal fit. The question is an incremental allocation within a large permanent-capital enterprise. [Analytical interpretation of S3]

For a public stock, a deputy-scale position and a Buffett-scale position need different sizing even when both are plausible investments. For a negotiated financing, the relevant cash commitment includes follow-on needs rather than only the opening check. No future trade, retrospective explanation or confidential public-equity holding is supplied as a recommendation. The issuer's current operating scale below helps compare the proposed exposure with the available fund capital. [Analytical interpretation of S1,S3]
