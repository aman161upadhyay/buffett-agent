## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**Late 1989 initial purchases / 1990 expansion:** the leveraged-buyout credit market was deteriorating and Berkshire could buy RJR Nabisco obligations in the secondary market. The detailed business and credit assessment below comes from the **March 1991** letter; favorable asset sales and equity injections discussed there must not all be assumed completed before the first 1989 purchase. Exact issue-by-issue entry yields, collateral and covenants remain unknown. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“The Company's credit is considerably better than was generally perceived for a while” and the yield plus potential capital gain “more than compensates for the risk we incur (though that is far from nil).” **March 1, 1991**, after major additions, not a recovered 1989 pitch. [1]

“RJR has made asset sales at favorable prices, has added major amounts of equity, and in general is being run well.” Same letter, dated credit improvement evidence. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
First bought bonds late in 1989 and added substantially during 1990, reaching $440 million invested at year-end, roughly then market value. Buffett distinguished buying dislocated existing credits from buying new junk issuance. During 1991 Berkshire eliminated one issue facing an exchange offer and subsequent call, while retaining RJR Nabisco obligations at $222.148 million amortized cost and $285.683 million market value at year-end. One issue's disposal is not proof the entire credit position was sold. [1–2]

## What happened after
At the March 1991 letter date market value had increased more than $150 million from year-end. The later 1991 table still showed appreciation on the retained bonds. No final coupon-inclusive realized profit or aggregate exit date is established here. This is distinct from the earlier Reynolds equity and the **1988–February 1989 takeover arbitrage**. [1–2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Mispricing:** differentiated this issuer from generally unattractive junk debt. [1]
- **Risk/reward:** nonzero loss risk was explicit despite attractive yield and potential price recovery. [1]
- **Deleveraging:** asset sales/new equity strengthened debt service rather than relying on heroic projections. [1]
- **Size/competence:** the investment was large enough to matter and business economics were understood. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: category flexibility under a margin-of-safety discipline. Buffett's criticism of junk-bond excess does not prohibit buying existing debt at prices that already reflect pessimism. [1]

## Public discussion
The 1990 letter attacks the leveraged-finance “dagger on the steering wheel” justification and distinguishes sound credits from structurally doomed capital structures. Original contemporary RJR bond research and market quotations remain gaps. [1]
