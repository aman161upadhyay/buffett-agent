## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**1991:** The Heffernan family wished to sell after Ray Heffernan's death. Brown was a leading North American work-shoe/boot maker, not the similarly named Brown Shoe of St. Louis. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“Much of my enthusiasm for this purchase came from Frank's willingness to continue as CEO.” **February 1992 explanation:** Rooney worked for love of the business rather than financial necessity. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
After a shareholder/golf-course referral prompted Rooney's call, acquired Brown July 1, 1991, as shown in the operating-earnings table. [1]

## What happened after
No full acquisition return established. This relatively successful operating assessment should not be indiscriminately transferred to the later Dexter purchase. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **People:** Rooney's record and continued service were decisive. [1]
- **Niche:** Work footwear leadership. [1]
- **Economics:** Strong returns on sales/assets, not merely a famous name. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: people plus economics; subsequent shoe-industry errors demonstrate that superficially similar products do not establish identical moats. [1]
