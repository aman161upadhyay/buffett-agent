The March 20 unadjusted closing price was $95.01. Using the prior profitable year's $17.85 EPS gives a mechanical 5.32 times P/E, while the latest year's negative EPS makes a current profit multiple meaningless. That contrast is the bull case and its principal weakness. Valuing old peak cash flow as normal requires a justified recovery path, extra working capital and dilution assumptions. A rescue security would require its own price, ranking, coupon, covenants and potential warrants; none is supplied as an executable offer. [S1,S2; calculations]

The affirmative case is normalization rather than perpetual crisis: safe return of the MAX, delivery of built inventory and eventual recovery in travel could restore cash generation. The $13.6 billion free cash flow in the preceding profitable year supplies evidence of earnings capacity, not a guarantee. Defense and services diversify the business away from new civilian orders. A long-horizon capital provider could seek a preferred coupon and equity upside while ordinary market investors flee near-term uncertainty. [S1; bull interpretation]

### Dated operating baseline

Full-year revenue was $76.559 billion, down 24% from $101.127 billion. Net loss was $636 million versus $10.460 billion of profit; GAAP EPS was negative $1.12 versus positive $17.85. Operating cash flow was negative $2.446 billion versus positive $15.322 billion. Capital expenditure was $1.834 billion, making company-defined free cash flow negative $4.280 billion versus positive $13.600 billion. Cash and marketable securities totaled $10.0 billion at year-end; consolidated debt was $27.3 billion. Commercial deliveries fell to 380 from 806. Company backlog was $463 billion, including over 5,400 commercial airplanes. The company anticipated approximately $4 billion of abnormal production costs, primarily during the current year, from suspension and gradual resumption of MAX production. Those estimates precede the full demand effect of pandemic restrictions. Fourth-quarter dividends alone consumed $1.2 billion despite negative operating cash flow. [S1]

### Underwriting distinction

Price is the amount paid for a particular legal claim, not a percentage below a previous high. A cash-rich balance sheet, a large backlog or an admired brand may support valuation, but none fixes the distribution to that claim. Common shares, a preferred security and an operating-asset acquisition require different loss and return calculations. For ordinary equity, estimate distributable earnings after recurring investment and liabilities; for financing, identify coupon, priority, security and conversion economics before computing an expected return. [Analytical interpretation of S1]

The figures below are the denominator checks for any valuation. No observed decline is called an adequate margin of safety without a justified earnings range. Missing contemporaneous prices or maintenance-investment figures remain missing. Forward scenarios may be useful, but their probabilities are analyst assumptions rather than facts or Berkshire's stated forecasts. [Analytical interpretation of S1]
