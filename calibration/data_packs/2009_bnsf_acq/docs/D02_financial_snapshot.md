The FY2008 cash-flow conversion measure is 85.2% of reported net income. This is not maintenance-only owner earnings: it deducts all stated capital expenditure. A five-year ROTC is not manufactured by treating the rail network's historical book value as current replacement cost.

The 2004 and 2005 earnings include asbestos/environmental and line-sale charges respectively; they are not normalized earnings. Debt is not net debt. No maintenance-capex assumption is embedded in this record.

The October 22 release reports third-quarter net income of $488 million, or $1.42 per diluted share, compared with $695 million and $1.99 in the prior-year quarter. Nine-month net income was $1.185 billion against $1.500 billion. The quarter includes a favorable coal-rate adjustment of $0.06 per share. Parent-company results should not be mixed with the separate railway subsidiary's earnings. [Source above.]

Technical context: track and terminal networks are costly to replicate but also costly to sustain. Depreciation is an accounting allocation, not a reliable measure of current maintenance investment when equipment prices and traffic requirements change. Separate maintenance and capacity expansion without assuming every reported dollar is optional. Earnings depend on traffic density, mix, fuel, labor and pricing; a network moat does not guarantee stable quarterly volume. Debt belongs in enterprise value and interest belongs in the bridge to equity earnings. A controlling investor's own stock is consideration with economic cost, not free acquisition currency. [Source above; analytical framework, not additional measured network statistics.]

Accounting discipline: the figures stated above are reported observations unless expressly labeled arithmetic. A period-end balance is not a flow and a market mark is not purchase cost. Do not mix fiscal years, currencies, share bases or enterprise and equity values. To construct owner earnings, begin with the relevant reported profit, reconcile noncash items and subtract the investment required to maintain economic position. Changes in working capital and recurring compensation remain economic costs even when a convenient adjusted metric excludes them. If maintenance investment, leverage or the proper share denominator is missing, state that the yield cannot be completed rather than present false precision.
