# 1992 Berkshire annual letter

**Information date:** 1993-03-01 (month-only where precise public release day was not independently established). **Era:** B. **Author:** Buffett. **Primary source:** [letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1992.html). All rules below cite sections of this source; section names are used rather than fabricated PDF page numbers. Annual-period labels are not publication dates.

## Process rules and decision explanations

- Common Stock Investments: General Dynamics began as a tender arbitrage; study of Bill Anders’s rational strategy changed the commitment to long-term ownership. A strategy change requires new diligence.
- Same section: the actual checklist changed from very attractive to attractive price; scale and market conditions were explicit reasons.
- Same section: value and growth are joined, and incremental capital returns determine whether growth creates or destroys value. Discounted cash economics apply to bonds and stocks alike.
- Fixed-Income Securities: USAir industry economics soured almost immediately; preferred structure and managerial effort did not create attractive industry economics.
- Insurance Operations: Andrew reveals catastrophe aggregation risk and inadequate industry protection. Float has an underwriting cost; extrapolating past catastrophes is insufficient.

## Verbatim evidence anchors

- **Common Stock Investments; General Dynamics thesis changed from tender arbitrage to long-term ownership.** “In short order, I dumped my arbitrage thoughts and decided that Berkshire should become a long-term investor with Bill. We were helped in gaining a large position by the fact that a tender greatly swells the volume of trading in a stock.”
- **Fixed-Income Securities; Purchase of USAir preferred stock.** “In the case of our commitment to USAir, industry economics had soured before the ink dried on our check. As I've previously mentioned, it was I who happily jumped into the pool; no one pushed me.”
- **Common Stock Investments; Increased investment in Wells Fargo shares.** “We added moderately to our holdings in Guinness and Wells Fargo, we more than doubled our position in Freddie Mac, and we established a new holding in General Dynamics. We like to buy.”
- **Two New Accounting Rules and a Plea for One More; Mistaken agreement to acquire a company with significant post-retirement health liabilities.** “In 1982 I made a huge mistake in committing to buy a company burdened by extraordinary post-retirement health obligations. Luckily, though, the transaction fell through for reasons beyond our control.”
