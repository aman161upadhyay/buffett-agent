## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**September 1999 onward:** Walter Scott introduced the opportunity. Public Utility Holding Company Act constraints shaped the ownership structure; economic ownership and voting control were not equivalent. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“The idea of being in partnership with Walter struck me as a good one.” Buffett called Sokol “talented and entrepreneurial.” **March 2000 explanation of the agreed transaction.** [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Read public reports, met Scott/Sokol and agreed roughly $2 billion comprising an 11% fixed-income security, common and exchangeable preferred. Structure provided about 76% of equity economics but under 10% voting power. The acquisition closed in 2000. [1–2]

## What happened after
The platform allowed additional utility commitments. The later regulated-asset compounding thesis should not erase the original people/structure rationale. [1–2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Partner:** Scott's substantial personal commitment supported alignment. [1]
- **Operator:** Confidence in Sokol. [1]
- **Structure:** Large economic interest without initially taking voting control. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: entry into regulated, capital-hungry infrastructure, with a trusted team and capacity to deploy much larger sums. [1]

## Public discussion
Original regulatory filings and independent contemporary discussion remain gaps. [1–2]
