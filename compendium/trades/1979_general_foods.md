## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**By December 31, 1979:** Berkshire owned 328,700 shares at $11.437 million cost, then worth $11.053 million. This establishes ownership before 1980, not a precise initial purchase month. The subsequent letter says **most** shares had been held since 1980 and bought far below business value; it does not say no shares existed in 1979. Acquisition-date branded-product economics and precise original earnings multiple are not reconstructed. [1,3]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“A bargain purchase price, a business with fine underlying economics, an able management concentrating on the interests of shareholders, and a buyer willing to pay full business value.” **Hindsight**, March 4, 1986, explaining four contributors to success. [3]

“In selecting common stocks, we devote our attention to attractive purchases, not to the possibility of attractive sales.” Same letter: a later takeover was not the original thesis. [3]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Owned shares by year-end 1979 and accumulated substantially thereafter. At year-end 1984 it held 4,047,191 shares at $149.870 million cost. Proportional redemptions in 1984 generated $21,843,601 while preserving 8.75% ownership; accounting treatment was disputed with the auditors. Berkshire exited in 1985 following Philip Morris's offer; exact final execution date is unknown here. [1–3]

## What happened after
The 1985 letter attributes $338 million of that year's pretax realized gains to General Foods. Buffett credits Jim Ferguson and Phil Smith with increasing underlying value before the buyer paid for it. Do not confuse this realized gain with the 1984 unrealized mark or add proportional-redemption proceeds as pure profit without basis adjustment. [2–3]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Price/value:** primary entry rationale in Buffett's own explanation. [3]
- **Economics:** a fine business, not a liquidation thesis. [3]
- **Management:** shareholder-focused allocation increased business value. [2–3]
- **Repurchases:** enhanced ownership and enabled efficient proportional cash distributions; later mechanism, not necessarily all foreknown at initial entry. [2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: quality at a discount with an unplanned full-value takeover exit. **Verified correction:** 1979 ownership coexists with the 1985 letter's statement that most stock was acquired in 1980. [1,3]

## Public discussion
The auditor disagreement is contemporaneous contrary evidence about reporting, not a dispute over whether Berkshire received cash. Independent original buy-side/press debate remains unrecovered. [2]
