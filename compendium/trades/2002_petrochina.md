## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**2002–03:** Purchase price valued PetroChina at about $37 billion. Buffett/Munger later said they assessed approximately $100 billion. These estimates are **reported in 2008**, not recovered contemporaneous public purchase guidance. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
By 2007 market value reached “about what we thought it was worth compared to other giant oil companies.” “So we sold our holdings for $4 billion.” **Sale post-mortem.** [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Bought 1.3% across 2002–03 for $488 million; sold during the second half of 2007. Exact first and final trade dates unknown. [1]

## What happened after
Gross proceeds less cost imply approximately $3.512 billion gain before dividends and taxes; the letter reports $1.2 billion US tax. Do not call the $4 billion proceeds profit. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Entry:** Large business-value discount. [1]
- **Revaluation:** Higher oil prices and management's reserve-building increased value. [1]
- **Exit:** Price reached comparative appraisal, not a claimed perfect oil-price forecast. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: value discipline includes selling a good business at full appraisal; contrasts with ConocoPhillips's expensive energy entry. [1]

## Public discussion
Contemporary Sudan/divestment debate and direct meeting responses require separately authenticated sources; not asserted as sale rationale here. [1]
