## Cutoff evidence — reconstructed

Historical quotations reconstructed from the scandal account put the stock at $61.81 on November 20 and about $40 on December 2 of the preceding year. Neither quotation is the February 20 close. The table supplies a January proxy and an issued-share denominator, not normalized earning power after disputed obligations. No February closing multiple is asserted. The valuation problem is to subtract a conservative range of financial claims from the continuing payments franchise, allowing separately for reputational harm.

## Economic reading of the supplied facts

Industry economics: travel payments combine service charges with funds held between receipt and settlement. Customer confidence and merchant acceptance matter because the product is a promise to pay, not just a piece of paper or plastic. More activity can expand fee income and balances, but neither is free capital if redemption, settlement or credit obligations grow with it. Competitive pressure can appear in merchant charges, cardholder benefits and the cost of funding. A loss in a separate activity must be assessed both as a direct financial claim and as a possible threat to trust in the continuing payments service. This is an economic interpretation of the business described in the cited sources, not a quantified forecast.

Valuation interpretation: the relevant asset is the stream of cash that can be withdrawn over the life of the business without weakening its competitive position. A low multiple is informative only when the earnings denominator is sustainable. A high return on tangible capital can justify a premium to book value, but does not justify an unlimited purchase price.

No closing quotation is inferred from a subsequent transaction average or from the low for a later month. If a dated market price is unavailable, a precise market capitalization, initial earnings yield and implied return are unavailable as well. A negotiated security can instead be evaluated from its proposed cash payments and priority, subject to credit risk. The adverse case should reduce sustainable earnings and allow for additional capital needs rather than simply apply a smaller multiple to optimistic earnings. Interest rates, taxes and reinvestment determine the opportunity cost; no unsupported discount rate or terminal growth estimate is presented as an observed fact.
