## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**1976 rescue:** GEICO was close to bankruptcy with major financial and operating problems. Buffett's later explanation distinguishes those problems from its persistent direct-distribution cost advantage. Jack Byrne's arrival was an identified intervention, not evidence that any talented manager could repair an inherently poor business. Exact entry month, original price and contemporaneous reserve-adequacy model remain unrecovered. [2–3]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“The fundamental business advantage that GEICO had enjoyed ... was still intact within the company, although submerged in a sea of financial and operating troubles.” **Hindsight**, February 27, 1981. [2]

“There aren’t many Jack Byrnes in the managerial world, or GEICOs in the business world.” Same letter; the exceptional combination is the thesis, not a generic turnaround strategy. [2]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Berkshire invested during 1976 and added later. At December 31, 1977, it reported GEICO convertible preferred at $19.417 million cost and common at $4.116 million cost. By 1980 it held 7.2 million common shares at $47.138 million cost, approximately 33% ownership, with about half the total invested in 1976 and most of the rest in 1980. The mixed-security rescue is classified as the minority equity episode; it is not a wholly owned acquisition at entry. [1–2]

## What happened after
The company recovered and repurchases subsequently increased Berkshire's ownership without proportional new purchases. In 1995 Berkshire agreed to buy the remaining approximately half for $2.3 billion; the minority episode therefore transitioned into control rather than a cash exit. The exact minority-to-control completion date is not established here. The initial 1951 personal trade is separate. [3]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Cost moat:** direct marketing had not lost its underlying customer-value advantage. [2–3]
- **Excisable damage:** financial distress differed from structurally unprofitable industry economics. [2]
- **Operator:** Byrne supplied the managerial skill the rescue required. [2]
- **Price:** Buffett contrasted the $47 million cost with at least $200 million needed to acquire similar earning power outright. That is a 1980 assessment, not an entry-date target. [2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: a rare acceptable turnaround because an exceptional franchise survived. It should not teach indiscriminate distressed-insurer buying or confuse preference shares with guaranteed principal. [1–3]

## Public discussion
The cited letters are management's retrospective and interim reports. Original rescue offering documents, regulatory negotiations and contemporary outside objections remain gaps. [1–3]
