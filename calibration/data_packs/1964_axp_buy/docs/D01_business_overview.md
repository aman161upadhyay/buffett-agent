## Cutoff evidence — reconstructed

American Express operates travelers-cheque and charge-card services in addition to the warehousing subsidiary implicated in a commodities fraud. The customer's continuing use of cheques and cards is a separate question from whether warehouse receipts represented actual oil. Travelers buy a recognized payment promise; merchants accept the charge card because customers use it and payment is expected. Those relationships, rather than tanks or warehouse property, are the core operating assets to examine. ## Economic reading of the supplied facts

Industry economics: travel payments combine service charges with funds held between receipt and settlement. Customer confidence and merchant acceptance matter because the product is a promise to pay, not just a piece of paper or plastic. More activity can expand fee income and balances, but neither is free capital if redemption, settlement or credit obligations grow with it. Competitive pressure can appear in merchant charges, cardholder benefits and the cost of funding. A loss in a separate activity must be assessed both as a direct financial claim and as a possible threat to trust in the continuing payments service. This is an economic interpretation of the business described in the cited sources, not a quantified forecast.

Economic interpretation, not a management forecast: the important distinction is between demand for the product and cash available to its owners. A recognizable name is not sufficient evidence of an economic franchise. The relevant tests are repeat purchasing, the customer's alternatives, the seller's ability to maintain price, and the capital needed to preserve those advantages. Physical assets can be copied; a durable customer relationship may be harder to reproduce. Conversely, an apparently entrenched relationship can disappear if customers obtain better service or lower prices elsewhere.

The business description therefore does not assume that historical profitability persists indefinitely. Separate ordinary operations from unusual charges and separate a good product from a good investment at the offered price. Management's operating record and the financing of the enterprise belong in the same assessment. No value is assigned here to an unannounced acquisition, hypothetical takeover premium, or an improvement that requires a new owner to change the basic business.
