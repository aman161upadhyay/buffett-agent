## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**2003:** Wal-Mart wished to sell a distributor of groceries/nonfood products. Some retailers avoided McLane because its parent competed with them. Following-year sales/margin descriptions must not be mistaken for dated entry financial statements. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“It's a good business” and “made to order ... for us.” “We knew everything would be exactly as Wal-Mart said it would be – and it was.” **February 2004 explanation.** [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
After Byron Trott introduced the opportunity, Buffett met Wal-Mart CFO Tom Schoewe for about two hours; Wal-Mart received payment 29 days later. Buffett says Berkshire did no due diligence in that transaction. [1]

## What happened after
Rosier began winning previously unavailable accounts. No transaction-level realized return established. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Fit:** Noncore to seller, appropriate for Berkshire. [1]
- **Operator:** Rosier's execution mattered. [1]
- **Ownership:** Removal of competitive conflict could grow accounts. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: trust accelerated this deal; “no due diligence” is not a universal instruction to skip underwriting. Thin margins are compatible with good economics. [1]

## Public discussion
Independent contemporary sale discussion not recovered. [1]
