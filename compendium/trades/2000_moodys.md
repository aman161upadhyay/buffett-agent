## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**2000 separation:** The legal mechanics matter: old D&B distributed New D&B and renamed itself Moody's. It is imprecise to assume the surviving Moody's shares were themselves simply distributed as a new holding. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
Original purchase-date quotation unknown. Later descriptions of ratings-company economics require separately dated transcripts before reasoning-overlap use. [1–2]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Held shares associated with the D&B separation; original first purchase date remains unverified. At December 2006 Berkshire reported 48 million Moody's shares costing $499 million, valued at $3.315 billion. [1–2]

## What happened after
Berkshire reduced the holding in 2009; see `2009_moodys_trim.md`. The 2006 mark is not realized P&L. [2–3]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Thesis gap:** Corporate mechanics are stronger evidence here than original rationale. [1–2]
- **Lineage:** Old D&B became Moody's. [1]
- **Valuation:** Later appreciation cannot supply an ex ante model. [2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: a potentially durable franchise, but do not reverse-engineer Buffett quotations from subsequent success. [2]
