## Cutoff evidence — reconstructed

Travelers cheques compete with cash and bank payment instruments; charge cards compete with other cards and established methods of paying merchants. A customer franchise need not own many physical assets, but confidence can be more fragile than a factory's productive capacity. The warehouse business is not a good operating comparable for the payments network even though both sit within the same corporate structure. No dated competitor share, fee schedule or comparable-company multiple has been recovered. The useful comparison is between a temporary financial subtraction and a permanent decline in repeat payments demand. Either can occur, and the stock decline alone does not distinguish them.

## Economic reading of the supplied facts

Industry economics: travel payments combine service charges with funds held between receipt and settlement. Customer confidence and merchant acceptance matter because the product is a promise to pay, not just a piece of paper or plastic. More activity can expand fee income and balances, but neither is free capital if redemption, settlement or credit obligations grow with it. Competitive pressure can appear in merchant charges, cardholder benefits and the cost of funding. A loss in a separate activity must be assessed both as a direct financial claim and as a possible threat to trust in the continuing payments service. This is an economic interpretation of the business described in the cited sources, not a quantified forecast.

Comparison discipline: differences in accounting, capital intensity and financial leverage can make two nominally similar earnings multiples incomparable. A high return on common equity can come from a strong operating franchise or merely from a thin equity base. The comparison that matters is the durable cash return on all necessary capital, followed by the division of that return among the different claimants.

An outside shareholder cannot assume control benefits or private acquisition synergies. A private buyer's price is useful evidence only if the assets, liabilities and ownership rights are comparable. The alternatives also include retaining liquid funds rather than purchasing a mediocre security merely because it is cheaper than an expensive peer. No industry-average multiple or precise competitor ranking is invented here. The qualitative comparisons identify which operating variables need examination; the absence of a recovered comparable-company table is a genuine limit on numerical relative valuation.
