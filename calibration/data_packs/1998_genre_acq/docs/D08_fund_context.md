## Cutoff evidence — reconstructed

Berkshire's February 27 letter reports $36.2477 billion total common-stock market value at the preceding year-end, with $7.2069 billion tax-basis cost. Major interests include Coca-Cola, Gillette, American Express and Wells Fargo. This is not cash available for the merger. Period transaction descriptions also refer to about $46 billion of stocks and bonds, a broader category that must not be substituted for the equity denominator. The proposed General Re exchange uses Berkshire shares and would transfer continuing participation in all the existing businesses to new owners. Current free cash and exact same-day dilution are unrecovered. The context gives scale and funding structure without recommending an exchange.

## Economic reading of the supplied facts

Industry economics: an insurer receives premiums before all associated losses are known or paid. The investment balance generated by that delay is economically valuable only when the eventual underwriting cost is acceptable. Inadequate prices or underestimated reserves can create reported profits that do not exist economically. A direct seller can avoid agency commissions, but saving distribution costs does not compensate for accepting unsuitable risks at inadequate rates. Regulatory capital and claims-paying ability constrain the amount that can be invested in equities or distributed to owners. Reinsurance adds counterparty exposure and can transfer risk without eliminating uncertainty about its measurement. This is a description of insurance mechanics, not a claim that any reported reserve balance is sufficient.

Capital-allocation interpretation: a portfolio allocation must fit the legal entity that supplies the money. An insurance subsidiary's invested assets are not all cash available for an acquisition. Premiums collected before claims are paid provide investment funds but also create obligations. Consolidated equity, publicly traded holdings, cash and insurance float are different denominators.

No target weight follows mechanically from the fund-size reference. Available cash at this exact cutoff, where unrecovered, remains unknown rather than being equated with the reference figure. Existing concentrated holdings create opportunity costs; selling them may incur tax and surrender durable earning power. A commitment involving stock also transfers a continuing fraction of the buyer's future economics. The investor has no obligation to purchase simply to remain fully invested. Conversely, waiting has a cost when a sound business is offered below conservative value. This context describes available choices and constraints without specifying an allocation or revealing a decision.
