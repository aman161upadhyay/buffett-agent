## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**November 2–3, 2009:** Berkshire already owned 22.6% at announcement. The offer valued all equity at roughly $34 billion and enterprise value at $44 billion including debt; the incremental remaining-share purchase was smaller. [1–2]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“It's an all-in wager on the economic future of the United States.” **Contemporaneous November 3 announcement**, unlike explanations in the following February letter. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Signed November 2; announced November 3 a $100/share offer for the remaining 77.4%, with cash/stock election mechanics and approximately 60/40 aggregate mix. [1–2]

## What happened after
The February 2010 letter describes the acquisition as completed and acknowledges the use of valuable Berkshire shares. Exact close day not independently extracted here. Do not conflate this bid with the December 2007 Marmon agreement. [3]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Economy:** Long-horizon goods movement, not next-quarter freight prediction. [1]
- **Infrastructure:** Durable network relevant to national activity. [1–3]
- **Capital:** Large reinvestment opportunities suit Berkshire's growing scale. [3]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: capital-intensive infrastructure became acceptable as Berkshire needed larger productive outlets; stock consideration still had opportunity cost. [3]

## Public discussion
The merger S-4 provides contemporary transaction terms and shareholder decision context. Independent valuation rebuttals remain a gap. [2]
