## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**2000:** Shaw initially approached Berkshire about insurance for a different merger involving asbestos exposure. Buffett refused an uncapped policy; that deal died, but his interest in Shaw remained. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
The resulting purchase associated Berkshire with “the best in the business.” **February 2001 explanation**, not an original public bid presentation. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Agreed the purchase in 2000; completed it in 2001. Initial ownership was 87.3%, with Shaw and Saul each retaining at least 5% as a key deal feature. [1–2]

## What happened after
No realized acquisition return supplied. Rejecting an uncapped insurance risk was part of the opportunity's history, not a liability Berkshire accepted in buying Shaw. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Scale:** Leading carpet operations. [1]
- **People/alignment:** Proven managers remained invested. [1]
- **Risk:** Never bet the ranch on unlimited asbestos costs. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: productive serendipity after saying no to the wrong risk. [1]

## Public discussion
Independent contemporary merger discussion not recovered. [1–2]
