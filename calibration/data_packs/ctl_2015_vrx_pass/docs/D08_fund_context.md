Berkshire's 2014 annual report, released February 28, supplies approximately $63.27 billion of cash and cash equivalents and $117.47 billion of equity securities. Its established operating companies and the Heinz partnership provide relevant examples of decentralized ownership and cost discipline. That resemblance is a proposition to test, not permission to treat a leveraged pharmaceutical acquisition platform as equivalent. Combs and Weschler can consider smaller healthcare investments, but mandate attribution and the quality of accounting still matter. [S3]

### Dated operating baseline

The February 23 presentation reports fourth-quarter revenue of $2.3 billion versus $2.1 billion, up 10%; management's cash EPS was $2.58 versus $2.15, up 20%. GAAP operating cash flow was $816 million versus $280 million. That cash figure included a $287 million net Allergan transaction gain; management's adjusted operating cash flow was $624 million versus $607 million, only 3% higher. Restructuring and integration costs were $47 million. Reported same-store organic sales growth was 16%; Bausch + Lomb organic growth was 8% for the quarter and 11% for the year. The presentation explicitly warns that forward cash-EPS and adjusted-cash-flow guidance is not reconciled to GAAP because excluded items are difficult to forecast. The contemplated Salix transaction and financing are additional commitments, not part of the historical quarter's recurring cash earnings. Cash EPS is a management-defined earnings measure, not distributable owner cash. [S1]

### Underwriting distinction

The denominator is capital available to Berkshire, not the issuer's enterprise value or an outside asset manager's client AUM. Insurance float carries obligations; reported cash is not all surplus. A new security should be compared with existing portfolio opportunities, retaining liquidity for operating subsidiaries and negotiated whole-business purchases. No leverage at the parent is assumed to make the proposal fit. The question is an incremental allocation within a large permanent-capital enterprise. [Analytical interpretation of S3]

For a public stock, a deputy-scale position and a Buffett-scale position need different sizing even when both are plausible investments. For a negotiated financing, the relevant cash commitment includes follow-on needs rather than only the opening check. No future trade, retrospective explanation or confidential public-equity holding is supplied as a recommendation. The issuer's current operating scale below helps compare the proposed exposure with the available fund capital. [Analytical interpretation of S1,S3]
