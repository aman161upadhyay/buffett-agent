Title: Business and the affirmative investment case
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000119312512240111/d287954d424b4.htm
Date: 2012-05-17

The social network lets users maintain identity, relationships, messages, photographs and shared content; advertisers pay to reach targeted audiences. Additional users and connections can improve the usefulness of the product for existing users. A large advertising platform can have substantial operating leverage because an extra ad impression does not require another physical store. The affirmative franchise case rests on habitual engagement and a difficult-to-replicate social graph, not the scarcity of IPO shares. [S1; analyst interpretation]

Revenue nearly doubled in 2011 and the business produced $1 billion of net income rather than a start-up loss. The 901 million monthly-user base offers a global distribution asset; 488 million mobile users show that use can follow customers away from desktop computers. Better ad relevance and payments can improve monetization without requiring equivalent growth in users. A long-horizon buyer could regard the company as a consumer habit and advertising toll bridge, with a large reinvestment runway. [S1; bull interpretation]

### Dated operating baseline

The May 17 prospectus reports 2011 revenue of $3.711 billion and net income of $1.000 billion; 2010 revenue was $1.974 billion and net income $606 million. First-quarter revenue was $1.058 billion, versus $731 million, while net income was $205 million versus $233 million. Monthly active users reached 901 million at March 31 and daily active users averaged 526 million in March. Mobile monthly active users numbered 488 million. Advertising supplied approximately 85% of 2011 revenue; payments and other fees supplied the remainder. The offer consists of 421,233,615 Class A shares at $38, including 180 million newly issued shares and 241,233,615 sold by existing holders. Gross offering size is $16.007 billion; most shares sold are secondary, not new cash for the company. Class A has one vote per share and Class B ten. Zuckerberg is expected to control approximately 55.9% of votes after the offering. [S1]

### Underwriting distinction

The economic unit to value is the cash-producing franchise, not its stock-market category. Separate evidence of repeat customer behavior from a claim that those customers cannot leave. The affirmative interpretation here is an analyst's case, not a representation that Berkshire endorsed it. Costs needed to retain customers, renew products and maintain legal or regulatory permission belong in the economics even when reported in different accounting lines. A minority interest also lacks the ability to redesign the business simply because the owner has permanent capital. [Analytical interpretation of S1]

For diligence, identify which activity earns the customer relationship, which assets must be replaced, and which liabilities could consume the cash before it reaches common equity. The facts below define the scale of the existing enterprise. They do not convert management's expectations into a demonstrated ten-year record. [Analytical interpretation of S1]
