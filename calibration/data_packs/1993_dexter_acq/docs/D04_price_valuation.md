## Cutoff evidence — reconstructed

The dated proposal is 25,221 Berkshire shares, worth approximately $420 million at the contemporaneous Berkshire quotation of $16,675. There is no traded Dexter share price because the seller is privately held. Without original Dexter cash earnings, no exact pretax multiple or owner-earnings yield can be computed. Stock payment is not economically free because it avoids cash expenditure: the buyer gives up future cash flows represented by the issued interest. No later Berkshire price is used to judge the exchange. Dexter's distribution and manufacturing economics must justify the ownership surrendered, without assuming a control resale or an unsupported national-growth premium.

## Economic reading of the supplied facts

Industry economics: footwear requires design, purchasing, manufacturing or sourcing, distribution and inventory across styles and sizes. Unsold inventory can be worth less than recorded cost. A good domestic factory can still be vulnerable if imports deliver an acceptable product at materially lower cost. Brand, quality and delivery speed may support customer loyalty, but each must be tested against the consumer's alternatives. Owning stores can capture retail margin while adding leases, inventory and fixed expenses. Efficient operations are valuable only to the extent that they sustain economic returns after all capital requirements. This is a description of competitive possibilities at the cutoff, not a prediction that domestic production must succeed or fail.

Valuation interpretation: the relevant asset is the stream of cash that can be withdrawn over the life of the business without weakening its competitive position. A low multiple is informative only when the earnings denominator is sustainable. A high return on tangible capital can justify a premium to book value, but does not justify an unlimited purchase price.

No closing quotation is inferred from a subsequent transaction average or from the low for a later month. If a dated market price is unavailable, a precise market capitalization, initial earnings yield and implied return are unavailable as well. A negotiated security can instead be evaluated from its proposed cash payments and priority, subject to credit risk. The adverse case should reduce sustainable earnings and allow for additional capital needs rather than simply apply a smaller multiple to optimistic earnings. Interest rates, taxes and reinvestment determine the opportunity cost; no unsupported discount rate or terminal growth estimate is presented as an observed fact.
