## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**Early 1989:** Borsheim's was an Omaha jewelry retailer run by Ike Friedman and family, combining broad selection, low operating expenses, rapid inventory turnover and buying skill. The February 1989 letter compares its formula to Nebraska Furniture Mart. Exact acquisition date, purchase price and original financial statements are unknown; an “1988 annual letter” source does **not** imply a 1988 purchase. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“If you don't know jewelry, know the jeweler.” **Post-purchase**, February 28, 1989, explaining the importance of trusted sellers in a difficult-to-assess product. [1]

“Ike simply told us what was so” — same letter describing reliance on the known operator rather than an audit. The favorable selection-and-cost economics were also explicitly described, so this was not trust without a business model. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Bought 80% early in 1989 using a simple contract, without audited statements, inventory verification or receivables audit. All family members continued to operate; Buffett and Munger stayed on the sidelines. The exact check size is unknown and should not be invented from unrelated retailer acquisitions. [1]

## What happened after
The 1989 letter says first-year results met expectations, sales rose significantly, and its expense ratio was around one-third of comparable jewelers'. Buffett described low expenses and buying power producing low prices, which generated more volume. This is a first-year operating assessment, not a verified lifetime realized return. [2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Selection/volume:** a large one-location assortment drew customers beyond the local market. [1–2]
- **Efficiency:** turnover, buying and expense control reinforced margins at low prices. [1–2]
- **Shared scale:** value flowed to customers rather than relying on high markups. [1–2]
- **Family:** integrity plus multigenerational operators justified a permanent decentralized relationship. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: the NFM-style low-cost retailer template applied to jewelry, not an acquisition based on forecasting gold or diamond prices. `open` denotes retained operating ownership in the cited follow-up, not a fresh 2026 check. [1–2]

## Public discussion
Original deal publicity and independent competitors' responses remain unrecovered. Buffett's later praise is company-owner commentary, not evidence that outsiders had no concerns about jewelry valuation or concentration. [1–2]
