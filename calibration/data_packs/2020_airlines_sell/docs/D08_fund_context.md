Title: Berkshire context without the allocation answer
Source: https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2019ltr.pdf (published 2020-02-22); https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2019ltr.pdf (last public fund baseline identified in text); https://www.congress.gov/bill/116th-congress/house-bill/748 (2020-03-27 CARES Act)
Date: 2020-04-02

Berkshire's year-end 2019 cash and bills of approximately $128 billion provide the scale reference. The airline positions are existing investments, not new rescue proposals. The company can survive a long interruption without redemption pressure, but permanent capital does not require retaining a position whose prospective economics are unknowable. Any ownership threshold and government-aid conditions must be distinguished from a view about franchise value. [Source above.]

The 2019 letter reports $129.423 billion float. Its public equity table includes long-held American Express and Coca-Cola as well as the large consumer-technology and banking positions. That diversification is not equivalent to a market-neutral portfolio. Float is owed to claimants, and no commitment-adjusted cash figure or complete current concentration calculation was recovered. [Fund baseline cited above.]

Technical context: airline economics have high fixed and semi-fixed costs and perishable capacity. Load factor, fare, ancillary revenue and unit cost should be read together; fuller aircraft can still produce weak returns if prices fall. Aircraft leases, pension liabilities and committed fleet purchases belong in the capital-risk assessment. Low fuel prices improve a period's profit but may induce competitive fare cuts. A four-company basket reduces single-carrier concentration but retains shared exposure to travel demand, fuel, labor and credit availability. Earnings before aircraft ownership costs can exaggerate cash available to common owners. [Source above; analytical framework, not an aggregate forecast.]

Allocation discipline: Berkshire is an operating and insurance company, not a redeemable investment partnership. The metadata liquidity figure is a dated scale reference, not contemporaneous discretionary NAV. Cash supports insurance, subsidiaries and contractual obligations before any new allocation. Equity-portfolio weight, percentage of issuer ownership and percentage of cash are different sizing measures. An existing position's basis affects tax but does not determine current intrinsic value. Distinguish a deputy's mandate from a Buffett-scale commitment, and avoid assigning a named decision maker without evidence. This context does not disclose the action being evaluated; the analyst must weigh opportunity cost and the durability of the underlying business independently.

Evidence boundary: missing figures are not available in contemporaneous sources recovered for this digest. Analytical questions are not additional reported facts or claims about the investor's private thinking. Read the financial, risk and fund-context documents together; no individual accounting ratio supplies a complete investment conclusion.
