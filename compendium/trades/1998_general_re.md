## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**1998:** Berkshire acquired a major reinsurer with a large investment portfolio. Subsequent underwriting deterioration and derivatives liquidation costs were not an established purchase-date forecast. [1–3]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
The 1998 letter calls General Re and Executive Jet “first-class in every way.” **Hindsight correction:** in 2002 he called derivatives “financial weapons of mass destruction”; in 2005 he acknowledged wasting years trying to sell the inherited operation. These are different information sets. [1–3]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Acquired General Re in 1998 using Berkshire stock. By 2002 it put the inherited derivatives business into termination rather than ongoing expansion. [1–2]

## What happened after
The 1998 letter already reports an underwriting loss. The 2005 letter records $409 million cumulative pretax derivatives runoff losses and faults Buffett for delay. Do not describe the entire insurer as liquidated or treat runoff losses as the acquisition's complete P&L. [1,3]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Assets/float:** The acquisition materially increased investments per Berkshire share. [1]
- **Error:** Original quality assessment was too generous. [1–3]
- **Complexity:** Accounting marks did not equal reliably realizable economic value. [2–3]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: both an insurance-scale investment and a due-diligence/complexity caution. Self-managed later index puts are not the same book as General Re runoff. [2–3]

## Public discussion
Buffett's systemic derivatives criticism is a subsequent public intervention, not a 1998 acquisition warning. Independent contemporary debate remains a gap. [2]
