## Cutoff evidence — reconstructed

At the cutoff the business is being evaluated at a negotiated $25 million purchase consideration through Blue Chip Stamps. The January 3 date is the assigned acquisition-decision boundary; exact signing versus settlement chronology is not independently established in this reconstruction. Seller information concerns an established operating business before transfer of ownership. A table covering a fiscal year after this boundary is not admissible as latest results. The negotiations and operating facts are reconstructed from acquisition histories, not portrayed as a recovered contemporaneous public tender document. No subsequent increase in prices, number of shops or distributions is included as evidence that the contemplated economics are certain.

## Economic reading of the supplied facts

Industry economics: boxed chocolates are purchased both for consumption and as gifts. The purchaser's concern with the recipient's reaction can make dependable quality and an established local name important. That does not eliminate substitution by other sweets or gifts. The retailer must carry inventory, staff shops and maintain product freshness even when demand is concentrated in holidays. Profits depend on realized price per pound after ingredients, labor, occupancy and selling costs. A premium brand has value only if the realized premium exceeds the extra cost of sustaining quality. Regional recognition cannot be assumed to transfer automatically to a new city. These are business-model implications, not an assumption of national expansion or a promised price increase.

Chronology controls: the cutoff is an information boundary, not proof of an execution on that day. A negotiated commitment, a public announcement, a purchase settlement and an annual-report disclosure are distinct events. Facts about the condition of the business may be reconstructed from archival accounts without turning a later explanation into a statement available to the market.

Only the events described above enter this chronology. No subsequent result is assumed when assessing the unresolved question. The absence of an original day-by-day price series prevents identification of a precise entry-day return or volume pattern. The same restriction applies to unpublished board discussions and security-specific purchase ledgers. This reconstruction does not claim access to either. Where a transaction is being considered, proposed terms are identified as proposed; regulatory approvals and operating improvements are not treated as completed. Where shares already exist in a portfolio, a request to study a new allocation must not erase that exposure.
