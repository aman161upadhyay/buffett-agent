## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**2002:** Energy-sector turmoil and ratings downgrades pressured Williams's liquidity. The issuer was Williams Companies, not the separately distressed Williams Communications. [1–2]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
Direct purchase quotation unknown. Collateral and contractual terms establish structure, not a complete Buffett valuation memorandum. [1–2]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
MidAmerican bought $275 million of 9.875% convertible preferred March 27. Later participated with Lehman in a $900 million secured credit facility backed by Rocky Mountain oil/gas interests. Do not attribute the entire consortium loan to Berkshire. [1–2]

## What happened after
Williams repaid the loan group's $1.17 billion obligation May 30, 2003; it also announced an approximately $289 million preferred repurchase. Aggregate obligation growth is not a Berkshire-only dollar profit. Exact preferred settlement remains unknown. [3]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Assets:** Security backed a liquidity bridge. [2–3]
- **Protection:** Specific reserves/interests supplied collateral. [2–3]
- **Returns:** Contractual yield/premiums, not oil-price appreciation alone. [1–3]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: rescue credit and underwriting assets under stress, distinct from buying distressed common shares. [1–3]

## Public discussion
CNN's contemporary report described the financing as helping avoid bankruptcy; the issuer later emphasized improved liquidity. [2–3]
