# 1996 Berkshire annual letter

**Information date:** 1997-02-28 (month-only where precise public release day was not independently established). **Era:** B. **Author:** Buffett. **Primary source:** [letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1996.html). All rules below cite sections of this source; section names are used rather than fabricated PDF page numbers. Annual-period labels are not publication dates.

## Process rules and decision explanations

- Common Stock Investments: Coke and Gillette as inevitables refers to franchise strength, not certainty about stock returns at every price.
- Insurance—GEICO: full ownership followed decades of knowledge; low-cost distribution/customer economics and management explain willingness to pay for the remaining interest.
- Acquisitions: FlightSafety is a specialized business with management and training infrastructure; cash/stock structuring sought to limit Berkshire issuance.
- USAir: retrospective identifies deregulated competitive revenue pressure overlooked at entry; a recovering security price does not erase the initial analytical error.
- Financings: Class B shares and associated equity issuance should not be confused with a stock split or ordinary investee purchase.

## Verbatim evidence anchors

- **Chairman's Letter; Acquisition of remaining 49% of GEICO.** “The restatement was required because GEICO became a wholly-owned subsidiary of Berkshire on January 2, 1996, whereas it was previously classified as an investment.”
- **Insurance - GEICO and Other Primary Operations; Purchase of remaining 49% ownership stake in GEICO.** “It is also why I felt Berkshire should pay $2.3 billion last year for the 49% of the company that we didn't then own.”
- **Acquisitions of 1996; Purchase of Kansas Bankers Surety.** “On February 13, I told Roy we would pay $75 million for the company - and before long we had a deal.”
- **USAir; Investment in USAir preferred stock.** “Unwilling to accept Branson's proposition on faith, your Chairman decided in 1989 to test it by investing $358 million in a 9.25% preferred stock of USAir.”

## Attribution and point-in-time boundary

Buffett is the source author, but an acquired subsidiary’s bolt-on or operating action is not automatically his personally originated investment. Individual decider unknown where not identified. Historical narratives are available only from this information date. A section discussing earlier purchases is retrospective; do not load its later performance, confession or corrected valuation into the original entry replay.

## Coverage limitation

This is a decision-process extraction, not a full transcription or a complete portfolio ledger. Numbers outside the source passage are not estimated.
