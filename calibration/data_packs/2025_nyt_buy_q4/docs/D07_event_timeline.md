Title: A dated digital transformation record
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/71691/000007169125000117/pressrelease06302025.htm ; https://s23.q4cdn.com/152113917/files/doc_events/2025/Apr/30/The-New-York-Times-Company-2024-Annual-Report.pdf
Date: 2025-09-30

The annual report available in the spring sets out the three-year revenue record and governance framework. August 6 brings the latest quarter: 230,000 net digital additions, $9.64 digital ARPU, $685.9 million revenue and $106.6 million GAAP operating profit. September 30 supplies a $57.40 Class A close. The quarter ending in September is not itself a published financial result. [1,2; historical price below]

The analyst can observe a transition from print exposure toward a paid digital bundle and rising profitability. It cannot use a later shareholder's name as validation of the business model, nor assume that a future acquisition or legal settlement supplies a catalyst. No particular investor is identified as privately accumulating shares. The open questions are subscriber retention, incremental margins, reinvestment and the price paid for durable earnings. The chronology intentionally contains no later earnings release and no retrospective description of what the market or the board ultimately decided.

Evidence ledger: annual revenue was $2.308321 billion in 2022, $2.426152 billion in 2023 and $2.585919 billion in 2024. Second-quarter revenue of $685.9 million grew 9.7%; operating profit was $106.6 million, up 34.2%, and adjusted operating profit $133.8 million. Diluted EPS was $0.50, or $0.58 adjusted. Total subscribers were 11.88 million, digital-only 11.30 million, and bundle/multiproduct 6.02 million. Digital-only ARPU was $9.64, up 3.2%. Digital subscription revenue was $350.4 million, total subscription revenue $481.4 million, digital advertising $94.4 million and total advertising $134.0 million. Affiliate/licensing/other revenue was $70.5 million. Marketing costs were $69.2 million; journalism and subscriber-servicing spending remain real operating costs. [1,2]

Timing discipline: the chronology separates publication dates from the economic periods discussed. A result for an earlier quarter only becomes an input when released. The market close is an observation available on the stated assessment date, not evidence of a particular investor's execution. This timeline includes no presumed private conversations or unannounced agreement. There is no catalyst completion forecast here: the analyst must decide whether the available business evidence is sufficient without relying on a future disclosure to reveal someone else's decision.
