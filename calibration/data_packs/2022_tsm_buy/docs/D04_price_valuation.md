A date-filtered historical price series gives November 14 ADR close of $72.80. Annualizing $1.79 quarterly ADR EPS gives $7.16 and a mechanical 10.2x multiple; this is an illustration, not normalized annual earnings or an entry-cost reconstruction. The quarter may be cyclically favorable. A valuation must account for capital spending and the geographic concentration of productive assets. [Source above.]

Technical context: foundry manufacturing has scale and process barriers but requires continual expensive reinvestment. Gross margin is not owner earnings after fabs, equipment, research and working capital. Distinguish leading-edge from mature processes, production location from customer location, and booked revenue from durable through-cycle utilization. A technically excellent company can have concentration risks outside management control. Overseas facilities require time, qualification and capital; they are not immediately interchangeable with an existing production cluster. An ADR may represent several ordinary shares, so EPS and price must use the same unit. A low multiple on a peak quarter is not automatically conservative. [Source above; analytical framework.]

Valuation discipline: use a consistent claim and denominator. Enterprise value should be compared with cash generation before financing; common value should be compared with cash available after senior claims. Explicitly distinguish sustainable earnings from annualizing one quarter. A current quote from a different date, an eventual transaction price and an investor's later disclosed average cost are not substitutes for the cutoff price. State a range of operating assumptions, compare the result with liquid alternatives and require a margin of safety that survives a less favorable case. When a current input is unavailable, leave the numerical valuation incomplete instead of embedding an untraceable estimate.

Evidence boundary: trailing multiples, historical FCF and annualized-quarter illustrations use different earnings bases. None should be relabeled a forward consensus forecast or an investor's acquisition cost.
