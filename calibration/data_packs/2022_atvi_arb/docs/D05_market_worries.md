Title: Adverse case and permanent-loss risks
Source: https://news.microsoft.com/2022/01/18/microsoft-to-acquire-activision-blizzard-to-bring-the-joy-and-community-of-gaming-to-everyone-across-every-device/ (published 2022-01-18); https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1067983/000095012322002973/0000950123-22-002973.txt (2022-02-14)
Date: 2022-02-15

The bear case is that governments block or materially delay the combination because a major gaming platform is acquiring important content. A rejected deal could leave the target trading on impaired standalone sentiment, litigation risk and slower game releases. Regulatory probability is not known merely because the bidder can afford the price. A longer timetable can erase a seemingly attractive annualized spread. [Source above.]

Technical context: merger arbitrage is a contingent cash-flow problem. Separate the promised consideration, financing, approvals, termination rights, outside date and possible remedies. Gross spread is not expected return: completion probability, time and the break-price loss all matter. A bidder's ability to pay does not settle regulatory approval. Stand-alone valuation is important even if the intended holding period ends at closing, because the target remains a public business if the transaction fails. Do not treat an original pre-announcement stock purchase and a post-announcement workout as the same investment thesis merely because they use the same ticker. [Source above; analytical framework.]

Risk discipline: the adverse cases above are analyst stress tests grounded in the disclosed business and current circumstances, not quotations attributed to an unnamed contemporary short seller. No authenticated bearish price target was recovered. Separate a temporary quotation decline from permanent impairment of earning power or of the investor's claim. Ask whether the issuer can fund necessary operations without assuming favorable capital markets. Identify which changes are within management control and which are not. A large cash balance at the investor does not cure an issuer's leverage, a faulty operating thesis or an excessive purchase price. Do not soften a serious downside merely because an admired investor might be interested.
