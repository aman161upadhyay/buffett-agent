Target FY2006 net margin is 20.6%, versus 24.1% in FY2005; a higher upstream proportion and regulated downstream economics make the reported margin unlike a pure efficiency ranking. Target capex rises from RMB124.8 billion to RMB148.7 billion while OCF falls from RMB203.9 billion to RMB198.1 billion. Consequently OCF less capex falls 37.6%, despite net-income growth of 6.6%. A common oil-price tailwind does not ensure equivalent cash conversion.

The March peer quotations are earlier than the June target proxy in D04; no synchronized July valuation claim is made. State-controlled capital allocation, foreign-currency distributions and potential domestic equity issuance are additional differences. FT P/E is reproduced as published, not reconstructed with inconsistent currencies or ADR units. Missing P/B, reserve-value and EV/EBITDA comparisons remain gaps rather than being filled with later flotation valuations.

The published peer earnings yields are approximately 8.85%, 10.64% and 11.90%, reciprocals of 11.3x, 9.4x and 8.4x. Compare them with the target's dated D04 sensitivity, retaining its explicit HK-dollar/RMB simplification. Target FCF yield around 2.4% is not interchangeable with accounting earnings yield; replacement investment can consume much of the latter.

Use other giant integrated oil companies as the comparative-value set. Match upstream versus downstream earnings, reserves, taxes and capital intensity before comparing P/E. Parent-control discounts and different share markets can create misleading headline valuations. Comparing free-float value with a peer's total capitalization would be an error. The central issue is present relative value, not whether the stock has risen from the original cost. [Source above.]

Technical context: petroleum accounting profits depend on realized prices, volumes, royalties, taxes and the treatment of exploration and development costs. A reserve is not cash in the bank: it needs extraction, transport and often replacement investment. Distinguish upstream earnings from refining and chemical margins rather than assuming they move together. For owner earnings, separate maintenance needed to sustain economic production from expansion capital. Check whether reported free cash flow deducts preferred distributions and whether acquisitions are being called capital expenditure. Proved reserves, production and reserve life use different units and assumptions. A conservative analysis should explicitly test a lower realized-price case without claiming to forecast the commodity. [Source above; analytical accounting framework, not additional reported figures.]

Comparison discipline: compare businesses on the source of their cash and the capital required to earn it, rather than treating the nearest ticker as an exact substitute. Normalize financing and accounting differences before interpreting relative multiples. A peer can have a better balance sheet but a worse franchise, or a better franchise at an unattractive price. Relative cheapness alone cannot prove absolute value. Consider the opportunity cost of adding to an existing understandable holding, retaining liquidity, owning an entire business or buying the investor's own shares. Where a numerical peer table was not recovered, keep the comparison qualitative rather than inventing an industry average.

Evidence boundary: missing figures are not available in contemporaneous sources recovered for this digest. Analytical questions are not additional reported facts or claims about the investor's private thinking. Read the financial, risk and fund-context documents together; no individual accounting ratio supplies a complete investment conclusion.
