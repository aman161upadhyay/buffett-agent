Nine-month OCF was $2,638m versus $3,303m, down 20.1%; quarter net income declined 29.8%. September book equity was $12,255m, or approximately $36.00 per outstanding share. Those are reported balance/flow observations or explicit division, not a claim that growth capital expenditure is optional. Gross debt includes its current portion consistently with D04.

Freight revenue fell approximately 27%, operating income 26%, and EPS 29% (calculated). EPS includes a $0.06 favorable coal-rate adjustment. These are consolidated parent-company results, not the separately filing railroad subsidiary's earnings. The release supplies no numerical earnings guidance.

Third-quarter results show recession pressure on freight profitability. Adjusting the favorable coal item gives roughly $1.36 quarterly diluted EPS, a simple analytical subtraction rather than company guidance. The earnings decline does not by itself demonstrate permanent loss of the network franchise; nor does the existence of scarce rights of way guarantee the restoration of prior freight volumes. Technical context: track and terminal networks are costly to replicate but also costly to sustain. Depreciation is an accounting allocation, not a reliable measure of current maintenance investment when equipment prices and traffic requirements change. Separate maintenance and capacity expansion without assuming every reported dollar is optional. Earnings depend on traffic density, mix, fuel, labor and pricing; a network moat does not guarantee stable quarterly volume. Debt belongs in enterprise value and interest belongs in the bridge to equity earnings. A controlling investor's own stock is consideration with economic cost, not free acquisition currency. [Source above; analytical framework, not additional measured network statistics.]

Results discipline: establish which period the release covers and which publication was actually available by the information boundary. Compare growth in units, prices, margins and cash separately, since a higher EPS figure can come from a smaller share count or an accounting item rather than stronger demand. Treat management targets as claims to test against demonstrated history, not as achieved earnings. A weak period may be cyclical, structural or exceptional; describe the evidence needed to distinguish these possibilities. Do not introduce a subsequent quarterly report to explain an ambiguous current observation, and do not confuse disclosure day with the date cash was earned.

Evidence boundary: missing figures are not available in contemporaneous sources recovered for this digest. Analytical questions are not additional reported facts or claims about the investor's private thinking. Read the financial, risk and fund-context documents together; no individual accounting ratio supplies a complete investment conclusion.
