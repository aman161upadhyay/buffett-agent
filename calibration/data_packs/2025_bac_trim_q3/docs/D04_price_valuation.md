Title: A bank multiple with an existing-owner tax hurdle
Source: https://d1io3yog0oux5.cloudfront.net/_70c8fb58fd3bea7a80c281a02cf2c22d/bankofamerica/db/806/10171/earnings_release/The+Press+Release_1Q25_ADA.pdf
Date: 2025-06-30

The June 30 close was $47.32, from the dated historical-price observation. Against reported first-quarter EPS of $0.90 multiplied by four, that implies 13.14 times a mechanical annualized earnings run rate. Against book value of $36.39 it is about 1.30 times book. These are neither a forward consensus P/E nor a tangible-book multiple. The book denominator and earnings period are March figures, not an unreleased June balance sheet. [1; historical price below]

At this price an existing low-basis owner faces a different decision from a new buyer. Selling realizes a tax liability; holding preserves a claim on future earnings and repurchases but retains credit and duration exposure. Exact tax lots and a contemporaneous independently verified target capital ratio are not available. An apparent earnings yield above Treasury yields does not by itself compensate for leverage or unknowable losses. No price target or precise value of the deposit franchise is supplied, and a regulatory ownership threshold should not be mistaken for a valuation model.

Evidence ledger from the April 15 release: first-quarter net income was $7.4 billion, versus $6.7 billion a year earlier; diluted EPS was $0.90, versus $0.76. Revenue net of interest expense was $27.4 billion, or $27.5 billion fully tax equivalent. Net interest income was $14.4 billion, or $14.6 billion FTE, up 3%. Noninterest expense was $17.8 billion and credit provision $1.5 billion; net charge-offs of $1.5 billion were flat versus both comparison quarters. Ending deposits were approximately $2 trillion. Standardized CET1 was 11.8%, backed by $201 billion common equity tier 1 capital. Book value per common share was $36.39. Capital returned totaled $6.5 billion, comprising $2.0 billion common dividends and $4.5 billion repurchases. [1]

Valuation discipline: the calculations in this document are analyst arithmetic, not management targets or a Berkshire underwriting model. Earnings multiples, book multiples and cash-flow yields answer different questions and are not interchangeable. A low multiple offers no protection if normalized earning power has been overstated. Conversely, growth has value only after the incremental capital required to obtain it. No verified contemporary consensus forecast or complete historical multiple distribution is supplied; an exact intrinsic value should therefore not be reverse-engineered from a subsequent transaction.
