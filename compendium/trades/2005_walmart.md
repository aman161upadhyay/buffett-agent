## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**2005:** Berkshire established a major position after an earlier episode of stopping purchases as the stock rose modestly. The omission episode and the 2005 purchase must not be assigned the same entry price or date. [1–2]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
Tilson's **2004 meeting notes**, not a verbatim official transcript, record Buffett saying: “We bought a little and it moved up a bit and I stopped buying.” The notes attribute approximately $10 billion of opportunity cost to the hesitation. Treat wording as a note-taker's transcription. [2]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
The 2005 letter says it established a Wal-Mart position; year-end cost was $944 million versus $933 million market value. [1]

## What happened after
The earlier omission cost is neither a realized loss nor the P&L of the later stake. Full later return/exit not verified here. [1–2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Anchoring:** Small price changes derailed a larger intended purchase. [2]
- **Execution:** A favorable business valuation must translate into action. [2]
- **Gap:** Exact 2005 price-to-value estimate unknown. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: discipline is not synonymous with waiting for every last increment of cheapness. [2]

## Public discussion
Secondary accounts disagree on whether the famous admission was at the 2003 or 2004 meeting. The recovered dated notes support **May 1, 2004** for this record. [2]
