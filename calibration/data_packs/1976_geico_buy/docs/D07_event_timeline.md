Title: GEICO — Information chronology
Source: Reconstructed from GEICO operating history and 1975 accounts; Jack Byrne Insurance Hall of Fame biography, https://www.insurancehalloffame.org/jack-byrne-simple (pre-cutoff events only)
Date: 1976-07-01

## Cutoff evidence — reconstructed

The 1975 financial year produces a major reported loss. In May, according to the reconstructed Byrne biography, a new chief executive accepts the task of repairing the insurer. At the July 1 boundary the financing and operational repair must still be underwritten. The pack contains no original settlement ledger fixing a purchase on this day and no assumption that all support arrangements are final. It excludes any later full-year result or preferred/common holdings table as current evidence. The relevant chronology is loss recognition, management change and unresolved recapitalization, not management change followed automatically by a recovery.

## Economic reading of the supplied facts

Industry economics: an insurer receives premiums before all associated losses are known or paid. The investment balance generated by that delay is economically valuable only when the eventual underwriting cost is acceptable. Inadequate prices or underestimated reserves can create reported profits that do not exist economically. A direct seller can avoid agency commissions, but saving distribution costs does not compensate for accepting unsuitable risks at inadequate rates. Regulatory capital and claims-paying ability constrain the amount that can be invested in equities or distributed to owners. Reinsurance adds counterparty exposure and can transfer risk without eliminating uncertainty about its measurement. This is a description of insurance mechanics, not a claim that any reported reserve balance is sufficient.

Chronology controls: the cutoff is an information boundary, not proof of an execution on that day. A negotiated commitment, a public announcement, a purchase settlement and an annual-report disclosure are distinct events. Facts about the condition of the business may be reconstructed from archival accounts without turning a later explanation into a statement available to the market.

Only the events described above enter this chronology. No subsequent result is assumed when assessing the unresolved question. The absence of an original day-by-day price series prevents identification of a precise entry-day return or volume pattern. The same restriction applies to unpublished board discussions and security-specific purchase ledgers. This reconstruction does not claim access to either. Where a transaction is being considered, proposed terms are identified as proposed; regulatory approvals and operating improvements are not treated as completed. Where shares already exist in a portfolio, a request to study a new allocation must not erase that exposure.
