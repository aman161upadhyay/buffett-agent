Itochu trading income fell ¥102.4bn to ¥72.1bn; revenue fell 12.4%, so trading margin fell from 3.75% to 3.02% (arithmetic). Its ¥254.1bn OCF benefited from working capital; June net debt was ¥2,227.2bn and equity ¥3,091.8bn. Marubeni operating profit declined 6.2% to ¥51.9bn, less than its 16.8% revenue fall; the operating-margin arithmetic is 3.27% versus 2.90%. These Japanese-practice operating measures are not IFRS standardized subtotals.

Mitsubishi gross profit declined ¥483.9bn to ¥379.7bn; equity-method earnings dropped to ¥14.8bn from ¥61.2bn. June net interest-bearing debt, excluding leases, was ¥4,349.6bn; OCF ¥296.4bn. Mitsui pretax profit fell 40.8% to ¥102.0bn and gross profit to ¥189.7bn from ¥209.2bn. Sumitomo gross profit fell ¥228.1bn to ¥173.3bn while SG&A remained near ¥159.7bn; net debt was ¥2,528.3bn.

Management full-year profit forecasts then available were Itochu ¥400bn, Marubeni ¥100bn, Mitsubishi ¥200bn, Mitsui ¥180bn and Sumitomo a ¥150bn loss. Forecast dividends were ¥88, ¥15, ¥134 and ¥80 for the first four, not guaranteed cash yields. These forecasts are contemporaneous issuer guidance, not analyst consensus or subsequent achievements.

Technical context: general trading houses combine operating assets, financial stakes and joint ventures. Equity-accounted earnings, commodity gains and asset dispositions require reconciliation with dividends actually received. Net debt must be evaluated alongside the liquidity and risk of the underlying holdings, not offset indiscriminately against all reported investments. Conglomerate discounts can reflect governance or cross-holdings, not just market error. Yen debt matched against yen assets limits one currency mismatch while leaving operating currency exposures and interest costs. A basket should use ordinary-share prices, consistent fiscal dates and the correct local units. Diversification does not by itself establish a superior return on capital. Results discipline: establish which period the release covers and which publication was actually available by the information boundary. Compare growth in units, prices, margins and cash separately, since a higher EPS figure can come from a smaller share count or an accounting item rather than stronger demand. Treat management targets as claims to test against demonstrated history, not as achieved earnings. A weak period may be cyclical, structural or exceptional; describe the evidence needed to distinguish these possibilities. Do not introduce a subsequent quarterly report to explain an ambiguous current observation, and do not confuse disclosure day with the date cash was earned.

Evidence boundary: missing figures are not available in contemporaneous sources recovered for this digest. Analytical questions are not additional reported facts or claims about the investor's private thinking. Read the financial, risk and fund-context documents together; no individual accounting ratio supplies a complete investment conclusion.
