## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**Contract inception dates vary:** Do not import 2008 volatility or later Black-Scholes liabilities into every earlier decision. This was Buffett's own book, separate from General Re runoff. Exact 2004 commencement remains unverified. [1–2]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“I believe each contract we own was mispriced at inception.” **February 2009 explanation.** The economic argument combined underwriting profit with investment income on premiums held. [2]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Sold long-dated puts on four indices and wrote credit protection. At 2007 year-end it described 54 high-yield-index contracts; in 2008 it separately discussed individual-company credit contracts. Equity puts settled only at maturity, not when markets temporarily fell. [1–2]

## What happened after
At 2008 year-end put notional was $37.1 billion, premiums $4.9 billion and accounting liability $10 billion. Notional, premiums, liability and realized losses are different quantities. Maturities ran September 2019–January 2028. Final results remain outside this record. [2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Price:** Buffett claimed favorable inception economics, not that derivatives are universally safe. [2]
- **Float:** Upfront money had investable value. [1–2]
- **Settlement:** Terminal index levels matter for puts; credit contracts have different triggers. [1–2]
- **Liquidity:** Collateral/cash demands can destroy otherwise solvent firms; avoid generalizing his precise terms to all derivatives. [2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: insurance-like underwriting under controlled terms, despite broader warnings about systemic derivatives risk. [1–2]

## Public discussion
The 2008 letter explicitly addresses the perceived contradiction with his earlier warnings. Independent contemporary contract criticism remains a gap. [2]
