## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**Summer 1992:** General Dynamics announced a Dutch tender for about 30% of its shares. Buffett initially evaluated a short-duration tender profit, not a permanent aerospace franchise. His subsequent business research altered the decision. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
Bill Anders had “a clearly articulated and rational strategy.” Buffett then “dumped my arbitrage thoughts” and elected to invest with him. **March 1993 account of a 1992 change of mind.** [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Used tender-related turnover to accumulate about 14% of the post-tender shares in a month. The year-end table reports 4.35 million shares costing $312.438 million, valued at $450.769 million. Exact first trade date unknown. [1]

## What happened after
The year-end unrealized appreciation verifies an early favorable mark, not a final return. Later exit chronology remains a gap. `open` denotes the verified 1992 checkpoint. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Catalyst:** Tender mechanics supplied the original opportunity. [1]
- **Management:** Anders's urgency and demonstrated results changed the thesis. [1]
- **Execution:** Unusually heavy turnover permitted rapid scale. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: retain the thesis transition. Labeling the entire purchase “merger arbitrage” would erase the central learning; labeling it a fully formed long thesis from inception would also be wrong. [1]

## Public discussion
No authenticated contemporaneous opposing tender analysis recovered. The letter is management's retrospective account, not an independent evaluation. [1]
