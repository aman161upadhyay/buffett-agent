## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**1998:** EJA sold fractional aircraft ownership and operated the fleet. Buffett's family had used it for about four years; customer enthusiasm was genuine but not a complete capital-return analysis. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“What a friendly, efficient, and safe operation EJA runs.” **March 1999 retrospective:** Buffett emphasized firsthand experience and Santulli's ability to turn an idea into an industry. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Santulli called Buffett in May 1998; they agreed a $725 million transaction, half cash and half stock. Exact close date unknown here. [1]

## What happened after
The 2009 letter describes heavy losses and David Sokol's corrective work. Do not equate the service's attractiveness with demonstrated acquisition profitability. [2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Experience:** Buffett had observed operations as a customer. [1]
- **Founder:** Santulli pioneered fractional ownership. [1]
- **Scale:** Largest operator with perceived growth potential. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: close customer knowledge helped assess product quality, but later losses show that product quality alone does not eliminate aircraft/fleet economics. [1–2]

## Public discussion
Independent 1998 commentary not recovered; the later corrective account must remain hindsight. [2]
