## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**1967:** Ben Rosner's privately operated retail chain was offered to DRC for cash. The original purchase memorandum, acquisition-date store count and consideration are unrecovered. The partnership's July 1967 letter already described the acquired subsidiary's progress as highly satisfactory, so that judgment is post-purchase, not an entry-date forecast. [1–2]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“Everything was as advertised or better.” **Post-purchase**, January 24, 1968, about the 1967 Associated Cotton Shops and National Indemnity acquisitions together. The surrounding passage emphasizes the responsible managers rather than a plan for Buffett to run the shops. [1]

The December 1969 discussion calls Associated Retail Stores “an excellent business with a strong financial position, good operating margins and a record of increasing sales and earnings in recent years.” This is an interim assessment, not an original purchase quote. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
DRC, 80% partnership-owned, acquired the operating business in 1967. Buffett retained Ben Rosner. The business later used the Associated Retail Stores name. Berkshire acquired 100% ownership through its year-end 1978 merger with DRC; this was **not** a new 1978 purchase of the shops from Rosner. [1–2]

## What happened after
Partnership letters singled out Rosner's results as outstanding, and Berkshire's 1978 letter praised the continued relationship. These support a successful early operating investment, not a reconstructed lifetime multiple. Ultimate disposition and exact total capital returned were not verified; historical status therefore remains unknown. [1–2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Operator:** Rosner was explicitly identified as the person responsible for results. [1–2]
- **Economics:** balance-sheet strength and operating margins differentiated the business from a generic department-store bargain. [1]
- **Continuity:** operations stayed with the existing owner-manager; no turnaround intervention is supported. [1–2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: a management-led private acquisition inside a controlled investment vehicle. Keep it distinct from Hochschild Kohn's disappointing retail economics, despite common DRC ownership. [1–2]

## Public discussion
No independent contemporary sale coverage was recovered. The main available discussion consists of communications to partners and Berkshire shareholders; later narrative should not be presented as a public 1967 consensus.
