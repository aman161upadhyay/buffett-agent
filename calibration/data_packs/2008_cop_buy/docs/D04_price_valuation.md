The TTM calculation adds rounded EPS across periods and is approximate. Annualizing $3.50 quarterly EPS gives $14, explicitly a mechanical scenario, not normalized earnings. The unusually high accounting yield requires commodity-cycle and maintenance-capital scrutiny.

Technical context: petroleum accounting profits depend on realized prices, volumes, royalties, taxes and the treatment of exploration and development costs. A reserve is not cash in the bank: it needs extraction, transport and often replacement investment. Distinguish upstream earnings from refining and chemical margins rather than assuming they move together. For owner earnings, separate maintenance needed to sustain economic production from expansion capital. Check whether reported free cash flow deducts preferred distributions and whether acquisitions are being called capital expenditure. Proved reserves, production and reserve life use different units and assumptions. A conservative analysis should explicitly test a lower realized-price case without claiming to forecast the commodity. Valuation discipline: use a consistent claim and denominator. Enterprise value should be compared with cash generation before financing; common value should be compared with cash available after senior claims. Explicitly distinguish sustainable earnings from annualizing one quarter. A current quote from a different date, an eventual transaction price and an investor's later disclosed average cost are not substitutes for the cutoff price. State a range of operating assumptions, compare the result with liquid alternatives and require a margin of safety that survives a less favorable case. When a current input is unavailable, leave the numerical valuation incomplete instead of embedding an untraceable estimate.

Read the financial, risk and fund-context documents together; no individual accounting ratio supplies a complete investment conclusion.
