## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**December 2001:** Craig Ponzio called Buffett about the business, which Buffett had not previously known. The later letter reports sales growth from $3 million to $300 million during Ponzio's ownership; those are historical business data, not Berkshire returns. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
Ponzio was “straightforward,” cared about the buyer and was “realistic as to price.” **February 2002 retrospective.** [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Received the call December 3; met Ponzio/CEO Steve McKenzie two days later and agreed in 90 minutes; signed within ten days. The acquisition had closed by the letter date. Exact close month unknown. [1]

## What happened after
The letter anticipated complementary acquisitions, not a proven future return. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Position:** Industry leadership across multiple regions. [1]
- **Seller:** Realistic price and compatible objectives. [1]
- **Platform:** Further related opportunities could deploy capital. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: fast decision-making followed straightforward economics and people, not an exemption from price discipline. [1]

## Public discussion
Independent contemporary acquisition criticism not recovered. [1]
