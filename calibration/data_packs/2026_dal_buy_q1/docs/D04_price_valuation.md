Title: A low earnings multiple is not a claim-free annuity
Source: https://ir.delta.com/news/news-details/2025/Delta-Air-Lines-Announces-September-Quarter-2025-Financial-Results/default.aspx
Date: 2025-12-31

The December 31 unadjusted close was $69.40. Compared with management's approximately $6 adjusted annual EPS guidance, the stock is at about 11.57 times indicated earnings. That is an issuer-guidance multiple, not a verified consensus forward multiple or a completed full-year result. Annualizing quarterly adjusted EPS of $1.71 gives $6.84, but September seasonality and operating leverage make that an unreliable normal earnings measure. [1; historical price below]

An earnings yield must be assessed after recurring fleet investment and balance-sheet risk. The release's $3.5-$4.0 billion cash-flow guidance is after its definition of capital spending, while adjusted debt includes claims not captured by a simple common-share P/E. No independently reconstructed owner-earnings model, date-matched market capitalization or long-run peer multiple series is recovered. A low-looking multiple could compensate for cyclicality or signal overestimated normal profit. Loyalty economics warrant investigation, but cannot be valued as though the aircraft operation and its obligations are absent.

Evidence ledger from the October 9 release: September-quarter GAAP revenue was $16.673 billion, operating income $1.684 billion, pre-tax income $1.777 billion, net income $1.417 billion and diluted EPS $2.17. Adjusted revenue was $15.2 billion, pre-tax income approximately $1.5 billion and EPS $1.71. Operating cash flow was $1.8 billion; reported free cash flow $833 million; gross capital spending approximately $1.1 billion. Debt and finance-lease obligations were $14.9 billion, adjusted net debt $15.6 billion, and liquidity $6.9 billion including $3.1 billion undrawn revolvers. Premium revenue grew 9%, loyalty revenue 9% and American Express remuneration 12% to $2 billion. Full-year adjusted EPS guidance was approximately $6 and free cash flow $3.5-$4.0 billion. [1]

Valuation discipline: the calculations in this document are analyst arithmetic, not management targets or a Berkshire underwriting model. Earnings multiples, book multiples and cash-flow yields answer different questions and are not interchangeable. A low multiple offers no protection if normalized earning power has been overstated. Conversely, growth has value only after the incremental capital required to obtain it. No verified contemporary consensus forecast or complete historical multiple distribution is supplied; an exact intrinsic value should therefore not be reverse-engineered from a subsequent transaction.
