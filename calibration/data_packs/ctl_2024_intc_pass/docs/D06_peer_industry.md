TSMC specializes in foundry manufacturing, while AMD and Nvidia rely on external manufacturing and compete in different compute markets. A low price-to-book for an integrated producer is not directly comparable to a fabless designer's high return on capital. The 18A roadmap, capacity utilization, customer commitments, yields and product execution determine whether incremental factory spending earns an attractive return. No contemporaneous harmonized peer owner-earnings table is recovered. Industry strategic importance alone does not set the return paid to common equity. [S1; analytical comparison]

Intel designs processors and manufactures semiconductors, with an ambition to manufacture for outside customers as well. The installed x86 software base, enterprise qualification processes and manufacturing expertise are important assets. A bull can view depressed earnings as the cost of renewing a strategic American manufacturing franchise. Foundry success could monetize large fabrication investments across both internal and external products. This is a business-recovery thesis, not a regulated utility with a guaranteed return on its capital base. [S1; analyst interpretation]

### Underwriting distinction

Comparable-company analysis is useful only after matching the economic claims. A supplier, a platform, a lender and a brand owner can all report revenue growth while requiring very different reinvestment and balance sheets. Growth funded by customers is different from growth funded by new common equity or acquisition debt. Market-share evidence should therefore be read alongside retention, pricing, capital requirements and the company's ability to retain incremental profit. [Analytical interpretation of S1]

No peer multiple is invented where a same-date, same-definition series is missing. The more useful comparison in that case is structural: who owns the customer, who can raise price, who must invest to preserve competitiveness, and who bears a sudden liquidity demand. The company-specific figures below supply the scale and margin evidence. A sector label is not a substitute for those economics. [Analytical interpretation of S1]
