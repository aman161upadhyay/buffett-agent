The historical February 28 close and capitalization are supplied above. Comparing $8.809 billion annual company-defined free cash flow with equity value requires deducting approximately $800 million annual preferred distributions before common owner earnings. A low spot-price FCF multiple is not a through-cycle owner-earnings yield. Debt repayment can add equity value but does not make the commodity price predictable. [Source above.]

Technical context: petroleum accounting profits depend on realized prices, volumes, royalties, taxes and the treatment of exploration and development costs. A reserve is not cash in the bank: it needs extraction, transport and often replacement investment. Distinguish upstream earnings from refining and chemical margins rather than assuming they move together. For owner earnings, separate maintenance needed to sustain economic production from expansion capital. Check whether reported free cash flow deducts preferred distributions and whether acquisitions are being called capital expenditure. Proved reserves, production and reserve life use different units and assumptions. A conservative analysis should explicitly test a lower realized-price case without claiming to forecast the commodity. [Source above; analytical accounting framework, not additional reported figures.]

Valuation discipline: use a consistent claim and denominator. Enterprise value should be compared with cash generation before financing; common value should be compared with cash available after senior claims. Explicitly distinguish sustainable earnings from annualizing one quarter. A current quote from a different date, an eventual transaction price and an investor's later disclosed average cost are not substitutes for the cutoff price. State a range of operating assumptions, compare the result with liquid alternatives and require a margin of safety that survives a less favorable case. When a current input is unavailable, leave the numerical valuation incomplete instead of embedding an untraceable estimate.

Evidence boundary: prices are dated historical observations and multiples are approximate arithmetic. Preferred capital is included in enterprise value and remains senior to common distributions.
