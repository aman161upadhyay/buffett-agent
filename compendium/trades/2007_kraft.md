## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**2007:** Berkshire built a major Kraft stake. The later Cadbury bid was not the original investment catalyst. Entry-date multiple and direct purchase quotation remain unknown. [1,3]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“I didn't like either the Cadbury decision or the pizza decision.” **May 2010 hindsight/management criticism**, not a 2007 buy rationale. Buffett also acknowledged Berkshire had made its own dumb deals. [3]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Reported 8.1% at December 2007, expanded to 130,272,500 shares by December 2009, with $4.330 billion cost versus $3.541 billion market value. [1–2]

## What happened after
The 2010 criticism concerned acquisition economics and disposal of the pizza business. It was not proof that every Kraft product lacked a moat; later Kraft Heinz history requires a separate case. [3]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Entry gap:** No invented brand/valuation quote. [1]
- **Currency:** Judge what is given up as well as what is received. [3]
- **Capital allocation:** Good existing assets have opportunity cost. [3]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: minority ownership does not imply approval of management's subsequent acquisition program. [3]

## Public discussion
The annual-meeting question itself addressed CEO compensation and the transactions, supplying an authenticated public challenge rather than reconstructed social-media sentiment. [3]
