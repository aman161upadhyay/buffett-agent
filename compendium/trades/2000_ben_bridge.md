## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**2000:** Fourth-generation managers Ed and Jon Bridge sought a buyer preserving their organization. Barnett Helzberg recommended Berkshire. Exact entry-date valuation unknown. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“Both the business and the family ... enjoy extraordinary reputations.” **February 2001 explanation:** preserving the organization mattered more than speculative merger synergies. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Agreed by telephone before meeting management face to face; consideration was half cash, half stock. Buffett promised Ed and Jon would remain in charge. [1]

## What happened after
No long-run acquisition return established. Sellers shared proceeds with coworkers, supporting the letter's people/culture assessment but not supplying an IRR. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Reputation:** Trusted referral and family record. [1]
- **Business:** Repeated same-store growth was evidence of execution. [1]
- **Home:** Preserve autonomy rather than impose another chain's cost-cutting plan. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: permanent-home differentiation can attract sellers without promising synergies Buffett cannot deliver. [1]

## Public discussion
Original independent deal reporting not recovered. [1]
