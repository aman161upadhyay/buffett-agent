## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**1956 initial investment:** manufacturer of farm implements and water systems, approximately break-even, with weak management and a difficult industry. Buffett later reports a price around $18 against $72 book value and $50/share current assets less all liabilities. These are his reconstruction of the entry facts, not a recovered 1956 filing. **1961 control decision:** years of purchases and disappointing earnings made asset control more important than passive rerating. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“Our business is making excellent purchases -- not making extraordinary sales.” **Hindsight**, 1963 annual partnership letter after sale of the operating assets. [1]

The contemporaneous 1961 annual discussion explicitly recognized “a poor management situation, along with a fairly tough industry situation.” It valued the stake conservatively rather than assuming a turnaround. This letter followed acquisition of control. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
| Date | Partnership action |
|---|---|
| 1956 onward | Bought stock initially as a general undervalued security, generally $16–$25 over the following years. [1] |
| August–September 1961 | Large purchases and tender at $30.25 brought ownership above 70%. [1] |
| April 23, 1962 | Harry Bottle became president after an unsuccessful internal management promotion. [1–2] |
| 1962–1963 | Reduced inventory, expenses and unprofitable branches; invested released capital in securities. [1] |
| 1963 | Sold operating assets; residual company renamed First Beatrice Corporation. [1] |

## What happened after
Inventory fell from $4.203 million at November 30, 1961 to $890,000 at July 31, 1963; cash and securities rose from $166,000 to $2.436 million. The 1963 letter attributes over $400,000 gains to redeployed securities. Operating-asset sale near book plus portfolio gains yielded about $80/share, with a $200,000 contingency reserve in the partnership valuation. This is not all operating profit. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Asset discount:** current-asset coverage, not an attractive industry, justified starting the investment. [1]
- **Control:** changed a passive cheap-stock position into an actionable capital-release situation. [1]
- **Operator:** Bottle was the specific catalyst; Buffett did not solve factory execution personally. [1–2]
- **Reinvestment:** securities gains materially contributed to the result. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: a successful cigar-butt/control workout, but a management-intensive path, not proof that all troubled manufacturers deserve rescue. **Date correction:** required 1961–1963 episode began with purchases in **1956**, so the filename follows the schema's first-entry rule. [1]

## Public discussion
No contemporaneous independent press account was recovered. The partnership letters give unusually detailed balance sheets and disclose the failed initial management solution; this is stronger evidence than later heroic summaries. [1]
