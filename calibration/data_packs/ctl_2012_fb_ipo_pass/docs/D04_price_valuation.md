The implied approximately $104 billion fully diluted equity value quoted with the offering is roughly 104 times 2011 net income, a 0.96% historical earnings yield; share-count definitions mean this is an approximate capitalization comparison, not audited enterprise value. Better ad relevance and payments can improve monetization without requiring equivalent growth in users. A long-horizon buyer could regard the company as a consumer habit and advertising toll bridge, with a large reinvestment runway. [S1; bull interpretation]

### Dated operating baseline

The May 17 prospectus reports 2011 revenue of $3.711 billion and net income of $1.000 billion; 2010 revenue was $1.974 billion and net income $606 million. First-quarter revenue was $1.058 billion, versus $731 million, while net income was $205 million versus $233 million. Monthly active users reached 901 million at March 31 and daily active users averaged 526 million in March. Mobile monthly active users numbered 488 million. Advertising supplied approximately 85% of 2011 revenue; payments and other fees supplied the remainder. The offer consists of 421,233,615 Class A shares at $38, including 180 million newly issued shares and 241,233,615 sold by existing holders. Gross offering size is $16.007 billion; most shares sold are secondary, not new cash for the company. Class A has one vote per share and Class B ten. Zuckerberg is expected to control approximately 55.9% of votes after the offering. [S1]

### Underwriting distinction

Price is the amount paid for a particular legal claim, not a percentage below a previous high. A cash-rich balance sheet, a large backlog or an admired brand may support valuation, but none fixes the distribution to that claim. Common shares, a preferred security and an operating-asset acquisition require different loss and return calculations. For ordinary equity, estimate distributable earnings after recurring investment and liabilities; for financing, identify coupon, priority, security and conversion economics before computing an expected return. [Analytical interpretation of S1]

The figures below are the denominator checks for any valuation. No observed decline is called an adequate margin of safety without a justified earnings range. Missing contemporaneous prices or maintenance-investment figures remain missing. Forward scenarios may be useful, but their probabilities are analyst assumptions rather than facts or Berkshire's stated forecasts. [Analytical interpretation of S1]
