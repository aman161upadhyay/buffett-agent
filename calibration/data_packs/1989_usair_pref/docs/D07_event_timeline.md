## Cutoff evidence — reconstructed

At the August 7 boundary the proposed terms are $358 million of newly issued preferred, 9.25% dividend, conversion after two years at $60 and mandatory redemption after ten years. A newspaper published the next day is an archival account of those terms, not a pre-existing desk publication. Exact intraday announcement and settlement times were not recovered. The prior acquisitions and Colodny's operating history are background. No future financial result, dividend event or attempted resale belongs in the chronology. The acquisition of a stable investment block is a financing choice; it does not itself establish that the airline's competitive cost position has improved.

## Economic reading of the supplied facts

Industry economics: airlines commit capital to aircraft and infrastructure before knowing the revenue each seat will earn. An empty seat cannot be stored for sale tomorrow. Routes, schedules and customer loyalty can support a network, but fare competition can transfer much of the value to travelers. Labor contracts, fleet obligations and fuel costs can remain burdensome even when fares fall. Acquiring another network adds both opportunities and integration exposure. A preferred dividend is paid by the same operating enterprise and therefore depends on cash generation, financing and senior creditor claims. Historical profits should be examined across competitive conditions rather than treated as a bond-like entitlement. No estimate of future fuel prices or an unverified cost-per-seat comparison is assumed here.

Chronology controls: the cutoff is an information boundary, not proof of an execution on that day. A negotiated commitment, a public announcement, a purchase settlement and an annual-report disclosure are distinct events. Facts about the condition of the business may be reconstructed from archival accounts without turning a later explanation into a statement available to the market.

Only the events described above enter this chronology. No subsequent result is assumed when assessing the unresolved question. The absence of an original day-by-day price series prevents identification of a precise entry-day return or volume pattern. The same restriction applies to unpublished board discussions and security-specific purchase ledgers. This reconstruction does not claim access to either. Where a transaction is being considered, proposed terms are identified as proposed; regulatory approvals and operating improvements are not treated as completed. Where shares already exist in a portfolio, a request to study a new allocation must not erase that exposure.
