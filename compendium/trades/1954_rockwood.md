## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**1954 event set, reconstructed from the later account:** Rockwood was a chocolate-products manufacturer with limited profitability. LIFO accounting traced to cocoa at five cents/pound in 1941, while a temporary 1954 shortage pushed cocoa above sixty cents. An ordinary inventory sale would have incurred a large tax. The announced restructuring terminated cocoa-butter sales and offered 80 pounds of beans per share against 13 million pounds allocated to that activity. These are the deal terms Buffett later described, not a recovered original tender circular. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“For several weeks I busily bought shares, sold beans, and made periodic stops at Schroeder Trust to exchange stock certificates for warehouse receipts.” **Hindsight**, February 28, 1989, describing his work while employed at Graham-Newman. “The profits were good and my only expense was subway tokens.” No contemporary pitch was recovered. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Berkshire was not the buyer. Buffett describes his own participation at age 24, buying stock, exchanging certificates for warehouse receipts, and selling beans. The cited letter does not allocate every transaction between his personal account and employer; the personal vehicle label follows his first-person account, with that accounting limitation explicit. Exact purchase/sale dates, position size and hedging mechanics are unknown. [1]

## What happened after
The arbitrage was profitable, but a precise personal profit is not disclosed. The letter separately says Rockwood stock rose from 15 to 100 around the restructuring despite operating losses. **Do not assign that equity appreciation to Buffett's exchange arbitrage return** or infer he held all the residual stock. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Specified catalyst:** the stock-for-inventory repurchase, not a general market rise, unlocked value. [1]
- **Tax structure:** the 1954 tax provision made the inventory distribution economically different from a taxable sale. [1]
- **Spread realization:** his account describes both buying shares and selling beans, not an unhedged long-cocoa thesis. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: a Graham workout with a concrete conversion mechanism. The `merger_arb` schema bucket includes non-merger corporate-event arbitrage; this was not a takeover. [1]

## Public discussion
No authenticated 1954 press/tender circular was recovered. The Jay Pritzker account and subsequent stock appreciation in Buffett's letter are retrospective context, not contemporary crowd sentiment. [1]
