## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**1999 letter, written March 2000:** Shareholders had suggested repurchases. The price-trigger discussion concerns shares “recently” below $45,000, not a verified transaction during calendar 1999. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
Repurchases require price “below its intrinsic value, conservatively-calculated.” “We will never make purchases with the intention of stemming a decline in Berkshire's price.” [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Considered repurchases but delayed until shareholders could review the annual report. The letter described possible purchase mechanics; authorization/willingness is not execution. [1]

## What happened after
No actual 1999–2000 repurchase is established here. Do not backdate a later formal program into this episode. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Value:** Conservative discount is necessary. [1]
- **Capital:** Compare repurchase with other uses and liquidity needs. [1]
- **Fairness:** Inform holders before potentially buying their shares. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: buybacks are conditional capital allocation, not a dividend substitute or mechanical response to a falling quotation. [1]

## Public discussion
Shareholder requests are documented in the letter; independently dated market commentary remains a gap. [1]
