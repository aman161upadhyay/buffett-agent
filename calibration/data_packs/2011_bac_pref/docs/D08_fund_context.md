The approximate year-end 2010 Berkshire cash baseline is $38.2 billion. Its insurance structure can supply patient funds, but liquidity is not unlimited and preferred capital cannot be assumed redeemable on demand. The new mode concerns a bespoke preferred investment rather than an assertion that Berkshire has never held the bank's common shares. No proposed allocation or accepted financing terms are disclosed in this document. [Source above.]

The 2010 letter reports $65.832 billion insurance float. That pool supports investment assets but is not incremental shareholder NAV to be added to cash. Coca-Cola, American Express and Wells Fargo are established equity franchises, and controlled rail and energy operations offer other capital-deployment channels. A complete current equity-portfolio concentration table was not recovered. [Fund baseline cited above.]

Technical context: a financial institution's earning assets are supported by deposits, wholesale funding, creditors and equity with different seniority. Regulatory capital cannot all be distributed to investors. Analyze credit quality, liquidity and capital separately: a solvent institution can still face a funding run, and liquid assets do not prove all credit losses are recognized. For a preferred claim, identify whether dividends are cumulative, the issuer's redemption rights and the creditor cushion ahead of the security. For common, normalize provisioning and assess return on tangible equity rather than using industrial free-cash-flow conventions. Reported book value, tangible common capital and market capitalization are different denominators. [Source above; analytical framework rather than additional issuer measurements.]

Allocation discipline: Berkshire is an operating and insurance company, not a redeemable investment partnership. The metadata liquidity figure is a dated scale reference, not contemporaneous discretionary NAV. Cash supports insurance, subsidiaries and contractual obligations before any new allocation. Equity-portfolio weight, percentage of issuer ownership and percentage of cash are different sizing measures. An existing position's basis affects tax but does not determine current intrinsic value. Distinguish a deputy's mandate from a Buffett-scale commitment, and avoid assigning a named decision maker without evidence. This context does not disclose the action being evaluated; the analyst must weigh opportunity cost and the durability of the underlying business independently.

Evidence boundary: missing figures are not available in contemporaneous sources recovered for this digest. Analytical questions are not additional reported facts or claims about the investor's private thinking. Read the financial, risk and fund-context documents together; no individual accounting ratio supplies a complete investment conclusion.
