## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**2002:** Distressed markets offered unusual bond prices. Berkshire's sixfold expansion was concentrated underwriting, not an assumption that all high-yield securities were bargains. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
The category is not called junk without reason; by the 2003 letter it was “decidedly unattractive.” These are **following-year assessments** of changing prices, not a permanent asset-class allocation. [1–2]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Expanded commitments to $8.3 billion in 2002. Level 3 and Williams have separate transaction files; do not sum them again as independent additions to an already aggregated book. [1]

## What happened after
The following year's price recovery reduced expected returns. Individual defaults and exact aggregate realized results remain unverified. [2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Price:** Weak business quality can be offset by sufficiently favorable security economics. [1]
- **Selectivity:** Not a junk-index purchase thesis. [1]
- **Discipline:** Declining prospective returns stop deployment. [2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: flexibility across the capital structure while preserving a margin-of-safety requirement. [1–2]

## Public discussion
Independent contemporary credit-market dissent not recovered. [1–2]
