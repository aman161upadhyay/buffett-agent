Title: Subscribers and ARPU rise together
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/71691/000007169125000117/pressrelease06302025.htm ; https://s23.q4cdn.com/152113917/files/doc_events/2025/Apr/30/The-New-York-Times-Company-2024-Annual-Report.pdf
Date: 2025-09-30

On August 6 the company reported approximately 230,000 net digital-only additions in the quarter and 1.08 million over the prior-year quarter. ARPU increased from $9.34 to $9.64, with subscribers moving out of promotional offers and price increases for some tenured customers. Digital subscription revenue rose 15.1%; digital advertising 18.7%. This combination is more informative than subscriber growth alone because it tests monetization alongside audience scale. [1]

Print subscriptions fell 2.8% to $131.1 million while print advertising was nearly flat. The growing businesses therefore still have to offset pressure on an older platform. Management also expects to change internal segment reporting; future comparability should not be assumed automatic. The current release reports both GAAP and adjusted EPS, with a material difference. No third-quarter result is public at the September cutoff. The operating evidence shows successful execution so far, but does not establish an infinite pool of profitable subscribers or a fixed lifetime value for every new account. [1]

Evidence ledger: annual revenue was $2.308321 billion in 2022, $2.426152 billion in 2023 and $2.585919 billion in 2024. Second-quarter revenue of $685.9 million grew 9.7%; operating profit was $106.6 million, up 34.2%, and adjusted operating profit $133.8 million. Diluted EPS was $0.50, or $0.58 adjusted. Total subscribers were 11.88 million, digital-only 11.30 million, and bundle/multiproduct 6.02 million. Digital-only ARPU was $9.64, up 3.2%. Digital subscription revenue was $350.4 million, total subscription revenue $481.4 million, digital advertising $94.4 million and total advertising $134.0 million. Affiliate/licensing/other revenue was $70.5 million. Marketing costs were $69.2 million; journalism and subscriber-servicing spending remain real operating costs. [1,2]

Interpretation: the issuer's latest released quarter is the information boundary, not the quarter containing this assessment date. Forward guidance is management's expectation, not a realized result. Growth percentages must be matched to the stated comparison period and accounting basis. Where adjusted measures differ from GAAP, both are retained rather than mixing the favorable numerator from one with the denominator from the other. A favorable quarter does not settle the durability question; the operational causes and cash conversion still require scrutiny.
