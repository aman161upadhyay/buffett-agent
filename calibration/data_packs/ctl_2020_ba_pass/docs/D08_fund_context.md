Date: 2020-03-20

Berkshire's 2019 annual report shows approximately $128 billion of cash, cash equivalents and Treasury bills, the metadata denominator. It also owns Precision Castparts and positions in major airlines, so an airframe rescue would add correlated aviation exposure rather than diversify it away. Insurance claims and operating-company liquidity require a substantial reserve. The permanent-capital advantage permits patience but not an assumption that any price decline is a bargain. The report's equity portfolio is approximately $248 billion. [S3]

### Dated operating baseline

Full-year revenue was $76.559 billion, down 24% from $101.127 billion. Net loss was $636 million versus $10.460 billion of profit; GAAP EPS was negative $1.12 versus positive $17.85. Operating cash flow was negative $2.446 billion versus positive $15.322 billion. Capital expenditure was $1.834 billion, making company-defined free cash flow negative $4.280 billion versus positive $13.600 billion. Cash and marketable securities totaled $10.0 billion at year-end; consolidated debt was $27.3 billion. Commercial deliveries fell to 380 from 806. Company backlog was $463 billion, including over 5,400 commercial airplanes. The company anticipated approximately $4 billion of abnormal production costs, primarily during the current year, from suspension and gradual resumption of MAX production. Those estimates precede the full demand effect of pandemic restrictions. Fourth-quarter dividends alone consumed $1.2 billion despite negative operating cash flow. [S1]

### Underwriting distinction

The denominator is capital available to Berkshire, not the issuer's enterprise value or an outside asset manager's client AUM. Insurance float carries obligations; reported cash is not all surplus. A new security should be compared with existing portfolio opportunities, retaining liquidity for operating subsidiaries and negotiated whole-business purchases. No leverage at the parent is assumed to make the proposal fit. The question is an incremental allocation within a large permanent-capital enterprise. [Analytical interpretation of S3]

For a public stock, a deputy-scale position and a Buffett-scale position need different sizing even when both are plausible investments. For a negotiated financing, the relevant cash commitment includes follow-on needs rather than only the opening check. No future trade, retrospective explanation or confidential public-equity holding is supplied as a recommendation. The issuer's current operating scale below helps compare the proposed exposure with the available fund capital. [Analytical interpretation of S1,S3]
