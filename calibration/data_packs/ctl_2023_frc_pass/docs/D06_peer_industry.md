Silicon Valley Bank and Signature's closures create a relevant confidence backdrop, but client mix and lending assets must be examined rather than treating all regional banks as interchangeable. Large diversified banks have broader funding and can support peers while also attracting their deposits. A stockholder's claim is subordinate to depositors, central-bank and other secured creditors. Banking risk should be compared using deposit concentration, asset duration, available collateral and post-stress margins rather than only nonperforming-loan ratios. [S1,S2; analytical comparison]

The bank specializes in relationship banking and wealth management for affluent clients, combining mortgages, deposits and advisory services. Low loan losses and referrals can support high customer lifetime value. The affirmative comparison is a service franchise suffering a generalized regional-bank confidence shock rather than an institution with demonstrated widespread borrower defaults. A recapitalization might preserve the relationships while replacing flight-prone funding, if the economics of the new funding are acceptable. [S1; analyst interpretation]

### Dated operating baseline

Full-year revenue was $5.9 billion, up 16.5%; net interest income $4.8 billion, up 17.5%; and net income approximately $1.7 billion, up 12.7%. Diluted EPS was $8.25, up 7.4%. Common book value was $75.38 and tangible common book value $74.19 per share. Loans were $166.9 billion, up 23.6%, while deposits were $176.4 billion, up 12.9%. Wealth-management assets were $271.2 billion; they belong to clients and are not bank liquidity. Wealth revenues were $877 million. Tier 1 leverage ratio was 8.51%. Reported nonperforming assets were just five basis points of total assets, and annual net charge-offs $2.9 million. Fourth-quarter net interest margin fell to 2.45% from 2.71% in the preceding quarter. Fourth-quarter EPS was $1.88 and net income $386 million, down 3.6% year on year. Historical credit quality and current funding resilience are different questions. [S1]

### Underwriting distinction

Comparable-company analysis is useful only after matching the economic claims. A supplier, a platform, a lender and a brand owner can all report revenue growth while requiring very different reinvestment and balance sheets. Growth funded by customers is different from growth funded by new common equity or acquisition debt. Market-share evidence should therefore be read alongside retention, pricing, capital requirements and the company's ability to retain incremental profit. [Analytical interpretation of S1]

No peer multiple is invented where a same-date, same-definition series is missing. The more useful comparison in that case is structural: who owns the customer, who can raise price, who must invest to preserve competitiveness, and who bears a sudden liquidity demand. The company-specific figures below supply the scale and margin evidence. A sector label is not a substitute for those economics. [Analytical interpretation of S1]
