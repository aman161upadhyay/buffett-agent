Title: Berkshire capital, mandate and opportunity cost
Source: https://digitalassets.tesla.com/tesla-contents/image/upload/IR/Q4_15_Tesla_Update_Letter; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1318605/000119312516457674/d124215d8k.htm; https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/2014ar/2014ar.pdf
Date: 2016-02-11

The last full annual cash benchmark used here is Berkshire's $63.27 billion at year-end 2014, explicitly stale; the year-end 2015 report has not yet been released. The corresponding equity portfolio was approximately $117.47 billion. Berkshire owns a prior interest in BYD, but an investment in a different company with different operating economics does not establish the value of this security. A smaller speculative consumer/technology proposal needs deputy attribution and sizing, while a Buffett-scale commitment needs predictable owner earnings. [S3]

### Dated operating baseline

The February 10 shareholder update reports more than 107,000 vehicles on roads in 42 countries and almost two billion cumulative miles driven. Fourth-quarter deliveries grew more than 76% year on year. Global Model S orders grew more than 35%. Fourth-quarter 'core operational cash flow' was $179 million, constructed from $30 million of GAAP cash used in operations plus $209 million received from sales to leasing partners. It is not positive GAAP operating cash flow. Management planned 60–80% vehicle-sales growth, approximately $1.5 billion of capital investment and full-year non-GAAP profitability for the current year; modest GAAP profitability was forecast only for the fourth quarter. The letter targeted Model S gross margin near 30% by year-end. A February 9 amendment increased asset-based credit commitments from $750 million to $1 billion. Model X production had been limited in January for quality reasons. [S1,S2]

### Underwriting distinction

The denominator is capital available to Berkshire, not the issuer's enterprise value or an outside asset manager's client AUM. Insurance float carries obligations; reported cash is not all surplus. A new security should be compared with existing portfolio opportunities, retaining liquidity for operating subsidiaries and negotiated whole-business purchases. No leverage at the parent is assumed to make the proposal fit. The question is an incremental allocation within a large permanent-capital enterprise. [Analytical interpretation of S3]

For a public stock, a deputy-scale position and a Buffett-scale position need different sizing even when both are plausible investments. For a negotiated financing, the relevant cash commitment includes follow-on needs rather than only the opening check. No future trade, retrospective explanation or confidential public-equity holding is supplied as a recommendation. The issuer's current operating scale below helps compare the proposed exposure with the available fund capital. [Analytical interpretation of S1,S3]
