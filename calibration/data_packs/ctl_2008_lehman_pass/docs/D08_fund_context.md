Title: Berkshire capital, mandate and opportunity cost
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/806085/000110465908018182/a08-8353_1ex99d1.htm; https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/2007ar/2007ar.pdf
Date: 2008-03-28

Berkshire's 2007 annual report reports $44.329 billion of cash and cash equivalents and roughly $74.999 billion of equity securities. The cash figure is the metadata denominator, not total corporate assets or client AUM. Existing large financial exposures include American Express and Wells Fargo; General Re also makes derivative complexity familiar. Marmon's initial controlling acquisition consumes capital. The mandate can consider a negotiated security, but no executable preferred terms are supplied here. [S3]

### Dated operating baseline

The March 18 release reports first-quarter net revenue of $3.5 billion, down 31%; net income of $489 million, down 57%; and diluted EPS of $0.81 versus $1.96. Negative mark-to-market adjustments were $1.8 billion, net of hedges and certain debt-liability gains. Capital Markets revenue was $1.7 billion; fixed income contributed only $262 million, down 88%, while equities contributed $1.4 billion, up 6%. Investment Banking revenue was $867 million, including $330 million of advisory fees. Investment Management revenue reached $968 million and client assets under management were $277 billion. These client assets are not Lehman's own liquidity. Reported common book value was $39.45 per share; total stockholders' equity was $24.8 billion. Holding-company liquidity was $34 billion, with $64 billion of other unencumbered assets; regulated entities held another $99 billion of unencumbered assets. Quarterly ROE was 8.6%, versus 24.4% a year earlier; pre-tax margin was 18.9%. Compensation absorbed 52.5% of net revenues. [S1]

### Underwriting distinction

The denominator is capital available to Berkshire, not the issuer's enterprise value or an outside asset manager's client AUM. Insurance float carries obligations; reported cash is not all surplus. A new security should be compared with existing portfolio opportunities, retaining liquidity for operating subsidiaries and negotiated whole-business purchases. No leverage at the parent is assumed to make the proposal fit. The question is an incremental allocation within a large permanent-capital enterprise. [Analytical interpretation of S3]

For a public stock, a deputy-scale position and a Buffett-scale position need different sizing even when both are plausible investments. For a negotiated financing, the relevant cash commitment includes follow-on needs rather than only the opening check. No future trade, retrospective explanation or confidential public-equity holding is supplied as a recommendation. The issuer's current operating scale below helps compare the proposed exposure with the available fund capital. [Analytical interpretation of S1,S3]
