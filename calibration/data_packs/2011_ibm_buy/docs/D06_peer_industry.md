The explicit labels matter: IBM pretax margin includes financing and non-operating items; Oracle's displayed margin is a single quarter, not its fiscal-year margin. Accenture net revenue excludes reimbursements. These are business-model contrasts, not an assertion that the same multiple should apply to every dollar of revenue.

Hewlett-Packard, Oracle and Accenture supply useful but imperfect comparisons across hardware, software and services. Pure software margins should not be applied to a mixed systems and labor-intensive services business. Compare recurring support revenue, contract renewal behavior, operating cash conversion, research spending and customer migration costs. The appropriate comparison is the role in enterprise IT, not the broad label technology. [Source above.]

Technical context: enterprise technology can contain long contractual relationships and recurring maintenance but still require persistent innovation. Customer switching costs are economic only if the supplier keeps delivering acceptable value. Distinguish backlog from cash, software licenses from support, and services revenue from labor-adjusted profit. Financing receivables can distort industrial cash-flow comparisons. Research expenditure, acquisitions, pension contributions and stock compensation belong in a conservative assessment of owner economics. A share repurchase increases continuing ownership only at a cost, and is beneficial only relative to the value acquired. Per-share growth produced by fewer shares must be reconciled with total earnings and capital spent. [Source above; analytical framework.]

Comparison discipline: compare businesses on the source of their cash and the capital required to earn it, rather than treating the nearest ticker as an exact substitute. Normalize financing and accounting differences before interpreting relative multiples. A peer can have a better balance sheet but a worse franchise, or a better franchise at an unattractive price. Relative cheapness alone cannot prove absolute value. Consider the opportunity cost of adding to an existing understandable holding, retaining liquidity, owning an entire business or buying the investor's own shares. Where a numerical peer table was not recovered, keep the comparison qualitative rather than inventing an industry average.

Evidence boundary: missing figures are not available in contemporaneous sources recovered for this digest. Analytical questions are not additional reported facts or claims about the investor's private thinking. Read the financial, risk and fund-context documents together; no individual accounting ratio supplies a complete investment conclusion.
