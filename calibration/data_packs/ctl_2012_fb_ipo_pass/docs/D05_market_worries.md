Title: The strongest contemporaneous bear case
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000119312512240111/d287954d424b4.htm
Date: 2012-05-17

The prospectus warns that mobile use grows faster than successful mobile monetization. More engagement does not necessarily mean proportionate advertising revenue. First-quarter profit fell despite substantial revenue growth. User preferences, privacy restrictions, competitors and distribution controlled by other platforms can affect the franchise. Dual-class voting prevents an ordinary minority buyer from controlling capital allocation. At an offering chosen and marketed by knowledgeable sellers, business quality and a fair buyer price are separate questions. [S1; analytical inference]

The contemporaneous offer price is $38, not a public-market close: trading has not begun by the cutoff. The implied approximately $104 billion fully diluted equity value quoted with the offering is roughly 104 times 2011 net income, a 0.96% historical earnings yield; share-count definitions mean this is an approximate capitalization comparison, not audited enterprise value. Gross IPO proceeds are not all retained corporate cash. A conservative valuation must distinguish existing franchise earnings from future mobile growth and subtract continuing infrastructure investment and economic employee compensation. [S1; calculations]

### Dated operating baseline

The May 17 prospectus reports 2011 revenue of $3.711 billion and net income of $1.000 billion; 2010 revenue was $1.974 billion and net income $606 million. First-quarter revenue was $1.058 billion, versus $731 million, while net income was $205 million versus $233 million. Monthly active users reached 901 million at March 31 and daily active users averaged 526 million in March. Mobile monthly active users numbered 488 million. Advertising supplied approximately 85% of 2011 revenue; payments and other fees supplied the remainder. The offer consists of 421,233,615 Class A shares at $38, including 180 million newly issued shares and 241,233,615 sold by existing holders. Gross offering size is $16.007 billion; most shares sold are secondary, not new cash for the company. Class A has one vote per share and Class B ten. Zuckerberg is expected to control approximately 55.9% of votes after the offering. [S1]

### Underwriting distinction

This objection is intended to be capable of defeating the affirmative case. Distinguish a reversible mark-to-market loss from permanent impairment, and a temporary earnings interruption from a permanently more capital-hungry business. The investor must survive the adverse scenario without relying on the next buyer, a friendly regulator or unrestricted external financing. A long holding period cannot restore capital that has been diluted away or consumed by obligations senior to the purchased security. [Analytical interpretation of S1]

Observable thesis-breakers include customer economics deteriorating despite continued spending, new funding coming only on punitive terms, or reported cash failing to reconcile to recurring obligations. These are analytical monitoring conditions, not claims that such outcomes have occurred. The record below supplies the current scale of exposure against which to test them. No later failure, recovery or management admission is used to strengthen the bear case. [Analytical interpretation of S1]
