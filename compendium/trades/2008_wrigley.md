## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**2008:** Mars needed financing for Wrigley. Contract commitment and actual funding were separate events; exact dates and debt/preferred split require deal documents. [1–2]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
The Wrigley/Goldman/GE securities carried “high current yields” making them satisfactory before “equity participation as a bonus.” **February 2009 explanation of the group.** [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Committed and supplied $6.5 billion toward the Wrigley purchase while financial panic disrupted other deals. This is not Berkshire buying all Mars or Wrigley common equity. [2]

## What happened after
The 2009 letter includes Wrigley among five nontraded positions with significant income/equity potential. Group income is not Wrigley-only return. [2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Income:** Underwrite the fixed-income leg independently. [1]
- **Upside:** Equity participation supplements, not rescues, an inadequate coupon. [1]
- **Certainty:** Berkshire funded a commitment amid panic. [2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: monetizing scarce reliable capital without surrendering liquidity discipline. [1–2]

## Public discussion
Independent original merger-financing debate not recovered. [1–2]
