## Cutoff evidence — reconstructed

Intel, software vendors and Internet-related companies participate in different parts of the computing ecosystem. A product can be ubiquitous without offering the same cash-flow predictability as a consumer staple. Low reproduction cost makes software margins attractive, but development, compatibility and distribution advantages must continually be defended. No matched contemporary peer valuation table is recovered; the qualitative comparison concerns the durability and comprehensibility of competitive advantage rather than a generic technology discount. Existing installed base is evidence, not a guarantee. A comparison with established consumer businesses should test how much of the return requires forecasting changes in technology rather than assuming all high-return businesses have identical risk.

## Economic reading of the supplied facts

Industry economics: software involves substantial development and selling expense but little physical manufacturing cost for another copy. Compatibility, application availability and user familiarity can support a platform. Those advantages are not guarantees: a change in computing architecture or distribution can alter which platform controls demand. Reported margins must also be considered alongside stock compensation and the cost of sustaining a development organization. Customer adoption is evidence of current usefulness, not a complete forecast of profits a decade away. Legal limits on business practices can affect the means by which a platform defends its position. A business can be excellent for customers and employees while its shares still require a careful estimate of future cash flows and purchase price.

Comparison discipline: differences in accounting, capital intensity and financial leverage can make two nominally similar earnings multiples incomparable. A high return on common equity can come from a strong operating franchise or merely from a thin equity base. The comparison that matters is the durable cash return on all necessary capital, followed by the division of that return among the different claimants.

An outside shareholder cannot assume control benefits or private acquisition synergies. A private buyer's price is useful evidence only if the assets, liabilities and ownership rights are comparable. The alternatives also include retaining liquid funds rather than purchasing a mediocre security merely because it is cheaper than an expensive peer. No industry-average multiple or precise competitor ranking is invented here. The qualitative comparisons identify which operating variables need examination; the absence of a recovered comparable-company table is a genuine limit on numerical relative valuation.
