# 2024 Berkshire annual letter

**Information date:** 2025-02-22 (month-only where precise public release day was not independently established). **Era:** D. **Author:** Buffett. **Primary source:** [letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2024ltr.pdf). All rules below cite sections of this source; section names are used rather than fabricated PDF page numbers. Annual-period labels are not publication dates.

## Process rules and decision explanations

- Mistakes—Yes, We Make Them: economic and personnel errors require action; delay is the cardinal sin. This is a process explanation, not a claim every disclosed sale is an error correction.
- Where Your Money Is: extraordinary cash does not abandon the preference for productive businesses. Equity sales and price-dependent repurchases require separate explanations.
- Last Year’s Performance: BHE minority interests were purchased; the consideration and control perimeter should be recorded, not inferred from an equity-portfolio filing.
- Berkshire Increases its Japanese Investments: patient ownership, issuer capital allocation and yen financing explain the exception to a simplistic US-only/no-borrowing rule.
- Surprise, Surprise: Berkshire textile control is again labeled a mistake. Tax payments are discussed; the 2024 meeting, not this later reflection alone, supplies the contemporaneous Apple tax rationale.

## Verbatim evidence anchors

- **Surprise, Surprise! An Important American Record is Smashed; Purchase of Berkshire Hathaway** “Though the price I paid for Berkshire looked cheap, its business – a large northern textile operation – was headed for extinction.”
- **Last Year’s Performance; Purchase of additional interest in BHE** “Late in the year we increased our ownership of the utility operation from about 92% to 100% at a cost of roughly $3.9 billion”
- **Berkshire Increases its Japanese Investments; Purchase of Japanese company shares** “It’s been almost six years since Berkshire began purchasing shares in five Japanese companies that very successfully operate in a manner somewhat similar to Berkshire itself.”
- **Surprise, Surprise! An Important American Record is Smashed; Issuance of cash dividend in 1967** “I can’t remember why I suggested this action to Berkshire’s board of directors. Now it seems like a bad dream.”

## Attribution and point-in-time boundary

Buffett is the source author, but an acquired subsidiary’s bolt-on or operating action is not automatically his personally originated investment. Individual decider unknown where not identified. Historical narratives are available only from this information date. A section discussing earlier purchases is retrospective; do not load its later performance, confession or corrected valuation into the original entry replay.
