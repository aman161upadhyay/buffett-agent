## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**1977:** marketable minority interests in attractive businesses sold below prices for equivalent wholly owned properties. Berkshire invested $10.9 million in Cap Cities during that year. Earnings attributable to the shares totaled about $1.3 million for 1977, but only $40,000 current annual cash dividends entered reported operating earnings. Full-year earnings are post-purchase disclosure, not proof of what was known before every purchase. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“Capital Cities possesses both extraordinary properties and extraordinary management.” **Post-purchase**, March 14, 1978. [1]

“In effect, we can obtain a better management result through non-control than control.” Same passage: direct property acquisition would cost about twice as much and would not let Buffett improve on the existing management. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Bought during 1977, ending the year with 220,000 shares at $10.909 million cost and $13.228 million market. Later sold during 1978–1980 at approximately $43/share according to his 1985 letter. Exact lot-level execution dates, split reconciliation and realized profit are unrecovered; don't derive a false total loss by comparing differently dated share prices. [1–2]

## What happened after
Buffett explicitly joked about his inability to reconcile that sale with committing in 1985 to buy at $172.50 in the ABC transaction. The later block is a **new investment**, not uninterrupted ownership. The earlier sale rationale itself remains unknown; subsequent regret supplies an opportunity-cost lesson but not a recovered original sell memo. [2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Properties:** high-quality media assets were specifically named. [1]
- **Managers:** both operations and corporate capital deployment were praised. [1]
- **Price:** roughly half estimated direct-property cost was the stated comparative valuation. [1]
- **Non-control:** superior incumbent management made a minority stake a benefit, not a defect. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: a particularly clear bridge from controlled asset bargains toward minority positions in great operators. The unexplained early sale is a break, and should stay unexplained rather than be rationalized after the fact. [1–2]

## Public discussion
Original 1977 press discussion and the contemporaneous sale explanation remain gaps. The 1977 shareholder letter itself is a public pitch for look-through business ownership rather than short-term quotation prediction. [1]
