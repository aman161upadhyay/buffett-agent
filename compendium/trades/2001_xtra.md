## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**2000 approach / 2001 acquisition:** Tiger Fund's wind-down prompted Buffett to contact Julian Robertson about XTRA. A discussion starting in 2000 does not make this a completed 2000 acquisition. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“Trailer leasing is a cyclical business but one in which we should earn decent returns over time.” **February 2002 explanation:** Lew Rubin added a new skill to Berkshire, which hoped to expand in leasing. Exact valuation model remains a gap. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
The tender offer expired September 11 and contained a market-closure withdrawal right. Despite the attacks and market shutdown, Berkshire completed. This is evidence of financing/commitment reliability, not a claim that contractual termination rights never matter. [1]

Bought XTRA in 2001 for cash. Exact first agreement and close dates unknown here. [1]

## What happened after
No standalone realized return established. Leasing remains cyclical; this is not an assertion of permanently stable utilization. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Position:** Leading truck-trailer lessor. [1]
- **Cycle:** Evaluate over time, not extrapolating peak utilization. [1]
- **People:** Confidence in operating management. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: a capital-intensive cyclical can qualify at appropriate economics, contrary to a simplistic asset-light-only rule. [1]

## Public discussion
The fund-liquidation origin is documented by Buffett; independent contemporary commentary not recovered. [1]
