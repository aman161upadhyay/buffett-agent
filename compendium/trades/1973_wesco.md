## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**1973 requested control episode:** Wesco was a financial holding company associated with a savings-and-loan business. Exact first purchase date, takeover chronology and original Buffett–Munger correspondence were not recovered. The 1978 Blue Chip report states acquisition at a discount to book and accounts for amortization of that discount. This is retrospective purchase-accounting evidence, not proof of a fully reconstructed 1973 valuation model. [1,4]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
**Original quotation gap:** no verified Buffett quote about the **1973** purchase was recovered. The 2011 prospectus explains a different, minority-buyout decision using book value and simplification of the structure; it must not be transplanted into the 1973 thesis. Munger's role is material and individual decision attribution remains `unknown`, rather than assigning him or Buffett sole authorship from control alone. [1,4]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Blue Chip accumulated control. The SEC's August 12, 1974 digest reports an August 2 tender for 150,000 shares at $14 and says Blue Chip **already owned 58%**. Blue Chip's 1978 report shows approximately 80% ownership. In 1983 Blue Chip itself became wholly owned by Berkshire; in February 2011 Berkshire and Wesco announced agreement to acquire the remaining 19.9%. These are distinct ownership stages. [1–3]

## What happened after
Wesco became a controlled capital-allocation platform rather than merely an arbitrage security. The later simplification ended the public minority structure; complete lifetime cost/earnings/stock consideration reconciliation is not reconstructed. A later filing's compressed historical ownership language should not override the contemporaneous 1974 evidence that ownership was then only 58%. [1–4]
