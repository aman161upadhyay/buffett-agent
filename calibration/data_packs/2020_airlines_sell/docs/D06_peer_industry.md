Primary filing URLs are given in D02. All margins are calculated from reported operating income rather than adjusted earnings. Revenue sums to $158.462bn (calculated); this is a scale measure, not an estimate of a merged airline's economics. Differences in international exposure, business travel, stage length, labor arrangements and lease obligations mean the same demand shock need not translate into the same cash burn. Historical shares also reflect repurchases, not current access to financing.

Within the four-carrier basket, domestic versus international mix, liquidity and labor structures differ, but all face the same collapse in passenger confidence. Compare the shared industry shock with business-specific disruptions that leave customer demand intact. A portfolio of four airlines is still concentrated in travel. The appropriate opportunity-cost comparison includes retaining liquid government instruments without needing to forecast the exact reopening date. [Source above.]

Technical context: airline economics have high fixed and semi-fixed costs and perishable capacity. Load factor, fare, ancillary revenue and unit cost should be read together; fuller aircraft can still produce weak returns if prices fall. Aircraft leases, pension liabilities and committed fleet purchases belong in the capital-risk assessment. Low fuel prices improve a period's profit but may induce competitive fare cuts. A four-company basket reduces single-carrier concentration but retains shared exposure to travel demand, fuel, labor and credit availability. Earnings before aircraft ownership costs can exaggerate cash available to common owners. [Source above; analytical framework, not an aggregate forecast.]

Comparison discipline: compare businesses on the source of their cash and the capital required to earn it, rather than treating the nearest ticker as an exact substitute. Normalize financing and accounting differences before interpreting relative multiples. A peer can have a better balance sheet but a worse franchise, or a better franchise at an unattractive price. Relative cheapness alone cannot prove absolute value. Consider the opportunity cost of adding to an existing understandable holding, retaining liquidity, owning an entire business or buying the investor's own shares. Where a numerical peer table was not recovered, keep the comparison qualitative rather than inventing an industry average.

Evidence boundary: missing figures are not available in contemporaneous sources recovered for this digest. Analytical questions are not additional reported facts or claims about the investor's private thinking. Read the financial, risk and fund-context documents together; no individual accounting ratio supplies a complete investment conclusion.
