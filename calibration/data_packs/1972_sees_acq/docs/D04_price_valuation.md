## Cutoff evidence — reconstructed

The contemplated purchase price is $25 million for the business, reconstructed from the transaction history. Against rounded $4–4.2 million pretax earnings that is approximately 6.0–6.25 times pretax earnings, a 16–16.8% pretax earnings yield before purchase adjustments. It is approximately 3.1–3.6 times the rounded $7–8 million tangible-capital range. These are analyst calculations on reconstructed figures, not an original valuation model. Paying above book value requires economic goodwill: customers must produce earnings in excess of the return available on commodity assets. No value is assigned to nationwide expansion, to an unverified amount of excess cash, or to a particular future selling price per pound.

## Economic reading of the supplied facts

Industry economics: boxed chocolates are purchased both for consumption and as gifts. The purchaser's concern with the recipient's reaction can make dependable quality and an established local name important. That does not eliminate substitution by other sweets or gifts. The retailer must carry inventory, staff shops and maintain product freshness even when demand is concentrated in holidays. Profits depend on realized price per pound after ingredients, labor, occupancy and selling costs. A premium brand has value only if the realized premium exceeds the extra cost of sustaining quality. Regional recognition cannot be assumed to transfer automatically to a new city. These are business-model implications, not an assumption of national expansion or a promised price increase.

Valuation interpretation: the relevant asset is the stream of cash that can be withdrawn over the life of the business without weakening its competitive position. A low multiple is informative only when the earnings denominator is sustainable. A high return on tangible capital can justify a premium to book value, but does not justify an unlimited purchase price.

No closing quotation is inferred from a subsequent transaction average or from the low for a later month. If a dated market price is unavailable, a precise market capitalization, initial earnings yield and implied return are unavailable as well. A negotiated security can instead be evaluated from its proposed cash payments and priority, subject to credit risk. The adverse case should reduce sustainable earnings and allow for additional capital needs rather than simply apply a smaller multiple to optimistic earnings. Interest rates, taxes and reinvestment determine the opportunity cost; no unsupported discount rate or terminal growth estimate is presented as an observed fact.
