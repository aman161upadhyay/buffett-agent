## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**2000:** The Berry family had owned U.S. Liability for 49 years. General Re's Ron Ferguson introduced Buffett to Bob Berry; Tom Nerney managed the insurer. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
Nerney had achieved “a rare combination of excellent growth and unusual profitability.” **February 2001 explanation**, not a public purchase-date forecast. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Agreed by telephone on a half-stock, half-cash transaction for the insurer and its sister companies. Exact closing date and consideration unknown here. [1]

## What happened after
Buffett said the company added to Berkshire's unusually strong collection of property-casualty businesses. No standalone IRR established. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Economics:** Profitable growth rather than volume at any underwriting price. [1]
- **People:** Nerney's record. [1]
- **Niche:** Established unusual-risk insurance operations. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: a negotiated operating-business purchase; the stock component is a real capital-allocation cost despite Berkshire's general preference for cash. [1]

## Public discussion
Independent contemporary acquisition discussion not recovered. [1]
