## Cutoff evidence — reconstructed

The contemplated buyer is Blue Chip Stamps, a separately capitalized company associated with Buffett and Munger; Berkshire is not purchasing 100% of See's directly with its own cash in this snapshot. The distinction matters for the check size and indirect ownership. Trading-stamp obligations must remain supported after any acquisition. Exact cutoff available cash and Blue Chip's attributable common-equity portfolio are not available in contemporaneous sources recovered here; the metadata's zero denominator is a missing-data sentinel. Munger's role in evaluating business quality is part of the deal context, but no proposed allocation or verdict is supplied. Management continuity and a known negotiated price are the available decision inputs.

## Economic reading of the supplied facts

Industry economics: boxed chocolates are purchased both for consumption and as gifts. The purchaser's concern with the recipient's reaction can make dependable quality and an established local name important. That does not eliminate substitution by other sweets or gifts. The retailer must carry inventory, staff shops and maintain product freshness even when demand is concentrated in holidays. Profits depend on realized price per pound after ingredients, labor, occupancy and selling costs. A premium brand has value only if the realized premium exceeds the extra cost of sustaining quality. Regional recognition cannot be assumed to transfer automatically to a new city. These are business-model implications, not an assumption of national expansion or a promised price increase.

Capital-allocation interpretation: a portfolio allocation must fit the legal entity that supplies the money. An insurance subsidiary's invested assets are not all cash available for an acquisition. Premiums collected before claims are paid provide investment funds but also create obligations. Consolidated equity, publicly traded holdings, cash and insurance float are different denominators.

No target weight follows mechanically from the fund-size reference. Available cash at this exact cutoff, where unrecovered, remains unknown rather than being equated with the reference figure. Existing concentrated holdings create opportunity costs; selling them may incur tax and surrender durable earning power. A commitment involving stock also transfers a continuing fraction of the buyer's future economics. The investor has no obligation to purchase simply to remain fully invested. Conversely, waiting has a cost when a sound business is offered below conservative value. This context describes available choices and constraints without specifying an allocation or revealing a decision.
