## Resource base and cost position (FY2021 10-K, filed 2022-02-24; Q4 2021 call, 2022-02-25)
The company is the largest acreage holder in the Permian Basin (about 2.8 million net acres, roughly 1.4 million in the Delaware Basin from the 2019 Anadarko purchase) and produced 1.17 million barrels of oil equivalent per day in 2021 (about 1.14 MMboe/d guided for 2022), of which roughly 490 thousand boe/d came from the Permian, with the balance from the Rockies/DJ Basin, the Gulf of Mexico, Oman, Algeria and the UAE. Management states that total company cash flow breaks even at about $40 WTI including sustaining capital and the common dividend, that 2022 sustaining capital is about $3.9 billion, and that Permian well costs fell about 30% from 2019 to 2021 (drilling days per well down ~40%). Proved reserves were 3.5 billion boe at year-end 2021 (reserve-replacement ratio above 100%). Capital-allocation priorities stated on the Q4 2021 call, in order: (1) reduce debt toward $20 billion net (debt fell from $36 billion at the Anadarko close to $29 billion at year-end 2021, with $6.7 billion repaid in 2021), (2) a $3 billion share-repurchase authorisation and a dividend raised from $0.01 to $0.13 per quarter, (3) only modest production growth ("not growing for growth's sake"). The chemicals segment (OxyChem, the largest US merchant producer of chlorine and caustic soda) earned $1.5 billion of pre-tax income in 2021, and the midstream segment includes a ~50% interest in Western Midstream.

Occidental is a US-centered oil and gas producer with chemicals and midstream interests. The common share is residual to debt and the existing preferred capital. Resource quality, operating costs, development needs and capital allocation determine whether higher commodity receipts become per-share owner cash. This is not the same security as a high-coupon preferred or a warrant with a fixed exercise price. [Source above.]

Technical context: petroleum accounting profits depend on realized prices, volumes, royalties, taxes and the treatment of exploration and development costs. A reserve is not cash in the bank: it needs extraction, transport and often replacement investment. Distinguish upstream earnings from refining and chemical margins rather than assuming they move together. For owner earnings, separate maintenance needed to sustain economic production from expansion capital. Check whether reported free cash flow deducts preferred distributions and whether acquisitions are being called capital expenditure. Proved reserves, production and reserve life use different units and assumptions. A conservative analysis should explicitly test a lower realized-price case without claiming to forecast the commodity. Underwriting questions: identify the actual paying customer, repeat-purchase or replacement mechanism, and what prevents a capable competitor from taking the profit. Distinguish an enduring economic advantage from an asset that is merely expensive to build. The ownership claim being evaluated must match the economics described: operating control, common shares and a preferred claim confer different rights. Do not infer honest or capable management solely from a famous name or a long corporate history. Where direct customer-retention or pricing evidence is absent, leave that criterion uncertain rather than supply a generic franchise label. Record what observable deterioration would invalidate the business thesis before considering position size.
