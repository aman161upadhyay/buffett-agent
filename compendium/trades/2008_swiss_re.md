## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**2008–09:** Berkshire held Swiss Re common stock before providing a hybrid capital security. Reinsurance contracts, common equity and the capital instrument must not be merged into a single undifferentiated “preferred” purchase. [1–2]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
The 2009 letter describes attractive income and equity potential across the five nontraded crisis investments. Original Swiss-Re-common purchase rationale remains unknown. [3]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
At December 2008 held 11.262 million common shares, cost $773 million, market $530 million. On March 23, 2009 bought CHF3 billion face/cost of a 12% convertible perpetual capital instrument. [1–2]

## What happened after
The 10-Q specified issuer redemption terms and no mandatory maturity. Exact eventual common-stock and hybrid realized returns not reconstructed. [2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Yield:** 12% on the hybrid, not common dividend yield. [2]
- **Structure:** Conversion and redemption provisions matter. [2]
- **Separation:** Common losses cannot be hidden by reporting only hybrid income. [1–2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: capital-structure-specific crisis underwriting in a familiar industry. [1–3]

## Public discussion
Independent contemporary criticism not recovered. [1–3]
