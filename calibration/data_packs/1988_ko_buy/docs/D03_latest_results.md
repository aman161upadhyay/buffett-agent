## Cutoff evidence — reconstructed

The operating narrative available before the cutoff is a renewed focus under Goizueta and Keough and expansion of the beverage business abroad. No post-September annual result, full-year unit-volume report or subsequent Berkshire investment disclosure is included. Exact latest-quarter revenue, margins and regional unit-case growth have not been independently recovered. A useful analysis separates price, volume, currency and changes in bottling ownership instead of equating all dollar-sales growth with brand progress. International opportunity should be assessed through repeat consumption and distribution capacity. The reconstruction therefore provides the business mechanism and management record while leaving the missing current earnings bridge explicit.

## Economic reading of the supplied facts

Industry economics: a concentrate producer sells a small part of the finished beverage's physical volume but supplies a large part of its consumer identity. Bottlers and retailers supply production, packaging and access to the customer. The division of capital and margin among these participants matters when judging returns. A strong brand can support repeat demand, but advertising, product quality and route-to-market access still require resources. Growth abroad may use a local partner's capital, yet the producer remains exposed to execution, currency and regulation. These are implications of the concentrate-and-bottler model, not an assertion that every incremental dollar of revenue becomes owner cash or that consumption can grow without limit.

Reporting discipline: a full fiscal year's results are not available merely because part of that year has elapsed. The observations above are restricted to the reporting periods identified, and no later quarter is backfilled. An annualized partial period would be a calculation, not a reported result; no such annualization is used unless expressly identified.

For decision purposes, the key question is whether the recent change alters recurring earning power or only its timing. Volume, realized price, operating expenses and capital requirements should be considered separately. A price increase that merely offsets higher input costs is not necessarily an improvement in economics. Similarly, a recovery in accounting profit is not proof that competitive advantage has returned. Where a current operating statistic has not been recovered, this document makes no assertion about its direction. The evidence permits a conditional assessment of durability, not a precise estimate of an unavailable earnings announcement.
