## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**1989:** USAir had a long profitable history and Ed Colodny was a manager Buffett admired. The 1989 letter explicitly admits no ability to forecast airline-industry economics and therefore favors a different security structure from Coke-type common equity. Original entry-date cost comparisons and labor-contract analysis were not recovered. The later postmortem says those omissions were central, not that he correctly foresaw and priced every risk. [1,3]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“Our lack of strong convictions about these businesses, however, means that we must structure our investments in them differently from what we do when we invest in a business appearing to have splendid economic characteristics.” **Post-purchase**, March 2, 1990, discussing investment banking, airlines and paper. [1]

“My analysis of USAir's business was both superficial and wrong.” **Hindsight**, February 28, 1997. Admiration for Colodny, historical profits and apparent senior-security protection distracted Buffett from regulated-era costs exposed to deregulated competition. [3]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Bought $358 million preferred during 1989, 9.25% dividend, conversion at $60 and ten-year mandatory redemption. Negotiated penalty dividends on arrearages. Following dividend suspension in 1994, Berkshire eventually wrote it down 75% to $89.5 million and unsuccessfully tried to sell at substantial discounts. Those attempted sales were not completed exits. [1,3]

## What happened after
USAir lost $2.4 billion in aggregate during 1990–1994, eliminating common book equity. Under Stephen Wolf the business recovered; by the 1996 letter Buffett reported $240.5 million cumulative dividends, including $30 million received during early 1997. The 1997 letter says arrearages were made good during 1997 and a March 15, 1998 preferred call would make the recovered conversion rights valuable. It predicts a decent profit but does not yet provide final realized proceeds. `win` reflects the recovery, **not** endorsement of the initial decision; the failure tag remains essential. [3–4]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Manager:** liking Colodny was real but insufficient. [1,3]
- **History:** past profits were extrapolated without adequately assessing the changed competitive structure. [3]
- **Protection:** seniority and dividend provisions helped but did not eliminate principal risk. [1,3]
- **Option:** favorable common performance was needed for returns beyond a mediocre fixed yield. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: structured yield cannot replace understanding unit economics and labor-cost rigidity. Distinguish **bad process**, temporary markdown and eventual monetary recovery rather than falsely labeling the preferred a permanent total loss. [1–4]

## Public discussion
Original deal documents and independent 1989 airline-cost critiques remain gaps. Buffett's later critique is unusually specific; it must be labeled hindsight and not inserted into the original thesis as if he had foreseen it. [3]
