# Fifteen untuned decisions: static predictions, not replay results

## What is being predicted

These decisions are not calibration/hold-out keys. “Untuned” does **not** mean unseen by the model or absent from the prompts: Gillette, Champion, Alleghany, Freddie and others are already examples. A newly sealed evaluation must use additional issuers. The predictions below concern the **staged chain**, with existing-position cases routed directly to prompt 08 (`tools/run_agent.py`). It does not load AGENT.md in that mode.

No numerical entry prices, solvency facts or private terms are fabricated. A hypothetical point-in-time pack would contain only the dossier's recoverable decision-time information; later quotes establish the observed action, not facts the agent may know. Where that produces an information-poor test, the predicted PASS is explicitly evidence-limited. Known negotiated terms are conditional desk inputs, **not** pretended publicly available pre-announcement information.

All bare dossier filenames in the table resolve under `compendium/trades/`; prompt references resolve under `prompts/`. Template labels describe the hypothesized economics and are not claims that Buffett used those labels.

Two requested “passes” are documented decisions to **stop further purchases**, not businesses Berkshire categorically rejected: Wal-Mart and Fannie Mae. The dossiers explicitly document partial ownership. Calling them never-owned passes would be false. They test the real refusal to complete an intended purchase; exact dates/valuation models are not recovered.

## Predicted hit rate and its limits

The point predictions give **7/15 direction-equivalent hits (46.7%)**: Disney, Blue Chip, Gillette, Champion, Exxon, textiles and Alleghany. This is a reviewer forecast, **not measured accuracy or a statistical confidence interval**. A rough judgmental range is 5–10/15: Blue Chip, Exxon, Kraft Heinz and Chubb are especially sensitive to pack completeness and rule precedence. Excluding the two deliberately irrational incremental passes raises the point fraction to 7/13; it does not prove generalization.

The sample is intentionally demanding and not population-representative. Some misses would be **correct abstentions on inadequate information**, not genuine process errors. Conversely, making every dossier a rich reconstructed pack risks inserting later explanations. Measure decision-time evidence sufficiency separately. Neither a successful purchase prediction nor a legitimate PASS establishes causal reasoning fidelity when the actual investor's explanation is unknown.

Coverage: three sell/close decisions (#6–8), two whole-company purchases (#3, #12), two negotiated preferreds (#4–5), two publicly explained refusals to buy more (#9–10), and two deputy-era positions (#14–15). No claim that these omissions were clean never-owned rejection cases.
