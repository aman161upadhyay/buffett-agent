Amazon Redshift, Google's BigQuery, Microsoft's offerings and traditional database vendors address overlapping workloads. A neutral platform's cross-cloud and data-sharing approach may differentiate it, but it rents resources from suppliers with strong bargaining power. Traditional database license revenue is not directly comparable with consumption revenue. The key industry evidence is customer expansion after infrastructure costs, implementation expense and competitive pricing. No standardized September 15 peer owner-earnings multiple exists for this loss-making platform in the source set. [S1; analytical comparison]

The platform separates cloud-data storage and compute, letting enterprises consolidate information, query it and share governed data. It runs on infrastructure supplied by large cloud vendors rather than requiring the customer to own an on-premise warehouse. The affirmative franchise case is that easier scaling and cross-organization data sharing increase customer use, making the product more valuable as data workloads migrate. Consumption-based revenue can align price with use, while making near-term revenue less contractually fixed than an ordinary subscription. [S1; analyst interpretation]

Retention measures the expansion of an existing cohort; it is not consolidated growth or guaranteed contractual revenue. The contemplated primary issuance is 28 million shares, excluding the possible underwriters' option. ### Underwriting distinction

Comparable-company analysis is useful only after matching the economic claims. A supplier, a platform, a lender and a brand owner can all report revenue growth while requiring very different reinvestment and balance sheets. Growth funded by customers is different from growth funded by new common equity or acquisition debt. Market-share evidence should therefore be read alongside retention, pricing, capital requirements and the company's ability to retain incremental profit. [Analytical interpretation of S1]

No peer multiple is invented where a same-date, same-definition series is missing. The more useful comparison in that case is structural: who owns the customer, who can raise price, who must invest to preserve competitiveness, and who bears a sudden liquidity demand. The company-specific figures below supply the scale and margin evidence. A sector label is not a substitute for those economics. [Analytical interpretation of S1]
