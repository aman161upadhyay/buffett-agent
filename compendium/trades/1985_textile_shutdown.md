## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**July 1985:** after years of disappointing returns, textiles faced structural cost competition and recurring requirements for more equipment investment. Each individual investment proposal could promise attractive productivity gains, but competitors' similar spending prevented those gains from becoming durable owner profits. Earlier letters already warned against committing more capital to terrible industries. Specific July board materials and liquidation appraisals remain unrecovered. [1–2]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“I should be faulted for not quitting sooner.” **Post-exit**, March 4, 1986. [1]

“the intellect should be the servant of the heart, but not its slave” — Buffett invokes Comte's admonition in explaining how concern for employees had delayed an economically necessary decision; these are Comte's words quoted by Buffett, not an invented original Buffett aphorism. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Decided in July 1985 to close the remaining textile operations; the letter says the job was **largely completed** by year-end. Exact final cleanup date is unknown. This ended the operating business originally taken over in 1965, not Berkshire's corporate existence. The separate initial-takeover dossier covers that earlier mistake; their opportunity costs must not be added as independent realized losses. [1]

## What happened after
The shutdown stopped continued reinvestment into a chronically poor business. The letter describes poor liquidation realization relative to asset carrying values and useful-equipment economics. Exact cumulative textile cash losses, shutdown costs and full liquidation proceeds are not reconstructed here. The outcome label records the acknowledged failed operating investment, not a negative return on all Berkshire stock. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Industry:** adverse economics outweighed reasonable management quality. [1]
- **Competition:** machine-level productivity was insufficient when rivals invested similarly. [1]
- **People versus structure:** hardworking managers and cooperative unions did not erase the cost disadvantage. [1]
- **Discipline:** emotional and historical commitments should not dictate future incremental spending. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: an explicit sell/close lesson, essential counterweight to “hold forever.” His willingness to preserve employment was genuine, but conditional on tolerable economics and limited additional capital; the later closure is not inconsistency concealed by a success story. [1]

## Public discussion
The annual letter acknowledges employees and unions, including the painful irony that cooperation helped postpone closure. Original local labor reporting and auction records remain gaps. This is not a claim that social costs were absent or irrelevant. [1]
