## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**1975, reconstructed from Buffett's 2014 retrospective:** Berkshire already owned a difficult textile business and bought another New England operation. The price appeared cheap relative to assets, with projected integration synergies. Original 1975 investment memorandum, exact price, asset appraisal and earnings remain unrecovered. The well-known later structural diagnosis is not an entry-date insight he is credited with having acted upon. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“Of course, the purchase price was a ‘bargain’ based on the assets we received and the projected synergies with Berkshire's existing textile business.” **Hindsight**, February 27, 2015; quotation marks around bargain signal Buffett's own irony. [1]

“Waumbec was a disaster” — same retrospective. These concise passages provide both the original stated logic and his eventual verdict without inventing 1975 optimism beyond what he recounts. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Acquired Waumbec in 1975. Original financing, ownership agreement, execution date and full follow-on capital spending remain unknown. This is a separate incremental acquisition from the 1962–1965 Berkshire stock/control trade, but it belongs to the same troubled operating complex. [1–2]

## What happened after
The mill closed not many years afterward. The broader textile operations were shut down in 1985; that final closure is a separate decision dossier and should not be mistaken for proof this particular mill stayed open until then. No precise aggregate Waumbec loss is established here. Do not count its unknown losses and Berkshire's retrospective opportunity-cost estimate as additive realized amounts. [1–2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Asset discount:** a stated purchase attraction, not necessarily a recoverable liquidation margin. [1]
- **Synergies:** improving an existing weak business seemed attractive but failed. [1]
- **Industry discipline:** the later lesson is that structural economics can overwhelm cheap assets and competent effort. [1–2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: a direct counterexample to a facile “cheap plus synergies equals buy” rule. A faithful simulation should recognize Buffett's acknowledged mistake, not turn the original failed rationale into a best practice. [1]

## Public discussion
No original acquisition announcement or worker/community reporting was recovered. The candid retrospective is strong attribution evidence but weak evidence for what the full original financial case contained. [1]
