## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)

**Cutoff: 2015-03-24.** Heinz/3G was an existing investment; this file concerns the combination with Kraft, not the [2013 preferred-plus-common investment](2013_heinz.md). Pre-entry transaction-specific valuation proof is incomplete. [1]

**Snapshot gaps:** Entry-day audited valuation, trading range and independently documented consensus: unknown; do not substitute the first 13F mark for purchase cost. Financial/consensus gaps are not filled with later outcomes.

## Buffett's thesis in his own words

The 2015 letter reports 325.4M shares, 27% ownership and $9.8B cost after the combination. It is a later accounting explanation, not independent evidence that every Kraft brand had a perpetual moat. No verified contemporary claim of a guaranteed cost-synergy return is included. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did

2015 merger changed the investment into public Kraft Heinz and produced a $6.8B accounting write-up. Gains from remeasurement are not sale proceeds. [1]

## What happened after

2018 financial results, disclosed in February 2019, included Berkshire’s roughly $3B non-cash impairment; KHC itself announced $15.4B impairments. Buffett then said “We overpaid for Kraft.” [2,3] The June 2026 report also records a separate $5B pre-tax KHC impairment in Q2 2025. [4]
