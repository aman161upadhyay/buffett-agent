## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**June 3, 1986:** the uniform business had a demonstrated strong earnings record and family operators wishing to continue, while financial owners wished to sell. The opportunity combined liquidity for some holders with a permanent partner for the Heldman family. Original standalone acquisition-date financial statements and exact equity payment remain unrecovered. The $55 million figure values the whole business; it is not the check for the approximately 84% purchased. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“Its economic record is superb; its managers are talented, high-grade, and love what they do; and the Heldman family wanted to continue its financial interest in partnership with us.” **Post-purchase**, February 27, 1987. [1]

“Both Fechheimer and NFM were right for us, and we were right for them.” Same letter, explaining fit between seller needs and Berkshire's non-resale, decentralized approach. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Bought approximately 84% on June 3, 1986, at a price based on a $55 million total business valuation. Family members retained their interest and operating responsibilities. Buffett and Munger had not visited the Cincinnati operation when the following letter described it; the underwriting centered on economics and people rather than a claimed proprietary factory-inspection insight. [1]

## What happened after
The 1989 letter reports $12.621 million company pretax earnings and says profits were down because of integration problems following a major 1988 acquisition, while returns on invested capital remained splendid. This is a successful operating relationship with a disclosed setback, not an uninterrupted growth story. Lifetime profit and final ownership changes are not verified here. [2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Economics:** consistent actual earning power rather than optimistic turnaround projections. [1]
- **People:** quality and detailed business knowledge of the Heldmans mattered directly. [1–2]
- **Continuity:** multiple generations plus continuing equity supported the future management supply. [1]
- **Seller fit:** Berkshire offered permanence and autonomy, useful non-price terms. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: a model private acquisition constrained mainly by being small relative to Berkshire. Buffett raised his advertised minimum earnings threshold afterward. `open` is retained operating ownership in the cited follow-up. [1]

## Public discussion
No independent original transaction coverage was recovered. Buffett's shareholder explanation contains both seller motivations and the later integration problem, rather than treating managerial trust as a guarantee against operational errors. [1–2]
