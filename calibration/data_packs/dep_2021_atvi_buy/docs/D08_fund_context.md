Title: Berkshire capital, mandate and opportunity cost
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/718877/000110465921099493/tm2123710d1_ex99-1.htm; https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/2020ar/2020ar.pdf; https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/qtrly/2ndqtr21.pdf
Date: 2021-10-31

Berkshire's 2020 annual report places cash, cash equivalents and Treasury bills around $138.3 billion; its already published June report raises the benchmark to approximately $144.1 billion, used in metadata. Its annual equity portfolio was approximately $281 billion. Combs and Weschler independently manage smaller portfolios; a sub-$1 billion opening position is a deputy-scale idea, not evidence of Buffett underwriting the entertainment pipeline personally. Existing capital commitments and insurance liquidity remain relevant, but the thesis is a passive investment without a financing role. [S3,S4]

### Dated operating baseline

Second-quarter GAAP revenue was $2.296 billion versus $1.932 billion; diluted EPS $1.12 versus $0.75; operating margin 42%. Digital-channel revenue was $2.03 billion. Non-GAAP operating margin was 44% and EPS $1.20. Quarterly operating cash flow was $388 million versus $768 million; trailing-twelve-month operating cash flow was $2.57 billion. Net bookings were $1.92 billion versus $2.08 billion, while in-game net bookings were $1.32 billion versus $1.37 billion. These bookings metrics differ from GAAP revenue because recognition and deferrals differ. Overall monthly active users were 408 million; Activision had 127 million and Blizzard 26 million. Call of Duty Mobile was on track, in management's forecast, to exceed $1 billion of annual consumer spending. That consumer-spending measure is not automatically recognized company revenue. The company raised its outlook but acknowledged serious employment, compensation and workplace allegations. [S1]

### Underwriting distinction

The denominator is capital available to Berkshire, not the issuer's enterprise value or an outside asset manager's client AUM. Insurance float carries obligations; reported cash is not all surplus. A new security should be compared with existing portfolio opportunities, retaining liquidity for operating subsidiaries and negotiated whole-business purchases. No leverage at the parent is assumed to make the proposal fit. The question is an incremental allocation within a large permanent-capital enterprise. [Analytical interpretation of S3]

For a public stock, a deputy-scale position and a Buffett-scale position need different sizing even when both are plausible investments. For a negotiated financing, the relevant cash commitment includes follow-on needs rather than only the opening check. No future trade, retrospective explanation or confidential public-equity holding is supplied as a recommendation. The issuer's current operating scale below helps compare the proposed exposure with the available fund capital. [Analytical interpretation of S1,S3]
