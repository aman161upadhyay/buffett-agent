Title: Cash drag, reinvestment risk and the cost of unnecessary action
Source: https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2025ltr.pdf ; https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/2025ar/202510-k.pdf
Date: 2026-05-01

The bear case against excess liquidity is substantial: inflation reduces real purchasing power, lower short-term rates can reduce interest income, and prolonged inactivity can miss compounding in productive assets. Shareholders may also fear that a new CEO feels pressure to make a large acquisition simply to demonstrate activity. These are analytical opportunity-cost and agency risks, not evidence of an announced policy change. The letter's response is explicit discipline and a preference for productive assets when terms are right. [1]

The opposing risk is deploying capital at a poor price or compromising claims-paying ability. Insurance tail losses, utility liabilities and large operating commitments make resilience valuable. A large nominal cash figure does not eliminate those obligations. The annual report supplies financial strength, but not a zero-loss guarantee. No subsequent cash decline or acquisition is imported to vindicate one side of the debate. The analyst must assess avoidable cash drag against the permanent-loss risk of reaching for returns, without asserting that caution is a market-direction forecast. [1,2]

Evidence ledger from the February 28 letter and annual report: 2025 operating earnings were $44.5 billion versus $47.4 billion in 2024; operating cash flow was $46 billion. Float was approximately $176 billion versus $171 billion a year earlier. The insurance-and-other cash, cash-equivalent and Treasury-bill balance, net of unsettled purchases, was $369.0 billion; broader headline cash definitions differ. The letter says total cash and U.S. Treasury holdings exceed $370 billion. Equity securities were $297.8 billion at year end, including holdings outside the U.S. 13F perimeter. There were no own-share repurchases during 2025. Holding-company/BHFC-type borrowings excluding BNSF and BHE were $45.8 billion. The report emphasizes redundant liquidity and independent financing capacity, not a guaranteed forecast of stock-market direction. [1,2]

Risk distinction: a temporary earnings interruption and a permanent impairment can initially produce similar reported numbers. The test is whether the cause can be bounded without assuming that management's preferred recovery path must occur. Position liquidity limits execution risk, not business risk. The adverse mechanisms set out here are scenarios grounded in the cited contemporary evidence, not claims that every adverse outcome has occurred. No independent short-seller target or numerical probability is invented where a dated opposing valuation was not recovered.
