## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**August 1991:** Salomon's crisis required direct executive intervention. This is a governance decision attached to a pre-existing investment, not an independent 1991 initiation. Detailed Treasury-bidding chronology and original Congressional testimony need a separate authenticated transcript. [1–2]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
The 1991 letter describes the need “to involve myself personally and intensely in the Salomon situation.” **Retrospective explanation:** it is not the August 18 announcement. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Buffett became interim chairman August 18, 1991. The 1991 letter says the responsibilities displaced his usual Berkshire work. He stepped down in June 1992. [1–2]

## What happened after
The 1992 letter reports Salomon Brothers' record pretax earnings, 34% above its previous high, and improved reputation. Those are post-intervention observations, not facts available in August 1991. The 1997 Travelers transaction is a separate exit case. [2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Capital protection:** Berkshire already had exposure. [1]
- **Credibility:** Direct involvement answered an exceptional institutional crisis. [1–2]
- **Repair:** Operational survival preceded any investment monetization. [1–2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: an exception to hands-off minority ownership. It demonstrates the cost of reputational entanglement, not a general aspiration to run securities dealers. [1]

## Public discussion
The 1992 letter cites an external reputation survey; original contemporary reporting and testimony remain gaps. Famous reputation quotations are deliberately not reconstructed from memory. [2]
