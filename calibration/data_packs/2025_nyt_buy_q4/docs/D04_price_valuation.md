Title: A premium multiple for recurring paid content
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/71691/000007169125000117/pressrelease06302025.htm ; https://s23.q4cdn.com/152113917/files/doc_events/2025/Apr/30/The-New-York-Times-Company-2024-Annual-Report.pdf
Date: 2025-09-30

The September 30 unadjusted Class A close was $57.40, from the historical daily-price observation. Using 164.3 million latest quarterly diluted weighted-average shares gives approximately $9.43 billion illustrative equity value, not an exact September share-count capitalization. Quarterly EPS of $0.50 multiplied by four gives $2.00 and a 28.70 times mechanical multiple; adjusted EPS of $0.58 gives $2.32 and 24.74 times. Neither is forward consensus and both ignore seasonality. [1; historical price below]

Subscriber growth and higher ARPU can produce substantial incremental margins, yet the price requires durability in the face of free news, customer churn and AI distribution changes. Annualizing quarterly adjusted operating profit would give $535.2 million, but that is not free cash flow. No separate economic value is assigned to each bundle product without an observed profit contribution. The stock is small enough for a deputy-scale position, but size compatibility is not valuation evidence. Contemporary consensus growth, price targets and a full historical multiple range are not recovered.

Evidence ledger: annual revenue was $2.308321 billion in 2022, $2.426152 billion in 2023 and $2.585919 billion in 2024. Second-quarter revenue of $685.9 million grew 9.7%; operating profit was $106.6 million, up 34.2%, and adjusted operating profit $133.8 million. Diluted EPS was $0.50, or $0.58 adjusted. Total subscribers were 11.88 million, digital-only 11.30 million, and bundle/multiproduct 6.02 million. Digital-only ARPU was $9.64, up 3.2%. Digital subscription revenue was $350.4 million, total subscription revenue $481.4 million, digital advertising $94.4 million and total advertising $134.0 million. Affiliate/licensing/other revenue was $70.5 million. Marketing costs were $69.2 million; journalism and subscriber-servicing spending remain real operating costs. [1,2]

Valuation discipline: the calculations in this document are analyst arithmetic, not management targets or a Berkshire underwriting model. Earnings multiples, book multiples and cash-flow yields answer different questions and are not interchangeable. A low multiple offers no protection if normalized earning power has been overstated. Conversely, growth has value only after the incremental capital required to obtain it. No verified contemporary consensus forecast or complete historical multiple distribution is supplied; an exact intrinsic value should therefore not be reverse-engineered from a subsequent transaction.
