## Cutoff evidence — reconstructed

Goizueta's move to the chief executive role in 1981 precedes the present review; the operating strategy with Keough is an existing record, not a new event at the cutoff. The stock-market crash in October of the preceding year is already public, but does not identify the investor's exact entry quotation. The requested September snapshot is a new-thesis reconstruction: the best archival account describes accumulation beginning during the summer, so it cannot be represented as demonstrably before every actual purchase. No eventual portfolio cost, share count or disclosure is in the documents. A separate metadata note flags this boundary limitation for the evaluator.

## Economic reading of the supplied facts

Industry economics: a concentrate producer sells a small part of the finished beverage's physical volume but supplies a large part of its consumer identity. Bottlers and retailers supply production, packaging and access to the customer. The division of capital and margin among these participants matters when judging returns. A strong brand can support repeat demand, but advertising, product quality and route-to-market access still require resources. Growth abroad may use a local partner's capital, yet the producer remains exposed to execution, currency and regulation. These are implications of the concentrate-and-bottler model, not an assertion that every incremental dollar of revenue becomes owner cash or that consumption can grow without limit.

Chronology controls: the cutoff is an information boundary, not proof of an execution on that day. A negotiated commitment, a public announcement, a purchase settlement and an annual-report disclosure are distinct events. Facts about the condition of the business may be reconstructed from archival accounts without turning a later explanation into a statement available to the market.

Only the events described above enter this chronology. No subsequent result is assumed when assessing the unresolved question. The absence of an original day-by-day price series prevents identification of a precise entry-day return or volume pattern. The same restriction applies to unpublished board discussions and security-specific purchase ledgers. This reconstruction does not claim access to either. Where a transaction is being considered, proposed terms are identified as proposed; regulatory approvals and operating improvements are not treated as completed. Where shares already exist in a portfolio, a request to study a new allocation must not erase that exposure.
