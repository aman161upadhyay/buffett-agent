# 2007 Berkshire annual letter

**Information date:** 2008-02 (month-only where precise public release day was not independently established). **Era:** C. **Author:** Buffett. **Primary source:** [letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2007ltr.pdf). All rules below cite sections of this source; section names are used rather than fabricated PDF page numbers. Annual-period labels are not publication dates.

## Process rules and decision explanations

- Businesses—The Great, the Good and the Gruesome: See’s throws off cash; FlightSafety earns acceptable returns but needs reinvestment; airlines can consume enormous capital with poor shareholder rewards.
- Same section: Dexter’s supposed durable advantage disappeared; paying with Berkshire shares compounded the opportunity cost. This is retrospective evidence, not available at 1993 entry.
- Insurance: Gen Re’s historical reputation outlived underwriting reality; Buffett admits missing the deterioration at the merger.
- Investments: PetroChina sold as assessed business value converged with market price. Do not rewrite the sale as purely political without the stated valuation reason.
- Acquisitions: staged Marmon purchase combines initial majority acquisition and an earnings-based formula for later shares. Valuation and future funding must reflect the structure.

## Verbatim evidence anchors

- **Businesses – The Great, the Good and the Gruesome; Mistake in purchasing U.S. Air preferred stock.** “I, to my shame, participated in this foolishness when I had Berkshire buy U.S. Air preferred stock in 1989. As the ink was drying on our check, the company went into a tailspin”
- **Businesses – The Great, the Good and the Gruesome; Mistake in purchasing Dexter for Berkshire stock.** “I made an even worse mistake when I said “yes” to Dexter, a shoe business I bought in 1993 for $433 million in Berkshire stock (25,203 shares of A).”
- **Insurance; Mistake in decision to merge with General Re.** “This reputation, unfortunately, outlived its factual underpinnings, a flaw that I completely missed when I made the decision in 1998 to merge with General Re.”
- **Investments; Sale of PetroChina holdings.** “In the second half of last year, the market value of the company rose to $275 billion, about what we thought it was worth compared to other giant oil companies. So we sold our holdings for $4 billion.”

## Attribution and point-in-time boundary

Buffett is the source author, but an acquired subsidiary’s bolt-on or operating action is not automatically his personally originated investment. Individual decider unknown where not identified. Historical narratives are available only from this information date. A section discussing earlier purchases is retrospective; do not load its later performance, confession or corrected valuation into the original entry replay.
