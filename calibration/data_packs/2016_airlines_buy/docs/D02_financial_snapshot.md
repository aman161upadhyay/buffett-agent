Delta's October 13 quarterly report provides a dated operating anchor: revenue was approximately $10.5 billion, down 5.6% year on year. This is one member's result, not the four-carrier basket's total. A fully reconciled four-carrier financial table was not recovered, so no synthetic aggregate net income, net debt or P/E is asserted. Pension obligations, leases, aircraft commitments and differences in fuel hedging complicate comparison. [Source above.]

Technical context: airline economics have high fixed and semi-fixed costs and perishable capacity. Load factor, fare, ancillary revenue and unit cost should be read together; fuller aircraft can still produce weak returns if prices fall. Aircraft leases, pension liabilities and committed fleet purchases belong in the capital-risk assessment. Low fuel prices improve a period's profit but may induce competitive fare cuts. A four-company basket reduces single-carrier concentration but retains shared exposure to travel demand, fuel, labor and credit availability. Earnings before aircraft ownership costs can exaggerate cash available to common owners. [Source above; analytical framework, not an aggregate forecast.]

Accounting discipline: the figures stated above are reported observations unless expressly labeled arithmetic. A period-end balance is not a flow and a market mark is not purchase cost. Do not mix fiscal years, currencies, share bases or enterprise and equity values. To construct owner earnings, begin with the relevant reported profit, reconcile noncash items and subtract the investment required to maintain economic position. Changes in working capital and recurring compensation remain economic costs even when a convenient adjusted metric excludes them. If maintenance investment, leverage or the proper share denominator is missing, state that the yield cannot be completed rather than present false precision.

Evidence boundary: missing figures are not available in contemporaneous sources recovered for this digest. Analytical questions are not additional reported facts or claims about the investor's private thinking. Read the financial, risk and fund-context documents together; no individual accounting ratio supplies a complete investment conclusion.
