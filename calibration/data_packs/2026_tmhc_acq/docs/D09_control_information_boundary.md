Information boundary for a whole-business assessment

The target's public operating releases establish products, earnings observations and selected investment needs. They do not supply an agreed sale price, a signed purchase agreement or a verified stand-alone schedule of liabilities to be assumed by a new owner. No post-cutoff announcement is a source for this document. This is not an announced-merger arbitrage and there is no observed deal spread, closing probability or financing package to annualize.

The core diligence questions are concrete. An owner needs normalized earnings through a weak cycle, capital investment required to preserve competitive position, management accountability, working-capital seasonality and legal claims. Reported segment or company profit should be reconciled with cash available after those obligations. Gross enterprise value and common equity value differ; a price without debt and liability definitions can be misleading.

Management quality can be investigated from public operating execution, incentives and disclosures, but a retention arrangement cannot be presumed. Existing relationships between the prospective investor and an industry are relevant experience, not evidence that the target has agreed to join it. Likewise, a permanent-capital balance sheet can reduce financing risk without making a high purchase price safe.

Any control thesis in this evidence set is conditional on obtaining satisfactory price and liability terms. Those unavailable facts remain unavailable rather than being reconstructed from an agreement disclosed after the assessment date. The analyst can decide whether the business merits further investigation and what conservative economics would be required. The analyst cannot claim a known negotiated return from public evidence that does not contain the price.

Evidence ledger from April 22: first-quarter total revenue was $1.387092 billion, home closings revenue $1.311421 billion and net income $98.625 million. Reported diluted EPS was $1.01, adjusted $1.12. There were 2,268 closings at $578,000 average price and 2,914 net orders at $603,000. Backlog was 3,465 homes worth $2.3 billion. Home closings gross margin was 20.0%, adjusted 20.6%, versus 24.0% and 24.8% a year earlier. The company owned/controlled 75,626 lots, 51% off balance sheet, and invested $503 million in land/development. Liquidity was approximately $1.6 billion including $653 million cash; net homebuilding debt/capital was 20.5%. Book value per share was $64. The company repurchased about 2.5 million shares for $150 million at roughly $61 per share. [1]
