Technical context: merger arbitrage is a contingent cash-flow problem. Separate the promised consideration, financing, approvals, termination rights, outside date and possible remedies. Gross spread is not expected return: completion probability, time and the break-price loss all matter. A bidder's ability to pay does not settle regulatory approval. Stand-alone valuation is important even if the intended holding period ends at closing, because the target remains a public business if the transaction fails. Do not treat an original pre-announcement stock purchase and a post-announcement workout as the same investment thesis merely because they use the same ticker. [Source above; analytical framework.]

Results discipline: establish which period the release covers and which publication was actually available by the information boundary. Compare growth in units, prices, margins and cash separately, since a higher EPS figure can come from a smaller share count or an accounting item rather than stronger demand. Treat management targets as claims to test against demonstrated history, not as achieved earnings. A weak period may be cyclical, structural or exceptional; describe the evidence needed to distinguish these possibilities. Do not introduce a subsequent quarterly report to explain an ambiguous current observation, and do not confuse disclosure day with the date cash was earned.

Evidence boundary: the quarter's operating performance and the announced transaction are separate observations. Neither guarantees a completion date or predicts a regulator's decision.
