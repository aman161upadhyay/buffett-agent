Title: Business and the affirmative investment case
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1640147/000162828020013518/snowflakes-1a2.htm
Date: 2020-09-15

The platform separates cloud-data storage and compute, letting enterprises consolidate information, query it and share governed data. It runs on infrastructure supplied by large cloud vendors rather than requiring the customer to own an on-premise warehouse. The affirmative franchise case is that easier scaling and cross-organization data sharing increase customer use, making the product more valuable as data workloads migrate. Consumption-based revenue can align price with use, while making near-term revenue less contractually fixed than an ordinary subscription. [S1; analyst interpretation]

Revenue more than doubles, existing-customer retention reaches 158%, and 56 customers already spend over $1 million annually on product revenue. This supports a land-and-expand thesis with potential operating leverage as an installed enterprise base grows. Data migration and workflow integration can raise switching costs. A small allocation could participate in a large software market without deploying Buffett-scale capital. The bull case depends on durable product economics, not an assumed first-day trading gain. [S1; bull interpretation]

### Dated operating baseline

The IPO registration reports fiscal-year revenue of $264.7 million versus $96.7 million, up approximately 174%. Fiscal-year net loss was approximately $348.5 million. For the six months ended July 31, revenue was $242.0 million versus $104.0 million, up approximately 133%, while net loss was $171.3 million versus $177.2 million. Net revenue retention was 158% at July 31. The customer count was 3,117, and 56 customers generated more than $1 million of trailing-twelve-month product revenue. Retention measures the expansion of an existing cohort; it is not consolidated growth or guaranteed contractual revenue. The prospectus describes a consumption model rather than fixed subscription revenue. Two share classes give Class B ten votes per share and Class A one. The contemplated primary issuance is 28 million shares, excluding the possible underwriters' option. A $250 million private placement and a 4,042,043-share secondary block are distinct financing legs. [S1]

### Underwriting distinction

The economic unit to value is the cash-producing franchise, not its stock-market category. Separate evidence of repeat customer behavior from a claim that those customers cannot leave. The affirmative interpretation here is an analyst's case, not a representation that Berkshire endorsed it. Costs needed to retain customers, renew products and maintain legal or regulatory permission belong in the economics even when reported in different accounting lines. A minority interest also lacks the ability to redesign the business simply because the owner has permanent capital. [Analytical interpretation of S1]

For diligence, identify which activity earns the customer relationship, which assets must be replaced, and which liabilities could consume the cash before it reaches common equity. The facts below define the scale of the existing enterprise. They do not convert management's expectations into a demonstrated ten-year record. [Analytical interpretation of S1]
