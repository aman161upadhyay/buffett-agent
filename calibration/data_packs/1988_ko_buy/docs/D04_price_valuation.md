## Cutoff evidence — reconstructed

The exact September 30 close and a consistent contemporaneous share count are not available in the recovered source set. A frequently cited roughly 15-times purchase earnings multiple is not independently verified at this date and is not asserted as a desk fact. Neither an eventual average accumulation price nor a subsequent year-end market value is a substitute for the cutoff quote. Valuing the brand requires sustainable owner earnings, reinvestment needs and conservative international growth. Paying above tangible book can be rational if customer preference persists, but the investor must still earn an adequate return from the actual price. No guaranteed 15% growth rate or original Buffett DCF is supplied.

## Economic reading of the supplied facts

Industry economics: a concentrate producer sells a small part of the finished beverage's physical volume but supplies a large part of its consumer identity. Bottlers and retailers supply production, packaging and access to the customer. The division of capital and margin among these participants matters when judging returns. A strong brand can support repeat demand, but advertising, product quality and route-to-market access still require resources. Growth abroad may use a local partner's capital, yet the producer remains exposed to execution, currency and regulation. These are implications of the concentrate-and-bottler model, not an assertion that every incremental dollar of revenue becomes owner cash or that consumption can grow without limit.

Valuation interpretation: the relevant asset is the stream of cash that can be withdrawn over the life of the business without weakening its competitive position. A low multiple is informative only when the earnings denominator is sustainable. A high return on tangible capital can justify a premium to book value, but does not justify an unlimited purchase price.

No closing quotation is inferred from a subsequent transaction average or from the low for a later month. If a dated market price is unavailable, a precise market capitalization, initial earnings yield and implied return are unavailable as well. A negotiated security can instead be evaluated from its proposed cash payments and priority, subject to credit risk. The adverse case should reduce sustainable earnings and allow for additional capital needs rather than simply apply a smaller multiple to optimistic earnings. Interest rates, taxes and reinvestment determine the opportunity cost; no unsupported discount rate or terminal growth estimate is presented as an observed fact.
