Title: Scale and permanent liabilities distinguish this portfolio
Source: https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2025ltr.pdf and https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/2025ar/202510-k.pdf (2026-02-28); https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS3MO and DGS10/BAMLH0A0HYM2/VIXCLS (2026-05-01); https://www.multpl.com/s-p-500-pe-ratio/table/by-month (completed April 2026)
Date: 2026-05-01

A concentrated permanent-capital insurer is not directly comparable with a fully invested equity index fund or a leveraged hedge fund. It has no normal external redemption cycle but does have insurance claims, operating capital needs and reputation commitments. At its size, an opportunity must be large enough to matter or be delegated appropriately. Its ability to act without a financing contingency is an advantage only if liquidity has been preserved. [1,2]

The capital framework compares whole businesses, minority equities, subsidiary reinvestment and own-share repurchases. Those assets have different liquidity, taxes and cash-flow duration; a simple industry allocation table misses the economics. No contemporaneous peer cash/AUM ranking is recovered, and high cash at another insurer would not prove equal opportunity cost or tail risk. The relevant benchmark is per-share intrinsic-value growth over long horizons while preserving independence. Corporate scale explains some selectivity, but cannot by itself justify never deploying cash when an adequately priced understandable opportunity is available. [1]

### Numeric alternatives, not a spurious insurer league table

| Broad equity index | April 25.49x earnings, 3.92% inverse | accounting earnings include cyclical risk |

| Portfolio liquidity versus float | $369bn / $176bn = 2.10x | liquidity is insurance/other net of unsettled purchases |

This is a market/hedge-regime pack, so there is no meaningful common-stock share price, industrial EV/EBITDA or hedge “book/share.” The first three claims are numerical opportunity costs rather than three fabricated operating-company peers. The liquidity/float ratio is calculated and does not mean float can be refunded or spent freely. The $297.8bn equity-security portfolio also excludes some equity-method interests and is not a total corporate asset denominator.

At the issuer's scale, a one-percentage-point annual yield change on a static $369bn balance corresponds to $3.69bn pretax arithmetic. That is not a forecast: balances, duration, taxes, classifications and subsidiary cash needs change. Reported 2025 operating earnings of $44.5bn and simple OCF less capex of $25.0bn supply distinct earnings/cash anchors. Do not treat either as a portfolio's total return. Financial statements, the FRED rate/credit series and completed-month valuation tables were checked; synchronized insurer cash/AUM and forward-consensus rankings remain unrecovered and are not replaced by hindsight.

Evidence ledger from the February 28 letter and annual report: 2025 operating earnings were $44.5 billion versus $47.4 billion in 2024; operating cash flow was $46 billion. Float was approximately $176 billion versus $171 billion a year earlier. The insurance-and-other cash, cash-equivalent and Treasury-bill balance, net of unsettled purchases, was $369.0 billion; broader headline cash definitions differ. The letter says total cash and U.S. Treasury holdings exceed $370 billion. Equity securities were $297.8 billion at year end, including holdings outside the U.S. 13F perimeter. There were no own-share repurchases during 2025. Holding-company/BHFC-type borrowings excluding BNSF and BHE were $45.8 billion. The report emphasizes redundant liquidity and independent financing capacity, not a guaranteed forecast of stock-market direction. [1,2]

Comparison discipline: industry membership does not make two businesses economically identical. Geography, customer mix, contract duration, capital intensity, leverage and accounting adjustments can dominate headline growth comparisons. No synchronized peer-price and consensus-earnings dataset was recovered for this cutoff; relative cheapness is therefore not asserted numerically. The useful comparison is between sources of durable return and exposure to common pressures. A competitor's weakness is not automatically this company's moat, and an industry recovery is not required to be uniform across participants.
