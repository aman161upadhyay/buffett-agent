The index's change is price-only, not total return or a forecast. Earnings yield and Treasury yield are not equivalent contractual claims. The same corporate tax assumption would reduce a 4.61% nominal bill yield to approximately 3.64% before state taxes and inflation. This is arithmetic, not a claim about the investor's marginal tax circumstances.

For additional market context, D04's cutoff closing drawdown is 2.32% from the October 18 peak and the completed-October dividend yield is 1.28%. Thus a modest retreat from a recent high is not by itself evidence of crisis pricing. The bill-versus-index-earnings-yield gap is approximately 1.10 percentage points in favor of bills on current reported yields, but growth and residual risk make that unlike a locked arbitrage spread.

Compare Treasury bills with long bonds, listed franchises, acquisitions and own shares on a risk-adjusted owner basis. Bills supply liquidity but not an indefinite locked yield. Long bonds add duration risk. Acquisitions may offer control and reinvestment capacity but require very large commitments. Own shares are attractive only at the right price; a repurchase is not automatically superior to a selective operating investment. [Source above.]

Technical context: cash, bills, long bonds and productive assets serve different purposes. Short government instruments preserve nominal liquidity but expose holders to reinvestment rates and purchasing-power erosion. A long bond fixes nominal payments while adding duration risk. A public equity is a residual ownership claim whose value depends on sustainable distributable cash, not tomorrow's quote. A controlled operating business adds capital-allocation rights and illiquidity. Compare after-tax expected economics on consistent horizons, retaining enough liquidity for obligations before allocating discretionary capital. The size of a cash balance alone does not identify a market forecast or explain every trade. [Source above; comparative investment framework, not a new macroeconomic estimate.]

Comparison discipline: compare each claim on consistent after-tax cash flows and investment horizons. A bill's contractual maturity, a long bond's duration and an equity's residual earnings are different risks. Relative valuation cannot alone establish absolute value. The numerical opportunity-cost table does not assume that a current interest rate persists or that an earnings multiple predicts next year's market return.

Evidence boundary: historical benchmark yields and index levels are recovered. Duration and tax calculations are explicitly illustrative, not measurements of the investor's precise portfolio holdings or private expectations.
