American's January adjusted FY2020 EPS outlook was $4–6; Delta's January March-quarter revenue-growth outlook was 5–7%. These are dated pre-pandemic guidance, not current forecasts. They must not be valued as if still reliable on April 2. No first-quarter 2020 financial results are backdated into this document. The CARES Act was enacted March 27, but eligibility and prospective assistance are not equivalent to unrestricted cash already on each carrier's balance sheet.

Travel restrictions, canceled journeys and large schedule reductions are observable by April 2. The March 27 CARES Act authorizes extraordinary airline support. Assistance can preserve operations and employment while imposing restrictions, additional claims or dilution on existing owners. A pre-crisis profitable quarter cannot answer how long demand remains depressed or how much liquidity the business needs during that interval. [Source above.]

Technical context: airline economics have high fixed and semi-fixed costs and perishable capacity. Load factor, fare, ancillary revenue and unit cost should be read together; fuller aircraft can still produce weak returns if prices fall. Aircraft leases, pension liabilities and committed fleet purchases belong in the capital-risk assessment. Low fuel prices improve a period's profit but may induce competitive fare cuts. A four-company basket reduces single-carrier concentration but retains shared exposure to travel demand, fuel, labor and credit availability. Earnings before aircraft ownership costs can exaggerate cash available to common owners. [Source above; analytical framework, not an aggregate forecast.]

Results discipline: establish which period the release covers and which publication was actually available by the information boundary. Compare growth in units, prices, margins and cash separately, since a higher EPS figure can come from a smaller share count or an accounting item rather than stronger demand. Treat management targets as claims to test against demonstrated history, not as achieved earnings. A weak period may be cyclical, structural or exceptional; describe the evidence needed to distinguish these possibilities. Do not introduce a subsequent quarterly report to explain an ambiguous current observation, and do not confuse disclosure day with the date cash was earned.

Evidence boundary: missing figures are not available in contemporaneous sources recovered for this digest. Analytical questions are not additional reported facts or claims about the investor's private thinking. Read the financial, risk and fund-context documents together; no individual accounting ratio supplies a complete investment conclusion.
