Title: A pre-meeting information boundary
Source: https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2025ltr.pdf ; https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/2025ar/202510-k.pdf
Date: 2026-05-01

January 1: Greg Abel assumes the CEO role under the already public succession plan. February 28: his first annual letter and annual report provide the complete latest public framework and year-end financial figures. May 1 is the assessment boundary. No May 2 meeting comment, new quarterly cash figure or subsequently disclosed security transaction is included. The letter is a prior statement of process, not a supplied answer about the forthcoming portfolio stance. [1,2]

This sequence lets the analyst reason from operating earning power, float, liquidity, obligations and valuation discipline. It does not permit treating an unreleased March balance sheet as a live portfolio statement. The annual letter's preference for productive assets and its insistence on patience must be considered together: one does not cancel the other. No market crash, broad index buy or liquidation is assumed. The decision concerns the conditions under which capital should move, and how much resilience is valuable, rather than a prediction of what the next market session or annual meeting will bring.

Evidence ledger from the February 28 letter and annual report: 2025 operating earnings were $44.5 billion versus $47.4 billion in 2024; operating cash flow was $46 billion. Float was approximately $176 billion versus $171 billion a year earlier. The insurance-and-other cash, cash-equivalent and Treasury-bill balance, net of unsettled purchases, was $369.0 billion; broader headline cash definitions differ. The letter says total cash and U.S. Treasury holdings exceed $370 billion. Equity securities were $297.8 billion at year end, including holdings outside the U.S. 13F perimeter. There were no own-share repurchases during 2025. Holding-company/BHFC-type borrowings excluding BNSF and BHE were $45.8 billion. The report emphasizes redundant liquidity and independent financing capacity, not a guaranteed forecast of stock-market direction. [1,2]

Timing discipline: the chronology separates publication dates from the economic periods discussed. A result for an earlier quarter only becomes an input when released. The market close is an observation available on the stated assessment date, not evidence of a particular investor's execution. This timeline includes no presumed private conversations or unannounced agreement. There is no catalyst completion forecast here: the analyst must decide whether the available business evidence is sufficient without relying on a future disclosure to reveal someone else's decision.
