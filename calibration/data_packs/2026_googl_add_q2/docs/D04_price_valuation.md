Title: A price applied to reported annual EPS, not a negotiated block

Date: 2026-03-31

The March 31 Class A market close was $287.56, from the date-specific historical-price observation. Against 2025 diluted EPS of $10.81, that is approximately 26.60 times reported annual earnings. Quarterly EPS of $2.82 multiplied by four would give $11.28 and approximately 25.49 times, but is not consensus forward EPS. Net investment gains make the annual EPS denominator less representative of repeatable operating profit. [1; historical price below]

A private block discount or a fixed future subscription price is not available in this information set. It would be hindsight to use one to improve a current valuation. The relevant capital alternative is cash, other productive assets or the existing portfolio, with full allowance for a capital budget of $175-$185 billion. No exact maintenance-capital estimate, independently verified near-term consensus or discounted cash-flow target is recovered. The analyst can identify strong earning power, but must make an explicit judgment about its persistence and how much incremental capital growth consumes. [1]

Evidence ledger: February 4 results show 2025 revenue of $402.836 billion versus $350.018 billion in 2024; operating income $129.039 billion versus $112.390 billion; net income $132.170 billion; diluted EPS $10.81. Fourth-quarter revenue was $113.828 billion, operating income $35.934 billion and EPS $2.82. Services revenue was $95.862 billion and Cloud $17.664 billion, up 48%; segment operating income was $40.132 billion and $5.313 billion respectively. Other Bets lost $3.617 billion, including a Waymo compensation charge, and central activities cost $5.894 billion. Cash/marketable securities totaled $126.843 billion. Management expects 2026 capital expenditures of $175-$185 billion. These plans are forward-looking; no second-quarter capital commitment by an outside investor is included. [1-3]

Valuation discipline: the calculations in this document are analyst arithmetic, not management targets or a Berkshire underwriting model. Earnings multiples, book multiples and cash-flow yields answer different questions and are not interchangeable. A low multiple offers no protection if normalized earning power has been overstated. Conversely, growth has value only after the incremental capital required to obtain it. No verified contemporary consensus forecast or complete historical multiple distribution is supplied; an exact intrinsic value should therefore not be reverse-engineered from a subsequent transaction.
