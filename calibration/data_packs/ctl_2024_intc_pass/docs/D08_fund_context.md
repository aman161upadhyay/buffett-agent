Title: Berkshire capital, mandate and opportunity cost
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/50863/000005086324000122/q224_earningsrelease.htm; https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/2023ar/2023ar.pdf; https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/qtrly/1stqtr24.pdf; https://www.cbsnews.com/news/warren-buffetts-firm-bails-out-of-intel-stock/
Date: 2024-08-02

Berkshire's already published first-quarter report places cash, cash equivalents and Treasury bills around $189 billion. Its second-quarter report is not yet public on August 2 and is not used. The last annual report lists approximately $353.8 billion of equity securities; Apple and Bank of America remain important disclosed concentrations. Berkshire previously owned Intel during a separate earlier episode, so this is a new-position re-entry question, not a lifetime never-owned assertion. Its permanent capital can tolerate volatility but does not eliminate technological obsolescence. [S3,S4,S5]

### Dated operating baseline

Second-quarter revenue was $12.8 billion, down 1%. GAAP net loss attributable to Intel was $1.6 billion and EPS negative $0.38; non-GAAP EPS was positive $0.02. GAAP gross margin was 35.4%, versus 35.8%, while non-GAAP gross margin was 38.7%, versus 39.8%. GAAP operating margin was negative 15.3%; adjusted operating margin was 0.2%. Operating cash flow was $2.3 billion and dividends paid $0.5 billion. The company guided next-quarter revenue to $12.5–13.5 billion, GAAP EPS to negative $0.24 and adjusted EPS to negative $0.03. It announced more than $10 billion of operating and capital spending reductions against prior plans, including headcount reductions exceeding 15%. Gross capex guidance was $25–27 billion for the year, while net capital spending after partner contributions and offsets was $11–13 billion. Dividend suspension begins in the fourth quarter. [S1]

### Underwriting distinction

The denominator is capital available to Berkshire, not the issuer's enterprise value or an outside asset manager's client AUM. Insurance float carries obligations; reported cash is not all surplus. A new security should be compared with existing portfolio opportunities, retaining liquidity for operating subsidiaries and negotiated whole-business purchases. No leverage at the parent is assumed to make the proposal fit. The question is an incremental allocation within a large permanent-capital enterprise. [Analytical interpretation of S3]

For a public stock, a deputy-scale position and a Buffett-scale position need different sizing even when both are plausible investments. For a negotiated financing, the relevant cash commitment includes follow-on needs rather than only the opening check. No future trade, retrospective explanation or confidential public-equity holding is supplied as a recommendation. The issuer's current operating scale below helps compare the proposed exposure with the available fund capital. [Analytical interpretation of S1,S3]
