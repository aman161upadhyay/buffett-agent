Third-quarter revenue was $27.714 billion, down 4%; operating income was $5.664 billion, down 46%; net income was $4.395 billion, down 52%; diluted EPS was $1.64, down 49%. Operating margin fell from 36% to 20%. Family monthly active people were 3.71 billion, up 4%, and daily active people 2.93 billion, up 4%. Facebook alone had 2.96 billion monthly active users. Ad impressions grew 17% while average price per ad fell 18%. Quarterly capital expenditure including finance-lease principal was $9.52 billion. Cash and marketable securities totaled $41.78 billion and long-term debt $9.92 billion. Buybacks consumed $6.55 billion. Headcount reached 87,314, up 28%. Management projected full-year capital expenditure of $32–33 billion and next-year capital expenditure of $34–39 billion; projected next-year expenses were $96–101 billion, including office-consolidation costs. These are forecasts, not completed investments or realized savings. [S1]

The November 4 historical close is $90.79. Approximate trailing reported EPS through September is $10.46: prior full-year $13.77 minus $10.13 for the first three prior-year quarters plus $6.82 for the first three current-year quarters. This mechanical combination of reported EPS gives approximately 8.68 times earnings; different weighted share counts make it an approximation, not recomputed aggregate TTM EPS. The shorthand 'about 9x' is not a forecast owner-earnings yield. Annualizing the latest $1.64 quarterly EPS gives $6.56, or 13.84 times earnings. High planned capex and growing new-platform losses require separate cash scenarios. [S1,S2,S5; calculations]

### Underwriting distinction

Owner earnings are not established merely by naming an adjusted profit measure. Start with the reported result; reconcile material noncash items, economic compensation, maintenance investment and working-capital requirements. An acquisition can improve reported per-share growth while requiring cash outside operating expenditure. In a financial institution, liquidity and capital requirements constrain what can be distributed even when accounting profit is positive. In an operating business, underinvestment can temporarily improve cash while reducing franchise value. [Analytical interpretation of S1]

The sourced figures below are a dated record, not a single normalized earnings estimate. Quarterly annualization is a sensitivity tool only. No undocumented maintenance-capex split, adjusted debt number or forward consensus is supplied. Where the evidence cannot support an owner-earnings estimate, the analyst should retain that uncertainty rather than insert a sector-average cash conversion rate. [Analytical interpretation of S1]
