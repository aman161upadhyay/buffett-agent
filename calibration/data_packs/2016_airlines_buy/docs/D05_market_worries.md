Title: Adverse case and permanent-loss risks
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/27904/000002790416000029/dal930201610q.htm (published 2016-10-13); https://www.usatoday.com/story/money/2016/10/13/delta-earnings/91987246 (2016-10-13)
Date: 2016-10-31

The strongest bear case is the industry's long history of poor aggregate returns: fixed costs, volatile fuel, powerful labor groups, competitive fare cuts and recurring fleet spending. Consolidation does not guarantee capacity discipline. A recession or disruption can destroy demand faster than costs can be cut. Buying four airlines reduces single-company risk but does not diversify the shared economics of air travel. [Source above.]

Technical context: airline economics have high fixed and semi-fixed costs and perishable capacity. Load factor, fare, ancillary revenue and unit cost should be read together; fuller aircraft can still produce weak returns if prices fall. Aircraft leases, pension liabilities and committed fleet purchases belong in the capital-risk assessment. Low fuel prices improve a period's profit but may induce competitive fare cuts. A four-company basket reduces single-carrier concentration but retains shared exposure to travel demand, fuel, labor and credit availability. Earnings before aircraft ownership costs can exaggerate cash available to common owners. [Source above; analytical framework, not an aggregate forecast.]

Risk discipline: the adverse cases above are analyst stress tests grounded in the disclosed business and current circumstances, not quotations attributed to an unnamed contemporary short seller. No authenticated bearish price target was recovered. Separate a temporary quotation decline from permanent impairment of earning power or of the investor's claim. Ask whether the issuer can fund necessary operations without assuming favorable capital markets. Identify which changes are within management control and which are not. A large cash balance at the investor does not cure an issuer's leverage, a faulty operating thesis or an excessive purchase price. Do not soften a serious downside merely because an admired investor might be interested.

Evidence boundary: missing figures are not available in contemporaneous sources recovered for this digest. Analytical questions are not additional reported facts or claims about the investor's private thinking. Read the financial, risk and fund-context documents together; no individual accounting ratio supplies a complete investment conclusion.
