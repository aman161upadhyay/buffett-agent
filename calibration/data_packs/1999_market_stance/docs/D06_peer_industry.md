It documents that a strong business with substantial current profits can nevertheless require considerable growth at its quoted valuation. Conversely, different capital intensity, financing subsidiaries, commodity exposure and accounting periods mean that the lowest numerical multiple is not automatically the best investment. No three-stock selection is inferred.

At the market level, October's historical dividend yield is about 1.28%, while the November 22 Treasury yield is 6.09%. Reinvestment and growth could supply additional return, but that is a business-performance requirement rather than an assured distribution. The latest GDP vintage shows equipment/software investment growing at 21.7% annualized versus residential investment falling 6.3%, providing a concrete reason not to equate all industry growth with the aggregate economy.

## Cutoff evidence — reconstructed

The comparisons include equities versus government bonds and transformative industries versus their actual profitability. Cars and aircraft changed society, but intense competition and many entrants limited the set of lasting financial winners. The historical illustration refers to about 2,000 car makes and about 300 aircraft manufacturers during 1919–39, not a forecast of the exact number of Internet failures. Bond values benefited greatly from declining rates in the historical comparison. The lesson for analysis is to identify the return on capital earned by the security holder, not simply predict adoption. No current Nasdaq short, individual technology valuation or hedging instrument is supplied.

## Economic reading of the supplied facts

Portfolio economics: aggregate stock ownership is a claim on business earnings, not a claim on the path of a market index. Dividends distribute part of those earnings; retained earnings add value only if employed productively. Interest rates affect the present value of both equities and bonds. An investor who sells one financial asset must still decide what to hold instead. Cash avoids duration and business risk but is exposed to inflation and the opportunity cost of missed investments. Market averages can obscure large differences among individual businesses. Comparing aggregate prices with book value or profits is a valuation exercise, not a mechanism for forecasting next month's quotation. The financial comparisons below are conditional on earnings durability and the stated measurement dates.

Comparison discipline: differences in accounting, capital intensity and financial leverage can make two nominally similar earnings multiples incomparable. A high return on common equity can come from a strong operating franchise or merely from a thin equity base. The comparison that matters is the durable cash return on all necessary capital, followed by the division of that return among the different claimants.

An outside shareholder cannot assume control benefits or private acquisition synergies. A private buyer's price is useful evidence only if the assets, liabilities and ownership rights are comparable. The alternatives also include retaining liquid funds rather than purchasing a mediocre security merely because it is cheaper than an expensive peer. No industry-average multiple or precise competitor ranking is invented here. The qualitative comparisons identify which operating variables need examination; the absence of a recovered comparable-company table is a genuine limit on numerical relative valuation.
