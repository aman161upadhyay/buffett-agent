Title: Dated statements, not inferred investment rationales
Source: Berkshire letters and releases; Reuters/CNBC interviews; citations [1–7]
Date: 2026-10-25

## November 10, 2025 — Buffett's Thanksgiving message

“I will no longer be writing Berkshire’s annual report or talking endlessly at the annual meeting.” “Greg Abel will become the boss at yearend. He is a great manager, a tireless worker and an honest communicator.” On investment supply: “Because of Berkshire’s size and because of market levels, ideas are few – but not zero.” These are not a liquidation instruction or quantitative targets for Apple, Occidental or Japan. [1]

## February 28, 2026 — Abel's first annual shareholder letter

**Cash/capital discipline:** “We will effectively and efficiently return capital to our owners through share repurchases when the value proposition is compelling.” On core Apple, AmEx, Coke and Moody's: “This concentrated approach will continue, with limited activity in these holdings, though we may significantly adjust a holding if we see fundamental changes in its long-term economic prospects.” [2, Equity Investments]

**Authority:** “At Berkshire, equity investments are fundamental to our capital allocation activities; responsibility ultimately resides with me as CEO.” “Ted Weschler manages about 6% of our investments, including a portion of the portfolio formerly overseen by Todd Combs.” Not attribution of inherited ideas. [2]

**Japan:** “The same criteria apply to our investments in Japan, which we view as comparable to our major U.S. holdings in importance and long-term value creation opportunity.” Financing/holdings in F06; no promise to add at any price. [2]

**OxyChem/Occidental:** Abel identifies Rebecca Liebert and Lubrizol's team as integral to acquisition and planned integration; Liebert assumes responsibility alongside her Lubrizol role, in partnership with OxyChem CEO Wade Alleman. The letter separately identifies Occidental and Kraft Heinz as equity-method investments. Do not interpret integration praise as a promise to acquire Occidental's parent. [2]

**Utilities/rail:** BHE will invest only with appropriately balanced risks/rewards and a functioning regulatory compact. On another Class I acquisition: “Berkshire has been clear that it is not interested in acquiring one of the other Class I railroads, since the current economics would not work in our shareholders’ favor.” This is direct contrary evidence to a rail-merger prediction. [2, BNSF/BHE]

## May 2, 2026 — meeting and sidelines interview

Reuters' May 2 report, updated May 3, records Abel: “It doesn’t mean you need to deploy all your capital and spend all your money.” He says “We want Berkshire to endure,” rejects a breakup and emphasizes understanding economic prospects and risk before investment. Ajit Jain: “It is very difficult to sit there and do nothing,” while others are entertained by brokers. Buffett says “Greg is doing everything I did and then some.” These statements support patience alongside operational engagement, not a categorical equity-buying ban. [3]

Reuters reports March liquidity $380.2bn, superseded by June F01. News Q1 buybacks $234m versus later equity-statement $235m: retain filing basis. [3; F01]

On CNBC at the meeting, Buffett: “We’ve never had more people in a gambling mood than now.” “That doesn’t mean investing is terrible, but it does mean that prices for an awful lot of things will look awfully silly.” Reuters also records praise for Apple and departing CEO Tim Cook, but does not provide a new Apple fair-value estimate. Do not turn this into an attributable instruction to sell Apple. [3]

## July 15, 2026 — Buffett/CNBC interview

On Alphabet: “I initiated it.” On Abel: “I am not doing anything that he doesn’t approve of. He’s not doing anything I don’t approve of. We talk all the time, but he is the decider.” On the general filter: “The trick in life is to find — I mean investing — is to find businesses that are going to earn high returns on capital for an extended period of time.” Buffett says he does not like Alphabet as much as at least four or five other Berkshire businesses, and flags enormous AI capital commitments. This is not an endorsement of capital-intensive AI spending at any valuation. [4]

On Apple: “I know more about Apple than I knew many years ago.” The interview reports it remains a favorite despite Cook's planned departure. Positive franchise judgment can coexist with prior concentration/tax-driven sales; the interview supplies no new numerical buy price. [4]

## September 2026 — Japan and final chairman transition

September 17 Bloomberg/Japan Times reports Abel's Japan visit and an interview with Nikkei in which he said Berkshire may increase stakes. Itochu chair Masahiro Okafuji, describing his meeting with Abel, says Berkshire values trading houses' “economic moat”; the reported discussion mentions 15% stakes. These are **Okafuji's words and third-party reporting**, not a verbatim Buffett promise or a recovered five-company legal permission schedule. [5; F06]

September 18 Buffett's shareholder message: “Greg runs the company; Howard will guard its culture and values – both worth more than anything on our balance sheet.” He says Abel exceeded sky-high expectations and had been making the decisions that matter. Warren becomes chairman emeritus, remains a director; Howard becomes chairman. [6]

**Quote gap:** no authenticated 2026 quote establishing an OXY takeover plan, fresh Apple tax-sale rationale, or firm five-house 15% commitment. The historical 2024 tax discussion is not automatically a later sale's reason. [1–6]
