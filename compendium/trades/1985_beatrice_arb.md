## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**1985:** an event-related transaction, not the economics of permanently owning a food conglomerate, supported this investment. The original announcement, precise financing conditions and purchase-date spread were not recovered. Year-end 1985 Berkshire held 2,350,922 shares at $106.811 million cost and $108.142 million market. Those figures must not be represented as the original spread available before purchase. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“This is a short-term arbitrage holding - in effect, a parking place for money” — **contemporary annual explanation**, March 4, 1986, immediately following the sentence naming Beatrice. Buffett immediately adds that it was not totally safe because deals can fail and create substantial losses. [1]

The 1988 letter's questions about closing probability, time, competing bids and downside provide a **later general framework**, not a recovered Beatrice-specific model. [2]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Acquired the 1985 arbitrage position and disclosed it in the year-end table. It was not a subsidiary acquisition. The subsequent permanent-holdings list does not include Beatrice; the exact closing/disposition ledger and any contingent consideration remain unknown in this dossier. [1–2]

## What happened after
No verified trade-level realized profit was recovered. The $1.331 million year-end mark-to-cost difference is not final P&L. Exact complete disposition, exit month and economic outcome remain unverified in the inspected sources, so status is `unknown`. [1–2]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
