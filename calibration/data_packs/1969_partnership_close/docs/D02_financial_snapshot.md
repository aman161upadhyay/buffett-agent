## Cutoff evidence — reconstructed

The scale reference is about $100 million of Partnership assets. The May letter discusses commitments around $3 million as the minimum scale needed for an idea to matter. Three million dollars is approximately 3% of that reference asset base, an analyst calculation rather than a required target weight. Exact May cash, security-by-security market values and accrued obligations are not reproduced in the recovered reconstruction. Asset growth can lower the impact of otherwise attractive small situations. It does not prove that those businesses are poor investments for a smaller investor. The financial question is capacity at the Partnership level rather than an index earnings forecast.

## Economic reading of the supplied facts

Portfolio economics: aggregate stock ownership is a claim on business earnings, not a claim on the path of a market index. Dividends distribute part of those earnings; retained earnings add value only if employed productively. Interest rates affect the present value of both equities and bonds. An investor who sells one financial asset must still decide what to hold instead. Cash avoids duration and business risk but is exposed to inflation and the opportunity cost of missed investments. Market averages can obscure large differences among individual businesses. Comparing aggregate prices with book value or profits is a valuation exercise, not a mechanism for forecasting next month's quotation. The financial comparisons below are conditional on earnings durability and the stated measurement dates.

Measurement notes: amounts in this reconstruction are nominal dollars, not inflation-adjusted dollars. A historical balance-sheet observation is not a current market quotation. Reported profit, distributable cash and return on tangible operating capital are different quantities. Where a component is missing, owner earnings cannot be calculated precisely by relabeling net income as free cash flow.

The useful reconciliation begins with recurring earnings, adds genuinely noncash charges, and subtracts the expenditures and working capital needed to maintain competitive position. Interest and taxes must be treated consistently with the security being valued. A pretax business yield must not be compared directly with an after-tax common-stock yield. Minority interests, debt, preference claims and cash needed for operations can all change what belongs to the common shareholder. Missing line items are not set to zero. No complete balance sheet, earnings bridge or leverage ratio is implied by the selected figures reproduced here.
