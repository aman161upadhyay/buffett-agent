# 1983 Berkshire annual letter

**Information date:** 1984-03-14 (month-only where precise public release day was not independently established). **Era:** B. **Author:** Buffett. **Primary source:** [letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1983.html). All rules below cite sections of this source; section names are used rather than fabricated PDF page numbers. Annual-period labels are not publication dates.

## Process rules and decision explanations

- Opening owner principles: maximize intrinsic value per share, not size; consider both controlled businesses and minority stocks.
- Goodwill and its Amortization appendix: distinguish economic goodwill from accounting amortization. See’s demonstrates earning power above tangible assets and inflation resilience.
- Nebraska Furniture Mart: manager integrity, low-cost customer proposition and a family ownership transition explain the acquisition; this is not an auction-based optimization.
- Controlled-business sale reluctance is explicitly qualified by relationships and manager/labor considerations; do not apply the same permanence to every public stock.
- GEICO tender: a proportional sale preserved ownership percentage and had tax treatment important to the economic outcome.

## Verbatim evidence anchors

- **Chairman's Letter - 1983; Delegation of investment authority** “We will not diversify by purchasing entire businesses at control prices that ignore long-term economic consequences to our shareholders. We will only do with your money what we would do with our own”
- **Nebraska Furniture Mart; Purchase of Nebraska Furniture Mart** “Mrs. B is wise as well as smart and, for far-sighted family reasons, was willing to sell the business last year. I had admired both the family and the business for decades”
- **Appendix; Purchase of See’s Candies** “In 1972 (and now) relatively few businesses could be expected to consistently earn the 25% after tax on net tangible assets that was earned by See’s – doing it, furthermore, with conservative accounting”
- **Sources of Reported Earnings; Sale/redemption of GEICO shares** “At GEICO’s request, we tendered a quantity of shares that kept our ownership percentage the same after the transaction as before. The proportional nature of our sale permitted us to treat the proceeds as a dividend.”
