## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**1987 after Texaco entered bankruptcy:** uncertainty over Pennzoil litigation depressed Texaco's short-maturity bonds. The critical distinction was issuer solvency/settlement value, not a forecast for oil prices. Exact first purchase day, issue identifiers and original legal recovery analysis remain unknown. The year-end $104 million carrying value is not stated to be aggregate initial cash cost. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“On a worst-case basis with respect to the Pennzoil litigation, we felt the bonds were likely to be worth about what we paid for them.” **Post-purchase**, February 29, 1988. [1]

“Given a sensible settlement, which seemed likely, we expected the bonds to be worth considerably more.” Same letter, with a clear scenario-based downside/upside comparison. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Bought various short-maturity Texaco bonds **after** the bankruptcy filing. At December 31, 1987 carrying value was $104 million and estimated market $119 million. During 1988 Berkshire sold practically all these short/intermediate bonds, realizing approximately $22 million pretax profit. Do not claim literal disposal of every last bond. [1–2]

## What happened after
The profitable resolution supported the litigation-credit assessment. Buffett emphasized that the commitment would be inappropriate without the insurance subsidiaries' unusually strong capitalization. The return therefore cannot be generalized into a routine way for thinly capitalized insurers to boost yield. Exact holding duration and coupon-inclusive IRR are unknown. [1–2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Downside:** even the litigation worst case was judged to preserve approximately the invested value, not guaranteed to do so. [1]
- **Catalyst:** a sensible settlement, rather than perpetual holding, drove upside. [1]
- **Relative value:** Buffett called these the most attractive available bond investment at purchase prices. [1]
- **Capacity:** defaulted-bond ownership was conditional on exceptional financial strength. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: an opportunistic credit position under a businesslike legal-value analysis. It is not common-stock merger arbitrage and does not require belief in a permanent oil-company moat. [1–2]

## Public discussion
Original bankruptcy filings and competing Pennzoil legal analyses remain unrecovered. The annual letter provides Buffett's scenario assessment, not a legal guarantee or proof of market consensus. [1]
