Templates:
B1 wonderful_business_fair_price — no crisis required; the market under-rates durability or growth of a franchise; evidence:
   long record, pricing power, reinvestment runway, price below what a private owner would pay
B2 franchise_under_temporary_cloud — scandal, lawsuit, cyclical panic or industry fear that does not impair the franchise.
   Apply the three-part test Buffett used at American Express 1964, GEICO 1976 and Wells Fargo 1990: (i) customers keep using
   the product through the cloud (cards and cheques still accepted; policies still renewing; deposits stable) — look for this in
   D01/D05/D07; (ii) the loss is a one-time charge that a few years of normal earnings can absorb — put a number on it; (iii) the
   fall in market value exceeds the bounded loss. If all three hold, the liability is a finite deduction from value, not a reason
   to pass, and the template calls for a LARGE position (Era A: up to 40% of the partnership). Fail the template only when (i)

B3 lender_of_last_resort_negotiated_preferred — a strong company needs capital or confidence and pays a coupon plus warrants/

   priced for a dilutive raise and the franchise is intact, the right answer can be "BUY — via a negotiated preferred with
   warrants on these terms", not PASS and not open-market common; name the terms you would ask for
B11 structured_partner_deal — Berkshire supplies the capital package (a 9% preferred for most of the money plus half the common

   subordinated notes and preferred; 3G at Burger King 2014 with a 9% preferred). The price the partner pays for the whole company
   is not Berkshire's cost: value the package — the senior coupon carries most of the return, the common is the kicker, and the
   partner's operating record is the catalyst. Evidence: coupon, seniority, partner's record, brand durability under the leverage
B4 whole_business_to_keep — consistent earnings, good returns, little debt, management in place, price known, no auction;
