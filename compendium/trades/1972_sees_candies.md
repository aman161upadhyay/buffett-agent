## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**Early 1972 business, reconstructed from later letters:** a well-known West Coast boxed-chocolate manufacturer/retailer earned approximately $4 million pretax using about $8 million net tangible assets. The selling family wanted $30 million. The entry financials and negotiating facts come from later Buffett accounts; no original acquisition memorandum was recovered. They are not 1972 public financial statements. [1–3]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“A broad and durable competitive advantage that gave it significant pricing power.” **Hindsight**, 2014 letter's explanation of the purchase. [3]

“My misguided caution could have scuttled a terrific purchase.” Same retrospective: Munger supported $30 million, while Buffett capped his bid at $25 million. This was a joint intellectual transition; the `Buffett` label must not erase Munger's role. [3]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Blue Chip bought See's early in 1972 for $25 million. Subsequent consolidation brought See's wholly under Blue Chip, and Berkshire's 1983 Blue Chip merger acquired the remaining indirect minority interests. The initial purchase and later group consolidations are not separate independent See's operating acquisitions for P&L aggregation. [1,3]

## What happened after
By the 2014 letter See's had generated $1.9 billion pretax with only $40 million of additional investment, enabling cash distributions for other purchases. Those are cumulative business economics, not an annualized return on Buffett's personal capital. The 1983 letter also discusses stalled poundage and cost pressures: success was not simply unlimited volume growth. [1,3]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Brand:** customer preference was an economic asset absent from tangible book value. [1,3]
- **Pricing power:** ability to raise prices distinguished See's from commodity manufacturers. [3]
- **Capital efficiency:** earnings were large relative to tangible capital. [1–3]
- **Low reinvestment:** earnings growth could become distributable cash instead of being absorbed by more factories. [2–3]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: a central move away from buying merely cheap assets toward paying for economic goodwill. Buffett's willingness barely exceeded his old tangible-value anchor; the near-miss is part of the lesson. `open` is retained ownership at the cited follow-up, not a 2026 disposition audit. [1–3]

## Public discussion
Original 1972 press coverage is unrecovered. The primary letters offer both the favorable long-term outcome and adverse operating-volume evidence; no later blog or social discussion is mislabeled contemporary. [1–3]
