## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**1966 decision set, reconstructed retrospectively:** Disney had earned approximately $21 million pretax in 1965, had more cash than debt, and its equity market valuation was below $90 million. Buffett recalled the $17 million Pirates of the Caribbean ride as about to open. Do not treat its later opening or eventual profitability as known at purchase. Exact first purchase month and trailing valuation as of that month are unknown. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“Imagine my excitement - a company selling at only five times rides!” **Hindsight**, March 1, 1996. This is Buffett's humorous comparison with the cost of one attraction, not a five-times-earnings valuation. [1]

“Oh well - we're happy to be once again a large owner of a business with both unique assets and outstanding management.” Same letter, on the later Disney position. This is not a 1966 management quote. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Buffett Partnership bought Disney in 1966 at a retrospectively stated split-adjusted 31 cents/share and sold in 1967 at 48 cents. The 1996 Cap Cities/Disney transaction and direct Disney purchases are separate episodes. The primary account here does not provide the initial month, exact cost or the original sale rationale. [1]

## What happened after
The approximately 55% price increase between the cited split-adjusted purchase/sale figures is arithmetic, not an audited fund return including distributions and costs. Buffett explicitly contrasted the sale with Disney's much greater subsequent value. The trade made money but the premature exit forfeited compounding. A quantified modern “lost billions” number would depend on unverified reinvestment and holding assumptions. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Earnings discount:** the cited 1965 earnings were substantial against the 1966 valuation. [1]
- **Unique assets:** films and theme parks supported a business-value case beyond liquidation assets. [1–2]
- **Balance sheet:** excess cash over debt strengthened the price/value comparison. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: an early quality-at-a-bargain investment followed by a sale inconsistent with his later compounding preference. Original 1967 sell reasoning remains unknown; do not invent a conclusion that the business was impaired. [1]

## Public discussion
Carol Loomis's Fortune article was originally April 1, 1996 and republished November 21, 2012. It is independent retrospective corroboration, not 1966 public sentiment. Original 1966 sell-side coverage remains missing. [2]
