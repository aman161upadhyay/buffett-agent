## Cutoff evidence — reconstructed

September 30 is an agreement-and-announcement boundary: the dated UPI report says the terms have shareholder approval at Dexter and require federal antitrust clearance. The replay supplies those terms while withholding the announcement's investor recommendation. Dexter operates plants in Maine and Puerto Rico and is to retain incumbent management. Berkshire's H. H. Brown ownership is pre-existing context. No completion event, final issued-share tally or post-acquisition result is included. The fact that the agreement has been announced means this is a proposal-economics replay on the announcement day, not a claim of an unpublished pre-signing memorandum. Regulatory clearance remains prospective.

## Economic reading of the supplied facts

Industry economics: footwear requires design, purchasing, manufacturing or sourcing, distribution and inventory across styles and sizes. Unsold inventory can be worth less than recorded cost. A good domestic factory can still be vulnerable if imports deliver an acceptable product at materially lower cost. Brand, quality and delivery speed may support customer loyalty, but each must be tested against the consumer's alternatives. Owning stores can capture retail margin while adding leases, inventory and fixed expenses. Efficient operations are valuable only to the extent that they sustain economic returns after all capital requirements. This is a description of competitive possibilities at the cutoff, not a prediction that domestic production must succeed or fail.

Chronology controls: the cutoff is an information boundary, not proof of an execution on that day. A negotiated commitment, a public announcement, a purchase settlement and an annual-report disclosure are distinct events. Facts about the condition of the business may be reconstructed from archival accounts without turning a later explanation into a statement available to the market.

Only the events described above enter this chronology. No subsequent result is assumed when assessing the unresolved question. The absence of an original day-by-day price series prevents identification of a precise entry-day return or volume pattern. The same restriction applies to unpublished board discussions and security-specific purchase ledgers. This reconstruction does not claim access to either. Where a transaction is being considered, proposed terms are identified as proposed; regulatory approvals and operating improvements are not treated as completed. Where shares already exist in a portfolio, a request to study a new allocation must not erase that exposure.
