## Cutoff evidence — reconstructed

Rounded pre-cutoff business-scale anchors in the reconstructed purchase discussion are approximately $56 billion assets and approximately $48 billion loans, with a prior return record above 20% on equity and 1.25% on assets. Exact quarter-end reconciliation and the current nonperforming-loan schedule were not recovered. The annual earning-power reference exceeds $1 billion pretax after more than $300 million annual loan-loss expense, but the original source is a purchase-period reconstruction rather than a recovered October earnings release. It should be stress-tested, not treated as an audited forecast. Owner earnings for a bank also depend on maintaining capital and providing realistically for credit losses.

## Economic reading of the supplied facts

Industry economics: a bank's assets consist largely of promises to repay. Small changes in the value of loans can have large effects on a leveraged common-equity base. Deposits provide funding, but do not make credit losses disappear. A bank's expense discipline matters because pre-provision earnings can absorb losses before capital is impaired. Commercial-property lending requires attention to borrower equity, collateral and refinancing rather than merely the last reported interest payment. Reported earnings can be overstated when loss provisions lag actual deterioration. Comparing banks therefore requires both operating efficiency and a conservative estimate of asset quality. These mechanics explain why a low price-to-earnings ratio alone is not sufficient evidence of a bargain, and why depositor funding is not a substitute for adequate capital.

Measurement notes: amounts in this reconstruction are nominal dollars, not inflation-adjusted dollars. A historical balance-sheet observation is not a current market quotation. Reported profit, distributable cash and return on tangible operating capital are different quantities. Where a component is missing, owner earnings cannot be calculated precisely by relabeling net income as free cash flow.

The useful reconciliation begins with recurring earnings, adds genuinely noncash charges, and subtracts the expenditures and working capital needed to maintain competitive position. Interest and taxes must be treated consistently with the security being valued. A pretax business yield must not be compared directly with an after-tax common-stock yield. Minority interests, debt, preference claims and cash needed for operations can all change what belongs to the common shareholder. Missing line items are not set to zero. No complete balance sheet, earnings bridge or leverage ratio is implied by the selected figures reproduced here.
