Title: Industry comparisons and opportunity cost
Source: five-house March 2020 financial reports and April–June releases linked in D02/D03 (May–August 2020); July 2020 historical ordinary-share price panel linked in D04
Date: 2020-08-30

Compare each trading house's resource and non-resource mix, governance, capital discipline, cash conversion and valuation. Japanese banks, industrial exporters and pure commodity producers are imperfect alternatives. A basket should not conceal poor capital allocation in one member. Joint ventures can create opportunities, but synergy value should not be included without a specific executable proposal and fair sharing of economics. [Source above.]

Net/revenue is arithmetic, not a standardized operating margin: equity-accounted profits sit outside revenue and principal/agent presentation differs. The numerical peer panel therefore compares book discounts with reported returns, not an assumed common revenue multiple. D04 supplies dated capitalization, P/E and dividends for all five, with the rounded July price convention; no synchronized independent forward consensus has been recovered.

Sumitomo's five-year earnings range, ¥74.5bn–¥320.5bn, and Marubeni's current-year loss warn against capitalizing one resource-cycle peak. Sumitomo OCF less total investing cash flow expenditure was ¥123.2bn in March 2020 versus ¥217.6bn a year earlier. That issuer-style free cash flow includes asset purchases/disposals, unlike OCF minus maintenance investment. Mitsubishi similarly reported OCF plus investing cash flow of ¥349.0bn in March 2020; Mitsui's equivalent was ¥341.1bn. Itochu's plain property/intangible-purchase deduction gives ¥678.6bn, but is a different definition and must not be ranked against those figures as if identical.

Three peer reports, the July price histories and the June quarterly disclosures were checked. The strongest recoverable distinction is actual return on book and pandemic profit sensitivity; no unsupported fair-value target, synergy premium or “industry average” EV multiple is supplied. Maintenance capital, associate look-through debt and cross-holding liquidity remain essential missing inputs before calling reported earnings owner earnings.

Technical context: general trading houses combine operating assets, financial stakes and joint ventures. Equity-accounted earnings, commodity gains and asset dispositions require reconciliation with dividends actually received. Net debt must be evaluated alongside the liquidity and risk of the underlying holdings, not offset indiscriminately against all reported investments. Conglomerate discounts can reflect governance or cross-holdings, not just market error. Yen debt matched against yen assets limits one currency mismatch while leaving operating currency exposures and interest costs. A basket should use ordinary-share prices, consistent fiscal dates and the correct local units. Diversification does not by itself establish a superior return on capital. Comparison discipline: compare businesses on the source of their cash and the capital required to earn it, rather than treating the nearest ticker as an exact substitute. Normalize financing and accounting differences before interpreting relative multiples. A peer can have a better balance sheet but a worse franchise, or a better franchise at an unattractive price. Relative cheapness alone cannot prove absolute value. Consider the opportunity cost of adding to an existing understandable holding, retaining liquidity, owning an entire business or buying the investor's own shares. Where a numerical peer table was not recovered, keep the comparison qualitative rather than inventing an industry average.

Evidence boundary: missing figures are not available in contemporaneous sources recovered for this digest. Analytical questions are not additional reported facts or claims about the investor's private thinking. Read the financial, risk and fund-context documents together; no individual accounting ratio supplies a complete investment conclusion.
