The July 18 period report gives second-quarter net income of $232.4 million versus $147.4 million and first-half income of $392.2 million versus $288.9 million. ## Economic reading of the supplied facts

Industry economics: a bank's assets consist largely of promises to repay. Small changes in the value of loans can have large effects on a leveraged common-equity base. Deposits provide funding, but do not make credit losses disappear. A bank's expense discipline matters because pre-provision earnings can absorb losses before capital is impaired. Commercial-property lending requires attention to borrower equity, collateral and refinancing rather than merely the last reported interest payment. Reported earnings can be overstated when loss provisions lag actual deterioration. Comparing banks therefore requires both operating efficiency and a conservative estimate of asset quality. These mechanics explain why a low price-to-earnings ratio alone is not sufficient evidence of a bargain, and why depositor funding is not a substitute for adequate capital.

Reporting discipline: a full fiscal year's results are not available merely because part of that year has elapsed. The observations above are restricted to the reporting periods identified, and no later quarter is backfilled. An annualized partial period would be a calculation, not a reported result; no such annualization is used unless expressly identified.

For decision purposes, the key question is whether the recent change alters recurring earning power or only its timing. Volume, realized price, operating expenses and capital requirements should be considered separately. A price increase that merely offsets higher input costs is not necessarily an improvement in economics. Similarly, a recovery in accounting profit is not proof that competitive advantage has returned. Where a current operating statistic has not been recovered, this document makes no assertion about its direction. The evidence permits a conditional assessment of durability, not a precise estimate of an unavailable earnings announcement.
