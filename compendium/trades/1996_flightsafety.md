## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**1996:** FlightSafety trained pilots using a capital-intensive simulator network. A shareholder intermediary suggested Berkshire as a home for founder Al Ueltschi's business. Do not describe this as an asset-light acquisition. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
The letter calls FlightSafety “the world's leader in the training of pilots.” Buffett reports longstanding familiarity with the business; the founder sought a buyer whose shares he could comfortably retain. **February 1997 account.** [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Met Ueltschi September 18, 1996, and completed the approximately $1.5 billion transaction in December, using cash and Class A/Class B stock. The letter identifies the shareholder referral and Bob Denham's intermediary role. [1]

## What happened after
This record verifies acquisition, not a realized investment return. Original merger documents and a standalone long-run capital-return calculation remain gaps. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Franchise:** Leadership in specialized training. [1]
- **People:** Ueltschi remained central to the acquisition assessment. [1]
- **Fit:** A permanent home and acceptable Berkshire consideration mattered to the seller. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: a negotiated quality-business purchase, despite significant reinvestment needs. Stock issuance must be evaluated rather than assumed costless. [1]

## Public discussion
The letter documents the shareholder-originated idea; independent contemporary investor reaction not recovered. [1]
