# 1999 Berkshire annual letter

**Information date:** 2000-03-01 (month-only where precise public release day was not independently established). **Era:** B. **Author:** Buffett. **Primary source:** [letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1999htm.html). All rules below cite sections of this source; section names are used rather than fabricated PDF page numbers. Annual-period labels are not publication dates.

## Process rules and decision explanations

- Investment discussion: refusal to forecast fast-changing technology was a circle-of-competence limit, not a universal judgment that technology businesses are worthless.
- Opening results: poor relative stock performance and a major Gen Re underwriting loss require candid recognition. Reputation was not sufficient protection against underwriting deterioration.
- A Managerial Story You Will Never Read Elsewhere: R. C. Willey’s manager demonstrated conviction and alignment in the Boise expansion; note the specific risk-sharing arrangement rather than invent a general manager-investment requirement.
- MidAmerican: utility-holding-company law shaped voting and security structure. Economic ownership and voting control are distinct.
- Guides to Intrinsic Value: consider operating earnings and investments together without double counting earnings on invested securities.

## Verbatim evidence anchors

- **Chairman's Letter - 1999; Acquisition policy focused on acquiring businesses with outstanding managers and economic characteristics comparable to those Berkshire already owns.** “Our goal is to run our present businesses well -- a task made easy because of the outstanding managers we have in place -- and to acquire additional businesses”
- **A Managerial Story You Will Never Read Elsewhere; Delegation decision allowing R. C. Willey manager Bill Child to manage his own expansion while personally underwriting the initial risk of the Boise store.** “Bill then insisted on a truly extraordinary proposition: He would personally buy the land and build the store -- for about $9 million as it turned out”
- **Investments; Refusal to invest in technology company stocks because Berkshire lacks insights into which participants possess a truly durable competitive advantage.** “This explains, by the way, why we don't own stocks of tech companies, even though we share the general view that our society will be transformed by their products”
- **Acquisitions of 1999; Acquisition policy to structure the MidAmerican Energy investment to comply with the Public Utility Holding Company Act of 1935 by limiting voting power.** “we had to structure a transaction that would avoid Berkshire gaining voting control. Instead we are purchasing an 11% fixed-income security, along with a combination of common stock”
