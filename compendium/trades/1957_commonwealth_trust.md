## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**1957–1958, reconstructed from February 1959:** no dividend despite about $10/share earnings contributed to an approximately $50 quotation; Buffett estimated $125 intrinsic value. A larger bank owned 25.5% and had long wanted a merger, blocked for personal reasons that might eventually change. With only about 300 holders and two monthly trades, market-index movements had little bearing on execution. Exact first purchase date remains unknown; the letter identifies it as the previous year's large holding. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“Very strong defensive characteristics” and “Good solid value building up at a satisfactory pace” were two of Buffett's three enumerated attractions. **Post-exit**, February 11, 1959. The third was eventual realization, which he acknowledged could take one or ten years. [1]

“Our block of stock increased in value as its size grew” — same letter, distinguishing value of meaningful ownership from an ordinary small market lot. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
The **partnerships, not Berkshire**, accumulated approximately 12% over about a year at an average $51/share. Competition lifted the quotation to $65, at which Buffett was neither buyer nor seller. Late in 1958 he sold the block at $80, about 20% over the quoted market, to finance a more attractive large-block opportunity. The date is year-only evidence, not a fabricated December closing. [1]

## What happened after
The exit yielded an approximate 57% gross price gain on the $51 average cost, **research arithmetic**, excluding costs and any other cash flows. Buffett estimated intrinsic value had risen to $135, said the buyer could still do well, and preferred the replacement opportunity. A successful sale need not imply the sold business became bad or fully valued. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Discount:** substantial margin between conservative value and quotation. [1]
- **Compounding:** earnings increased value while realization was delayed. [1]
- **Catalyst:** merger potential existed without a firm timetable. [1]
- **Block:** second-largest holder could expect consultation on proposals and a block premium. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: early value investing with endogenous ownership leverage rather than a fixed-date announced merger. It provides an explicit opportunity-cost sell rule, countering the claim that every attractive bank must be held forever. [1]

## Public discussion
No contemporary independent bank-merger reporting recovered. The post-exit partner letter is the direct thesis source; the claimed intrinsic values are Buffett's estimates, not audited liquidation proceeds. [1]
