## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**September 1989:** Berkshire could issue long-duration convertible debt despite Buffett's criticism of many zero-coupon/PIK financings. The relevant distinction was borrower capacity and economic cost, not the security label itself. Original prospectus, balance-sheet stress calculation and issue-day common price were not recovered; the following letter supplies the terms and explanation. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“Thus the net effect to us, resulting from the reduced taxes, is positive cash flow. That is a very significant benefit.” **Post-issue**, March 2, 1990, describing annual accrued-interest deductions without cash payments. [1]

“Neither our bonds nor those of certain other companies that issued similar bonds last year ... resemble the great bulk of zero-coupon bonds that have been issued in recent years.” Same explanation, with an ellipsis omitting named peer issuers. He condemns using zeros merely to conceal inability to service debt. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Issued $902.6 million face in September 1989, at 44.314% of maturity value, raising $400 million before about $9.5 million expenses. Fifteen-year maturity and 5.5% semiannually compounded yield. Each $10,000 face bond converted into 0.4515 Berkshire shares, equivalent to about $9,815/share conversion price and a 15% premium at issuance. Berkshire could call after September 28, 1992 at accreted value; holders had puts on September 28, 1994 and 1999. [1]

## What happened after
The letter says the tax benefit made Berkshire's effective cost well below 5.5%, while unknown variables prevented an exact calculation. Future accretion remained a genuine liability even without cash coupon. Final redemption/conversion accounting was not recovered, so `status: unknown` and no manufactured financing IRR are supplied. `flat` denotes no comparable long-position profit, not zero economic value. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Cash timing:** no current coupon did not mean interest-free financing. [1]
- **Taxes:** annual deductions created cash benefits before eventual bondholder payment. [1]
- **Equity option:** conversion at a premium exchanged potential future dilution for lower financing cost. [1]
- **Credit discipline:** sound borrower economics mattered more than the promise to pay nothing currently. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: a capital-structure decision, not a preferred-stock purchase. Criticizing dangerous zeros while issuing a conservatively supportable one is not a contradiction; evaluate substance, repayment capacity and dilution. [1]

## Public discussion
Buffett's letter criticizes Wall Street's indiscriminate enthusiasm for zero/PIK instruments. No original underwriting prospectus or independent issue-day debate was recovered. Salomon's underwriting role is disclosed in the primary letter. [1]
