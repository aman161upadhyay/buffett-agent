Title: General Re Corporation — Security and transaction terms
Source: Proposed June 19 merger terms and pre-cutoff issuer business; archival June 19 agreement in https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/317745/000089882298000658/0000898822-98-000658.txt (filed later; only agreement-date terms reconstructed); fund reference https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1997.html
Date: 1998-06-19

## Instrument and consideration — reconstructed

The proposed all-stock transaction is valued at about $22 billion at announcement prices. General Re adds approximately $24 billion of invested assets in period deal descriptions. Each General Re share is to receive 0.0035 Berkshire Class A shares or 0.105 Class B shares. The stock ratios, rather than an invariant dollar amount, define the economic exchange. A fully reconciled latest loss-reserve schedule, capital requirement and look-through minority adjustment were not recovered in the pack. The invested assets cannot simply be subtracted from the price as surplus cash because policyholder and reinsurance obligations support much of that portfolio. Financial-products exposures require their own capital and counterparty analysis.

## Valuation units

The agreed exchange ratio is 0.0035 Class A or 0.105 Class B shares for each General Re share. Announcement-value consideration is approximately $22 billion; the precise value changes with Berkshire's quotation. The exact cutoff common close and a clean stand-alone owner-earnings multiple are not reconstructed. Assets under investment are not equivalent to purchase-price cash recovery, because reserves and regulatory capital must remain. A stock exchange also requires appraising Berkshire's own per-share intrinsic value. No tax advantage or diversification benefit is valued as a certainty without a source. The return must arise from sound underwriting and responsible use of investments, not merely from enlarging the balance sheet.

## Ownership and contractual boundaries

The security and the operating business are separate objects of analysis. A whole-company buyer obtains the residual economics after the business meets its obligations, while a minority shareholder cannot unilaterally direct distributions. Preferred stock normally ranks ahead of common for its specified claims, but its legal priority is not a guarantee of payment and does not supersede all creditors. Conversion changes the form and ranking of exposure rather than creating a second independent asset that can be counted twice.

Where the complete original agreement is unavailable, this summary does not imply that an unmentioned covenant exists or that an unmentioned restriction is absent. Proposed consideration can change in dollar value before closing when the exchange uses shares. Regulatory conditions, issuance date, payment date and accounting acquisition date should be verified separately. No subsequent financial result or investor recommendation is supplied by this terms summary.
