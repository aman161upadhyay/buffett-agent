Title: Credit, duration, deposit pricing and regulatory capital
Source: https://d1io3yog0oux5.cloudfront.net/_70c8fb58fd3bea7a80c281a02cf2c22d/bankofamerica/db/806/10171/earnings_release/The+Press+Release_1Q25_ADA.pdf
Date: 2025-06-30

The strongest bear mechanisms are a credit cycle, higher funding costs and a mismatch between the duration of assets and liabilities. Stable quarterly charge-offs do not rule out delayed deterioration. Approximately $2 trillion deposits constitute both a franchise and a large obligation; competition can raise the cost of retaining them. A bank's securities and loan yields can reprice on a different schedule from deposits. These are analytical stress scenarios, not a claim that this release reports a new run on deposits. [1]

The current release gives positive net interest income growth, an 11.8% CET1 ratio and substantial liquidity sources. A skeptic nevertheless needs a margin of safety against adverse credit and rate assumptions. Wealth and trading earnings diversify revenue but can be cyclical. Operational, conduct and regulatory requirements can consume capital or increase costs. No independently recovered bearish price target is attributed to a named analyst. The useful opposition is specific to the balance sheet and the price paid, not the unsupported assertion that any bank is uninvestable.

Evidence ledger from the April 15 release: first-quarter net income was $7.4 billion, versus $6.7 billion a year earlier; diluted EPS was $0.90, versus $0.76. Revenue net of interest expense was $27.4 billion, or $27.5 billion fully tax equivalent. Net interest income was $14.4 billion, or $14.6 billion FTE, up 3%. Noninterest expense was $17.8 billion and credit provision $1.5 billion; net charge-offs of $1.5 billion were flat versus both comparison quarters. Ending deposits were approximately $2 trillion. Standardized CET1 was 11.8%, backed by $201 billion common equity tier 1 capital. Book value per common share was $36.39. Capital returned totaled $6.5 billion, comprising $2.0 billion common dividends and $4.5 billion repurchases. [1]

Risk distinction: a temporary earnings interruption and a permanent impairment can initially produce similar reported numbers. The test is whether the cause can be bounded without assuming that management's preferred recovery path must occur. Position liquidity limits execution risk, not business risk. The adverse mechanisms set out here are scenarios grounded in the cited contemporary evidence, not claims that every adverse outcome has occurred. No independent short-seller target or numerical probability is invented where a dated opposing valuation was not recovered.
