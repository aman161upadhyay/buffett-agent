Title: Adverse case and permanent-loss risks
Source: https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2019ltr.pdf (published 2020-02-22)
Date: 2020-08-30

A skeptical view is that conglomerate discounts are justified by opaque holdings, commodity cycles, leverage, low returns on equity and minority shareholders' limited influence. Currency-matched debt reduces one exposure but does not hedge business losses or dividend reductions. Five names diversify idiosyncratic holdings while retaining substantial shared Japanese and global trade exposure. A high dividend yield can signal risk rather than a permanently safe carry. [Source above.]

Technical context: general trading houses combine operating assets, financial stakes and joint ventures. Equity-accounted earnings, commodity gains and asset dispositions require reconciliation with dividends actually received. Net debt must be evaluated alongside the liquidity and risk of the underlying holdings, not offset indiscriminately against all reported investments. Conglomerate discounts can reflect governance or cross-holdings, not just market error. Yen debt matched against yen assets limits one currency mismatch while leaving operating currency exposures and interest costs. A basket should use ordinary-share prices, consistent fiscal dates and the correct local units. Diversification does not by itself establish a superior return on capital. [Source above; analytical framework.]

Risk discipline: the adverse cases above are analyst stress tests grounded in the disclosed business and current circumstances, not quotations attributed to an unnamed contemporary short seller. No authenticated bearish price target was recovered. Separate a temporary quotation decline from permanent impairment of earning power or of the investor's claim. Ask whether the issuer can fund necessary operations without assuming favorable capital markets. Identify which changes are within management control and which are not. A large cash balance at the investor does not cure an issuer's leverage, a faulty operating thesis or an excessive purchase price. Do not soften a serious downside merely because an admired investor might be interested.
