GDP acceleration and strong equipment/software investment support a contemporaneous bull case; declining residential investment and slowing consumption provide counterweights. The advance estimate is explicitly subject to revision, with the next release scheduled for November 24, after this cutoff. No later revised GDP number is substituted. The economy's growth rate cannot be equated with per-share equity returns: starting valuation, distributions, share issuance, sector composition and the proportion of output captured as profit all intervene. A numerical aggregate next-year EPS consensus remains unrecovered rather than being inferred from GDP.

## Cutoff evidence — reconstructed

The article notes a July survey in which newer investors expected 22.6% annual returns over ten years and investors with over twenty years of experience expected 12.9%. Those are expectations, not realized or promised business returns. It contrasts the latest strong market experience with much slower underlying economic growth. The historical Dow comparison is 875 to 9,181 over the end-1981 to end-1998 period, with dividends adding to owner returns. A complete current-quarter aggregate earnings release is not recovered. The useful latest evidence is the gap between return expectations and the profits available at the observed prices, not any knowledge of a subsequent correction.

## Economic reading of the supplied facts

Portfolio economics: aggregate stock ownership is a claim on business earnings, not a claim on the path of a market index. Dividends distribute part of those earnings; retained earnings add value only if employed productively. Interest rates affect the present value of both equities and bonds. An investor who sells one financial asset must still decide what to hold instead. Cash avoids duration and business risk but is exposed to inflation and the opportunity cost of missed investments. Market averages can obscure large differences among individual businesses. Comparing aggregate prices with book value or profits is a valuation exercise, not a mechanism for forecasting next month's quotation. The financial comparisons below are conditional on earnings durability and the stated measurement dates.

Reporting discipline: a full fiscal year's results are not available merely because part of that year has elapsed. The observations above are restricted to the reporting periods identified, and no later quarter is backfilled. An annualized partial period would be a calculation, not a reported result; no such annualization is used unless expressly identified.

For decision purposes, the key question is whether the recent change alters recurring earning power or only its timing. Volume, realized price, operating expenses and capital requirements should be considered separately. A price increase that merely offsets higher input costs is not necessarily an improvement in economics. Similarly, a recovery in accounting profit is not proof that competitive advantage has returned. Where a current operating statistic has not been recovered, this document makes no assertion about its direction. The evidence permits a conditional assessment of durability, not a precise estimate of an unavailable earnings announcement.
