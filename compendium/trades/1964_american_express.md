## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**1964 crisis decision set:** the salad-oil warehousing fraud created substantial claims against American Express. The relevant underwriting question was whether those claims permanently destroyed the value and customer acceptance of the separate travelers-check and card businesses. Original 1964 filings and Buffett's purchase ledger have not been recovered, so exact liability estimates and entry-date normalized earnings are unknown here. [1–2]

The Fortune retrospective reports the stock falling from $61.81 to $40 between November 20 and December 2, 1963, and reaching $35.31 on June 2, 1964. These are historical quotations in a later article, not proof of Buffett's execution price. It reports $3 million invested by June 1964, 17% of partnership assets. This dated observation must not be replaced with the widely repeated later $13 million/40% peak. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“Both were one-of-a-kind companies, temporarily reeling from the effects of a fiscal blow that did not destroy their exceptional underlying economics.” **Buffett's retrospective**, February 27, 1981, explicitly comparing American Express in 1964 with GEICO in 1976. This is direct primary-source evidence for separating a temporary financial injury from exceptional franchise economics. [3]
