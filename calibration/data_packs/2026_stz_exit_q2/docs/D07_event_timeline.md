January 7: the company publishes the quarter ended November 30, with reported EPS of $2.88, comparable EPS $3.06 and continuing beer depletions pressure. The same release gives updated reported annual guidance and unchanged comparable guidance. February 17: the last public Berkshire 13F provides an existing-holding checkpoint, described in D08. March 31: the stock closes at $150.00. [1; fund source in D08; historical price below]

There is no completed annual earnings release in this set despite the fiscal year having ended. There is also no publicly known current-quarter ownership change merely because another calendar quarter has elapsed. The analyst can reassess the original plausible brand-franchise thesis against lower volumes, high current margins, capital needs and valuation, without importing a later corporate announcement. Nothing in the timeline proves who originated the holding or assigns a particular sell reason to that person. The distinction between an observed economic concern and a documented investor explanation is maintained throughout.

Evidence ledger from the January 7 release, covering the November quarter: total net sales were $2.223 billion, down 10% reported but 2% organically; reported EPS $2.88 and comparable EPS $3.06. Beer net sales of $2.0097 billion fell 1%; shipments fell 2.2%, depletions 3.0%, and beer operating income was $763.5 million. Beer operating margin was 38.0%. Modelo Especial depletions fell about 4%, Corona Extra nearly 9%, while Pacifico and Victoria grew over 15% and 13%. Year-to-date operating cash flow was $2.1 billion and free cash flow $1.45 billion. Annual comparable EPS guidance remained $11.30-$11.60; reported guidance was $9.72-$10.02. Guided capital spending was approximately $1.2 billion, including approximately $1.0 billion for Mexican beer operations. Comparable net leverage target was approximately 3.0 times. [1]

Timing discipline: the chronology separates publication dates from the economic periods discussed. A result for an earlier quarter only becomes an input when released. The market close is an observation available on the stated assessment date, not evidence of a particular investor's execution. This timeline includes no presumed private conversations or unannounced agreement. There is no catalyst completion forecast here: the analyst must decide whether the available business evidence is sufficient without relying on a future disclosure to reveal someone else's decision.
