The target's same FT snapshot is market value $65.48 billion, P/E 6.3x and common dividend yield .8%. This is a March historical screen, not a September solvency ranking. Deposit-funded groups and a securities dealer do not have interchangeable funding franchises, asset marks or regulatory capital. Earnings multiples based on 2007 trading profits may be low because the denominator is cyclically high. Dividend yield is also not a promised loss-absorbing cushion.

The target's August common book $99.30 and tangible book $87.11 per share differ by $12.19; comparing current price with tangible book avoids treating goodwill as creditor protection. However, the tangible amount can still move with asset marks. The proposed preferred's 10% coupon, 110% issuer call and separate five-year warrants form a different claim from every common-equity row. P/TBV on a common share does not prove preferred credit safety or quantify a warrant's option value.

Morgan Stanley is the closest US investment-bank comparison, but its capital structure and funding situation must be examined separately. Large commercial banks have deposit franchises that differ from a securities dealer's funding model. Common-stock multiples are not direct substitutes for preferred return analysis. Compare seniority, redemption provisions, dividend capacity, dilution and warrant tenor before deciding whether the proposed security compensates for the risks. [Source above.]

Technical context: a financial institution's earning assets are supported by deposits, wholesale funding, creditors and equity with different seniority. Regulatory capital cannot all be distributed to investors. Analyze credit quality, liquidity and capital separately: a solvent institution can still face a funding run, and liquid assets do not prove all credit losses are recognized. For a preferred claim, identify whether dividends are cumulative, the issuer's redemption rights and the creditor cushion ahead of the security. For common, normalize provisioning and assess return on tangible equity rather than using industrial free-cash-flow conventions. Reported book value, tangible common capital and market capitalization are different denominators. [Source above; analytical framework rather than additional issuer measurements.]

Comparison discipline: compare businesses on the source of their cash and the capital required to earn it, rather than treating the nearest ticker as an exact substitute. Normalize financing and accounting differences before interpreting relative multiples. A peer can have a better balance sheet but a worse franchise, or a better franchise at an unattractive price. Relative cheapness alone cannot prove absolute value. Consider the opportunity cost of adding to an existing understandable holding, retaining liquidity, owning an entire business or buying the investor's own shares. Where a numerical peer table was not recovered, keep the comparison qualitative rather than inventing an industry average.

Evidence boundary: missing figures are not available in contemporaneous sources recovered for this digest. Analytical questions are not additional reported facts or claims about the investor's private thinking. Read the financial, risk and fund-context documents together; no individual accounting ratio supplies a complete investment conclusion.
