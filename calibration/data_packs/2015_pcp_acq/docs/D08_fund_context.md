Title: Berkshire context without the allocation answer
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/79958/000007995815000049/pcp2015062810-q.htm (published 2015-08-07); https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2014ltr.pdf (last public fund baseline identified in text)
Date: 2015-08-09

Berkshire's approximately $63.3 billion year-end 2014 cash provides the metadata scale. Existing public shares establish familiarity but are not equivalent to control. A full acquisition could be large relative to liquid resources and would need to preserve insurance liquidity. The new mode denotes a different security and governance proposition: a permanent wholly owned industrial business rather than a small marketable holding. [Source above.]

The 2014 letter reports $83.921 billion float and identifies American Express, Coca-Cola, IBM and Wells Fargo as its Big Four. Their aggregate unrealized gains were approximately $42 billion at year-end. Embedded tax and confidence in durable franchises create an opportunity-cost hurdle for redeploying capital. Float remains an obligation-supported funding source, not cash free of claims. [Fund baseline cited above.]

Technical context: qualified industrial production can have a moat based on process expertise, certification and reliability rather than a consumer brand. Long program lives support repeat business but customer concentration can transfer bargaining power to the aircraft or engine maker. Backlog is not distributable cash and can move with production schedules. Test inventory, material costs, maintenance investment and acquisition accounting when translating reported profit into owner earnings. A whole-business price includes common equity and assumed net obligations; using enterprise value with after-interest earnings mismatches numerator and denominator. Normalize across relevant end markets instead of treating one strong period as permanent. [Source above; analytical framework.]

Allocation discipline: Berkshire is an operating and insurance company, not a redeemable investment partnership. The metadata liquidity figure is a dated scale reference, not contemporaneous discretionary NAV. Cash supports insurance, subsidiaries and contractual obligations before any new allocation. Equity-portfolio weight, percentage of issuer ownership and percentage of cash are different sizing measures. An existing position's basis affects tax but does not determine current intrinsic value. Distinguish a deputy's mandate from a Buffett-scale commitment, and avoid assigning a named decision maker without evidence. This context does not disclose the action being evaluated; the analyst must weigh opportunity cost and the durability of the underlying business independently.

Evidence boundary: missing figures are not available in contemporaneous sources recovered for this digest. Analytical questions are not additional reported facts or claims about the investor's private thinking. Read the financial, risk and fund-context documents together; no individual accounting ratio supplies a complete investment conclusion.
