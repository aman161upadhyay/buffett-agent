# 1989 Berkshire annual letter

**Information date:** 1990-03-02 (month-only where precise public release day was not independently established). **Era:** B. **Author:** Buffett. **Primary source:** [letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1989.html). All rules below cite sections of this source; section names are used rather than fabricated PDF page numbers. Annual-period labels are not publication dates.

## Process rules and decision explanations

- Mistakes of the First Twenty-five Years: Berkshire control was enticed by cheapness despite weak textile economics; cheap purchase price did not make capital reinvestment attractive.
- Same section: quality at a fair price replaces indiscriminate cigar-butt reasoning; Munger pushed this change. The statement is retrospective, not a 1972 transcript.
- Preferred-stock discussion: Gillette and USAir were negotiated convertibles with coupons, redemption and conversion terms. Different underlying economics are not erased by similar security labels.
- Arbitrage remained a substitute for short-term cash equivalents, so the franchise shift did not abolish workouts.
- Financing: zero-coupon issuance, conversion and tax accrual require valuing future obligations; no inference that noncash interest is free funding.

## Verbatim evidence anchors

- **Mistakes of the First Twenty-five Years (A Condensed Version); Acquisition of Berkshire Hathaway textile business.** “My first mistake, of course, was in buying control of Berkshire. Though I knew its business - textile manufacturing - to be unpromising, I was enticed to buy because the price looked cheap.”
- **Marketable Securities; Purchase of USAir Group, Inc. convertible preferred stock.** “We next purchased $358 million of USAir Group, Inc. preferred stock with mandatory redemption in ten years, a dividend of 9 1/4%, and the right to convert into common at $60 per share.”
- **Marketable Securities; Purchase of The Gillette Co. convertible preferred stock.** “In the first transaction, which took place in July, we purchased $600 million of The Gillette Co. preferred with an 8 3/4% dividend, a mandatory redemption in ten years, and the right to convert into common at $50 per share.”
- **Sources of Reported Earnings; Resignation from the U.S. League of Savings Institutions.** “In addition, we have reprinted on page 71 Charlie's May 30, 1989 letter to the U. S. League of Savings Institutions, which conveyed our disgust with its policies and our consequent decision to resign.”

## Attribution and point-in-time boundary

Buffett is the source author, but an acquired subsidiary’s bolt-on or operating action is not automatically his personally originated investment. Individual decider unknown where not identified. Historical narratives are available only from this information date. A section discussing earlier purchases is retrospective; do not load its later performance, confession or corrected valuation into the original entry replay.
