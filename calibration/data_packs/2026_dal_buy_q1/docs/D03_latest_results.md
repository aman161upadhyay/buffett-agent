Title: Improving sales with a comparison-period caveat
Source: https://ir.delta.com/news/news-details/2025/Delta-Air-Lines-Announces-September-Quarter-2025-Financial-Results/default.aspx
Date: 2025-12-31

The October release reports adjusted revenue growth of 4.1%, premium growth of 9%, loyalty growth of 9% and corporate sales growth of 8%. Non-fuel unit cost rose only 0.3% in the quarter. Those observations indicate improved operating execution and demand for the differentiated product. The same release says comparisons benefited from lapping the previous year's CrowdStrike-caused outage: 2.6 points of total revenue growth and 1.1 points of unit revenue growth. [1]

Management guides to approximately $6 annual adjusted EPS and $3.5-$4.0 billion free cash flow. The December-quarter forecast was $1.60-$1.90 EPS and 10.5%-12% operating margin. Those are forecasts at the release date, not completed December results. The analyst should separate genuine underlying improvement from an easier comparison and should not extrapolate peak seasonal margins across an entire year. The current information set contains no later annual result, no new industry-wide shock and no statement identifying an outside investor's re-entry thesis. [1]

Evidence ledger from the October 9 release: September-quarter GAAP revenue was $16.673 billion, operating income $1.684 billion, pre-tax income $1.777 billion, net income $1.417 billion and diluted EPS $2.17. Adjusted revenue was $15.2 billion, pre-tax income approximately $1.5 billion and EPS $1.71. Operating cash flow was $1.8 billion; reported free cash flow $833 million; gross capital spending approximately $1.1 billion. Debt and finance-lease obligations were $14.9 billion, adjusted net debt $15.6 billion, and liquidity $6.9 billion including $3.1 billion undrawn revolvers. Premium revenue grew 9%, loyalty revenue 9% and American Express remuneration 12% to $2 billion. Full-year adjusted EPS guidance was approximately $6 and free cash flow $3.5-$4.0 billion. [1]

Interpretation: the issuer's latest released quarter is the information boundary, not the quarter containing this assessment date. Forward guidance is management's expectation, not a realized result. Growth percentages must be matched to the stated comparison period and accounting basis. Where adjusted measures differ from GAAP, both are retained rather than mixing the favorable numerator from one with the denominator from the other. A favorable quarter does not settle the durability question; the operational causes and cash conversion still require scrutiny.
