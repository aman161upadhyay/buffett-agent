Title: A September price, not a summer entry multiple
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1652044/000165204425000056/googexhibit991q22025.htm ; https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/department-justice-wins-significant-remedies-against-google ; https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/LSB/PDF/LSB11362/LSB11362.1.pdf
Date: 2025-09-30

Alphabet Class A closed at $243.10 on September 30; Class C's $243.55 close is a different security and should not be substituted silently. MarketWatch's September 30 market-close report records the Class A price and a 38% quarterly rise. For scale, multiplying $243.10 by June's 12.104 billion total shares across all classes gives approximately $2.943 trillion. This is an illustrative capitalization using a stale share count and one class's price, not an exact September market value.

Against that illustrative denominator, $66.728 billion trailing free cash flow yields about 2.27%. The $2.31 quarterly diluted EPS annualized mechanically gives $9.24 and a 26.31 times multiple, not consensus forward EPS. The latest release supplies the financial inputs; the dated market-close report supplies the price. No original purchase price or private underwriting discount is given. Rising capital expenditure makes the difference between accounting earnings and distributable cash particularly important. [1; dated price below]

Evidence ledger: the July 23 release reports second-quarter revenue of $96.428 billion, up 14%; operating income of $31.271 billion; net income of $28.196 billion; and diluted EPS of $2.31. Services revenue was $82.543 billion, Cloud $13.624 billion and Other Bets $373 million. Services operating income was $33.063 billion, Cloud $2.826 billion, Other Bets a $1.246 billion loss and central activities a $3.372 billion cost. Cash plus marketable securities totaled $95.148 billion, versus $23.607 billion long-term debt. Trailing operating cash flow was $133.708 billion, capital expenditures $66.980 billion and free cash flow $66.728 billion. Management increased planned annual capital spending to approximately $85 billion. September court remedies prohibit specified exclusivity arrangements and require access to certain data/services. [1-3]

Valuation discipline: the calculations in this document are analyst arithmetic, not management targets or a Berkshire underwriting model. Earnings multiples, book multiples and cash-flow yields answer different questions and are not interchangeable. A low multiple offers no protection if normalized earning power has been overstated. Conversely, growth has value only after the incremental capital required to obtain it. No verified contemporary consensus forecast or complete historical multiple distribution is supplied; an exact intrinsic value should therefore not be reverse-engineered from a subsequent transaction.
