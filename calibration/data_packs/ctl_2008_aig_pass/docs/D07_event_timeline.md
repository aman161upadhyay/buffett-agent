A buyer can regard the insurance operations as a portfolio of customer relationships whose value may survive investment marks. A negotiated asset transaction could separate productive insurance operations from opaque financial guarantees, subject to regulatory approval and enforceable separation. The May capital raise and June equity balance give an analyst a starting point for diligence, although neither establishes the September cash requirement. [S1; bull interpretation]

### Dated operating baseline

Second-quarter net loss was $5.36 billion, or $2.06 per diluted share, versus $4.28 billion of profit a year earlier. Six-month net loss was $13.16 billion, or $5.11 per share. Adjusted quarterly net loss was $1.32 billion; the adjustment does not make derivatives harmless. AIG Financial Products' super-senior credit-default-swap portfolio generated a $5.56 billion pre-tax unrealized valuation loss in the quarter. Investment impairments and other realized capital losses were $6.08 billion pre-tax. May's approximately $20 billion capital raise included $7.47 billion of common shares, $5.88 billion in equity units and $6.91 billion of high-equity-content fixed maturities. June 30 shareholders' equity was $78.09 billion and book value was $29.04 per share. Consolidated assets were $1.050 trillion. The scale of assets, reported equity and previously raised money is known; the ultimate cash required in a collateral stress is not bounded by these figures. [S1]

### Underwriting distinction

The information set is ordered by publication, not by the date printed on a later historical chart. The financial measurement date can precede the publication date by weeks, so a balance sheet in this record is not automatically a live liquidity statement. Announced future plans are retained as plans. The cutoff also means an investor cannot infer the result of an uncompleted transaction from a later disclosure. [Analytical interpretation of S1]

For the contemplated holding period, identify the next observable operating checkpoint and the evidence needed to distinguish normalization from deterioration. A permanent investor can wait for value realization only if the business and its claim on cash remain intact. The facts below are the current public operating baseline. They are repeated here to make the timeline's financial starting point explicit, not to introduce any post-cutoff result. [Analytical interpretation of S1]
