Title: Dexter Shoe — Security and transaction terms
Source: UPI, September 30, 1993, proposed transaction terms: https://www.upi.com/Archives/1993/09/30/Berkshire-Hathaway-to-buy-Dexter-Shoe-for-420-million/5986749361600/
Date: 1993-09-30

## Instrument and consideration — reconstructed

The September 30 transaction terms call for approximately $420 million in Berkshire stock, specified as 25,221 shares. At the day's quoted $16,675 Berkshire price, that is about $420.56 million, an analyst calculation. This is the announced proposal, not a fixed cash check. Dexter's audited trailing earnings, working-capital needs and maintenance-capital budget have not been recovered. No acquisition earnings multiple is invented. Paying in shares exchanges a continuing fraction of the buyer's operating businesses for the seller's business. That requires valuing both sides. No final issued-share count or retrospective alternative purchase-price estimate is inserted into the proposal as a fact already known.

## Valuation units

The dated proposal is 25,221 Berkshire shares, worth approximately $420 million at the contemporaneous Berkshire quotation of $16,675. There is no traded Dexter share price because the seller is privately held. Without original Dexter cash earnings, no exact pretax multiple or owner-earnings yield can be computed. Stock payment is not economically free because it avoids cash expenditure: the buyer gives up future cash flows represented by the issued interest. No later Berkshire price is used to judge the exchange. Dexter's distribution and manufacturing economics must justify the ownership surrendered, without assuming a control resale or an unsupported national-growth premium.

## Ownership and contractual boundaries

The security and the operating business are separate objects of analysis. A whole-company buyer obtains the residual economics after the business meets its obligations, while a minority shareholder cannot unilaterally direct distributions. Preferred stock normally ranks ahead of common for its specified claims, but its legal priority is not a guarantee of payment and does not supersede all creditors. Conversion changes the form and ranking of exposure rather than creating a second independent asset that can be counted twice.

Where the complete original agreement is unavailable, this summary does not imply that an unmentioned covenant exists or that an unmentioned restriction is absent. Proposed consideration can change in dollar value before closing when the exchange uses shares. Regulatory conditions, issuance date, payment date and accounting acquisition date should be verified separately. No subsequent financial result or investor recommendation is supplied by this terms summary.
