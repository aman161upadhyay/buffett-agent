Title: Acquisition-contingent preferred and warrant capital
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/797468/000095015719000529/form8k.htm (2019-04-30 securities-purchase agreement; capital-provider identity and acceptance withheld)
Date: 2019-04-30

The financing package under consideration is $10 billion cash for 100,000 shares of cumulative perpetual preferred, each carrying $100,000 face value, together with warrants on 80 million common shares. It supports the proposed Anadarko acquisition and is conditional on the transaction. A conditional financing commitment is not the same as an unconditional purchase of common stock on the exchange. [April 30 agreement.]

The preferred's 8% annual cash distribution rate produces an $800 million annual claim on $10 billion principal. That is arithmetic on the stated security, not an estimate of the issuer's incremental operating profit. Cumulative status matters to unpaid obligations but does not transform preferred equity into a senior secured loan. Debt claims stand ahead of preferred, and common capital stands behind it. [April 30 terms; claim analysis.]

The initial warrant strike is $62.50 per common share. Exercising 80 million warrants at that price requires an additional $5 billion. The $10 billion initial cash funding and the potential exercise consideration are different commitments. The warrant count is not current cash-purchased common ownership and should not be added indiscriminately to a common-only quarterly portfolio filing. [April 30 terms; calculated exercise cost.]

A security-level valuation should examine dividend coverage after debt service, the issuer's redemption rights, acquisition leverage and the common option's economic value. No independent option valuation or allocation of the initial purchase price between the two instruments was recovered. It would be misleading to infer such values from the warrant notional or to call the warrants an immediate $5 billion profit. [Analytical interpretation.]

The acquisition introduces execution and commodity risks. Asset dispositions, operating savings and the price of petroleum affect cash available for every financing layer. A preferred can be an attractive claim for a financier while adding a substantial fixed distribution burden to the common owners. These are different perspectives on the same capital structure, not a guarantee that either stakeholder will earn an adequate return. [Analytical stress case.]

The opportunity must also be compared with preserving liquid resources: preferred principal is not redeemable on demand merely because the investor has other cash. This digest withholds the proposed provider, endorsement and accepted allocation. No acquisition completion, actual funding after the cutoff, later common accumulation or realized preferred return is supplied to resolve the underwriting question. [Information-boundary note.]
