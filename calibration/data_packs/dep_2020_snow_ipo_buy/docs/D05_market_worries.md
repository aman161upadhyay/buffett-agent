The company is unprofitable and has a short public financial history. It relies on cloud providers that can also compete, and gross infrastructure expense is a real cost, not costless software replication. Customers may reduce consumption, optimize workloads or buy rival services. Stock compensation and a dual-class structure reduce the connection between headline growth and minority-owner returns. Retention above 100% is a cohort observation, not assurance that every customer expands forever. A premium IPO valuation leaves little room for slower growth. [S1; analytical inference]

There is no public-market close by the cutoff. The offering is priced at $120 on the evening of September 15, above the revised $100–110 indicated range. The pricing release has a September 15 dateline; contemporaneous CNBC reporting at 6:38 p.m. Eastern corroborates availability that evening. At $120, a $250 million primary allocation purchases approximately 2,083,333 shares, while a separate 4,042,043-share secondary block costs $485,045,160. Combined illustrative cash is approximately $735.045 million. Fiscal-year sales of $264.7 million and net losses cannot support a conventional earnings-yield valuation. No first-day trading price is available. [S1,S6; calculations]

### Dated operating baseline

The IPO registration reports fiscal-year revenue of $264.7 million versus $96.7 million, up approximately 174%. Fiscal-year net loss was approximately $348.5 million. For the six months ended July 31, revenue was $242.0 million versus $104.0 million, up approximately 133%, while net loss was $171.3 million versus $177.2 million. Net revenue retention was 158% at July 31. The customer count was 3,117, and 56 customers generated more than $1 million of trailing-twelve-month product revenue. Retention measures the expansion of an existing cohort; it is not consolidated growth or guaranteed contractual revenue. The prospectus describes a consumption model rather than fixed subscription revenue. Two share classes give Class B ten votes per share and Class A one. The contemplated primary issuance is 28 million shares, excluding the possible underwriters' option. A $250 million private placement and a 4,042,043-share secondary block are distinct financing legs. [S1]

### Underwriting distinction

This objection is intended to be capable of defeating the affirmative case. Distinguish a reversible mark-to-market loss from permanent impairment, and a temporary earnings interruption from a permanently more capital-hungry business. The investor must survive the adverse scenario without relying on the next buyer, a friendly regulator or unrestricted external financing. A long holding period cannot restore capital that has been diluted away or consumed by obligations senior to the purchased security. [Analytical interpretation of S1]

Observable thesis-breakers include customer economics deteriorating despite continued spending, new funding coming only on punitive terms, or reported cash failing to reconcile to recurring obligations. These are analytical monitoring conditions, not claims that such outcomes have occurred. The record below supplies the current scale of exposure against which to test them. No later failure, recovery or management admission is used to strengthen the bear case. [Analytical interpretation of S1]
