Title: Berkshire context without the allocation answer
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/46640/000004664012000029/hnz8k102812ex991.htm (published 2012-11-20); https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2011ltr.pdf (last public fund baseline identified in text)
Date: 2013-02-13

The last publicly released annual baseline is year-end 2011, about $37.3 billion cash; this is the metadata scale rather than a not-yet-published current annual figure. Any acquisition must compete with other large investments and preserve insurance liquidity. A partner-operated business would require a clear allocation of capital and operating responsibilities. No accepted ownership arrangement or current investment commitment is established in this document. [Source above.]

The 2011 letter reports about $70 billion float and identifies American Express, Coca-Cola, IBM and Wells Fargo as its four major marketable business interests. Those four are existing alternatives, not a model instruction to keep buying them at any price. Their retained earnings contribute economic value beyond the dividends recognized in Berkshire's income statement. [Fund baseline cited above.]

Technical context: branded food can generate recurring consumer demand, yet retailers control shelf space and private labels create alternatives. Evaluate price, volume and mix independently; a price increase with collapsing units is not unambiguous pricing power. Commodity inflation, foreign exchange, promotion and working-capital seasonality affect cash conversion. A control purchase can use several layers of debt, preferred and common, each with different return rights. The same enterprise can offer an attractive preferred coupon while leaving common owners exposed to leverage. Operating cost reduction should be evaluated alongside brand investment and customer retention, not counted as recurring growth automatically. [Source above; analytical framework.]

Allocation discipline: Berkshire is an operating and insurance company, not a redeemable investment partnership. The metadata liquidity figure is a dated scale reference, not contemporaneous discretionary NAV. Cash supports insurance, subsidiaries and contractual obligations before any new allocation. Equity-portfolio weight, percentage of issuer ownership and percentage of cash are different sizing measures. An existing position's basis affects tax but does not determine current intrinsic value. Distinguish a deputy's mandate from a Buffett-scale commitment, and avoid assigning a named decision maker without evidence. This context does not disclose the action being evaluated; the analyst must weigh opportunity cost and the durability of the underlying business independently.

Evidence boundary: missing figures are not available in contemporaneous sources recovered for this digest. Analytical questions are not additional reported facts or claims about the investor's private thinking. Read the financial, risk and fund-context documents together; no individual accounting ratio supplies a complete investment conclusion.
