## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**1958 observation:** Sanborn published detailed fire-insurance maps. Its quoted stock price was approximately $45, while the investment portfolio was worth approximately $65/share, according to Buffett's subsequent account. The operating franchise faced competing “carding” underwriting methods. Do not treat the combined **1958–1959** operating-profit comparison, reported afterward, as a fully known 1958 financial statement. Exact entry month and original information memorandum are unknown. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“This means, in effect, that the buyer of Sanborn stock in 1938 was placing a positive valuation of $90 per share on the map business ($110 less the $20 value of the investments unrelated to the map business) in a year of depressed business and stock market conditions.” **Retrospective**, 1960 annual partnership letter. The following sentence says the more vigorous 1958 market effectively valued that business at **minus $20**, paying only about 70 cents per dollar of investment portfolio. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
The predecessor partnerships, not Berkshire, accumulated Sanborn and Buffett joined its board. He described a position of about 24,000 shares and opposition to portfolio separation from incumbent directors. In 1960 a negotiated exchange allowed holders of approximately 72% of outstanding stock to receive investment assets, while the map company retained a reserve and its business. This was an in-kind restructuring, not a simple whole-company cash takeover. [1]

## What happened after
The investment portfolio was separated and the highly concentrated position resolved in 1960. Buffett explained that it had represented approximately 35% of partnership net assets. The letter supports a successful value-release catalyst; exact trade-level realized dollar profit and tax treatment are not reconstructed here. [1]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Separable assets:** quoted price below portfolio value provided the quantitative starting point. [1]
- **Business/asset divergence:** poor map earnings did not imply the marketable securities were impaired. [1]
- **Governance route:** coordinated ownership and board negotiations converted a discount into distributable assets. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: asset-value activism rather than the later passive ownership of a compounding consumer franchise. The catalyst required agency, not merely faith that an irrational quotation would correct itself. [1]

## Public discussion
The letter records incumbent resistance and disagreement over the use of the securities portfolio, a concrete counterposition. Original newspaper reports and proxy materials remain unrecovered; no modern discussion is presented as 1958 sentiment. [1]
