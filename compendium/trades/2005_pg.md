## Situation before the trade (point-in-time)
**2005:** Berkshire already owned Gillette. P&G stock entered through the fourth-quarter merger; this is not a fresh purchase of the entire reported market value. [1]

## Buffett's thesis in his own words
“We didn't intend to sell our Gillette shares before the merger; we don't intend to sell our P&G shares now.” **February 2006 explanation**, not a promise never to change allocation. [1]

## What Berkshire Hathaway did
Received P&G stock and recognized a required $5 billion pretax accounting gain without incurring merger tax. Buffett explicitly said the bookkeeping gain was economically meaningless for evaluating the year's earnings. [1]

## What happened after
Sold portions in 2008 to help finance more attractive crisis securities while maintaining liquidity. That is not evidence the original consumer-franchise thesis vanished. [2]

## Reasoning drivers checklist
- **Franchise:** Continued ownership rather than automatic cash-out. [1]
- **Management:** Kilts's capital/operating discipline increased Gillette value. [1]
- **Combination:** Buffett saw expanded potential in the merger. [1]

## Why this trade fits / breaks his pattern
Research interpretation: distinguish accounting realization, tax realization and business-value creation. [1]

## Public discussion
Independent contemporary merger objections not recovered; the letter is a supportive shareholder's account. [1]
