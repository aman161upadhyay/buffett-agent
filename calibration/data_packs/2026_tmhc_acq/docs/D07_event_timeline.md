Title: A public operating update before any control terms
Source: https://s27.q4cdn.com/448041563/files/doc_earnings/2026/q1/earnings-result/TMHC-1Q26-Earnings-Release_Final.pdf
Date: 2026-05-29

April 22: first-quarter results establish reported EPS of $1.01, adjusted EPS of $1.12, book value per share of $64 and a sequentially improved backlog. They also establish year-on-year revenue and margin declines. May 29 is the information boundary for this assessment; no later announcement or agreement is an input. The company's own quarterly repurchase price of approximately $61 is historical, not a May quote. [1]

The analyst can evaluate whether the management team, locations and diversified product strategy warrant investigation by a permanent-capital owner. That does not establish that a transaction is offered or identify its terms. No equity price, enterprise price, financing package, closing timetable, shareholder vote or combined-platform structure is supplied before it is public. This boundary deliberately leaves a harder underwriting problem than an announced-merger arbitrage: there is no spread to annualize and no signed deal to de-risk. A business-quality judgment is possible; a precise negotiated return is not recoverable from the public record alone.

Evidence ledger from April 22: first-quarter total revenue was $1.387092 billion, home closings revenue $1.311421 billion and net income $98.625 million. Reported diluted EPS was $1.01, adjusted $1.12. There were 2,268 closings at $578,000 average price and 2,914 net orders at $603,000. Backlog was 3,465 homes worth $2.3 billion. Home closings gross margin was 20.0%, adjusted 20.6%, versus 24.0% and 24.8% a year earlier. The company owned/controlled 75,626 lots, 51% off balance sheet, and invested $503 million in land/development. Liquidity was approximately $1.6 billion including $653 million cash; net homebuilding debt/capital was 20.5%. Book value per share was $64. The company repurchased about 2.5 million shares for $150 million at roughly $61 per share. [1]

Timing discipline: the chronology separates publication dates from the economic periods discussed. A result for an earlier quarter only becomes an input when released. The market close is an observation available on the stated assessment date, not evidence of a particular investor's execution. This timeline includes no presumed private conversations or unannounced agreement. There is no catalyst completion forecast here: the analyst must decide whether the available business evidence is sufficient without relying on a future disclosure to reveal someone else's decision.
