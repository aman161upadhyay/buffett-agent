Title: Berkshire context without the allocation answer
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1108329/000114554907000420/h01096e6vk.txt (published 2007-03-20); https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2006ltr.pdf (last public fund baseline identified in text)
Date: 2007-07-12

This is an existing-position review. Berkshire's previously disclosed stake and historical cost are context, not a recommendation. The metadata uses approximately $43.7 billion year-end 2006 Berkshire cash, not current equity NAV. Realizing a large embedded gain would create a material tax cost. Continuing to hold must be judged against current earning power and alternative uses of capital rather than an attachment to historical cost. [Source above.]

The 2006 letter reports $50.887 billion year-end insurance float. Float is investable money held against future claims, not shareholders' cash or an amount to add mechanically to cash when calculating spendable capital. Coca-Cola, American Express and Wells Fargo are established large equity positions; their continuing economics provide alternatives to a new allocation. [Fund baseline cited above.]

Technical context: petroleum accounting profits depend on realized prices, volumes, royalties, taxes and the treatment of exploration and development costs. A reserve is not cash in the bank: it needs extraction, transport and often replacement investment. Distinguish upstream earnings from refining and chemical margins rather than assuming they move together. For owner earnings, separate maintenance needed to sustain economic production from expansion capital. Check whether reported free cash flow deducts preferred distributions and whether acquisitions are being called capital expenditure. Proved reserves, production and reserve life use different units and assumptions. A conservative analysis should explicitly test a lower realized-price case without claiming to forecast the commodity. [Source above; analytical accounting framework, not additional reported figures.]

Allocation discipline: Berkshire is an operating and insurance company, not a redeemable investment partnership. The metadata liquidity figure is a dated scale reference, not contemporaneous discretionary NAV. Cash supports insurance, subsidiaries and contractual obligations before any new allocation. Equity-portfolio weight, percentage of issuer ownership and percentage of cash are different sizing measures. An existing position's basis affects tax but does not determine current intrinsic value. Distinguish a deputy's mandate from a Buffett-scale commitment, and avoid assigning a named decision maker without evidence. This context does not disclose the action being evaluated; the analyst must weigh opportunity cost and the durability of the underlying business independently.

Evidence boundary: missing figures are not available in contemporaneous sources recovered for this digest. Analytical questions are not additional reported facts or claims about the investor's private thinking. Read the financial, risk and fund-context documents together; no individual accounting ratio supplies a complete investment conclusion.
